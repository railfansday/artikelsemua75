Diplomats and delegates from nearly 200 countries are meeting in Antalya, Turkey.

A fresh draft deal omits any mention of how to phase out fossil fuels, in what campaigners call a step 'backwards' and a 'glaring regression'.

Like in previous years, the climate summit is likely to be extended until a consensus is reached.

These are the major developments from the closing phase of the UN's COP31 Climate Summit in Antalya, Turkey, on Friday, November 20, 2026:.

Shortly after 9 p.m. local time in Antalya, delegates gathered for a plenary session, well past the official closing time.

The Turkish presidency's opening address was partly drowned out by a protester shouting objections in English such as "you are killing the future," and gaining applause within the chamber. (Related: Latest Update: Turkish Miles & Smiles — Hawaii for ...)

Industry observers note that "We've been negotiating for days to find the common ground," COP31 President Murat Kurum, Turkey's environment minister, told the plenary. He hailed the progress made and said the world "waits for good news from this room.". (Related: Industry News: Judge blocks construction of Trump’s arc...)

But the plenary session noted how several of the more ambitious aspects of a political declaration remained incomplete, with no agreement yet reached at the summit.

Several national delegates spoke during the plenary to bemoan the elusive progress or even backsliding during the two weeks of negotiations.

The evening's session, however, has been dealing with technical challenges, and the possibility that discussions continue into the weekend remains.

Attacks on climate science and disinformation risk delaying key measures at COP31 in Antalya. As the UN climate talks draw to a close, how will that affect efforts to come to an agreement?

The UN's IPCC should have already started work on its next series of multi-year scientific assessments on climate change's progress, but the process is on hold at the urging of countries including fossil fuel-producing giants Russia, Saudi Arabia and Iran.

In a recent development, the US government is snubbing UN climate talks in Turkey. That's prompted a delegation of American lawmakers, mayors and governors to make the trip instead, aiming to show that climate action in the US is alive and well.

Head over to this report to learn more about who is representing the US at the summit, even if President Donald Trump, who has repeatedly dismissed global warming as a "hoax", didn't send a formal delegation for the second year in a row.

According to recent reports, funding from wealthy countries to help deal with the impacts of climate change hasn't materialized, claims Uganda's environment minister. The East African country is hoping oil and carbon credits will help boost finances.

Learn more about what Uganda's environment minister Beatrice Atim Anywar remarked about climate financing in this report.

Veteran climate justice advocate Victor Menotti of the Demand Climate Justice NGO told DW that European governments appeared to be copying US President Donald Trump's negotiating playbook at COP this year.

It has been revealed that "We know the US isn't here, but we can see that Germany and other European governments have learned a few of the Trump tactics, maybe in the trade disputes with the US," Menotti remarked. "And we see them applying here in Antalya, where it's like: Don't admit to anything. Try to dilute your responsibilities. Weaken whatever obligations you have. And then take even more.".

He argued these countries were also expecting unreasonable or unfeasible commitments from developing countries, for instance restrictions on what they can spend money on and obligations to report on their expenditures.

"Germany is subsidizing quite a bit of coal still and fossil fuels, LNG expansion infrastructure," he said, arguing that wealthier countries' energy spending also had room for improvement.

Recent findings suggest that "We need the North to act first," Menotti said. "The deal from Paris, the deal from Rio 30 years ago, is that the North will lead. But yet we see too much bullying of the global South and not enough leadership like they agreed to.".

You can cross off another box on your COP31 bingo cards, dear readers.

As of the top of this hour, the session has officially gone past its scheduled end time of 1500 GMT/UTC on Friday, or 6 p.m. local time on site in Turkey.

Sources indicate that this is something of an annual tradition at COP summits, which are always scheduled to finish at the end of a working week but also almost invariably run into the closing weekend. Delegates and members of the media tend to book their travel plans with this in mind.

The revised draft declaration, which critics have slammed as "watered down" due to the omission of fossil fuels, highlights the "electrification" of the global economy as a central pillar of the energy transition.

"Electrification is the key to transitioning away from fossil fuels," Australian Climate Minister Chris Bowen told reporters.

What are the key points to know about the "35-by-35" electrification target?

Industry observers note that but the text does not specify what energy sources would supply the electricity. Some countries are demanding that the text call for "clean" electrification to clearly favor renewable sources over coal, oil or gas.

Bowen, who has been trying to secure compromises, pledged to continue working over the "coming hours, for as long as it takes, to get the job done.".

According to recent reports, "People have strong views of course — I am not stepping away from that. But no one has said to me let's give up and go home," Bowen said.

The Climate Action Network (CAN), a global network of over 2,500 civil society organizations, has accused wealthy developed nations, led by the EU, "of blocking every track" towards a substantive agreement on fighting climate change and curbing fossil fuel use.

At its final press conference of the summit, CAN executive director Tasneem Essop said the group was "rejecting what is currently on the table.".

Sources indicate that "There is nothing (...) that protects people or addresses the priorities of the Global South. In fact, the opposite is true. The current text has opened the door to the most harmful and dangerous options of any COP so far," Essop mentioned.

She pointed out that this year's COP had the highest ever number of lobbyists for fossil fuels, for AI, for CCUS (Carbon Capture, Utilization, and Storage) and for CDR (Carbon Dioxide Removal), which only represent corporate interests and do not have the interests of the people from developing nations who are most at risk from global warming in mind.

"What we’re leaving this COP with is decisions made to promote the interests of those corporations making profits from the destruction of people," Essop said.

Industry observers note that mohamed Adow from Power Shift Africa, which is also part of CAN, mentioned: "The COP Presidents want to tell us this has been a great COP. Well, I can tell you without any doubt that this has been the worst COP ever.

"Overseeing a failing COP is bad enough, but trying to sell it as a success rubs salt into the wounds of those people who need it to deliver.".

Adow cautioned that communities that live with the constant threat of drought, floods and failed harvests were "promised action.".

"They cannot survive on an annual renewal of promises," Adow said.

He laid the blame squarely with the world's most developed nations: "They demand action from developing countries, then block public finance, block Just Transition, block climate finance, and they expect ambition. You cannot keep your foot on the brake and complain that countries are not moving fast enough.".

Xiye Bastida, a New York-based youth activist from Mexico, spoke to DW's Katharina Schantz to describe her frustration over the lack of progress and ambition on display at this year's COP Summit, which is the seventh the 24-year-old has attended.

Bastida, who has played a leading role in the Fridays for Future climate movement in New York, stated she did not think she "could be more disappointed" at how climate targets are "being rolled back.".

"This is really the time of reckoning," Bastida said.

She explained that she had changed her flight aims to be able to stay until the end, because she feels "we have to be here. We at least to be witnesses of how they're taking things away.".

Bastida pointed to the hypocrisy from wealthy nations, who have blamed some from the Global South for not wanting the summit's joint declaration to mention fossil fuels.

"But they (the Global North) are the ones not bringing the money, and they're the ones who are not bringing the ambition.

"And they want to show themselves as the leaders. But if you want to mention fossil fuels so bad, where's the money for the transition? Where are the roadmaps?

"Are you serious?" Bastida concluded.

The new draft declaration, which, as we've reported, has been panned by the EU and civil society, has also disappointed some of the world's least developed countries (LDCs) that are most vulnerable to climate change.

The LDC Group, which comprises 44 of the world's least wealthy nations, came into this year's COP summit hoping for stronger climate action than what has been achieved so far.

"It’s a moment of anticipation," Lamin Sheriff, a press attache for the Sierra Leone government, told DW's Katharina Schantz.

According to recent reports, "Ministers from LDCs have requests on the table for the presidency. So whilst they wait on what the outcome would be, everybody is waiting patiently. So the moment is really: we are hopeful for the future, but we are also worried because if proper care is not taken, all what we have debated about here in the last couple of weeks will just be a talk show (…) We can't do anything at the moment.".

From clashes over fossil fuels to disputes over climate finance, COP31 exposes the contradictions at the heart of global climate diplomacy.

Here's the latest dispatch from DW's Tim Schauenberg, who is in Antalya covering the COP31 summit for us:.

It has been revealed that "Formally speaking, the final hours of the climate conference have begun. The countries' pavilions have been coming down since Thursday: empty halls, chopped-up timber, lonely lecterns.

Things don't look quite so bleak yet at the negotiations in Antalya, Turkey, and yet, time is running out here. If this is indeed to be, as the COP31 President has already announced, the "best COP ever," the delegations here will have to step up their efforts considerably.

At present, the corridors are buzzing with discussions amongst scientists, NGOs and media representatives. The delegations are doing what they are here to do: negotiating. So, the long wait has begun — not just for a final decision, but also for substance.

Because that is what has been lacking here in Antalya.

It has been revealed that under discussion is a global target to generate 35% of primary energy from electricity by 2035. The EU is determined that this electricity should be generated from solar and wind power. Other countries, particularly those reliant on fossil fuels, want to block the target altogether.

Industry observers note that positions have hardened on this issue — and others — even after two weeks of negotiations. An EU spokesperson is currently making her way through the media center; anyone who doesn't bump into her in the corridors will receive a phone call. The message: the EU will not accept the latest draft of the negotiations as it stands.

It has been revealed that this is partly because the glitch of carbon capture and storage features prominently in the presidency's latest summary of the negotiations. However, this is expensive and technically complex, and will not halt the climate crisis. Nor is there currently any consensus on turning our backs on the root cause — namely, the burning of coal, oil and gas.

The likelihood that an agreement on this last point will be reached is close to zero, since all 197 countries must agree to this, and Saudi Arabia, Russia, Iran and India have no interest in fundamentally questioning their revenue streams and energy aims in the near future.

So the coffee stands are well stocked, and everyone is keeping at least one eye on the flickering screens in the long halls that display all the events on today's agenda. So far, there is a gaping void when it comes to the sessions intended for the press. That could change at any moment — or take hours.

Quite a few media representatives have already brought bags full of provisions with them this morning. They are clearly preparing for a long weekend.".

Climate and social justice activist Harjeet Singh has called on wealthy nations to "stop blocking" efforts to phase out fossil fuels and cut harmful emissions.

In a statement sent to DW reporters at the COP31 summit in Antalya, Singh said, "Civil society firmly rejects this COP31 draft text" which omitted any mention of fossil fuels, despite these being established by scientists as the main causes of climate change.

Singh, Global Convenor of the Fill the Fund campaign, also highlighted that the declaration failed to address funding for developing nations that would help them transition away from fossil fuels.

"Our message to rich countries is simple: stop blocking. Put real money on the table for adaptation, for loss and damage, and for developing countries to move away from fossil fuels. And cut your own emissions at home, in line with your fair share. You caused this crisis. You have the money. What you lack is the will, and history will judge you for it," he stated.

The draft text, presented on Thursday night, "erases fossil fuels, deletes the money and buries the just transition," Singh remarked, adding that climate NGOs would not sign it in its current form.

"We call on developing country governments not to sign away their people's survival for a deal that offers workshops instead of finance," he remarked.

European Union negotiators have told German news agency dpa that the current COP31 draft declaration, which omits any mention of fossil fuels, is "not acceptable," as they aired their frustration with the current state of negotiations.

The summit is formally set to end at 6 p.m. local time (1500 UTC), but it is likely that the deadline will be extended, as it has in previous years.

The draft declaration's main proposal aims to increase electricity's share of global energy consumption from 23% to 35% by encouraging nations to favor cleaner electric alternatives over fossil-fuel-powered cars, heating systems and industrial processes in order to cut emissions.

Some 90 governments, including many EU states, back the proposal, although it is likely to be watered down amid opposition from oil-producing states.

Meanwhile, a number of African countries have said they will only support the electrification target if they receive increased funding to help deal with the impacts of climate change.

A Turkish seismic research vessel is set to start searching for oil and gas off the coast of Pakistan next year, Turkish Energy Minister Alparslan Bayraktar mentioned Friday.

The announcement that Turkey will start drilling offshore for oil and gas resources comes as the country hosts COP31.

"At a time when global oil and natural gas markets are experiencing a historic supply shock, we are bringing the experience we have gained in our own waters and our domestic engineering expertise to our friend and brother, Pakistan," Bayraktar stated in a post on X.

Turkey's state oil organization ​TPAO signed hydrocarbon exploration and production agreements last year with several ​Pakistani companies for three offshore fields in Pakistan, as well as two onshore fields.

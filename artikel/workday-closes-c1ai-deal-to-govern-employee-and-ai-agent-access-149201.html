📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Workday declared on November 23, 2026 that it had completed its acquisition of C1.ai, adding identity governance software for employees and AI agents to its HR, finance and IT platform. The deal is intended to extend Workday’s workforce records into a security layer that controls what people and agents can reach across business applications and cloud infrastructure.

Sources indicate that workday plans to combine its employee data with C1.ai’s identity controls to simplify access management and reduce manual processes. The intended system would govern permissions, monitor activity and apply policy-driven security controls for human workers and autonomous agents. Sources indicate that workday announced the definitive agreement on October 13, 2026, and expected the deal to close in the fourth quarter of its fiscal 2027 year, ending January 31, 2027, subject to customary closing conditions. The completed acquisition falls within that timetable.

It has been revealed that workday already records the workforce events that trigger permission changes: hiring, job moves and departures. Its planned C1.ai integration would act on those events across email, customer-relationship management, code repositories, finance systems and cloud infrastructure, granting access when needed and removing it when no longer required. Manual administration through service tickets, spreadsheets and periodic reviews is the process the acquisition is meant to streamline. Workday expects the planned workflow to shorten employees’ waits for permissions, reduce manual approvals for managers and remove outdated access that could remain available to attackers.

It has been revealed that workday’s Agent System of Record already gives customers a place to see and manage agents. C1.ai is expected to build on that registry by governing what agents can access across Workday and other enterprise systems, rather than simply recording their existence. Access could be granted when an agent needs it and revoked immediately when its work or role ends.

Workday distinguishes agents acting on behalf of an employee, which may need only part of that person’s access, from autonomous agents whose broader permissions require closer oversight. Under that model, registration is only the first step: governance also depends on why an agent has access and when that access should end.

C1.ai’s current platform description says agents can receive policy-controlled access to models, tools and credentials as they operate, with human approval for sensitive changes. The platform also lists tools for discovering unsanctioned AI tools, agents and credentials, and for vaulting secrets while replacing plaintext keys in configuration files with vault references.

In a recent development, those features extend the acquisition beyond conventional employee access reviews into AI-specific administration: finding unmanaged agents, protecting the credentials they use and inserting human approval into sensitive operations. They also add enforcement while agents are working, alongside the lifecycle workflow triggered by HR changes.

Gerrit Kazmaier, Workday’s president of product and technology, remarked, “Today’s combined workforce of people and AI agents requires a upcoming approach to identity governance and security.” He described the acquisition as an investment in governing access, monitoring activity and enforcing policies so people and agents remain within enterprise guardrails.

The acquisition therefore ties Workday’s agent strategy to enforcement rather than inventory alone. Workforce and agent records identify who or what should have access, while C1.ai contributes mechanisms to grant, monitor and revoke it. The combined platform’s significance rests on applying those controls consistently across Workday and the external systems where employees and agents operate.

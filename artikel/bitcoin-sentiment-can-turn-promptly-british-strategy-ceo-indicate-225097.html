Andrew Webley, CEO of The Smarter Web Tech giant, said that Bitcoin's drop has been sharper than he had anticipated, but he does not think the digital asset's price will decline much further.

The British CEO, whose company recently added 55 BTC to its treasury, stated investors' opinions on Bitcoin can shift considerably without any changes to Bitcoin itself. (Related: Tech Report: Rockstar explains why GTA 6 added its Crim...)

Industry observers note that "Sentiment towards Bitcoin can change very quickly. Without Bitcoin changing at all," Webley stated on X. (Related: Industry News: Uber Eats driver saves elderly woman aft...)

Industry observers note that webley advised investors to consider Bitcoin's movements relative to other assets. Bitcoin's price decline indicates investors how much the cryptocurrency has lost in absolute terms.

It has been revealed that however, Bitcoin's performance relative to the broader market may indicate BTC is being sold specifically or whether investors are taking profits across multiple markets. Webley did not specify which assets he was comparing Bitcoin against.

"Do I expect Bitcoin to go significantly lower from here? No. But as the past few weeks have shown, predicting short-term price movements is a very challenging task," Webley said.

Some of the most challenging periods for investors, he remarked, were followed by some of his most profitable periods.

His most definitive prediction concerned Bitcoin's longer-term outlook: "Bitcoin has an incredible future.".

In fact, Webley's tech giant has been buying more Bitcoin as its price has been dropping.

On Nov. 17, Smarter Web said it spent around £2.75 million on 55 BTC, bringing its total holdings to 2,905 BTC.

The average purchase price was £49,991 per Bitcoin, roughly 38% below the treasury's overall average purchase price of £81,234 following the transaction.

It has been revealed that smarter Web borrowed around £2.6 million under its Coinbase credit facility to finance most of the purchase. The company used its existing Bitcoin holdings as collateral.

Following the transaction, Smarter Web had around £19 million outstanding under the dollar-denominated credit facility.

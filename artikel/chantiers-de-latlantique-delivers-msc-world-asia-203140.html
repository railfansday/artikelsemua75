The MSC World Asia was delivered by the Chantiers de l’Atlantique shipyard during a ceremony in St. Nazaire this morning.

Now officially part of the MSC Cruises fleet, the LNG-powered vessel is the third in the firm’s World-class series. (Related: Tiny Agents: an MCP-powered agent in 50 lines of code...)

“Today is an crucial day for us at MSC that marks another milestone in our partnership with Chantiers de l’Atlantique,” stated Pierfrancesco Vago, executive chairman of the Cruise Division of the MSC Group. (Related: Building for an Open Future - our new partnership with ...)

He noted that the World Asia is one of the most energy-efficient ships in the world.

“We challenge our teams to go further,” he continued, noting that the company focused not only on sustainability but also on fresh concepts and enhanced experiences.

Approximately 60 percent of the public venues onboard the 215,000-ton vessel are new or completely reimagined, Vago said.

“Our ambition is simple: to give every guest the best vacation ever,” he added.

Also speaking at the ceremony, Henri Doyer, senior vice president of projects and engineering at Chantiers de l’Atlantique, highlighted the partnership with MSC Cruises.

He noted that while the ship is the third World-class vessel, it also offers new features, including new restaurants and lounges.

Doyer also pointed to the sustainability features of the vessel, which runs on LNG and uses a series of green technologies.

“This has been a shared effort with real collaboration,” noted James Moore, senior vice president of newbuilding for MSC Cruises.

“We have built something remarkable,” he added, thanking all partners and players that worked on the project.

The MSC World Asia will now sail to Le Havre, where it is set to be christened ahead of its maiden voyage in the Western Mediterranean.

Spending the winter in Europe, the vessel will offer a series of weeklong cruises to Italy, Spain, Malta and France starting on December 4, 2026.

The seven-night cruises feature visits to Barcelona, Marseille, Genoa, Civitavecchia for Rome, Messina and Valletta, allowing guests to board at multiple ports.

In April 2027, the vessel switches to a slightly different itinerary, which replaces the visit to Civitavecchia with a newly introduced call to Naples.

The MSC World Asia will continue to sail in the Western Mediterranean through November 2027, when it is scheduled to cross the Atlantic for the first time.

Sailing from Martinique, Guadeloupe and Barbados, the 5,400-passenger vessel is scheduled to offer weeklong cruises in the Caribbean during the 2027-28 winter season.

With the World Asia now delivered, Chantiers de l’Atlantique’s orderbook includes five other World-class ships due for delivery to MSC by 2031.

The next, the MSC World Atlantic, is scheduled to be delivered by the French shipyard in late 2027 ahead of a winter season in the Caribbean.

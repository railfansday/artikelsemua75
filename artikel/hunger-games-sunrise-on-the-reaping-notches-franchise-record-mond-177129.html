It has been revealed that ENHANCEMENT AFTER EXCLUSIVE: The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping has notched a Monday record for the franchise with $12.88M, ahead of 2013’s Catching Fire which did $12.3M. The movie is bound to be No. 1 again at the U.S. box office with at least $60M over the 5-day Thanksgiving holiday, and potentially much more (some think even $70M). Note that whenever there was a Hunger Games movie (except for the first one which bowed in March 2012) in release, it has always ranked No. 1 over the Thanksgiving 5-day with the biggest hauls being for Catching Fire ($109.9M), Mockingjay Part 1 ($82.6M), Mockingjay Part 2 ($75.9M) with Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes way behind ($42.2M). EXCLUSIVE: After scoring the best domestic opening weekend for Lionsgate in 12 years, The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping, could notch a Monday franchise record of $12M+.

The previous Monday record for the feature take of the Suzanne Collins’ novels belonged to 2013’s Catching Fire which did $12.3M. Seriously, Lionsgate resuscitated life back into this YA series after it hit an all-time low opening and result three years with The Hunger Games: Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes ($44.6M domestic opening, $166.3M North American take, $363.9M worldwide). The first Monday of Songbirds & Snakes came in at $5.2M.

If Sunrise of the Reaping‘s Monday eases below Catching Fire‘s, then it will wind up being the second best Monday in the franchise ahead of 2012’s The Hunger Games ($10.8M).

The fifth directed movie in the six-title franchise has enough mojo to lead throughout Thanksgiving per sources with a least $50M+, if not much more over the Wednesday through Sunday frame, well head of newly introduced openers, Universal/Paramount’s Focker-in-Law and Walt Disney Animation’s original movie Hexed, both at $45M apiece.

In a recent development, sunrise on the Reaping came in a little lighter than the $120M forecasted with $119.3M, still the fourth best in the Lionsgate Collins canon after 2013’s Catching Fire ($70.9M Friday/previews, $158M 3-day), the first 2012 Hunger Games ($67.2M Friday/previews and $152.5M opening), and 2014’s Hunger Games: Mockingjay Part 1 ($55.1M Friday/previews and $121.8M 3-day). Catching Fire still owns the record as Lionsgate’s biggest opening ever at the North American box office.

Sunrise on the Reaping posted a $55.6M Friday (versus $56M reported yesterday, which included $21M previews), Saturday of $36.1M (versus $36.3M) and Sunday of $27.5M (versus $27.3M). The running cume through four days on the Joseph Zada, Mckenna Grace, Elle Fanning, Jesse Plemons, Ralph Fiennes and Kieran Culkin ensemble.

Before Sunrise on the Reaping, the last big opening for Lionsgate at the domestic box office was 2014’s The Hunger Games: Mockingjay Part 1.

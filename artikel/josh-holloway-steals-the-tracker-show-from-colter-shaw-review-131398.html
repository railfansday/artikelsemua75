Industry observers note that tracker may have outdone itself this week by pairing Colter Shaw (Justin Hartley) with an unlikely ally who couldn't be more different from our hero on paper. "That's The Job" brings newcomer Josh Holloway into the picture as bounty hunter/gun-for-hire Luke Kelso, who finds himself on the wrong side of Colter's latest job. Just after our favorite rewardist united with his entire cast last week, the CBS program introduces a one-off character who we hope to see again.

Sources indicate that this week's episode picks up in Gray Haven, South Carolina, where bed and breakfast owner John Iddo (Tinpo Lee) closes everything down for the night. However, upon seeing someone outside, he goes out to investigate, only to be suddenly attacked. The next day, Colter is on the road when he gets a call from his latest flame, Jenna Nova (Gillian Bolt), who is on a job collecting a vintage car with her father, Mario (Daniel Edward Mora). The pair rapid-fire some banter about drinks and darts, reminding us why this pair work so well together. It's a sweet little moment as Colter pulls into his next job, and quite the juxtaposition with how the episode ends. Arriving at the B&B, Colter speaks with Eliza (Ruby Jay), John's employee and daughter figure who is concerned about his disappearance. Colter presses her to tell him everything, and Eliza reveals that he takes sleeping pills for insomnia. Walking outside, he finds some blood and evidence of a struggle, as well as what looks like a kill switch for a water sports vehicle. It's then that Eliza reveals that John had gotten into a recent confrontation with some jet ski guys who may have stolen credit card information from B&B guests. Tracker works because the case changes every week and the four people solving it never do. Here is which of them you are, what it says about you, and the case for yours being the best of the four.

According to recent reports, you would rather handle it yourself and you are usually capable of doing so. Colter is a survivalist who lives in an Airstream and takes reward money for finding missing people, which is a premise that only works because he is clearly running from something of his own.

It has been revealed that you know somebody, and that somebody owes you a favour. Reenie is the lawyer Colter calls when a case needs a door opened legally, and she is the only person on the show who can get further with a phone call than he can on foot.

You turn up, change the temperature of the room, and leave people arguing about you. Russell is Colter's brother, ex-military, and the show has never once let the audience settle on whether he is a help or a problem, which is exactly why he keeps coming back.

When Colter goes to confront the parties responsible, he finds himself surrounded by two guys — which really just means that they're outnumbered. Colter pushes back, pinning one of the jet ski bums and pressing him for more information about John. He’s told that another employee, Lenny Remy (Dylan Kenin), is the man to talk to. Apparently, Lenny went by John's place to send a message, but they hadn’t heard from him since. So, Colter calls Randy (Chris Lee), and it seems like Lenny is bad news. The tech whiz sends our hero to the last pinged location: a trailer park on the other side of town. Entering a condemned manufactured home, Colter finds Lenny beaten and tied up in the closet. According to Lenny, “some psycho” beat him up after he attacked John, knocking him out before he woke up there. When Lenny finally came to, he heard an argument between John and the mystery man — this was a few hours ago. That mystery man? None other than Josh Holloway's Luke, who drops John off at some location after telling him none of it is personal.

Industry observers note that colter continues to look around and finds a few security cameras nearby. Typically, this is when he dials Randy and gets all the footage he needs, but according to Tracker's resident tech guru, it appears that the camera went blank for an hour or so. Colter theorizes that the hired man ran a jammer, which Randy discovers came from a laptop, which is then tracked to the local Dogwood Motel. When Colter arrives, he breaks into a nearby room next to Luke, who readies a firearm of his own. After blasting through the door, Colter narrowly avoids being shot. The man runs, and Colter follows. The result is probably the best fight sequence we've seen this season, maybe on the show period, before Colter gets Luke to talk. From what the bounty hunter knows, he simply dropped John off at a lawyer's office. With the police on the way, Luke decides to go with Colter, albeit as more of a prisoner than a partner.

In a recent development, as Luke soon reveals, he was paid $100K to grab John and not ask questions. This leads to a quick debate over the ethics of their respective professions, where Luke eventually reveals that John’s real name isn't John at all. Rather, he was told that he was really David Watanabe, a man who previously harmed the lawyer in question by supposedly ripping off a Japanese hotel, killing a man, and escaping to the United States. This rattles Colter a little, but a phone call from Reenie (Fiona Rene) puts all the facts back in order. As it turns out, John didn’t kill anyone at all, but rather saved a woman from her abusive husband, Hiro Tanaka (Sonny Saito). After the ex-wife, Mika (Chika Kanamoto), escaped, she turned over evidence of Hiro’s crimes, sending him to prison for decades. Now that Hiro is back, he's itching for revenge.

Although unsure about it at first, Luke eventually agrees to help Colter, who tells him that he needs to make things right. Upon arriving at Hiro's offices, they head inside to find the place empty. It seems as if, after Luke delivered John to the former gangster, he took him and ran. They even find John’s fingernails ripped off and littered on the floor. At this point, it seems as if the pair have hit a dead end, only for Tracker to lay down the tragedy quite thickly this week. After Colter notices a broken lock to a nearby dumpster, they find John dead inside. Although Luke at first wants to take a step back from it all, Colter gives him a choice: either let John die for nothing or use this to help him find and save Mika. Of course, Colter's heroic charm is quite convincing, and Luke agrees to help, tracing Hiro’s number to a local library.

It has been revealed that at the library, Mika closes up for the night only to be surrounded by Hiro and his men. The criminal reveals that he killed John, only for Mika to fight back, killing one of the two goons and escaping into the nearby bookshelves. It isn't long before Colter and Luke arrive, splitting up to cover more ground. This was the right call, as Hiro corners Mika, ready to deliver the final blow when Luke stops him. Unfortunately, Hiro gets a shot off and wounds Luke pretty bad — but before he can finish the job, Colter shoots the gangster dead and stabilizes his newfound partner. With all this fast-paced gunplay, we need to get Colter, Luke, and Jensen Ackles' Russell Shaw in a room.

In a recent development, the next day, Colter runs into Mika at the hospital, who stuck around to thank Luke when he woke up — it turns out that the bounty hunter is out of surgery and is in recovery. Colter dips in to check on Luke, who wonders if our favorite rewardist has a "walk-away number" when it comes to the job. Of course, Colter doesn't. “I like what I do,” he explains. “I like fixing things. I like bringing people back together.” For Colter, the job gives him purpose and others hope, and so, despite all the profit he makes, it’s never been about the money. But "That's The Job" doesn't end here. True to the title, Colter now has to go and do the hard thing by informing Eliza about John's death, and it's a heartfelt, intimate moment that reminds us that, sometimes, not even Colter Shaw can bring about a happy ending.

Tracker goes beyond Colter Shaw's abilities as he partners with an unlikely ally to see things through.

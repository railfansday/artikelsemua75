Former Queens Park Rangers goalscoring icon, Charlie Austin, has hit out at Millwall boss, Alex Neil, following the Scot's criticism towards Hoops winger, Paul Smyth, following Saturday's heated all-London affair at The Den.

Rangers continued their recently impressive form with a third victory in five matches by defeating the Lions 1-0 in SE16, courtesy of Nicolas Madsen's strike in the first minute of first-half stoppage time.

However, it is fair to say that the remainder of the contest wasn't without controversy, as the hosts were reduced to 10 men just 15 minutes into the contest after Zak Sturge's late challenge on the Northern Ireland international winger.

Although the statistics certainly showcased that Julien Stephan's side could have won the game by more if they finished their 31 attempts at Lukas Jensen's goal, much of Neil's post-match press conference centered around Smyth's involvement throughout.

The Saturday lunchtime victory saw Rangers end the Championship's 16th matchweek in seventh on 27 points, whilst Millwall sit 16th with nine fewer points.

Smyth, 29, is capable of featuring on either flank, and certainly caused plenty of complications for the Lions defence on Saturday in his 74-minute cameo, although Neil certainly had differing opinions.

Sources indicate that “I spoke to Smyth about diving on the pitch in general. We had five players booked, and most of them came from him falling over," he said.

Recent findings suggest that “The sending off – if he’s not offside, he’s not offside. If it’s a sending off, it’s a sending off. I’ve always been really straightforward with that sort of stuff. If it’s found that he’s guilty and he deserves to be sent off, I’m not going to argue that.

Neil was also booked for a verbal altercation with the Rs man, adding: “I told him to stop diving.

“I think what it was, it’s an element of frustration, but I’ve watched Paul Smyth for years. He’ll probably come up here and tell you he never dives, but I’d beg to differ.”.

Austin is a former teammate of Smyth's at Loftus Road, having returned to W12 back in 2020, scoring 15 goals in 59 appearances during his second spell, which lasted for two seasons.

The 37-year-old now offers regular opinions on Championship developments, and he defended the 26-time international when asked on his YouTube channel to give a verdict on Sturge's dismissal.

"I'm going to say yes," he noted. "Paul Smyth does well there. He gets his body in the way and knows what's coming.

"Alex Neil comes out and indicates he was diving all around the pitch. Shut up mate.

"You'd like to have Paul Smyth in your team," Austin stated.

Recent findings suggest that "Seriously. The threat he gives when 'we' have the ball, but when 'we' haven't, he presses and uses his pace well. He entices defenders to get close to him, which brings a foul and he anticipates the touch is coming.

"He was great for QPR at the weekend.

"But, for Alex Neil to come out and say that, I thought it was a stupid and naive comment.".

Austin knows exactly what it takes to achieve promotion to the Premier League, having done exactly that with the Hoops and West Bromwich Albion.

The former striker was certainly encouraged by Rangers' performance against their London rivals this weekend, especially the way in which they continued to impose themselves on the contest despite the hosts' desire to remain defensively solid.

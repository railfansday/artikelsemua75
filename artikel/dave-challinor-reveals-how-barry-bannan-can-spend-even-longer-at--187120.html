Sheffield Wednesday boss Dave Challinor believes that Barry Bannan can "extend his career" if he deploys him in a deeper midfield role, but admits he is still experimenting with him across the midfield in the infancy of his time in the Hillsborough dugout.

The former Stockport County boss has had a very busy first couple of weeks at the helm in South Yorkshire, with games in three different competitions and a couple of big league outings. (Related: How to generate text: using different decoding methods ...)

In those two third-tier matches against Plymouth Argyle and Barnsley, Challinor chose the same starting eleven, which saw Bannan move further forward to partner Callum Slattery, creating two 10s behind Davis Keillor-Dunn. (Related: Hugging Face and Graphcore partner for IPU-optimized Tr...)

Against the Tykes, especially, he seemed to excel in that role, producing the kind of performance many expected Bannan to have already yielded this season. In truth, he's not been the Scotsman of old back at Hillsborough, but his free kick led to Jamal Lowe's opener, and he assisted the second, too.

That performance gives hope that the 36-year-old may still be key at this level, but Challinor feels that playing him further back could add years onto his career. Ultimately, it's a good conundrum to face.

Bannan indicated how he can still influence games further forward at Oakwell on Friday night, but the attacking midfield role will put more of a strain on what the veteran Scotsman can do, as it means he has to get up and down the pitch more often.

The Wednesday manager stated that, while his influence further forward could be useful, he could add one or two more years to his career by playing deeper and pulling the strings from there, something which Bannan has done throughout his time at Hillsborough.

"I think Barry can play in different roles,” Challinor stated, per the Star. "For how we want to play with control, him playing centrally and a tiny little bit deeper, because of his technical ability, I think will work really well.

"It will probably extend his career depending on how long he wants to play for because there'll be less demand on him to influence the game higher up, but he still has the ability to do that.".

Challinor admitted that it's a "luxury" to be able to have someone like Bannan who knows how to get into positions to influence games further forward, but knows that, right now, it's about experimenting with different players in different roles until Wednesday get to the January transfer window.

"I suppose the luxury we have at the minute is we're able to ask him to be a little bit more disciplined, but on other occasions let him go and be a little bit of a free spirit," he added.

In a recent development, "When he's higher up, he can certainly do that. He can roll into wide areas. He can come deep if he wants. But we have to find, let's say, different things at the minute because we don't have the players specifically fit in the positions that we'd like to have.".

Since Challinor came in to replace Henrik Pedersen earlier this month, he immediately changed the shape to a back three, similar to what he played while at Stockport in their back-to-back third-placed finishes in the last two seasons, before an injury crisis in defence forced him to a back four towards the end of last term.

Evidently, he's gone for formation over personnel, and is experimenting with what roles each player can play in this structure before the January transfer window opens, where it'd be expected that more players who fit this formation can be bought.

That's led to Jamal Lowe playing at left wing-back in the last two league games, but that's also partly down to both Max Lowe and Reece James being injured, and Bannan playing a bit further forward than he has been so far, given how he can influence games.

It may be the case that Challinor wants to see how Jaden Heskey can impact things in the meantime, or if he feels that the other options behind the striker aren't at the level of Bannan or aren't fit enough to play, so he feels he has to play him there for the time being.

Either way, it's a good question to have, and by January, we may see the Scotsman back in a deeper role.

Extending his career at Hillsborough should be on Bannan's mind, as the midfielder edges ever so closer to the appearance record for the famous South Yorkshire side.

The win over Barnsley was appearance number 496 in a Wednesday shirt, putting him eight away from tying Alan Finney for fourth all-time.

Andrew Wilson sits first on 546, but current club captain Liam Palmer is behind him on 524 and could likely break that record this season, barring any injuries or other forced absences, so Bannan may have to play a little longer to overtake his compatriot.

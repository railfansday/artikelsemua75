The one workplace time-suck even artificial intelligence can’t fix

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, AI can instantaneously crunch numbers, analyze information, and write emails. One thing it still can’t do? Save you the time spent in meetings. Gallup surveyed 21,648 U.S. employees and found that among frequent AI people (32% of respondents, who were defined as those who use AI a few times a week or more), 52% spend less time gathering information and 47% spend less time writing. But 83% mentioned their time in meetings hasn’t changed, while 78% reported no change in time spent collaborating with coworkers. Although meeting time has dropped from its pandemic-era peak, studies show that meetings still eat up an extraordinary amount of the typical worker’s schedule. A 2024 survey by scheduling platform Reclaim.ai, for example, found that employees spend nearly 15 hours a week in meetings on average—roughly 37% of a standard workweek. The survey’s results suggest that while artificial intelligence is making some tasks more efficient, other aspects of workplace life may be essentially AI-proof. Along with meeting time (and time spent collaborating with coworkers), Gallup also found that 82% of frequent AI users spend about the same amount of time working with customers or clients. And despite AI’s ability to save time on many tasks, even frequent users find their jobs haven’t gotten any easier overall. Nearly two-thirds of frequent AI users (65%) said their roles require about the same mental effort as before, while 21% reported expending less mental effort and 14% claimed their work has become even more demanding. This could be because AI is replacing routine tasks while creating new responsibilities: 21% of frequent users said they now spend more time reviewing and correcting work, and the same percentage reported spending more time learning new skills and tools. However, AI-driven productivity gains have not translated into dramatically higher expectations from employers (or at least not yet). Among employees at organizations that have adopted AI, just 29% told Gallup the technology has raised expectations for how much work they should complete in a day. Frequent AI users are more likely to feel pressure, however: 36% said AI has raised expectations for their output, and 33% said they felt pressure to use the technology to keep up.

Choosing between Nike (NKE -4.11%) and Procter & Gamble (PG +0.91%) involves weighing the high-performance growth potential of athletic apparel against the defensive stability of household essentials as investors look toward 2027.

Nike dominates the footwear market by blending product innovation with powerful brand marketing, while Procter & Gamble operates a vast empire of daily necessities like soaps and detergents. Both companies offer immense global reach, but they serve different roles in a diversified portfolio, making it vital to understand their current financial health. In a recent development, nike designs and sells athletic footwear and apparel globally, focusing on its Nike Direct business and a vast network of wholesale accounts. The organization utilizes a mix of retail stores and digital platforms to reach consumers in over 40 countries. In the United States, customer concentration is notable as the three largest customers account for roughly 29% of sales, which adds a layer of risk to the business. According to recent reports, in the 12 months ended Aug. 31, 2026, revenue reached nearly $45.9 billion, which was a decrease of approximately 1.2% from the previous twelve-month period. Despite this slight decline in sales, the firm generated a net income of roughly $3.1 billion. This performance resulted in a net margin of close to 6.7% for the period.

Recent findings suggest that as of its May 2026 balance sheet, the tech giant maintained a debt-to-equity ratio of approximately 0.7x. Debt-to-equity is a measure of total debt relative to shareholder equity, indicating how much the company relies on borrowing to fund its operations. The current ratio is roughly 2.0x, which measures a company's ability to cover its short-term liabilities with short-term assets. In the fiscal year ended May 31, 2026, free cash flow was approximately $2.2 billion. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 24.9% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Industry observers note that procter & Gamble manages a diverse portfolio of essential brands, operating as a leader among consumer staples stocks across categories like healthcare and family care. The organization sells these daily necessities through various channels, including grocery stores, pharmacies, and mass merchandisers. A significant portion of its business is concentrated with Walmart (WMT +1.51%), which accounts for approximately 16% of total sales.

In a recent development, in the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached approximately $87.9 billion, representing a growth rate of roughly 3.5% compared to the prior period. This consistent performance resulted in a net income of nearly $16.0 billion for the year. High net margin levels of roughly 18.2% highlight the manufacturer's ability to maintain profitability while navigating fluctuating costs.

According to recent reports, as of its June 2026 balance sheet, the company carries a debt-to-equity ratio of roughly 0.6x. Debt-to-equity is a measure of total debt relative to shareholder equity, and this level suggests a moderate use of borrowing. The current ratio, which measures a company's ability to cover short-term debts with assets it can quickly convert to cash, is approximately 0.7x. In the fiscal year ended June 30, 2026, free cash flow was close to $15.1 billion.

Sources indicate that nike faces intense global competition from various athletic and lifestyle footwear and apparel companies such as adidas (ADDDF -6.04%) and Under Armour (UAA -1.56%). The company relies heavily on a concentrated base of contract manufacturers and suppliers located outside the United States, which creates vulnerability to supply chain disruptions. Additionally, volatility in global economic conditions and a significant reliance on complex information technology systems pose ongoing operational risks.

Procter & Gamble contends with significant competition across diverse product categories from both global competitors and private-label brands. The tech giant is dependent on complex global supply chains and faces constant pressure from fluctuations in the costs of commodities, energy, and transportation. Furthermore, managing compliance with a wide array of global legal, regulatory, and tax requirements remains a primary challenge for the organization.

Procter & Gamble currently offers a lower valuation based on future earnings estimates, while Nike provides a lower entry point based on its P/S ratio.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

I'd go with Procter & Gamble. It owns the everyday brands like Tide, Pampers, and Gillette that people buy again and again, and it turns that loyalty into steady cash for shareholders. P&G has raised its dividend for an incredible 70 straight years and has paid one for well over a century. What really stands out to me right now is how broad the growth is. Sales rose 4% in its latest quarter, above what the firm had guided for the year, and all five of its business segments grew.

To give Nike its due, its running and other sports businesses are growing, and it has a CEO working to win back customers. But there's no way around it: Nike's business is in a tough spot right now. It expects sales to fall this fiscal year as it cuts back some lifestyle shoes and overhauls how it sells in China.

Sources indicate that P&G, by contrast, is growing from a very steady base. For long-term investors, that mix of trusted brands, steady growth, and a rising dividend makes P&G the better buy for 2027.

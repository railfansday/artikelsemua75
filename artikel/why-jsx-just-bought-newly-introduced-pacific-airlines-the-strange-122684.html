Public charter air carrier JSX has just acquired Latest Pacific Airlines, and with this, New Pacific Airlines intends to resume flights, with Embraer E145 aircraft.

If you’re not familiar with New Pacific Airlines, you’re probably thinking “huh?” If you are familiar with New Pacific Airlines, you’re probably thinking “WTF?” There’s a sneaky and logical explanation here, so let me explain.

In a recent development, let’s start with a little context. JSX is a public charter air carrier, which operates a fleet of nearly 60 planes, most of which are Embraer E135s and E145s.

It has been revealed that it operates as a Part 135 operator, meaning it’s not technically an airline, as it’s using a loophole that allows it to not use standard TSA security screening, hire pilots with fewer than 1,500 hours, etc. Basically what’s happening is that one subsidiary sells the tickets, and then another subsidiary operates the flights, so it’s technically a “charter” (with 30 people who don’t know one another). Sources indicate that then there’s Upcoming Pacific Airlines, one of the strangest airline startups we’ve ever seen, and which I’ve covered extensively. The airline acquired some Boeing 757s, and the business strategy was to connect North America and Asia via Anchorage (ANC).

In a recent development, the business model made zero sense in the first place, though there was some funding there, and they even invested in constructing a lounge in Anchorage, even though those flights never did happen. The airline did briefly fly from (ONT) to Nashville (BNA) — as one does — before ultimately going out of business. (Related: Leveraging Pre-trained Language Model Checkpoints for E...)

That brings us to the news. New Pacific Airlines intends to resume flights with Embraer E145s that are leased from JSX, as reported by IshrionA. According to a Department of Transportation filing, the airline intends to resume flights with planes featuring 42 seats, which is higher than the 30-seat limit you’ll find on current JSX planes.

Recent findings suggest that on the surface, this of course makes absolutely no sense. New Pacific Airlines was out of business, and the business model made no sense. So why would JSX buy an airline and then lease planes to it?

Industry observers note that long story short, this seems to be a play to acquire a cheap Part 121 certificate. A Part 121 operator is a manufacturer that’s allowed to operate scheduled air transport… in other words, a “real” airline. As I mentioned above, JSX has historically been a Part 135 operator, and that explicitly means it’s not an airline. It’s a public charter air carrier, so it’s not a “real” airline.

Industry observers note that going back a couple of years, we know that the Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) was considering cracking down on carriers like JSX, since they definitely don’t operate within the spirit of the rules. That’s not to say that JSX did anything wrong, because by the letter of the law, they’re following it exactly. It’s just that the letter of the law makes no sense, and clearly doesn’t reflect the intent behind distinguishing between Part 121 and Part 135 operators.

Under Trump, there was no scrutiny of this. However, with Democrats soon controlling both the House and Senate, I imagine JSX leaders may be worried that there could again be more scrutiny. If nothing else, buying a cheap Part 121 certificate is a good insurance policy, in case the government does crack down. After all, these can basically be “sold,” while acquiring one from scratch can be grueling process.

I wouldn’t assume that anything will really operate with New Pacific Airlines branding, or based on a New Pacific Airlines business model. Instead, I imagine the idea is that JSX will start dabbling in Part 121 operations, which is why these planes will have more seats (above the 30-seat cap that’s allowed), since that’s no longer a factor.

Recent findings suggest that if the government were to crack down on Part 135 operators, JSX could move to Part 121 operations. However, they’d have to make lots of changes, from their pilot hiring, to the way passengers are screened.

JSX has just bought New Pacific, the airline with an absurd business model that went bankrupt. No, JSX isn’t inspired by the concept of flying Boeing 757s to Asia. Instead, this is clearly the cheapest way for JSX to acquire a Part 121 certificate, which would be required for the carrier to continue operations in the event that the government were to crack down on the Part 135 operator loophole.

It’s going to be interesting to see how this evolves, both since JSX seems to have prepares to operate Part 121 operations in the near future, and also to see how JSX’s business model would evolve if the Part 135 concept were blocked.

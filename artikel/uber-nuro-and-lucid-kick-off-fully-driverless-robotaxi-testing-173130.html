Sources indicate that uber’s high-stakes robotaxi bet is officially ditching its human safety net. Nuro, Lucid, and Uber are starting fully driverless public road testing in the San Francisco Bay Area, with employee-only rides launching shortly after.

In a recent development, first confirmed in July 2025, this three-way alliance represents Uber’s aggressive comeback in autonomous ride-hailing, after having abandoned its own troubled in-house efforts years ago. By pulling human safety operators from behind the wheel of Lucid Gravity SUVs, the companies say they are clearing a critical hurdle toward a public launch. That debut is now slated for early 2027, marking a slight slip from the partnership’s original late-2026 target. When it finally goes live, it will pit Uber directly against entrenched rivals like Waymo and Zoox on their home turf. (Related: House Democrats will start next Congress with largest m...)

Driverless testing kicks off across four South Bay cities—Palo Alto, Mountain View, Los Altos, and Sunnyvale—before expanding across the broader Bay Area next year. To fuel this push, Lucid has delivered more than 100 upgraded Gravity SUVs, up from the 75 engineering vehicles on the road earlier this year.

It has been revealed that the automaker began building pre-production models in October to clear regulatory safety approvals, and it intends to start manufacturing the actual commercial fleet in the coming weeks. Each SUV is powered by Nuro’s Level 4 “Nuro Driver” system, pairing cameras, solid-state lidar, and radar with a roof-mounted LED “halo” that flashes a rider’s initials and pickup status.

It has been revealed that removing the human backup comes after nearly a year of round-the-clock supervised testing in the Bay Area and Houston. Before letting the cars loose on public streets alone, Nuro put the software through grueling, high-risk edge cases on a closed track in Las Vegas to prove it could handle the unpredictable chaos of real-world driving.

Sources indicate that the robotaxis are scheduled to hit the Uber app for Bay Area riders in the first quarter of 2027, with Houston following later that year. Looking further ahead, Uber has committed to buying at least 35,000 Lucid vehicles—including the automaker’s upcoming midsize model—over the coming years as it races to scale a global autonomous fleet.

Recent findings suggest that solana (SOL +1.65%) isn't a cryptocurrency focused on providing privacy, but given the new set of features it just rolled out, that could well change, and the result might be sensational for its holders. On Nov. 10, the Solana-focused infrastructure firm Helius launched a new system called Solana Privacy Rings. The Rings enable customers to hold and send Solana tokens with amounts encrypted, making its functionality similar to a subset of the privacy features of dedicated privacy coins like Zcash (ZEC +2.40%). Solana's Privacy Rings are shielded pools of tokens, meaning they're shared vaults where balances stay encrypted on the blockchain.

Recent findings suggest that in the default Ring, the asset being held and its amount are private, but the sender and recipient are public. Only customized Rings intended for financial institutions, slated to launch sometime in Q4 of 2026, will add an anonymous mode that masks the senders and recipients of the funds.

So, while a upcoming set of privacy features on Solana is a modest plus for holders, and it could get even better over time if they take off, the coin is still not a direct competitor to Zcash or other privacy coins.

CRYPTO · As of 7:28 AM ET 11/22/26.

Nor is privacy completely upcoming to Solana.

Industry observers note that its Confidential Transfers feature, which also hides transaction amounts, was restarted on June 17 after a year offline for security fixes. But in 2025, the Solana Foundation called the feature's usage "extremely low," a reminder that availability doesn't guarantee use. That's not too surprising, as again, Zcash and other privacy coins have more and stronger (and more tested) privacy features anyway, so they are better positioned to capture demand within crypto.

Recent findings suggest that another wrinkle here is that those other privacy coins tend to lack features Solana has, such as native tokenized assets, smart contract support, and a cheap and speedy network. For the most part, these are what make the fresh Ring feature on Solana interesting, but as of now, the market hasn't sent a strong signal that its differentiating capabilities are actually valuable.

Solana itself will mostly benefit from the fresh privacy features if they encourage more capital to flow to and around its network, which hasn't happened yet.

Sources indicate that for its part, Helius will charge usage credits per private transaction proof its system validates. Solana's holders will gain only from the network fees that each transaction requires, which are fairly minimal on an individual basis. Across the entire chain, Solana collected $353,012 in fees over the 24 hours through Nov. 16. If that pace held for a year, fees would equal about 0.3% of Solana's $41.8 billion market cap.

The stronger argument for buying Solana here is what the fresh privacy capabilities could unlock.

Industry observers note that the chain currently hosts $14.1 billion in stablecoins and $5.1 billion in tokenized real-world assets (RWAs). Few businesses would want their payroll data or supplier payments visible to their competitors, and customized Rings that are equipped with auditor access are built for them.

In a recent development, nonetheless, regulation could blunt that demand, if it even exists within Solana's ecosystem at all. Starting July 1, 2027, the European Union will bar licensed crypto exchanges and custodians from handling privacy coins, and it's unclear if the Rings will mean Solana is classified in that same bucket.

As an investor who's bullish on privacy tech and privacy coins, I think Solana's investment thesis is stronger now than before, and I'm optimistic about the expansion of the Rings feature set. But for most investors, the upside here is incremental at best, and so there's no newly introduced reason to buy Solana.

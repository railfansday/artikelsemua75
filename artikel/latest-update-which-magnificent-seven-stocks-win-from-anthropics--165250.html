Industry observers note that anthropic (ANT -1.43%) started its life as a publicly traded company with a bang on Nov. 19. The artificial intelligence company behind Claude priced its initial public offering (IPO) at $83 per share, opened at $111.50, and finished its first day at $97.17. (Related: Industry News: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

According to recent reports, anthropic instantly joined an exclusive club. With a valuation of $1.2 trillion (using only shares outstanding), it's one of only 15 companies with a market cap over $1 trillion as of this writing.

But there's an even more exclusive grouping -- the Magnificent Seven, which are seven tech-centric stocks that have been responsible for a large part of the stock market's gains in recent quarters.

Several members of the Magnificent Seven have substantial investments in Anthropic, and those lucrative business relationships could pay off for years.

Amazon (AMZN +3.47%) and Alphabet (GOOGL +6.34%) (GOOG +5.88%) were early investors in Anthropic, and as Claude has grown, so have their stakes.

According to Anthropic's prospectus, Amazon owns approximately 14.7% of Anthropic, and Alphabet holds another 13.7%. Based on its current valuation of $1.21 trillion, Amazon's stake is worth $177.87 billion, and Alphabet's is worth $165.77 billion.

But it doesn't stop there. Anthropic also has business relationships with Amazon Web Services (AWS) and Google Cloud for the computing power needed to train and operate Claude.

Anthropic has committed to spending at least $110 billion with Amazon between May 2026 and April 2036, and at least $121.3 billion with Google, which also supplies its custom Tensor Processing Units (TPUs) for AI workloads.

So, Amazon and Alphabet benefit in two ways -- their ownership stakes become more valuable as Anthropic grows, and their cloud businesses collect revenue directly from Anthropic.

Nvidia (NVDA +31.48%) and Microsoft (MSFT +1.65%) are also members of the Magnificent Seven. Both have smaller financial stakes in Anthropic and are also positioned to benefit from its growth.

Industry observers note that nvidia agreed to purchase $10 billion of Anthropic shares through a private placement associated with the IPO. Microsoft committed another $1 billion.

Recent findings suggest that nvidia also supplies the graphics processing units (GPUs) used in AI computing. Anthropic's infrastructure strategy includes Nvidia GPUs alongside chips from Amazon, Google, and Advanced Micro Devices (AMD +6.85%).

Microsoft also has cloud computing arrangements with Anthropic through its Microsoft Azure cloud, but its financial exposure is considerably smaller than that of Amazon or Alphabet.

The relationships among major technology companies are complicated, particularly with regard to artificial intelligence. Companies such as Anthropic, Amazon, Alphabet, Microsoft, and Nvidia are investors, suppliers, customers, and competitors -- seemingly all at the same time.

Billions of dollars flow among these companies through investments, cloud computing contracts, and chip purchases. And while that creates opportunities for multiple companies to benefit from AI spending, it also raises questions about how much demand comes from companies financing their own customers.

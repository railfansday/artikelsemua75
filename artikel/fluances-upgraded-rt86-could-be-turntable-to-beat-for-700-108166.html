According to recent reports, with the explosion of vinyl came a corresponding flood of turntables, but among them all the Fluance RT82 is my pick for the best turntable. Yet, for those who want a step up, the company now offers the RT86 Reference High Fidelity Vinyl Turntable.

The Fluance RT86 seems to have made improvements in the areas that matter most, including a newly introduced nine-inch carbon fiber tonearm and better vibration isolation. (Related: Industry News: Introducing Azure Catalyst agentic moder...)

Firstly, the turntable is driven by a servo-controlled belt-drive motor, which uses an optical sensor to regulate speed. Next, the RT86 includes a nine-inch carbon fiber tonearm with adjustable VTA, which helps to level cartridges of varying heights, and a removable headshell which lets you install multiple cartridges more easily.

Sources indicate that the Fluance includes a 4.1-pound antiresonant acrylic platter. The platter sits on a high-mass wood plinth with a motor isolation system and adjustable isolation feet. (Related: Tech Report: University releases $5m smart system that ...)

In addition, buyers now have a choice of two different cartridges: the Audio-Technica AT-VM95ML Microlinear cartridge or the Ortofon 2M Blue. Having tested the 2M Blue as part of the RT85, I would suggest consumers opt for the Audio-Technica as I found the Ortofon too bright for that setup.

At its price of $700, it’s even cheaper than the Pro-Ject Debut Carbon EVO 2, which is the turntable to beat under $1,000. The Fluance RT86 is available now.

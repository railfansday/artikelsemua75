Fluidkey stated it restored its privacy wallet on Nov. 20, a day after taking it offline following unauthorized transfers.

Sending funds requires an app update, and users who spot an unfamiliar transfer can look up their own receiving address or transaction hash on the relevant blockchain.

The firm confirmed the return of its web, iOS, and Android apps in v1.79.22. It mentioned older versions would stay offline and told people to open Settings and select Update to re-enable transactions. (Related: Latest Update: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

According to recent reports, fluidkey attributed the incident to flaws in a third-party logging tool and said users’ private keys stayed on their devices, and said it took services offline on Nov. 19. In its enhancement that evening, it said it had seen no further unauthorized transactions since the shutdown and was working with SEAL 911 to investigate and trace funds.

Its initial warning to avoid the site and app came before the newly introduced version was available.

Blockchain analytics firm Bitquery traced roughly $1.6 million in payouts to wallets it linked to the incident across six chains, including Base and Ethereum.

Sources indicate that the independent Fluidkey drain tracker, maintained by pepebndc, put the total near $1.63 million across seven chains in its Nov. 20 morning snapshot. That estimate includes suspected destinations and excludes likely owner withdrawals.

Bitquery indicates the tracker labeled one Polygon payout as an owner withdrawal, linking it to the incident after following the recipient’s activity on Base.

The firm also separated the incident-linked payments from about $349,000 sent to wallets with an established history during its hour-long scan.

That group includes ordinary payments before the drain and about $277,000 sent during it to an older wallet across Ethereum and Base. Bitquery interpreted the latter as one user moving their own funds, and left another $65,000 unclassified.

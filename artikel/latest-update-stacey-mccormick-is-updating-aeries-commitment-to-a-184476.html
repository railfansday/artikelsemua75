📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that AI is supercharging a long-term problem—the fashion industry’s tenuous relationship with real bodies. As it gets increasingly difficult to tell whether the images you’re seeing are real or not, brands such as Gucci, Valentino, and Rainbow have all faced consumer backlash for using AI-generated models (some featuring real people’s likenesses). Aerie has gone the other way. CMO Stacey McCormick has spent the last year taking a stand against synthetic imagery with the “100% Aerie Real” campaign. “We felt strongly that we should be a place where people could see themselves,” McCormick claims. The firm announced on Instagram in October 2025 that Aerie would never use AI-generated bodies or products in any of its marketing. It quickly became one of the brand’s most-liked and most-engaged-with posts of the year. The brand followed up with an ad the next spring that featured Pamela Anderson and called attention to the image’s lack of AI retouching. McCormick, a 30-year veteran of American Eagle, was instrumental in creating Aerie’s iconic 2014 #AerieREAL campaign, in which the brand pledged never to retouch images of models. The ethos of using real women, flaws and all, positioned Aerie as an early trailblazer and meshes well with Anderson’s late-career focus on rarely wearing makeup. The recent “100% Aerie Real” campaign helped grow brand engagement by 30% postlaunch, and the Anderson spot helped boost search volume by 33%. It didn’t hurt sales either: Aerie reported 11% sales growth in the third quarter of 2025 and a 23% jump in Q4, with the acceleration continuing into 2026. For McCormick, keeping humanity at the core of fashion imagery isn’t just a marketing strategy; it’s about preserving trust in an era of digital fabrication. “Our community knows that when they come to us, what they see is what they get,” says McCormick. “Our commitment to truth is important.”.

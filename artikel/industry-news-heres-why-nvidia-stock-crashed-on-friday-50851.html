Recent findings suggest that shares of Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) plunged 14% on Friday following reports that the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) is investigating the tech titan's accounting practices.

In a recent development, it's a challenging time to be an Nvidia shareholder. The dominant designer of artificial intelligence (AI) chips has shed more than a quarter of its value since Jensen Huang stepped down as CEO on Nov. 12.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

On Thursday, California Attorney General Rob Bonta served an investigative subpoena on Nvidia related to Huang's reported affair with an alleged Chinese spy. "My office is investigating whether Nvidia or Jensen Huang's actions resulted in China obtaining advanced chips or confidential information -- and if Nvidia misled investors and the public about this risk, in violation of the law," Bonta noted.

In a recent development, and on Friday, Reuters reported that the SEC is probing Nvidia's accounting practices. Reuters noted that "Investigations are not evidence of wrongdoing and many close without resulting in enforcement action." Still, the report adds to the fear and uncertainty surrounding the manufacturer following Huang's departure.

Nvidia's share price is now down nearly 50% from its 52-week high of $243.37 in October. It has lost a staggering $2.8 trillion in market capitalization during that time.

Nvidia's quarterly results released on Nov. 17 were impressive. Revenue rose 103% year over year to $115.8 billion. Net income increased 105% to $65.3 billion.

In a recent development, however, Nvidia's regulatory and legal problems are driving more investors to question whether its torrid growth will continue. And buyers are demanding a lower price to accept those risks.

Sources indicate that space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -6.19%) is arguably the top space stock on the market today. But it's far from the only one. There are dozens of companies directly profiting from today's space race. But there are hundreds more that will benefit in more indirect ways.

In a recent development, if you're also looking to profit from the space race, the best strategy may not be to invest directly in space stocks. Instead, look for companies with ways to benefit that aren't fully appreciated by the market. With this in mind, one trillion-dollar business stands out. For decades, investors have considered Tesla (TSLA -6.92%) an automotive stock. That paradigm is no longer true. Tesla's $1.5 trillion valuation has little to do with making cars. Instead, that valuation is tied to future growth opportunities, with robotaxis at the top of the list. (Related: Industry News: What Is Considered Upper Middle Class? I...)

At first glance, robotaxis don't seem to be directly related to space. But if you understand the value and limitations of autonomous driving technology, the connection becomes clear.

Robotaxis are widely believed to be a multi-trillion-dollar opportunity. With the ability to manufacture its own cars, Tesla has both a scaling and cost advantage over its competitors, many of which lack internal manufacturing capabilities.

Tesla intends on leveraging these advantages by launching its own robotaxi platform. In effect, the manufacturer could become the Uber Technologies (UBER -1.13%) of robotaxis, albeit while operating a closed ecosystem exclusively for Tesla models.

At the top of the list is connectivity.

"Autonomous driving will increasingly demand more and more reliable network-based structures, requiring redundant, real-time architectures," concludes a report from the Institute of Electrical and Electronics Engineers. "Car manufacturers will be required to continuously increase bandwidths for both point-to-point data pipes and distributed network structures to meet fresh data demands. Level 5 cars will send 25 gigabytes of data to the cloud every hour.".

It has been revealed that in other words, robotaxis will require huge amounts of bandwidth to operate. If a robotaxi relies solely on terrestrial data networks, it creates connectivity vulnerabilities. That could cause service outages, customer experience problems, or even safety challenges. Network redundancy will matter. That's exactly the value that Starlink can provide for Tesla.

"We can't have Robotaxis getting stuck in these Bermuda Triangles of lack of cellular connectivity," Elon Musk warned investors earlier this year. "There are many places, even in Silicon Valley, where the cellular coverage is terrible or sometimes non-existent, which is surprising for Silicon Valley. I know when I drive to work, those first 10, 15 minutes, I can't actually do any calls because the cellular connectivity is so bad ... Starlink with its ability to do connectivity anywhere is actually quite important, so we don't have Robotaxis missing in action.".

Robotaxis are a huge component of Tesla's current valuation. The total robotaxi opportunity could ultimately be valued at more than Tesla's market cap today. Robotaxi execution will therefore be critical, and the tech giant's close relationship with SpaceX and Starlink should ultimately make it an unlikely winner of today's space race.

Spoilers for the end of Mass Effect 3 follow, naturally.

It has been revealed that you know what? I didn't have many questions left at the end of Mass Effect 3. Joker was a robot now. His robot girlfriend was perhaps less of a robot? Shepard converted herself into a dotfile to make the Reapers chill out, and Buzz Aldrin's talents lie in areas other than voice acting.

Sources indicate that I had plenty of questions when BioWare unveiled Mass Effect 5 was gonna be a thing though. Could you really write a Mass Effect story in the radically changed galaxy we were left with at the end of Shepard's adventure? Apparently, you could. In a recent AMA on Reddit (via IGN), BioWare writer Cat Valente wrote that the now-cancelled Mass Effect sequel's team put a lot of work into handling ME3's endings "in a way that wouldn't piss everyone off, [or] at least not piss them off completely".

It has been revealed that the goal, indicates Valente, was to write a continuation of Mass Effect's narrative that "wouldn't privilege any one kind of player over another". That tracks with what we've heard before: leaks have suggested ME5 was set to more or less wrap all the previous game's endings into one (Deus Ex: Invisible War style, hell yeah).

It has been revealed that of course, it'd have to do that while allowing BioWare "to actually write a coherent story going forward, within the limits of having to do it in a playable mainstream videogame and not a post-modern 3000 page novel", adds Valente. "Not for nothing, guys, but that's *kind of a tall fucking order*. Part of the reason it took so long and Andromeda was in Andromeda is that figuring out an elegant solution to that problem turns out to be *really hard*". The sheer challenge of creating a coherent, satisfying follow-up to ME3 that took into account everyone's choices had Valente, at one point, "thinking *god, if I'd known when I finished ME3 that I'd be one of the people who had to find the mops and buckets to clean up that mess...*". Nevertheless, she reckons the studio did eventually alight on a way of handling it that made sense. "Yes, there was an answer, yes, I know it, as least inasmuch as I know what it was when I was working there… We knew all the discourse on the endings, of course we did, and I do think as a fan I would have been happy with the solution (at least what it was when I left) if I booted up the game having had nothing to do with it".

Recent findings suggest that singapore’s central bank looks to help develop a common way for banks to verify who an AI agent represents and what the customer has authorized it to do. Chia Der Jiun, managing director of the Monetary Authority of Singapore (MAS), outlined the proposal on Nov. 19 at the Singapore FinTech Festival. A separate Singapore sandbox would test safeguards for AI agent payments, with stablecoins and tokenized deposits among the options.

Under the proposed know-your-agent protocol, a financial institution would receive information about the agent’s identity, the customer it acts for, its delegated authority and the rules it must follow. The protocol would also convey any assurances about the agent’s origin and reliability. The institution could use that information to set the scope of access and give the agent a dedicated, secure channel.

In a recent development, chia mentioned the customer’s mandate should specify the actions the agent may take and the limits and conditions on them. The more discretion the agent has or the greater the potential impact, the stronger the controls should be. Sources indicate that MAS and the Future of Finance Institute confirmed the planned Agentic Money Sandbox on Nov. 18. The institute would set up a controlled environment for institutions to test payments and corporate treasury management with digital money ahead of any live deployment.

Singapore has proposed a way to check an agent’s authority and separate sandbox trials for payments and corporate treasury.

The proposed treasury trial separates the tech giant’s mandate from the bank’s terms for accepting an instruction.

Sources indicate that the customer sets the mandate. The bank or payment provider sets controls before acting on an instruction.

Recent findings suggest that in the proposed payments test, the participating bank or payment provider would verify the agent’s identity and authority and apply risk controls before executing an instruction. Chia cited tokenized deposits and stablecoins as examples of the digital money the trial could use.

In the treasury test, a organization would define what its agent could do. The agent could monitor cash balances and upcoming payments, decide when and where funds should move, and use tokenized deposits for permitted transfers. The bank would set its own controls and terms for accepting each instruction.

In a Nov. 18 speech, Singapore Deputy Prime Minister and MAS Chairman Gan Kim Yong gave the example of an agent making a payment under a contract once the relevant terms had been met and checked. The joint release named DBS, OCBC, Standard Chartered and UOB as the institute’s launch partners. The same release gave no sandbox start date or participant list.

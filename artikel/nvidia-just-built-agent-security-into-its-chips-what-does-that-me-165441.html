Sources indicate that with all the buzz surrounding Nvidia's (NVDA +31.48%) earnings report on Nov. 17 and the turmoil brought on by Jensen Huang's resignation and reinstatement, you may have overlooked something interesting unrelated to financial results.

In the company's earnings call with analysts, CFO Colette Kress discussed Nvidia's prepares to secure artificial intelligence (AI) agents, and one of the companies she mentioned was cybersecurity specialist CrowdStrike (CRWD -8.92%).

That's a name familiar to Nvidia investors. In September, Huang called CrowdStrike Nvidia’s top cybersecurity partner. The companies were already collaborating on AI-powered security software. (Related: Hugging Face and Graphcore partner for IPU-optimized Tr...)

Now that relationship is getting bigger. Nvidia is putting security controls directly into the computing infrastructure that runs AI agents. And CrowdStrike is helping to provide the protection.

AI agents are often much more powerful than the chatbots most people are familiar with, and they're getting more powerful every day. Thanks to agentic AI, agents can perform tasks, access databases, execute software, and interact with other systems. (Related: Industry News: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

That's useful if you want your agent to help you create a shopping list, schedule a doctor's appointment, or order a prescription refill.

It has been revealed that but there are also obvious security concerns right now, as the U.S. has been hit with a variety of computer-related outages affecting banks, the airline industry, and government services. AI agents can be subverted to do harmful things, such as access confidential records or sensitive tech giant data.

It has been revealed that nvidia wants to prevent that behavior before it causes damage. In October, the company introduced its OpenShell Reference Architecture, which combines software controls with security enforcement running on Nvidia's BlueField-4 data processing units.

OpenShell limits which files and tools an agent can access. Nvidia Sentry, running on BlueField-4, independently monitors agent behavior and enforces security policies at the hardware level. Nvidia notes that Sentry can quarantine and stop an agent that attempts to operate outside its permitted boundaries in milliseconds.

It has been revealed that kress emphasized the technology during the recent earnings call and identified CrowdStrike among the companies working with Nvidia on OpenShell. It's part of a relationship between Nvidia and CrowdStrike that is developing rapidly.

In a recent development, in September, CrowdStrike introduced SafeMind, an AI cybersecurity system built using Nvidia's Nemotron models. By using offensive and defensive AI models to identify vulnerabilities and improve protection, SafeMind achieved a 29% higher detection rate and six times faster remediation than the comparison models in its evaluations, CrowdStrike remarked.

Then in October, CrowdStrike announced it was integrating Falcon Guardian, its AI detection and response product, with Nvidia OpenShell. It also announced plans to integrate with Nvidia Sentry on BlueField-4.

Nvidia's hardware provides another layer of enforcement -- CrowdStrike can supply the intelligence needed to recognize threats, while Nvidia's infrastructure can help enforce restrictions.

According to recent reports, businesses need cybersecurity protection for their employees, devices, cloud applications, and networks. And as they develop and deploy autonomous agents, those agents create additional activity that security teams need to monitor.

For example, an employee might interact with a handful of applications during the workday. An autonomous agent could execute hundreds of tasks, access multiple databases, and communicate with other agents without requiring constant human involvement.

That's a more efficient way to work, but it also creates a potentially enormous amount of activity for security companies to monitor to make sure someone isn't misusing an AI agent, or that the AI agent hasn't gone rogue.

In a recent development, crowdStrike isn't simply using Nvidia's technology to improve its existing products anymore. It's working to become part of how Nvidia-powered AI agents are secured in the first place -- and, in doing so, to cement the organization's position as one of Nvidia's leading security partners.

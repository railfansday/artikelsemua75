Every vendor says the same thing: buy our AI, and testing gets easy. Don't believe it. AI testing tools do help, though. Some teams save real hours with them, while others waste a quarter and a budget. The difference usually comes down to how they start. (Related: How to Budget for a Wedding Without Going Broke...)

They support your QA team by writing test cases, running them, spotting failures and suggesting repairs. Some tools read a requirements document. Others watch real people click around and then replay what they did. Playwright, for instance, now ships built-in test agents: a planner that explores your app and writes a test strategy, a generator that turns the plan into test files, and a healer that runs the suite and tries to repair failing tests. (Related: Hobbled at home, Trump turns abroad...)

It has been revealed that old-school automation needs a person to write every script. Then a developer moves one button and the whole thing falls over. Somebody loses a morning to repairs, and QA engineers hate that part of the job.

In a recent development, quality is the substantial worry. Generated tests can look clever and prove nothing. A tool might confirm that a checkout page opens and then mark checkout as covered, but opening a page is not testing it. Even Playwright's documentation states generated tests may contain initial errors and that you should review what the healer changes before accepting it. A person has to read the output every time.

Recent findings suggest that business logic is the next problem. The tool has no idea that your refund window dropped from 30 days to 14, and it cannot feel that a signup form is confusing. Intent lives in people's heads.

It has been revealed that coverage numbers can mislead. A dashboard says 90%, and the room relaxes. Meanwhile, the odd edge cases nobody thought of sit untouched. Bugs do not read dashboards.

Recent findings suggest that check fit first. Does it cover web, mobile and API tests, and does it work with your stack? Ask before any sales call, not after. Then test its honesty: when something fails, a good tool tells you why. A vague red mark costs your team time because nobody can verify it. Read the pricing page slowly, too, since some vendors charge per seat and some per test run.

Finally, throw your ugliest feature at it. Demos use tidy little apps, but your product has pop-ups, slow pages and odd corners. Watch how the tool handles those.

Expecting instant results. People switch the tool on and wait for magic. What they get is noisy reports and grumpy engineers. Some managers even cut QA headcount right away, which is a bad idea: somebody has to tune the tool and handle the cases it cannot. Good testers become more valuable, not less.

Skipping training. Your team needs to learn how the tool behaves. Two short workshops and a shared doc go a long way.

Industry observers note that trusting machine-written text without a second look. AI tools now draft test descriptions, bug reports and release notes too. If your team wants to know how much of a document was machine-generated, an AI-content checker such as ZeroGPT can give a rough signal. Treat the result as a hint rather than proof, because such detectors make mistakes and publish their own accuracy claims.

Industry observers note that agents are the obvious next step. Instead of running fixed scripts, they poke around your app like a curious user, clicking, typing and reporting anything strange. Early versions already exist, and they are rough but improving.

It has been revealed that editors will change as well. Your code editor may soon suggest a test the moment you finish a function, and some bug reports may arrive with a fix attached. The wall between development and QA is getting thin.

Yes, but carefully. AI testing tools will not replace your QA team. They take the boring work off testers' hands and spot patterns people might miss, but they still need a human to say "that result is wrong." Start small, measure what happens and keep your testers close to the tool. Do that, and you will ship faster without losing control.

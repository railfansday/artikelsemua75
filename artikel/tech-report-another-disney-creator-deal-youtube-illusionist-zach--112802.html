Disney continues to prove that Netflix isn’t the only competitor sniffing around YouTube‘s top talent.

In a recent development, for their deal, 36-year-old L.A.-based King will make 10 original short-form videos, which Disney will have exclusive rights to distribute. Disney claims the agreement “will also bring [King’s] signature illusions to fresh content for Disney+ and [King’s] own channels inspired by Disney’s Hexed, Star Wars, and other Disney films and experiences.”. One of the King projects coming to Disney+ is Cardboard Wars, his 52-minute intentionally-low-budget tribute to Star Wars: A Latest Hope. King uploaded the film to YouTube in August, and soon will cross-post it to Disney’s streaming service.

A post shared by Zach King (@zachking).

But there’s still more: King will pop up on Disney’s social accounts as well, hosting behind-the-scenes content about the VFX and filmmaking techniques he uses in his content.

“I fell in love with filmmaking and storytelling as a young kid, grabbing my parents’ home video camera and teaching myself to edit,” King wrote in an Instagram post. “A lot of my early homemade films were inspired by the Disney movies and the behind-the-scenes footage they would show in the DVD featurettes. There was always a sense of magic I would see on the screen, and so I’m thrilled to be working with Disney to continue that magic of filmmaking for the next generation of storytellers.”.

King has worked with Disney regularly in recent months: He was at its glitzy Created in L.A. event in September, and earlier this month played a game of viral format Cake or Fake? with Hexed (and Arcane, and Sinners, and Spider-Verse, and Hawkeye) star Hailee Steinfeld. But this is his first official, expansive content deal.

Recent findings suggest that “Zach makes the impossible look real, bringing the kind of imagination and originality that has always defined Disney,” Asad Ayaz, The Walt Disney Company’s CMO and Brand Officer, stated in a statement. “He brings both a filmmaker’s ingenuity and a fan’s passion to our stories, along with a unique connection with audiences. We want to build lasting relationships with creators like Zach, giving their ideas room to grow across Disney. That’s what makes this next chapter of our work together so full of possibility.”.

As we mentioned above, everyone–including YouTube–has had their eye on Netflix’s obvious poaching efforts…leaving room for Disney to sneak in the back.

Sources indicate that in the last couple months, Disney has announced deals with major creators Dhar Mann, Dude Perfect, and Like Nastya. Crucially, these are all development deals–the exact kind of agreement that has YouTube on guard with Netflix.

It has been revealed that and Disney’s not quite showing its full hand just yet. During its earnings call Nov. 17, executives only mentioned YouTubers once, in passing and in reference to Disney+’s growing crop of approved creator content.

We suspect more is in the pipeline–and that YouTube may have to start paying creators to turn down Disney deals, too.

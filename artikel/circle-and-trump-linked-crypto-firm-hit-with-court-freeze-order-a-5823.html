An Abu Dhabi court has ordered Circle and 17 other companies to keep tokens linked to Binance’s Oct. 29 theft frozen while the exchange tries to recover them.

Industry observers note that binance disclosed the order on Nov. 18 and said some issuers, exchanges and other partners ask for a court order before they will release or transfer assets they have frozen. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

The exchange said the order from the Abu Dhabi Global Market Courts helps open that route, describing ongoing efforts to trace the stolen funds and giving no details of any transfer under the order.

The named firms include Circle, which concerns USDC, and World Liberty Financial, the organization behind USD1.

Reports noted that the firms must maintain the freezes pending further proceedings and cannot lend, swap, or mix the affected tokens with other assets.

Recent findings suggest that the court did not accuse the firms of wrongdoing, and Dec. 1 is scheduled to consider whether to keep the injunction in place and on what terms.

The injunction covers specific assets Binance claimed are tied to the theft, and remarked it is still tracing the stolen crypto and taking legal steps to reclaim it. A transfer for firms seeking a court order depends on whether they have authority to release the specific balances they have frozen.

In a recent development, tether falls outside the order covering the 18 firms, and the manufacturer stated on Nov. 2 it had frozen more than $1.5 billion tied to the theft.

A spokesperson said Tether would work directly with Binance to reissue the tokens, in consultation with the relevant authorities.

The Abu Dhabi order directs 18 firms to maintain freezes. Tether says it will work separately with Binance on reissuing USDT.

Binance is still seeking the return of assets held under the court order.

According to recent reports, the Abu Dhabi order follows earlier US court action. On Nov. 12, the US Justice Department stated it had obtained court authority to seize or restrain more than $1.2 billion in stolen crypto. That was part of more than $2.7 billion that US authorities and private firms had helped freeze.

Industry observers note that in its Nov. 3 incident report, Binance estimated that about $6.3 billion in digital assets were affected and about $2.85 billion was frozen or intercepted, subject to reconciliation and market prices.

The separate disclosures do not establish how much the figures overlap or how much Binance can ultimately recover. Binance notes it absorbed the financial impact on customers while continuing to pursue the stolen assets.

In one of his first public appearances since the close of the $110 billion Skydance merger, ex-Warner Bros. Discovery CEO David Zaslav on Monday recalled a key sequence during the deal’s regulatory review.

“We were in this awkward moment,” Zaslav noted Monday at the International Emmys in Fresh York. “We had a hearing, we needed to keep the entire tech giant motivated.”.

Gerhard Zeiler, WBD’s former international chief, decided to visit all of the organization’s global offices, Zaslav remembered. “There was a lot of trepidation, and people were concerned about their own jobs, and where would they be, and how would they, how would this affect their families. When it comes down to it, we’re all just people,” he mentioned in presenting Zeiler with the International Emmys Directorate Award. Sources indicate that paramount finally clinched its debt-laden deal for WBD, which resulted in Zaslav and other key execs being showered with more than $600 million, after a pitched battle with Netflix. After the streaming giant initially secured a deal for Warner’s streaming and studio assets, Paramount then prevailed in a proposal accepted last February by the WBD board. After a lengthy international review, which included a brief period when UK regulators were looking askance at the deal, a lawsuit by a number of state attorneys general in the U.S. waylaid the merger for another couple of months. It has been revealed that “In those first few weeks [after Paramount had emerged as the proposed acquirer], I got very caught up in the transaction. I would call Gerhardt, and each time I called him, I would ask him, ‘Where are you?’ And you could tell me a different city,” Zaslav stated, looking across the stage at Zeiler. “Finally, after about a month, and 15 different places that he had visited, I said, ‘How many countries are you gonna go to? What are you doing?’ And he said, ‘You know, this is a really tough time for the employees. It’s difficult, and I want to go, and I want to meet with everyone, I’m going face to face. I want about lunch. I want to go to dinner.'” Zaslav said WBD colleagues were “inspired” by the global tour. (Related: Latest Update: How to generate text: using different de...)

Zaslav noted Zeiler’s “working-class” origins and his rise to lead “the largest international media firm in the world, now even larger, with this great merger, with Paramount.” On the week of the merger’s completion, Zeiler said confirmed he was stepping away after a lengthy run at the company, opting to enter the political arena in his native Austria.

The former CEO also said Zeiler “fought inside our company for local content,” especially on flagship streamer HBO Max, which had a major reboot and global relaunch during the Zaslav-Zeiler years. “The feeling was, we can just push out the great content that we have at Warner Brothers, the great HBO content, on all of our channels. You can rely on a library. But he fought hard, and he never stopped, and it was one of the great strategic advantages for us. And we had local content in every one of these markets, and local sports, and local news. And it made us so much more relevant.”.

Zeiler’s acceptance speech was straightforward and a fair bit shorter than Zaslav’s introduction.

According to recent reports, he called his run at WBD and its predecessors (and RTL before that) “a journey to which I never stopped learning. Not one single day. And you get to know this nuances, the differences, specialties of the individual cultures all around the world. That was the biggest gig I got in my professional life.”.

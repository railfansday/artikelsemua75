This is still one of the most impressive Lego sets ever made even after years of amazing kits

I've said it before, and I'll say it again; Lego Rivendell is, in my opinion, one of (if not the) best Lego sets around. Even with more modern rivals like Minas Tirith or Barad-dur, I don't think any of them manage to blend spectacle with diorama-style interiors as well as this kit does.

Recent findings suggest that modelled after the elven refuge as it appears in Fellowship of the Ring, this kit is a sprawling recreation of the Last Homely House; with over 6,000 pieces to play with, it can create a level of depth and detail most kits can't hope to match. This is the kind of Lego set where you'll discover something new every time you stop to admire it. Lego Rivendell is also impressive enough on display that it'll draw a "woah" worthy of Owen Wilson from most onlookers.

This model often enjoys a price cut in time for the annual Lego Black Friday deals, but this time it's been given an even bigger saving. Actually, it's dropped to an all-time low price that we haven't seen before. Usually, the best we can expect is a reduction down to £365. According to recent reports, there are four distinct areas for Lego Rivendell; the main house with its tower and display showing off the broken shards of Narsil, the council area with its ring of seats and plinth for the One Ring, a bridge leading to Arwen's pergola, and a woodland smithy where the elves are busy creating masterworks at the forge. Many of these have a mix of exposed and hidden areas you can reveal by lifting off the lid, which really sells the dollhouse-like experience here.

Industry observers note that the top half of the tower can be removed to show the room where Bilbo Baggins is staying, for instance, and there's a desk set up where he's writing in his memoirs. (Yes, his minifigure has an alternate face so you can recreate that moment as well - shudder). Meanwhile, Arwen's pergola is removable so that you can get a good look at the forge beneath. Oh, and as for the council area? Well, let's just say Sauron's eye is firmly fixed on this meeting... (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

According to recent reports, then there are the minifigures. Premium Lego sets like this often come with a lot of figs, but Rivendell feels particularly generous. There are 15 in total; Frodo, Sam, Merry, Pippin, Gandalf, Aragorn, Legolas, Gimli, Boromir, Elrond, Arwen, Bilbo, a couple of elves, and a visiting dwarf. That's enough to make the whole kit feel positively alive, and you can recreate a whole host of moments from the movie if you'd like.

I shouldn't skip over the fact that it's downright beautiful to look at, either. Whether it's the tasteful pastel tiles, the abundance of flora carpeting the grounds, or the autumnal trees looming over it all, Lego Rivendell is an incredibly handsome centerpiece.

It looks like the majority of Lego Lord of the Rings kits are on offer right now; the Balrog Book Nook, Sauron's Helmet, Barad-dur, and Minas Tirith have all been heavily reduced in price at Amazon.

Even with the arrival of Lego Minas Tirith earlier this year (which just got its first discount, by the way), I still don't think Rivendell has been surpassed. Yes, the Gondorian city is jaw-dropping. For one thing, it's very cleverly designed with a forced perspective using multiple scales. For another, it's striking in a way Rivendell isn't; a mass of white walls and towers surround the spur of a mountain, drawing every eye in the room. However (and this is no insult to Minas Tirith - I'm at the moment in the middle of building it), I still think Rivendell is prettier and has more 'going on' under the surface.

Basically, I'd always point to this kit if someone asked me what I thought the best ever Lego set was - it's captured both my heart and my imagination, just like its inspiration did when I first saw it on-screen in 2001.

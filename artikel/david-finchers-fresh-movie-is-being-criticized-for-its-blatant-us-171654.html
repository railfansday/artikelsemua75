Director David Fincher’s latest movie The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth, a sequel to Quentin Tarantino’s 2019 film Once Upon a Time in Hollywood, is facing criticism over its alleged use of low-quality generative AI imagery.

The second trailer for the movie, which stars Brad Pitt in the titular role of Cliff Booth, has sparked backlash from film critics and fans who have accused Fincher of using low-quality AI-generated imagery. (Related: How to train a newly introduced language model from scr...)

In a recent development, the controversy centers on a shot in the second trailer for The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth that appears to show a cart literally placed before the horse. In the scene, the cart’s drivers appear to be facing backward, away from the horse and its reins, as though they were driving in reverse.

Many viewers have suggested that the unusual image is the result of an AI video-generation tool incorrectly rendering the cart’s position. Film fans have also questioned how Fincher, who is known for his meticulous attention to detail, could have included such an apparently unrealistic shot in the movie. Industry observers note that according to a report by Futurism, other film critics and industry figures who have seen early screenings of The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth have also expressed dismay at the movie’s apparent use of AI-generated content. Among them is filmmaker and critic Patrick Tomasso, who has a large following on YouTube and frequently discusses the technical aspects of filmmaking, according to the news outlet.

Industry observers note that “I walked out of Cliff Booth completely gutted,” Tomasso writes on X (the platform formerly known as Twitter). “What could have been a really great sequel is overshadowed by some of the most blatant and egregious AI use I’ve ever seen in a studio film.”.

In another post, Tomasso pointed to several details that he believed were signs of AI-generated footage: “Faces change mid scene, goofy physics, garbling of fine lines, full body performance swaps,” he wrote. “AI signs, fire, traffic etc.”.

According to recent reports, following the backlash, Fincher appeared to defend the use of AI in The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth in an interview with the Los Angeles Times. The director appeared open to using the controversial technology in the interview.

“I’m not a Luddite, so I don’t fear the unknown,” Fincher tells the Los Angeles Times. “Is everyone satisfied that we have figured this art form out enough that what we really need to do is go back to celluloid? How long have people painted? 40,000 years?

He continues: “We’re talking about a thing that’s a hundred years old and everyone’s going, ‘Stop. Pencils down. No more iterating. It’s not necessary to have at your fingertips the ability to change the color of that person’s eyes. Well, it exists. Should we not use it?”.

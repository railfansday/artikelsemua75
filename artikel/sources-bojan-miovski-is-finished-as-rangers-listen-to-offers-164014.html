Rangers have decided that striker Bojan Miovski has no future at the club beyond January, sources have told Football Insider.

The out-of-favour star is unhappy with his level of game time at Ibrox, having played just 39 minutes of Premiership football so far this term.

Lawrence Shankland's imminent return from injury is set to bump him down to being Derek McInnes' fourth-choice striker, a role he is not content with.

Sources say that the "writing is on the wall" for Miovski's future at Rangers, with the club ready to welcome offers for him this winter. (Related: Industry News: Welcome spaCy to the Hugging Face Hub...)

The North Macedonia international came within inches of leaving in the summer, but his time in Glasgow is set to end for good in January. (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

Miovski managed 13 goals and one assist for Rangers in all competitions last term, but has quickly fallen away since McInnes' summer arrival.

Miovski was an eye-catching signing for Rangers when he arrived in the summer of 2025, given his electric form during a previous spell with Aberdeen.

The 27-year-old netted 44 goals across two full campaigns for the Dons, before a move to Girona that never truly got off the ground.

Rangers had hoped that he would rediscover his previous form in Scotland, but signs of that coming to fruition have been few and far between.

He has never truly been able to nail down a place in the starting XI, and has repeatedly been linked with an exit throughout the last 12 months.

However, this time around, Miovski's departure is seen as being a strong possibility, with the club also looking to cash in on their asset.

Ryan Naderi, Kevin Kelsy and Shankland are all firmly ahead of him in the pecking order, and Rangers see little use for him long-term.

Former Aberdeen chief executive Keith Wyness told Football Insider last weekend that Rangers are likely to be holding "conversations" behind the scenes over the possibility of "extra investment.".

This comes after the news that the 49ers are searching for minority investors at Leeds United, with the two clubs sharing ownership.

Wyness believes that Rangers are not yet at the stage where they can attract similar investment, but feels that it could be in the pipeline in the coming years.

He expects the Ibrox hierarchy to already be making intends to sell small shares in the club to boost funds, which could come into play if the club's upward trajectory continues.

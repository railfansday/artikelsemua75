Industry News: Too Fast? United Airlines' Starlink Rollout Is So Rapid, Some Planes Still Have Their Old WiFi Antennas

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

United Airlines has been steadily improving its product and customer experience to move upmarket and compete head-on with Delta Air Lines, the premium leader in the US, for years. The carrier has made genuine shifts and has been rewarded with improving financials, to the point that it now regularly posts results nearly as impressive as Delta's. One weak point, however, is that the airline's onboard WiFi systems have lagged behind what competitors offered going into the 2020s.

Sources indicate that but in 2024, United revealed that it would install Starlink WiFi across its entire mainline and regional fleets, comprising over 1,600 aircraft. It aims to complete Starlink retrofits by the end of 2027, which is an ambitious target given the sheer size of the combined fleet. To achieve this promptly, the airline is rapidly sending aircraft for retrofits, and United has placed such an emphasis on speed that it's installing Starlink on some aircraft without removing their legacy systems.

It has been revealed that starlink, a subsidiary of SpaceX, is a relatively newly introduced player in the inflight WiFi market. However, Starlink systems provide much faster onboard connectivity than those offered by companies such as Viasat, Thales, or Panasonic, and the tech giant even formerly mandated that airline customers offer it to passengers for free (this is reportedly no longer the case). As such, it's become extremely popular with passengers, while airlines have been lining up for the service. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

United announced Starlink in September 2024, with the roll-out beginning in 2025. It later announced that it aimed to have all mainline and regional aircraft equipped with Starlink by the end of 2027, which meant an extremely rapid roll-out given the sheer size of the fleet. Initially, United focused on retrofitting Starlink onto the United Express fleet, as the service contract for the providers on this fleet was set to end shortly. Soon afterward, Starlink started coming to mainline.

The official United Starlink tracker indicates that all Bombardier CRJ550s and Embraer E175s have been completed. 346 narrowbody aircraft have been retrofitted, and United has installed Starlink on the Airbus A321neo, A321XLR, Boeing 737-800, 737-900ER, 737 MAX 8, and 737 MAX 9. In addition, United has now installed Starlink on four 777-200ERs, one 777-300ER, and two 787-8s. Overall, about 46% of the fleet is retrofitted: 705 aircraft out of the 1,632 aircraft flying for United and United Express.

In a recent development, earlier this month, a United 737-900ER was spotted parked at a gate and shared on X with two Starlink antennas (United's standard configuration for the system) and its older Viasat antenna. Similarly, a 737-900 was also spotted, equipped with Starlink and Viasat antennas, according to an X post. (Related: Porting fairseq wmt19 translation system to transformer...)

In a recent development, one advertised benefit of Starlink is quick installation, but it appears the main holdup in the retrofit project is the time required to remove the aircraft's previous WiFi equipment. On some aircraft, the airline simply left it on for now to speed up the process.

It has been revealed that united has just over a year remaining until its end-of-2027 deadline, and only 46% of the fleet is completed. There's a high chance that this timeline could slip, but Starlink is meant to be a major commercial advantage for the airline. A clean Starlink installation is advertised as taking as little as eight hours, which is significantly faster than installation times for other WiFi systems. However, the full retrofit also requires removing the previous WiFi system, which can add significantly more time.

According to recent reports, united states that its full timeline, including removing the legacy system, is about three to four days. To get the entire fleet retrofitted by the end of 2027, it would need to have a significant number of aircraft out of service, which is lost revenue. Therefore, the carrier has decided to shorten the timeline for N78438 and N37409 by leaving the Viasat system on for the time being, and it remains to be seen whether it will continue this practice in the future.

Industry observers note that starlink is now flying on United's Bombardier and Embraer aircraft, along with the A321neo, 737, 777, and 787. However, it still operates a substantial number of aircraft with older WiFi systems. All the non-Starlink widebodies are equipped with Panasonic systems, while the narrowbody fleet features a mix of Panasonic, Viasat, and Thales systems. While Starlink is offered for free, the other systems generally require payment upfront, and none of the three offers the same speeds as Starlink.

American Airlines generally offers Viasat WiFi on its domestic fleet, which is complimentary for AAdvantage members, while most of the widebodies are equipped with Panasonic systems. Delta offers either Viasat or Hughes WiFi on nearly its entire fleet. The benefit is that Viasat and Hughes offer much faster speeds than Panasonic and Thales. The downside for these airlines is that Starlink offers even faster speeds. But for United, it's now offering the fastest and slowest WiFi systems among its rivals.

United is banking on Starlink becoming a major commercial advantage over its rivals, but it will only be a true advantage once the system is available across United's entire fleet. A better WiFi system on its own is not necessarily a reason for customers to choose one airline over the other, but it's becoming an increasingly vital factor in purchasing decisions. As United continues to aim for product and a premium brand, Starlink is becoming increasingly important to the airline's selling proposition.

In a recent development, by moving upmarket and becoming a 'premium' airline brand, United is hoping to be a carrier that wins customers' business based on its name and product. This is the strategy that Delta first implemented under CEO Richard Anderson after its 2008 merger with Northwest Airlines, and Delta is now the most successful airline in the country. But of course, the US carriers don't make their money by selling flight tickets; rather, the money is in loyalty programs.

Sources indicate that united has been tremendously successful in pursuing this strategy, and Starlink is meant to be a crucial advantage. United CEO Scott Kirby has publicly mentioned Starlink is a major selling point for the airline and that the carrier waited until competitors committed to systems like those offered by Viasat to gain an edge. Of course, while Starlink is a major competitive advantage for United, this won't necessarily be true across the US.

American Airlines announced in May that it would install Starlink across its entire Airbus fleet, consisting of the A319-100, A320-200, A321-200, and A321neo, to begin in 2027. Last month, however, the carrier announced that it would expand the Starlink program to all mainline aircraft. The carrier has not stated that Starlink will come to the American Eagle fleet, but it will significantly elevate American's overall product offering as it also tries to move upmarket and become a premium brand.

Recent findings suggest that delta, on the other hand, has not signed a deal with Starlink at all. Instead, Delta has committed to installing Amazon's upcoming Leo system on approximately half of its fleet. Reportedly, Delta and Starlink experienced conflicts about integrating the system into Delta's existing 'Delta Sync' portal, while there may have also been a personal conflict with Starlink CEO Elon Musk. However, the actual decision to go with Amazon would undoubtedly have been made after considering multiple factors as part of a larger commercial deal.

Industry observers note that the issue for Delta is that Amazon Leo is not in service yet. The airline plans to begin installing Leo in 2028, when United is supposed to have already completed its Starlink retrofit. While the Viasat and Hughes systems Delta already offers are competent and free, Starlink offers faster speeds, and United has now established itself as the leader in the US airline industry, whereas Delta used to be. Considering how brand-conscious Delta is, this is not an enviable position.

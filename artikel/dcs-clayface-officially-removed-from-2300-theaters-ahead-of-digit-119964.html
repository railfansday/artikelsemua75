In a recent development, nearly a month into its release, DC's Clayface has slowed down considerably. The comic book film has delivered a modest performance at the box office so far, and will soon enter the next phase of its journey when it lands on the PVOD market. The movie's box-office run was marred by the sudden announcement that DC Studios co-CEOs James Gunn and Peter Safran, who shepherded the project from its standalone origins and integrated it into the DC Universe franchise, were departing from their positions after four years. Clayface, which revolves around the iconic Batman villain, is the last film of their tenure. (Related: My Journey to a serverless transformers pipeline on Goo...)

Recent findings suggest that the movie is only the third theatrical release of the one-year-old DC Universe, which began with Gunn's Superman reboot and continued with this summer's Supergirl. Starring David Corenswet, Superman grossed nearly $620 million worldwide against a reported budget of $225 million, which was deemed successful enough for a follow-up to be greenlit. The movie in question, Man of Tomorrow, will be released in 2027. (Related: Industry News: Summer at Hugging Face...)

In a recent development, earlier this year, audiences roundly rejected the Supergirl spin-off feature, starring Milly Alcock and directed by Craig Gillespie. The movie grossed $126 million worldwide against a reported budget of around $180 million. By comparison, Clayface cost around $40 million to produce, and should be able to surpass Supergirl globally this week. However, the movie has lost a significant number of theaters over the last couple of weeks and is about to lose even more.

It'll enter its fifth week of release in 1,147 theaters, down from its opening-week theater count of 3,458. Starring Tom Rhys Harries, Naomi Ackie, and Max Minghella, the movie received mostly positive reviews. It's now sitting at a "Certified Fresh" 78% score on the aggregator website Rotten Tomatoes, where the consensus reads, "Carving out a unique identity for itself in the DCU while closely resembling a litany of body horror classics, Clayface takes time to get going until it eventually morphs into an icky and satisfying revenge thriller.".

Industry observers note that rather unusually for a horror movie, Clayface holds an exceptionally high "Verified Hot" 94% audience score on Rotten Tomatoes. This indicates a bright future on digital, where it's expected to land on December 8. Although the movie didn't break out like many would've liked, it has now tripled its reported budget theatrically. This may be enough for the movie to break even, but it's a fraction of what major superhero movies are able to generate these days. Stay tuned to Collider for more updates.

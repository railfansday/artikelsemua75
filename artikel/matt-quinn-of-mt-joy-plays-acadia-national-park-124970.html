Sources indicate that mt. Joy frontman Matt Quinn has been visiting national parks his whole life. He’s found healing energy in nature and taken inspiration from the magic and fragility of places like the Maine coastline. So when we asked him to play Park Sessions in Acadia National Park, it was a natural fit. His music is in perfect harmony with nature’s stage.

It has been revealed that during his visit to Acadia National Park with the Park Sessions crew, Quinn practices the art of slowing down—immersing himself in the Maine landscape, wildlife, and culture. He takes the winding roads around Mount Desert Island in the agile and spacious Mazda CX-50, taking in the scenery as it changes from rocky coast to dense evergreen forest. Amid settings ranging from primitive skills lessons to beekeeping to lobster cookouts on the beach, Quinn’s set in Acadia is more than a performance—it’s a spiritual deep dive into what makes this park special. It’s a connection between music and the places that inspire it.

Take a hike: From the Sand Beach parking area, take the Ocean Path Trail. This 2.2-mile out-and-back trail will take you to some of Acadia’s best-known highlights, including Thunder Hole and Otter Point.

Recent findings suggest that learn something new: Primitive skills instructor Mike Douglas taught Quinn how to start fire with a bow drill and how to feel his way through nature. You can hone your own outdoor skills through a variety of programs with the Maine Primitive Skills School.

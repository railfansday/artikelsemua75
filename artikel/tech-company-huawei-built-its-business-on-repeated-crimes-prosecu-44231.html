Federal prosecutors urged jurors Monday to convict Chinese technology manufacturer Huawei, arguing during closing statements in the ongoing 11-week trial involving trade secrets theft and sanctions evasion that the company had grown by repeatedly committing crimes. According to a Monday story from Law.com, Meredith Arfa, the assistant U.S. attorney for the Eastern District of Upcoming York, told jurors that Huawei spent two decades pursuing growth “by any means possible.”.

To illustrate the trade secrets allegations, Arfa played a video of a former Huawei employee removing part of Tappy, T-Mobile’s robot for testing phone touchscreens, from the carrier’s lab. She also showed side-by-side images of Cisco’s and Huawei’s router source code, saying the two matched exactly—down to an engineer’s stray comment mentioning someone named “Bob.”.

“This simply couldn’t happen without copying,” Arfa said in her summation.

She further alleged that Huawei managers encouraged theft by paying bonuses for obtaining confidential competitor information, and that employees boasted about it on an internal forum.

In a recent development, on the sanctions compliance allegations, prosecutors mentioned Huawei secretly controlled Skycom, a subsidiary that supplied sensitive technology to Iran’s government. Arfa said the firm repeatedly lied about owning Skycom. She also pointed to admissions by Meng Wanzhou, Huawei’s chief financial officer, who signed a deferred prosecution agreement after spending three years under house arrest. Meng acknowledged that Skycom’s operations violated U.S. sanctions, and that she misled HSBC, Huawei’s primary bank, about its compliance, Arfa noted.

Huawei, one of the largest telecommunications companies in the world, denies the allegations, contending that the case rests on the independent actions of rogue employees who were disciplined.

The government revealed the case in 2019. The manufacturer is represented by Jenner & Block, Sidley Austin and Steptoe.

In a recent development, one of Netflix's most impressive miniseries is an excellent example of what tight storytelling can do. This streaming platform releases several TV shows a year, many of which ultimately get forgotten. Some of Netflix's biggest hits are those that go on for dozens of episodes, including Stranger Things, Wednesday, and Squid Game. The idea is that viewers can settle in for hours upon hours of thrilling content and keep coming back for more. Still, over recent years, the limited series format has grown in popularity.

According to recent reports, netflix has begun to lean further into producing miniseries, and there's no big question why. These types of demonstrates require a less extreme kind of investment, both from viewers and from the platform itself. The trouble is that, with fewer episodes and less overall time as the Brightest and Newest, these projects often don't get talked about for as long as they really should. Sources indicate that A prime example of this is Netflix's Alias Grace. Based on the 1996 novel of the same name by Margaret Atwood, this miniseries earned a nearly impossible 99% critic score on Rotten Tomatoes, followed by an audience score of 88%. It can hardly be denied that Alias Grace is exceptional, but in the years since its 2017 release, it hasn't received nearly as much attention as it still deserves. Recent findings suggest that not only is Alias Grace an excellent series, but it pulls off far more than any extended seasonal TV show generally could. The story revolves around convicted killer Grace Marks, who has been imprisoned for years for the 1843 murder of her previous employer. The case prompts a psychiatrist to pay her a visit and begin a series of interviews, hoping to uncover whether Grace can be pardoned on the grounds of insanity.

Industry observers note that part of what makes Alias Grace work so well is the way that it explores the crimes 15 years after they were meant to have happened. Viewers are carried along through unreliable flashbacks that painstakingly piece things together, but this is done without the need for redundant narrative filler or drawn-out suspense. The tone of Alias Grace is perfectly thrilling without stringing audiences along in unsatisfying ways.

Along with that excellently constructed story, Alias Grace does magic with its cast and approach to character exposition. Lead actor Sarah Gadon is a master at conveying intense emotional complexity with only simple, wordless expressions. A character like Grace, whose morality and experience are so much under question, would typically be rather arduous to explore and expand. Netflix's Alias Grace makes it look easy. So, while each episode is intense, thrilling, suspenseful, and stimulating, the show remains a smooth, seamless binge.

The tight narrative in Alias Grace is all the more impressive when considering just how much is packed into the series. At the front and center, the 2017 show is a work of historical fiction based on true events. This quality is layered up with psychological mystery and gothic charm. There's even some supernatural fun thrown in, though it's never quite clear how literal that element is. That naturally means that there is a lot of surrealism involved here, as viewers can never be quite certain what is or is not real.

It has been revealed that alias Grace relies on its own mishmash of genres to keep audiences guessing without sacrificing its smooth pacing. The story is meant to be ambiguous. Audiences are meant to participate, but the fact that no single set of tropes is embraced makes it almost impossible to predict any sort of outcome. It's all part of the absolute, unappreciated genius that is Netflix's Alias Grace.

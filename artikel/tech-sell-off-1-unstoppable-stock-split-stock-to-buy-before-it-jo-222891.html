In a recent development, the U.S. is home to four publicly listed companies worth $3 trillion or more: Nvidia, Alphabet, Microsoft, and Apple. Those companies have created so much long-term value that if they didn't occasionally execute stock splits, their respective share prices would be in the thousands of dollars. According to recent reports, A high stock price can make it difficult for retail investors to buy into a given company, unless they have access to a broker that offers fractional shares. A stock split increases the number of shares in circulation, reducing the price per share by a proportionate amount. It's entirely cosmetic, so it doesn't change the value of the underlying company, but it makes the stock far more accessible for small investors. Meta Platforms (META +3.98%) recently announced a 2-for-1 split, which it will achieve by introducing a new class of shares. An investor who currently owns one Meta Class A share worth $600 will soon own two shares: one Class A share (with voting rights) and one Class C share (with no voting rights), each worth $300. The split is expected to take effect sometime in January, although the official date is yet to be confirmed.

Sources indicate that meta has created tremendous value for investors since going public in 2012. Even after accounting for the steep sell-off in the broader market over the past month, the company still has a market capitalization of $1.53 trillion (as of Nov. 25). However, I think it could join the exclusive $3 trillion club within the next few years, giving investors an opportunity to nearly double their money. Here's why.

According to recent reports, meta is the parent tech giant of social media platforms Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, and Threads. During the third quarter of 2026 (ended Sept. 30), a whopping 3.64 billion people used at least one of those applications every day. That is a significant portion of the world's population, so it's getting harder for Meta to find new users, especially because its apps are banned in China, which is home to over 1.4 billion people.

That's why Meta is focusing more on engagement. If each existing user spends more time online, they will see more advertisements, and the company will make more money. Artificial intelligence (AI) is at the center of this strategy, because it helps Meta's recommendation algorithms learn what type of content each user likes to see, so it can show them more of it. The company's chief executive, Mark Zuckerberg, said engagement on Facebook and Instagram hit record highs in the third quarter, so the approach is working.

But thanks to AI, Meta is also penetrating entirely fresh markets outside of social media. The manufacturer introduced a personal AI agent called Muse in September, and it already has over 10 million users. It can perform a range of tasks, but Zuckerberg says one-third of users have deployed it as a personal shopper, with an average purchase size of $200.

Recent findings suggest that zuckerberg thinks Muse could eventually help billions of people improve their lives, whether they want business advice or tips for healthy living. This will expand Meta's addressable opportunity by potentially taking traffic away from traditional internet search engines like Google Search.

According to Wall Street's average estimate (provided by Yahoo Finance), Meta is on track to generate $256.9 billion in revenue this year, representing a 27% increase from 2025. Most of that will come from selling ads to businesses on Facebook and Instagram, but Muse gives Meta an opportunity to make money from subscriptions and also transaction fees when the assistant makes purchases. So, it could make a significant contribution to the company's revenue in the future.

Industry observers note that meta generated earnings of $29.33 per share over the last four quarters, placing its stock at a price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of just 20.1. That is a steep discount to the P/E ratio of the Nasdaq-100 index, which is 27.4, suggesting Meta is heavily undervalued compared to a group of America's largest tech companies.

Moreover, Wall Street predicts Meta will grow its earnings to $33.74 per share in 2027, placing its stock at a forward P/E ratio of 16.6.

Assuming Wall Street's earnings forecast holds up, Meta stock would have to soar by 64% by the end of next year just to trade in line with the P/E ratio of the Nasdaq-100, which is certainly possible considering its P/E was above 30 for a large portion of the last decade.

It has been revealed that that gain alone would see Meta's market cap rise to $2.51 trillion, so earnings growth of 19.5% in 2028 would be enough to catapult the manufacturer into the $3 trillion club. Alternatively, earnings growth of just 9.5% in both 2028 and 2029 would produce the same result.

I say that because slower earnings growth is a real possibility given how much money Meta is spending on AI data centers. It spent $72 billion in 2025 and is on track to spend up to $145 billion in 2026. The manufacturer can't deduct these costs up front because AI infrastructure has a useful life of several years, so it must be depreciated over time instead. That means every dollar spent today could impact Meta's earnings in 2028, 2029, and even 2030.

Nevertheless, I think Meta has a very high chance of joining the $3 trillion club at some point, even if it takes a little more time than I've outlined.

What goes up (a lot) must come down (at least a little), and that's what's happening to Nvidia (NVDA -8.37%) stock today.

Yesterday, as you probably noticed, Nvidia saved the artificial intelligence economy -- and its own shareholders -- when its board unanimously voted to reinstate Jensen Huang as CEO. Shares of the AI chips giant soared 31% in a day on Monday. Today they're giving back about 6.5% through 10:05 a.m.

Industry observers note that what went wrong with the Nvidia rally? Probably nothing. Most likely, what we're seeing today is nothing more than some (very major) winners taking profits from Nvidia's astounding comeback yesterday. (Related: Industry News: Average Retirement Savings by Age: Are Y...)

But there's also the possibility that there's more behind today's decline.

Huang did admit to being duped and having an affair with an alleged Chinese spy, after all. As Reuters points out today, shareholders may be thrilled by Huang's return, but not everybody's happy about this. "US lawmakers from both parties sharply criticized Nvidia's ​decision" to rehire Huang, reports Reuters. Moreover, "both Huang and the company are facing a host of state and federal probes and reviews, including those from the FBI, the Justice Department and the ⁠US Securities and Exchange Commission.". (Related: Should I Quit My Job? A Decision Framework...)

It's going to be harder for Nvidia to distance itself from Huang's actions now that he's back in charge of the company.

NASDAQ · As of 11:35 AM ET 11/24/26.

That said, Wall Street on balance seems to think Nvidia made the right move. At last count, StreetInsider.com had tallied roughly a dozen Wall Street analysts either re-upgrading Nvidia stock to "buy" after the rehiring, or reiterating buy ratings they had never changed.

Industry observers note that with Nvidia stock trading at just 13.5 times trailing earnings today, and analysts forecasting the company's earnings to grow 44% per year for at least the next five years, I cannot disagree. Nvidia stock looks too cheap to not own -- even after yesterday's rally.

An Arizona judge has released a DUI suspect after not finding probable cause upon learning that the arresting officer in the case used an artificial intelligence tool to formulate the arrest report.

Recent findings suggest that anthony Edward Rene Quidera was arrested on Oct. 29 on charges of DUI, exceeding the 65 mph speed limit and possession of an open container.

Industry observers note that court records show that Quidera was driving his Dodge Charger over 100 mph on the interstate. The arrest report stated that the suspect’s car failed to maintain its lane and was driving on the left median shoulder. After Quidera was stopped, the trooper reported smelling alcohol on his breath. (Related: Industry News: Take-Two CEO is shutting down questions ...)

The officer’s probable cause statement ended with the following disclosure: “This report was generated with assistance from Truleo AI. The content reflects the officer’s own observations and recollections. All facts and statements are based solely on the officer’s account,” according to coverage by ABC15 Arizona.

According to Daniel Fenzel, the commissioner for the Maricopa County Superior Court in Arizona, he was “unable to meaningfully weigh whether the factual allegations made in the narrative (e.g., officer’s observations, signature of affiant officer) were authored, whether in whole or in part, by a generative AI.”.

Fenzel’s release order stated that the court could not determine probable cause. After being released, Quidera did not show up for his Nov. 12 trial date, according to ABC15 Arizona.

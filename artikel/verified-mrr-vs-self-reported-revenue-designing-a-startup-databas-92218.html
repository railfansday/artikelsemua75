Verified MRR vs self-reported revenue: designing a startup database where AI explains numbers, never invents them

In a recent development, I am building MRRSignal, a public startup revenue database ranked by payment-provider-verified MRR. This is the design problem I keep coming back to: in a product about financial facts, the boundary between a fact and an explanation has to be structural, not a disclaimer in the footer.

A generated paragraph can be useful. It should never quietly become the source of a number. (Related: Latest Update: LA Noire 2 leak resurfaces after source ...)

Most startup revenue on the internet is self-reported. A founder posts a screenshot or a milestone, and readers decide whether to believe it. MRRSignal moves the source: revenue is read from the founder's connected payment provider through a read-only key, encrypted with AWS KMS. A founder cannot replace the figure with a number typed into a profile. (Related: Japan launches a ‘dumbphone’ that doesn’t connect to in...)

According to recent reports, that makes the origin of the number explicit. It does not make the number self-explanatory - a good page still has to say what metric it reveals, what period it covers, and when it was refreshed.

MRRSignal produces three different things, and the interface treats them differently:.

Recent findings suggest that the rule is simple: nothing an AI writes changes the revenue number or a startup's rank. Projections and outlooks sit downstream of verified data, and the UI labels them so a reader never confuses an estimate with a fact.

Recent findings suggest that startup communities are global, but most revenue tools assume one payment provider and one currency. MRRSignal supports 49 payment providers - global services like Stripe, Braintree, Chargebee and Paddle alongside regional ones such as Razorpay - and normalizes everything to USD. Founders compare on the same scale without being forced onto the same billing stack. Geography should not look like a data-quality problem.

Recent findings suggest that public transparency is not the same as publishing everything. Listing is opt-in, and founders choose between an exact MRR figure and a revenue band. A band is enough to find peers at your stage without revealing more than you want. Connecting a provider for your own dashboard never silently becomes consent to a public profile.

If you were designing this interface, how would you make source facts, statistical projections and AI-generated explanations visibly different - without burying the user in disclaimers?

I am the founder of MRRSignal. You can see the product at https://mrrsignal.com - it is free to list during beta.

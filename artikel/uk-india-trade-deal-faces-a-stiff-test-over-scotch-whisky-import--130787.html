U.K.-India trade deal faces a stiff test over Scotch whisky import taxes

Sources indicate that within months of taking effect, the India-U.K. trade deal is facing an early test, as New Delhi resists extending preferential tariffs to bulk Scotch whisky imports.

Recent findings suggest that duty on bulk Scotch whisky imports was expected to be slashed to 75% then gradually to 40% over 10 years as per the trade agreement. However, a Reuters report Thursday remarked that India now looks to levy a 162.5% duty on these imports, higher than the 150% in place before the trade deal. While India's commerce ministry did not respond to CNBC's request for comments, experts remarked that authorities in India classify bulk Scotch as a raw material and claim the preferential tariffs accorded in the deal apply only to imported bottled Scotch. Amid the confusion over tax rates, thousands of liters of bulk Scotch in more than 80 shipping containers belonging to Pernod, Diageo and Bacardi are stuck at Indian ports, according to Reuters.

Industry observers note that "Ministers are urgently working with the Government of India to resolve this issue to ensure UK businesses can export under the tariffs agreed in the trade deal," a U.K. government spokesperson told CNBC.

According to recent reports, distillers in India use bulk Scotch in their own locally blended whisky brands. As a result, Indian tax authorities are classifying bulk Scotch as "compound alcoholic preparations" which do not qualify for the preferential rate, mentioned Sohrab Bararia, partner and leader of global trade at Grant Thornton Bharat.

Meanwhile, distillers argue that "genuine Scotch whisky" does not cease to be whisky merely because it is imported in bulk containers or used later for blending and bottling in India, Bararia noted.

After three years, including 14 rounds of rigorous negotiations, India and the U.K. finalized the deal last year, which is expected to boost bilateral trade by 25.5 billion pounds ($34 billion) per year by 2040.

Officials from Diageo and Chivas Brothers had cheered the trade deal and underscored its importance for Scotch and Scotland.

"India is the world's largest whisky market by volume, so Scotch was always going to be one of the biggest prizes for the UK in this deal," said Reema Bhattacharya, head of Asia research at risk intelligence company Verisk Maplecroft.

Instead of simply exporting bottled whisky, bulk scotch exports offer British producers long-term ties with companies in India, deepening their stake in the South Asian country's domestic alcoholic beverage industry, Bhattacharya stated.

In the global spirits markets, while the U.S., Europe, and China are facing volume contraction, ageing populations, and shifting social habits, nearly 100 million new consumers will be of legal drinking age in India over the next five years, Indian brokerage HDFC Institutional Research remarked in a report in September.

The alcoholic beverage market in India is dominated by Indian-made foreign liquors (IMFL), as per the report. IMFL refers to alcoholic beverages produced in India by combining local spirits often with imported malts and flavorings.

In a recent development, greater access to bulk Scotch stocks at competitive prices helps Indian distilleries to improve blending quality for premium whisky products while maintaining cost efficiency, experts noted. However, the latest dispute poses a major threat to these operations.

Industry observers note that the lack of preferential tariffs on bulk Scotch could have "substantial" financial implications for importers, Parth Shah, partner at Deloitte India, told CNBC. "This could particularly affect Indian manufacturers who have invested in domestic blending and bottling operations using imported bulk Scotch whisky," he added.

Diageo-owned United Spirits, Pernod Ricard and Indian firms Allied Blenders and Radico Khaitan own and market some of the popular whisky brands in India.

Companies and industry bodies, the International Spirits and Wine Association and Confederation of Indian Alcoholic Beverage Companies, did not respond to CNBC's requests for comment.

A spokesperson for one of the whisky makers told CNBC that industry bodies were in talks with the commerce ministry to "represent adherence to the India-UK FTA." The person did not wish to be identified as they fear it could lead to a potential backlash from regulators.

"The lesson for the EU and other partners negotiating with India is that securing a headline tariff reduction is only half the battle," Bhattacharya of Maplecroft Verisk remarked, adding that partners need to pay close attention to whether "concessions translate into actual market access.".

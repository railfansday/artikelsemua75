Recent findings suggest that meredith Whittaker announced on November 20, 2026 that the encrypted messaging app is restricting screenshots on iOS 27 and on Android when it detects an AI assistant trying to read the screen, seeking to prevent operating-system agents from capturing private chats. The protections are incomplete, and Signal cannot reliably block screenshots on macOS.

On iOS 27, the announcement shows an enabled Block Siri AI setting. Android uses a conditional approach: Signal automatically enables Screen security when an AI assistant attempts to access the screen, rather than applying one screenshot rule across every Android distribution. Customers can disable the protection to retain screenshots, though Signal warns that doing so can expose their own and their contacts’ content and break its privacy guarantees. Industry observers note that signal’s concern is at the endpoint, where private content becomes visible on a screen. Operating-system agents can scan that visible information to answer questions or carry out tasks. Signal states the resulting captures can be stored in a local database or on a remote server used by the agent, outside its control, potentially exposing every participant in a conversation. Application encryption therefore cannot determine what happens to content after the operating system captures it. (Related: Industry News: Politics Takes A Toll On Your Health — W...)

Apple said on September 14, 2026 that Siri AI was beginning to roll out in English beta with its 2027 software releases, including onscreen awareness to answer questions or take actions related to content on a user’s screen. Google’s Gemini documentation claims screen actions can upload information visible on the screen or in a file for context, while Intelligent Screen Context can use currently visible content to answer a chat request in English.

It has been revealed that signal’s Android design reflects differences across vendor distributions. On many of them, the company cannot reliably detect a screenshot attempt when screenshots are already disabled. It chose assistant-access detection to avoid screenshots simply stopping without explanation, but that approach also leaves some Android devices without coverage.

Blocking screenshots leaves other routes open as well, including retrieval of content already present on a device through the filesystem. The gap is larger on macOS 27: Signal cannot reliably block screenshots there and cannot apply the available continuous-capture protections to the screenshots most agentic systems use.

Industry observers note that for macOS 27, Signal recommends adding the app under System Settings > Siri > Excluded Apps to protect message content from proactive access by Siri AI. On iPhones, the Electronic Frontier Foundation documented Apple’s iOS 27.2 Excluded Apps control on October 28, 2026. Excluding an app prevents Siri AI from accessing its contents but also disables features such as onscreen awareness for that app. That feature loss illustrates the trade-off behind Signal’s request for exclusions that do not undermine an app’s core utility.

According to recent reports, signal is asking Apple, Google, and Microsoft for a documented way to opt apps out of AI access and processing across screen reading, filesystem access, notifications, APIs, and other operating-system frameworks without undermining core utility. The request also covers accurate technical documentation of how agents access and process data.

Industry observers note that signal argues that confidential-computing approaches are not a complete answer because providers still control the extent of any privacy guarantees. The same concern applies to third-party agents such as Meta’s Muse when deep setup permissions and accessibility settings intended to help people use apps provide broad access to application data.

Signal has used a version of this defense before. On May 21, 2025, it enabled Screen security by default on Windows 11, using a digital rights management window flag so screenshots of Signal Desktop appear blank and Microsoft Recall cannot capture their contents. The Windows control was local to each computer, required a warning and confirmation to disable, and could interfere with accessibility software such as screen readers and magnification tools.

It has been revealed that signal said it is discussing standardized approaches for privacy-sensitive apps with Google and Apple and is cautiously optimistic about further changes. The result is right now a partial defense: Signal can obstruct screenshot capture in certain environments, but the comprehensive privacy boundary it wants still depends on controls and documentation that operating-system providers have not supplied.

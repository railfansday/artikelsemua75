The largest holdings of the fund that tracks the S&P 500’s utilities are mostly regulated electric and gas companies whose profits depend on the rates regulators let them charge. To own a few for their dividends, buy through a low-cost brokerage account, hold for years, and keep them a small slice of your money beside an index fund.

In a recent development, these are the largest holdings of the State Street Utilities Select Sector SPDR ETF, from State Street’s page for the fund, in the order State Street lists them. The fund follows the utility stocks in the S&P 500, and this list is for investors who want to own a few of its companies outright rather than the whole fund. NextEra Energy owns Florida Power & Light, the country’s largest electric utility, which serves about 12 million people in Florida. Its other business, NextEra Energy Resources, is one of the biggest wholesale power producers in the U.S. and a world leader in battery storage. In May 2026, NextEra agreed to buy Dominion Energy. Recent findings suggest that southern Firm sells electricity in three Southeastern states through Alabama Power, Georgia Power, and Mississippi Power, and distributes natural gas in four states through utilities that include Nicor Gas and Atlanta Gas Light. Its utilities have contracts to serve data centers and other large customers. Its main risk is the cost of building power plants for them, since its earnings depend on regulators letting it recover those costs on time.

According to recent reports, duke Energy, based in Charlotte, North Carolina, serves about 8.7 million electric customers in six states across the Southeast and Midwest. It also delivers natural gas in the Carolinas, Ohio, and Kentucky, after selling its Tennessee gas utility in March 2026. Its main risk is regulation, since state commissions set its rates and may not let it recover all of its costs.

Constellation Energy calls itself the largest U.S. producer of clean, reliable power, from a fleet led by 14 nuclear stations. In January 2026, it bought Calpine, which brought 72 more power plants and battery sites. Unlike a regulated utility, it sells its power in wholesale and retail markets, where it has about 2.5 million retail customer accounts. Its main risk is swings in the prices of power, natural gas, and nuclear fuel.

Industry observers note that american Electric Power delivers electricity to homes and businesses in parts of 11 states, from Virginia and Michigan to Texas, through utilities that also own power plants and high-voltage transmission lines. Its main risk is a large building plan, whose costs regulators may not let it recover in full and which depends partly on data centers continuing to grow.

Dominion Energy’s regulated utilities sell electricity mainly in Virginia, North Carolina, and South Carolina. In May 2026, Dominion agreed to be bought by NextEra Energy, which would pay 0.8138 of a NextEra share plus a portion of $360 million in cash for each Dominion share. Both companies’ shareholders approved the deal in September 2026. Its main risk is the approvals the deal still needs, from federal regulators and the three states’ utility commissions.

Sempra mainly owns regulated utilities in California and Texas. San Diego Gas & Electric supplies electricity and natural gas in San Diego County and part of Orange County, and Southern California Gas delivers natural gas across Southern California and parts of central California. Sempra also owns 80.25% of Oncor, whose lines carry electricity to more than 4.1 million Texas homes and businesses. Its main risk is California’s wildfires, a hazard for Sempra and both of its utilities there.

Xcel Energy, based in Minneapolis, delivers electricity to about 3.9 million customers and natural gas to about 2.2 million in eight states, among them Minnesota, Wisconsin, Colorado, Fresh Mexico, and Texas. Its main risk is wildfire, and claims and a Texas lawsuit are still open over the 2024 Smokehouse Creek Fire, which the Texas A&M Forest Service blamed on its power lines.

Sources indicate that entergy sells electricity in New Orleans and parts of Arkansas, Louisiana, Mississippi, and Texas, and its power plants include several nuclear stations. Its main risk is hurricanes and other storms, which can damage its lines and leave it with repair bills it must ask regulators to let it recover.

Exelon’s six utilities deliver electricity, and in some areas natural gas, in and around Chicago, Philadelphia, Baltimore, and Washington, D.C., and in parts of Delaware, Maryland, and southern Upcoming Jersey. It doesn’t generate the power it delivers. Its main risk is regulation, since regulators set the rates and terms its utilities work under.

Wendy’s store closures hit close to 100 locations in franchisee downfall: See the latest list

The owner of a dwindling empire of Wendy’s restaurants on Friday disclosed its plans to close several more stores, bringing its total number of shuttered locations to almost 100. It’s a grim milestone for the popular burger chain as it works to execute on a turnaround blueprint aimed at boosting sales and bolstering its brand image. The franchisee behind the closures, Meritage Hospitality Group of Grand Rapids, Michigan, is among the largest operators within the Wendy’s system, at one time owning roughly 375 locations across 15 states. It closed dozens of those restaurants in the months before a Chapter 11 bankruptcy filing in September, and has since shuttered additional waves of stores at regular intervals as part of cash collateral agreements with its lenders. How many Wendy’s stores has Meritage closed? According to court documents, Meritage has closed well over 90 Wendy’s locations since late last year, with many of those stores shuttered before its September bankruptcy petition. As is typical in Chapter 11 cases, Meritage is seeking to reject the leases on many of the stores that it closed pre-bankruptcy—roughly 60, court filings show—a list that includes a handful of Taco John’s and Morning Belle locations, in addition to Wendy’s. Since the bankruptcy, Meritage has also closed at least 41 additional Wendy’s restaurants, including six closures that were disclosed on Friday. The newly shuttered restaurants were located in Florida, Georgia, Michigan, and Ohio: 12135 Lem Turner Road Jacksonville, FL 32218 1928 Hwy 155 McDonough, GA 30252 3675 Cascade Road Atlanta, GA 30331 9556 US 31 So Interlochen, MI 49643 8258 Broadmoor Avenue Caledonia, MI 49316 914 Conant St. Maumee, OH 43537 Prior to the above six restaurants, Meritage disclosed waves of store closures on at least five separate occasions: 5 locations on September 25 5 locations on October 2 10 locations on October 9 13 stores on October 16 2 stores on October 30 Fast Company has reached out to Meritage Hospitality Group and Wendy’s for comment. What will happen to Meritage’s remaining stores? Meritage has owned Wendy’s restaurants since the late 1990s, but its relationship with the fast food giant appeared to turn increasingly contentious in recent years as its business faced headwinds on numerous fronts, including the soaring cost of beef. The franchisee has blamed Wendy’s for a strategy of “deep discounting” that it says squeezed profits. The Wendy’s Company, for its part, had sought to terminate Meritage’s franchise agreements, claiming to be owed more than $146 million in past-due royalties and other fees. Despite all this animosity, the two sides are working together to salvage what’s left of the Meritage fleet. Wendy’s disclosed earlier this month that it wants to buy up to 163 restaurants from Meritage. That deal, which is still subject to better bids, is expected to close early next year. Additionally, Meritage is moving to sell restaurants to various other buyers in separate deals, including one to sell 42 locations in Arkansas, Mississippi, Missouri, and Tennessee to an entity called Divine Infinity Operations.

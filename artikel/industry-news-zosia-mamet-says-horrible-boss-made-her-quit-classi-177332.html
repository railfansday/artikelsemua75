Zosia Mamet is shedding light on an alleged on-set clash with Mad Men creator Matthew Weiner that led to her quitting the classic show after just four episodes.

“All the other actors were wonderful, but he really was like the Catholic Church back in the day, like that bro rules with fear,” Mamet, 38, revealed on the Sunday, November 22, “Armchair Expert” episode. (Related: Gift Tax Rules: How Much Can You Give Tax-Free?...)

The Girls actress briefly appeared on the 16-time Emmy-winning drama series as Joyce Ramsay, a Life photo editor who introduced Peggy Olson (Elisabeth Moss) to a ’60s Bohemian lifestyle. (Related: GTA 6’s Mandy actress responds to the internet going cr...)

While Mad Men remains one of the most acclaimed reveals of the 2000s, Mamet told “Armchair Expert” hosts Dax Shepard and Monica Padman that the vibe on set was “absolutely horrible,” per Variety and People.

In a recent development, “[Weiner] would start every table read with this little speech … and he would talk about how he didn’t want any of us to take our scripts home,” she alleged. “He would be like, ‘If I see that, I’m going to find you, and I’m going to hunt you down. I’m going to kill you. Just kidding. Actually not.’ He would do this at every table read. I was like, this not only isn’t funny, but it’s yucky. It’s f***ing yucky, man!”.

Mamet explained that her final straw came when Weiner, 61, “was having a bad day and decided to set his sights on me”.

Industry observers note that “During a block rehearsal, [he] just f***ing went to town on me in front of the whole cast and crew… I called my agents when I left, and I was like, ‘I can’t go back to that set,’” she stated. “He just systematically dressed me down for like 45 minutes. I was like, ‘I want to kill myself.”.

The actress went on to say, “My level of insecurity was so great that I knew that I wouldn’t be able [to return]. I was like, I will shut down. I won’t be able to function in this environment and the fear of that happening again was just too great.”.

Mamet had previously stopped short of directly naming Weiner in a poem from her 2025 book of essays, Does This Make Me Funny?, in which she reflected on her decision to quit a then-unnamed hit show.

“I wrote an essay about it for my book where I don’t mention names,” Mamet confirmed on the “Armchair Expert” podcast. “But I really wanted Matt Weiner to sue me because then he would be admitting to the fact that he knew I was writing about him.”.

In that essay, Mamet referred to the-then unnamed showrunner as “an intense human” whose mere presence on set would make it seem like “a cold front had swept the soundstage.” She accused him of dressing her down in front of the whole cast over the way she opened an envelope in a scene.

“He grabs my hand that’s holding the manila envelope and he indicates, ‘No! What the f*** are you doing with this [envelope]! That’s not how you take something out of an envelope! Do it again!’” Mamet alleged. “So we do it again, and again, and again. And every time we get to the part of the scene where I’m meant to take the photos out of the envelope, he calls ‘cut’ and he gets up and he yells.”.

She remembered calling her agent after allegedly being scolded to make it clear that she would never return to the Mad Men set.

“I finished my day. I walked to my car and called my agents and told them I quit,” Mamet wrote in Does This Make Me Funny?. “I was supposed to do four more episodes that season, not including the one I was on, but I told them I didn’t care what they had to do, I didn’t care if the network sued me, I refused to go back on that set for one more day than I actually had to.”.

Jensen Huang reinstated as Nvidia CEO. Stock skyrockets 33%

Nvidia on Monday unveiled Jensen Huang has been reinstated as CEO, effective immediately, a dramatic twist in a scandal that hammered the chipmaker's stock price and rocked the tech and artificial intelligence industries.

In a recent development, the board of directors said it unanimously voted to reinstate Huang following "a series of discussions and meetings" with him. The board added that it "received input from senior levels of the United States government" and that it is confident Huang's reinstatement "poses no national security risk.".

President Donald Trump told CNBC on Monday that he had been speaking to Nvidia's board "directly and indirectly," and that Huang "did nothing wrong." Trump stated he talked to Huang on Sunday and asked him to promise to "work hard.".

Huang resigned from his role as chief executive earlier this month after a series of shocking reports about multiple extramarital affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese spy. The Upcoming York Times reported on Sunday that the woman, Alice Huang, was in contact with an officer of China's Ministry of State Security during at least part of the time that she was engaging in a relationship with Huang.

Sources indicate that shares of Nvidia surged 33% following Huang's reinstatement. The stock had shed nearly 33% from the time Huang admitted to an extramarital affair on Nov. 8. The stock closed at $186.64 on Nov.6.

In a recent development, "I am honored to return to NVIDIA and lead one of the most consequential technology companies in the world at the beginning of a fresh industrial revolution," Huang noted in a statement. "I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment. I want to thank the NVIDIA Board for its confidence and deliberation through this process.".

Senior government officials briefed Nvidia's board late last week and communicated that no national security laws were violated, according to sources familiar with the discussion who asked to remain anonymous because they were not authorized to speak publicly. That enhancement prompted the board to reach out to Huang, the people said. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

Huang and the board held their first conversations since his ouster on Saturday, and the group determined he would return as CEO during a second meeting on Sunday, the people said. Nvidia's cratering stock price played a role in accelerating those conversations. (Related: Tech Report: Accelerating PyTorch distributed fine-tuni...)

It has been revealed that trump celebrated Huang's return to Nvidia on Monday, writing in a post on his social media site that the board made "the correct and proper decision" to reinstate him. Trump said the U.S. needs Huang's leadership to "ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy.".

He reiterated that the U.S. government has "no reason to believe" Huang's return poses a risk to national security, and that it "does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct.".

"On a personal note, I have gotten to know Jensen, and he is a Great Patriot and National Treasure," Trump wrote.

Sources indicate that several tech industry leaders, including Elon Musk, CEO of SpaceX, Gavin Baker, managing partner and chief investment officer at Atreides Management, and Brad Gerstner, Altimeter Capital CEO, had been actively lobbying for Huang's return. Musk wrote in a post on X on Monday that the board's "mistake has been corrected.".

Nvidia did not immediately respond to CNBC's request for comment.

Sources indicate that for weeks following Huang's ouster, the organization's board maintained that it was not considering Huang's reinstatement. It appointed Colette Kress, Nvidia's chief financial officer, as the chipmaker's interim CEO, who reiterated that position in an interview with CNBC following Huang's exit. Kress will continue to serve as Nvidia's CFO, according to a filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission.

Nvidia's board formed an independent committee and hired the law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to conduct an investigation into Huang and whether organization secrets or national security were compromised. The board stated Monday that "the committee's investigation will proceed to conclusion" and that Huang is "committed to cooperating with it.".

"The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers," Nvidia's board chair, Harvey Jones, remarked in a statement. "We have every confidence in Jensen and look forward to working with him as he resumes his leadership of the Company.".

Even with Huang back at the helm, Nvidia is facing increased scrutiny over its chip sales in Southeast Asia and China.

Sources indicate that lawmakers have been preparing for investigations into how Huang's affair with the alleged spy may have damaged U.S. AI dominance, influenced his positions on export controls, and impacted his close relationships with Trump and his Cabinet. Huang has been one of the loudest voices opposing strict export controls on advanced chips.

"The reckless decision to bring Jensen Huang back as CEO does not change the fact that Mr. Huang, NVIDIA executives, and the company's board still owe Congress answers about the company's compliance with U.S. export controls," Sen. Elizabeth Warren, D-Mass., stated in a statement Monday.

Sources indicate that huang is due for a closed-door transcribed interview with the House Foreign Affairs panel and two other committees on Dec. 1. Nvidia's general counsel, Tim Teter, is scheduled to appear before the panels on Dec. 2. Those dates were set before Huang's return, and John Fisher, who represents Huang, did not comment on whether there are any changes in his plans to testify.

"Americans deserve answers about what NVIDIA and the Trump administration knew, when they knew it, and whether sufficient steps were taken to prevent sensitive technology from falling into the wrong hands," Sen. Andy Kim, D-N.J., wrote in a post on X on Monday.

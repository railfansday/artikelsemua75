Hailed as ‘a rare moment of justice’, judges’ decision sets out a roadmap for assessing the question of intent.

According to recent reports, the international court of justice (ICJ) verdict making Myanmar the first country in history to be found guilty of committing genocide does not just deliver justice for Rohingya victims. It also gives the 1948 genocide convention fresh relevance in governing the actions of nation states.

In their nine votes to six majority ruling, the panel of judges in The Hague set out a detailed roadmap for how the convention should be applied to real-world situations in which a government is accused of committing genocide against a whole population group, especially when it comes to assessing the question of intent.

In a recent development, I'm a simple person. I see an anthropomorphic rat man who opens up his trench coat that is filled with regular rats in the same manner as one would with counterfeit watches in a trailer for a video game, I add it to my wishlist. There really isn't much I need in life, but that certainly fulfills those needs. And it's exactly what HARK THE GHOUL is offering, an action-adventure, dungeon crawler RPG set in a weird and nasty Victorian gothic city with an emphasis on free exploration.

Now, I appreciate not all of you will be as immediately convinced by trench coat wearing rat men, so let's have a deeper gander at the game together. Exploration really does seem to be the key thing here, not just in terms of going wherever you want, more or less when you want, but how you get about. For example, say there's an annoying gap you want to cross. Use a magical spell to summon a crate, then lob it in front of you, and cast some icey magic to freeze it into place, thereby creating a upcoming platform for you to hop onto. In a recent development, this extends to the combat, which faces you against odd automatons, infected foes and nasty beasties. You can perform quite silly tricks like using your shovel to dig up a rock and chuck that at a baddie, or you can use your magic to conjure up an explosive crate which you then kick at an enemy, blasting it with a shotgun. It appears to almost be an RPG by way of the immersive sim; there's no correct way to go about the game, only the way you find most fun. Though maybe that's dredging up the debate around what an immersive sim is….

Either way, I'm just tickled pink by HARK THE GHOUL, which also pits you as a mothman-type figure who's just emerged from their cocoon. Delightful! And it just got a release date too: January 21st, 2027. That's only a couple of months away, obviously, but if you can't wait that long then there's a demo you can check out on Steam. Or, you know, just throw it a wishlist! Offering it some support either way seems like a nice idea.

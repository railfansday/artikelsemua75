While casual side hustles can sometimes pay off, the most successful people prioritize their passion to turn it into profit.

Business coach and ex–Wall Streeter Codie Sanchez says all the successful people she’s met have one thing in common: “They’re obsessed.”. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

Industry observers note that “That’s it. That’s the secret,” says Sanchez, who has nearly 2 million TikTok followers and has interviewed nearly a dozen billionaires, including Palantir cofounder Joe Lonsdale. (Related: Industry News: Hyperparameter Search with Transformers ...)

While all of the billionaires she’s interviewed on her podcast BigDeal are “wildly different,” she says in a video on her TikTok account that they’re all obsessed with what they do “almost to the point of it being uncomfortable.”.

One of the biggest debates in the workforce and entrepreneurship is whether work-life balance can really exist and whether it’s effective.

In a recent development, if in fact, as Sanchez claims, the most successful entrepreneurs and businesspeople are “obsessed,” this leaves little room to achieve what one might consider traditional work-life balance. It requires being more than just curious, Sanchez says, and obsession can help you beat someone who is richer and smarter than you.

Sources indicate that “Obsession means that you’re thinking nonstop until your relentless action makes it unreasonable for you to not win,” she claims. “Quantity overwhelms failure. Repetition steers fate.”.

There are countless examples of successful entrepreneurs and investors who were obsessed with their work—so much so that they never took a break during years of hard work.

Billionaire Mark Cuban went seven years without a vacation while starting his first business and worked late nights learning newly introduced software details until he sold it for $6 million. Today he’s worth more than $10 billion.

Industry observers note that amazon founder Jeff Bezos worked 12-hour days, seven days a week, in the early days of his company, staying up until 3 a.m. to fulfill orders. Now he’s the second-richest person in the world with a $367 billion net worth.

And Apple cofounder Steve Jobs was also obsessed with his craft, and was infamous for going into “founder mode.” He was known for “rolling up his sleeves and, true to his word, working more closely with all of us, at every level, to make sure that when we got our next opportunity, we hit it out of the park,” Shalini Govil-Pai wrote in a 2024 op-ed for Fortune. Govil-Pai is vice president and general manager of Android TV at Google and previously worked with Jobs.

And although these entrepreneurs were dedicated to their craft for years, Sanchez notes, it doesn’t necessarily require a lifetime of dedication to be successful.

“What is shocking to me is what can happen with three to five years of absolute dedication,” she claims. “If you make that 10 years, that’s where billionaires start to land.”.

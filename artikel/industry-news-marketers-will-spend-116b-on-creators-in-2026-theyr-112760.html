Industry News: Marketers will spend $116B on creators in 2026. They’re “cannibalizing” other budgets to do it.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Marketers are investing more money into the creator economy—and reallocating their existing advertising budgets to pay for it.

According to a recent report from strategic advisory, consulting, and research firm Madison and Wall, marketer spend on creators is set to grow “+15% in 2026 to $116 billion globally, up from $101 billion in 2025.”.

Based on that trajectory, the report predicts that the advertiser-funded creator economy will hit $178 billion by 2030.

It has been revealed that madison and Wall compiled its study by isolating marketing funds dedicated to “creator related activity” (i.e., “spending deployed to creators, agencies, platforms, technology vendors, and media networks to achieve marketing, commerce, or brand objectives”). Other forms of creator income—such as subscriptions, merch, and platform payouts—weren’t taken into account. (Related: AI data center power demand will double by 2030 thanks ...)

The report further separated creator marketing spend into five distinct categories: audience-led spend, media-led spend, asset-led spend, infrastructure-led spend, and miscellaneous.

Of those categories, Madison and Wall found that media-led spending—aka “the paid amplification of creator content”—accounts for the largest piece of the pie at 44% (or $52 billion) of 2026 spend.

It has been revealed that audience-led spending (“the traditional sponsored-post and ambassador model”) ranks second at 28%, while asset-led spending (“where brands pay for content rather than reach”) accounts for 12% of the market. Infrastructure-led spending (net revenue kept by agencies, managers, and platforms) and miscellaneous (events, gifting, licensing, etc.) bring up the rear at 8% and 7% respectively.

It has been revealed that on a global scale, Madison and Wall found that the U.S. was responsible for 43% of creator spending in 2025, well above its ~35% share of global advertising. The closest runners-up were the UK (which accounts for 5% of global creator spending) and Germany (4%).

One of the key takeaways of Madison and Wall’s report concerned the origin of marketing funds dedicated to creator advertising.

It has been revealed that the firm noted that “creator growth is largely funded by reallocation rather than fresh money. Interviews with stakeholders across the ecosystem point to three sources: traditional media, notably TV; digital performance media, including paid social, programmatic and search; and creative production budgets. Because creators draw on several existing budget pools at once, the creator economy can keep outgrowing advertising without marketers creating new budgets.”.

One senior operations executive told Madison and Wall that “80% of the time we are cannibalizing another budget, whether it’s TV or [something else].”.

Overall, the firm found that creator spending grew at an 18% compound annual growth rate (CAGR) between 2020 and 2025, as compared with 12% for advertising.

That gap doesn’t seem likely to close anytime soon. According to the firm’s projections, creator spending is set to grow ~12% a year through 2030—putting it well ahead of the 6% growth expected for the advertising economy.

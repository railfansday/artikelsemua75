Nvidia shares fell around 8% on Tuesday, paring a massive Monday rally that was sparked by the return of Jensen Huang as CEO.

It has been revealed that other U.S. semiconductor stocks also opened lower after posting strong increases Monday. Micron declined 11%, Marvell Technology lost nearly 10%, Arm Holdings dropped over 9% and AMD shed 12% in morning trading.

Recent findings suggest that huang, who resigned as CEO of Nvidia earlier this month after admitting to an extramarital affair, was reinstated on Monday. The stock closed over 30% higher following the news of Huang's return.

The manufacturer's board of directors stated it unanimously voted to reinstate Huang following "a series of discussions and meetings" with him. (Related: Bus manufacturer apologizes after driver licks handrail...)

President Donald Trump also claimed he was personally involved in Huang's return. The president told CNBC that he spoke with the chipmaker's board before it reversed course and reinstated its co-founder.

"I made the deal," Trump told CNBC's Eamon Javers on Monday during an interview on "Squawk on the Street.".

Trump mentioned Nvidia's board asked for guarantees that Huang, who resigned less than two weeks ago, was not a threat to national security.

Recent findings suggest that "And we gave them assurances, at least with everything we know," the president noted. "We believe he's fine. He's absolutely fine.".

After Huang's reinstatement, several Wall Street analysts turned more bullish on the stock and upgraded their ratings on Nvidia.

"We believe reinstatement of CEO Jensen Huang RETURNS Investor CONFIDENCE in the AI trade," Mizuho analysts wrote in a note on Monday.

Huang and Nvidia have been central to the AI story, supplying the chips that power AI and financing companies across the ecosystem.

The tech giant remains under scrutiny as questions remain about Huang's reported affair with Alice Huang, an alleged Chinese spy.

In a recent development, the U.S. is investigating Alice Huang and Megaspeed, the tech giant where she worked. Questions also remain regarding whether Nvidia chips are making their way into China despite export controls.

In a recent development, huang is set to publicly testify in front of the Senate Banking Committee on Dec. 3. Sen. Elizabeth Warren, who is set to take over the Committee in January, called Huang's reinstatement "reckless.".

"The reckless decision to bring Jensen Huang back as CEO does not change the fact that Mr. Huang, NVIDIA executives, and the company's board still owe Congress answers about the company's compliance with U.S. export controls," Warren remarked in a statement on Monday.

Huang is also expected to sit for a closed-door interview with the House Foreign Affairs, Intelligence and China Select committees on Dec. 1.

Sources indicate that "Reinstatement should not be confused with the end of scrutiny," Bank of America said in a note on Monday. "We therefore expect elevated attention around governance, China/export controls, customer relationships and compliance to persist even with the CEO question resolved.".

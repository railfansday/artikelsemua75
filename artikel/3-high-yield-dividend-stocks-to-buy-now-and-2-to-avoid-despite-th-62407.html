High-yield dividend stocks often result from a falling share price. In many cases, this is a red flag. When investors bail on a dividend stock, it's typically due to falling confidence in future results. Deteriorating financial performance can, in turn, lead to dividend cuts or, worse, suspensions.

Recent findings suggest that that said, there are a few diamonds in the rough, like Enterprise Products Partners (EPD -0.91%), VICI Properties (VICI +0.04%)and Verizon Communications (VZ +1.62%). Conversely, there are dividend stocks with such severe "yield trap" status that they are instant candidates for the "avoid" pile, like Fidelity National Information Services (FIS -0.42%) and Icahn Enterprises (IEP -7.31%).

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

It has been revealed that enterprise Products Partners is a master limited partnership (MLP) that owns and operates midstream energy assets, such as oil and gas pipelines. Units in this MLP have a forward dividend yield of around 6.15%. Enterprise Products Partners also has a nearly 30-year track record of consecutive dividend growth.

In a recent development, over the last decade, the MLP's quarterly payouts, known as distributions, have increased by an average of 3.6% annually. This trend appears sustainable, given the percentage of cash flow devoted not only to distributions but also to stock buybacks. Per the MLP's latest quarterly earnings release, it used just 54% of its cash flow from operations (CFFO) for return-of-capital purposes, with the remainder reinvested in the business. For comparison, most major MLPs and pipeline-owning C-corps have payout ratios of 60% and above. (Related: The one thing Putin, Trump and Xi agreed on — fewer lim...)

Speaking of growth, management has guided for $2.9 billion to $3.4 billion in growth-related capital spending. While not providing concrete guidance for future growth, Enterprise Products Partners has grown discretionary cash flow (DCF) from around $0.90 to $4.07 per share, or around 7.9% annualized. I wouldn't necessarily expect Enterprise to keep growing at such a clip, but even at a lesser extent, steady cash flow growth could lead to solid long-term total returns. NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

VICI Properties is a real estate investment trust (REIT) specializing in casino real estate; it has since expanded to own such properties from other major gaming companies, including MGM Resorts and Golden Entertainment.

According to recent reports, this gives REIT ownership of much of the Las Vegas Strip, as well as real estate holdings across numerous regional casino markets in the United States and overseas. VICI Properties right now has a forward dividend yield of around 7.5%. The REIT has increased its distributions for six consecutive years, with payout growth over the last five years averaging nearly 10%. Having noted this, the increase in distributions has diminished more recently. The latest dividend increase, for instance, came in at just 2.2%.

Talk of rising interest rates has put pressure on VICI Properties, as it has on REIT stocks across the board. The pending leveraged buyout of Caesars by Fertitta Entertainment also creates some uncertainty over future rental income. Nevertheless, as the REIT continues to report solid results, including an increase in adjusted funds from operations (AFFO) per share, the REIT's equivalent of earnings per share (EPS), it remains a strong long-term choice for capital growth and a high dividend yield.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

Sources indicate that verizon Communications is a legacy telco, built primarily from the regional Bell operating companies spun off from the old AT&T in the 1980s. Verizon's background may be what gives it its blue chip dividend stock status, but in more recent years, its legacy has been more of a liability than an asset.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

In a recent development, fidelity National Information Services is a leading banking and payment technology company. With a 6.6% forward dividend yield and its low valuation of just 5 times forward earnings, this fintech may at first seem like a bargain buy, but take a closer look at the reasons behind its high yield and low valuation.

So far this year, shares have fallen by over 60%, with the stock tumbling 24% alone following its Q3 2026 earnings release earlier this month. Chalk this up to continued guidance downgrades, largely due to banks delaying their move to the tech giant's subscription-based software products. The company also has a large debt load from past acquisitions and has decided to pause buybacks to focus on deleveraging.

The manufacturer's latest efforts have yet to lead to a dividend cut, and its payout ratio remains relatively low at around 25.7%. However, with earnings expected to drop next year and Fidelity National's history of dividend cuts, this isn't off the table. Worse yet, even if the dividend stays as is, a further deterioration in operating performance could cause the resulting stock price drop to outweigh the benefit of the company's higher-than-average yield. Until results and guidance appear more promising, consider this a "stay away" situation among dividend stocks.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

Icahn Enterprises is an MLP controlled by billionaire financier Carl Icahn. However, it is not like your typical MLP that owns pipelines or other natural resource assets. Instead, it operates like a holding tech giant, with its main assets including a majority stake in refiner CVR Energy. Icahn also has holdings in other operating companies, real estate, and $1.4 billion invested in funds managed by the famed investor.

Icahn Enterprises has long had one of the highest forward dividend yields among U.S.-listed stocks. However, its nearly 71% forward yield is very misleading. For one, Carl Icahn, who owns 87.7% of the outstanding units, receives his quarterly distributions in newly issued units, not cash. The MLP has also relied on the sale of fresh units to fund cash dividends. In other words, outside investors are trading a high yield for high shareholder dilution.

It has been revealed that this dynamic, coupled with its recurring high investment losses and recent credit downgrade, helps to explain why this stock has lost nearly 95% of its value over the last five years. Although shares may be reaching the point where they trade below breakup value, with debt and investment loss concerns and concerns about a dividend cut persisting, a further drop appears likely.

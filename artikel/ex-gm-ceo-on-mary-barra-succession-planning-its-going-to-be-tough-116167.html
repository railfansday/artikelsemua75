Former General Motors CEO and Chairman Dan Akerson doesn't mince words when asked about the challenges the automaker faces in finding a successor for GM CEO Mary Barra: "It's going to be tough.".

He should know. The 78-year-old businessman and U.S. Navy veteran helped with his own succession planning and recommended to GM's board in 2013 that Barra replace him as CEO. She has held the top job since Jan. 15, 2014.

Akerson remarked Barra will be hard to replace, citing her success and prominence as the first female CEO of any major automaker and the ongoing challenges facing the automotive industry, such as artificial intelligence, vehicle digitalization, and the rise of China and autonomous vehicles. In a recent development, "It's going to be very, very dynamic," Akerson told CNBC during a phone interview. "It's going to be a big change.".

Akerson declined to discuss potential internal candidates such as GM Chief Product Officer Sterling Anderson, CFO Paul Jacobson or President Mark Reuss, but he remarked the eventual successor needs to be dynamic and, if from the outside, "adaptable.".

Industry observers note that his comments to CNBC follow a Wall Street Journal report on Tuesday that GM is working with an executive search firm to identify possible outside candidates to succeed Barra, in addition to internal prospects. GM declined to comment on the report or Barra's succession. (Related: Industry News: How we sped up transformer inference 100...)

Akerson, who came to GM as an outsider from The Carlyle Group in 2010, said selecting an outside candidate might be risky if there are already executives within the organization who have similar leadership and technical expertise.

It has been revealed that "Is there anybody in the organization that has that background that you know better day to day over a period of time? You may be taking a risk by bringing in an outsider," he stated.

The prospect of an outsider leading GM would be unusual in the auto industry, where major automakers have generally promoted internal CEO candidates, apart from periods of exceptional disruption such as the Great Recession.

Stellantis, BMW, Toyota Motor, Nissan Motor and Hyundai Motor have all appointed internal executives to their CEO positions in recent years.

Barra has noted several times that her successor and her longevity in the role would be determined by the board.

A notable influence is likely Hewlett Packard veteran Patricia Russo, GM's longest-serving board member who has been on the board since 2009 and leads its CEO succession planning as independent lead director.

According to recent reports, GM's CEO succession planning is done "at least annually," according to the tech giant's yearly proxy filing. That process includes Barra providing the board "with recommendations for, and evaluations of, potential CEO successors and discusses developmental plans for these successors," according to the filing.

That is followed by board members engaging "with CEO candidates and senior management talent at Board and committee meetings and other forums to enable directors to personally assess candidates.".

When Akerson was preparing to retire, Barra was among a pool of four candidates — two longtime GM employees and two relative newcomers — who were finalists for CEO in 2013, he mentioned.

Akerson noted he put the finalists through an extended evaluation, asking how they viewed GM's strengths and weaknesses, how they would organize the firm, and their willingness to work together, potentially with other CEO candidates who didn't get the job.

Industry observers note that akerson said the final decision to appoint Barra was done at a day-and-a-half-long board meeting in Washington. The candidate field was narrowed to Barra and former GM executive Dan Ammann, an ex-Wall Street banker who was GM's CFO before leaving the automaker's Cruise unit, reportedly over a disagreement in strategy with Barra, in 2021.

"Mary's very deserving of all the accolades she's received," Akerson mentioned. "She's done a terrific job.".

Barra has been leading the automaker for nearly 13 years, which makes her the longest-serving CEO of the automaker since Alfred Sloan retired after a 23-year career as CEO in 1946.

Akerson remarked he believes that, like he did, Barra will have major influence in picking her successor. He described that dynamic as integral to continuing her legacy leading the automaker.

According to recent reports, "This is vital. I think if she doesn't pick the right person, in four or five years from now and things are back in the trough, that will tarnish her history. But I think she'll pick the right one," he said.

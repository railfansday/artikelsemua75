Earlier this year, things seemed to be going exactly right for fuel-cell manufacturer Bloom Energy (BE -15.30%). (Related: Latest Update: LA Noire 2 leak resurfaces after source ...)

Sources indicate that it's not hard to see why. The race to build AI data centers had stimulated such an enormous appetite for electricity that developers, facing grid bottlenecks and tight construction timelines, simply couldn't afford to wait years for traditional power grid connections. (Related: Build A Racetrack codes (November 2026)...)

In that context, latest orders for Bloom's fuel-cell systems were coming in hot: First Oracle (ORCL -8.48%) contracted for 1.2 gigawatts (GW) of capacity, then Brookfield Asset Management (BAM -2.53%) expanded its financing framework with Bloom for AI projects from $5 billion to a whopping $25 billion.

Both were significant developments for Bloom's business, and Wall Street absolutely agreed: The stock soared to an all-time high of roughly $350, more than quadrupling in value from where it started the year.

If you take a look at Bloom's stock chart today, however, you'll see a different picture: It has fallen from its high to roughly $168 per share.

In a recent development, that raises the obvious question: What the heck is happening? And, more importantly, should you take this as an occasion to buy Bloom stock?

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

According to recent reports, for starters, Bloom is a fuel cell manufacturer. In simple terms, it makes equipment that let data centers and other customers generate electricity on-site rather than waiting years to connect to the grid. These fuel cells are also a source of (relatively) clean power: Instead of burning fuel, they use an electrochemical reaction to convert fuel (such as natural gas) into electricity. It's a pretty neat concept, and it is especially useful for those who need round-the-clock power.

Recent findings suggest that as mentioned, Bloom stock soared earlier this year as sales of Bloom boxes went through the roof. This hasn't changed. Indeed, third-quarter sales surged 138% year over year to $1.24 billion, prompting management (yet again!) to raise full-year revenue guidance to between $4.4 billion and $4.6 billion. For perspective, its full-year 2025 revenue was about $2 billion.

According to recent reports, what has changed, however, is the environment, or more specifically: Wall Street's willingness to pay sky-high valuations for companies associated with the AI boom. Investors have become increasingly concerned about the billions being poured into AI infrastructure, and Bloom, whose future growth is tied to AI spending, has found itself caught in this broader wave of bearish sentiment.

It has been revealed that the irony, of course, is that the very numbers that once sent Bloom's stock soaring are no longer enough to reassure investors. But why is that?

If I could capture that shift in perspective in an image, it would be this: Bloom's breathtaking growth this year is like a star's light. It's brilliant to look at, but it tells us more about the past than it does the future. It tells us just how bullish data center developers were on AI infrastructure earlier this year, but it doesn't necessarily indicate how Bloom will fare if that spending slows or stalls.

In other words, Bloom's extraordinary financial results could be a lagging indicator of an AI spending frenzy that is fading, while its stock price reflects anxiety about what will come next.

So, given that Wall Street is concerned about Bloom's future sales, should you reverse course and buy?

Personally, I think this is a great opportunity to pick up Bloom stock. The reaction to concerns over data center construction strikes me as a bit overdone. Even if AI spending slows, the underlying problem Bloom is solving -- an electricity grid struggling to keep up with rising demands -- isn't going away anytime soon. The stock might be volatile today, but for those willing to hold through the volatility, I think today's sell-off offers an attractive opportunity to buy a company with long-term growth potential.

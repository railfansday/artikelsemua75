UPDATED 20:50 EST / NOVEMBER 20 2026.

CrowdStrike Holdings Inc. is reportedly in talks to buy Zscaler Inc., a publicly traded provider of cybersecurity software.

Sources told Bloomberg today that the acquisition could “come together soon.” However, they cautioned that it’s still possible the talks will fall through.

Investors seem to be confident that the deal will materialize. Zscaler’s share price jumped more than 16% in after-hours trading, while CrowdStrike dropped about 4.8%. (Related: How to Start a Side Business While Working Full-Time...)

Sources indicate that software buyouts tend to value the acquired company at a premium to its last unaffected market capitalization. As a result, CrowdStrike is likely to pay more than the $32 billion Zscaler was worth at the end of today’s trading session. That means the deal could take CrowdStrike’s market capitalization above $250 billion. (Related: What Is Considered Upper Middle Class? Income and Net W...)

Recent findings suggest that crowdStrike sells one of the world’s most popular cybersecurity platforms. Falcon can detect and block malicious activity across cloud instances, on-premises servers, employee devices and other assets. It also automates certain related tasks, such as finding sensitive records that are stored in an insecure manner.

Zscaler competes in a different part of the cybersecurity market. It sells software that protects the network connections through which a company’s employees access its business applications.

Industry observers note that enterprises historically used virtual private network, or VPN, tools for that task. Such programs tend to be quite slow. More significantly, they give employees access to the entire corporate network rather than to the specific applications they need for their work. That broad access can create cybersecurity risks.

Sources indicate that zscaler sells a zero-trust network access, or ZTNA, platform designed to replace legacy VPN software. It limits the number of applications that workers can access within the corporate network. If Zscaler detects risky activity, it asks the affected user to complete additional authentication steps.

In a recent development, the tech giant’s ZTNA software doubles as an observability tool. Administrators can monitor employees’ connections for technical challenges that may hinder productivity. A built-in artificial intelligence assistant, the ZDX Agent, generates troubleshooting advice.

According to recent reports, zscaler also competes in other product categories where CrowdStrike has a limited presence. Notably, it offers a tool that deploys bogus applications, user passwords and files in customer networks. Those simulated assets act as sensors. When an attempt is made to access one of them, Zscaler Deception notifies the customer that a cyberattack is underway.

The potential deal would mark CrowdStrike’s largest acquisition by a wide margin. The company’s biggest buyout to date is its $970 million purchase of startup SGNL Inc., which closed earlier this year. The deal bought CrowdStrike a platform that can automatically end users’ application sessions when it detects cybersecurity issues.

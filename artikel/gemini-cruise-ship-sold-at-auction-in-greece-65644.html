The Gemini, a 960-passenger ship formerly operated by Miray Cruises, was reportedly sold in an auction held by Greek authorities on November 18, 2026.

According to financial and business news portal New Money, the ship, which was auctioned to settle debts of its former operator and owner, was acquired by an undisclosed buyer.

The 3,806,562.75 euro paid is slightly higher than the auction’s opening bid, which was set at Gemini’s project scrap value of 3,805,462 euros. (Related: Google Home speakers get a major Gemini upgrade ‘soon,’...)

New Money mentioned that four companies expressed interest in acquiring the 19,093-ton vessel that was originally built in 1992 for Crown Cruise Line.

As previously reported by Cruise Industry News, the auction was originally scheduled to take place in late October but was later postponed due to an objection filed by DenizBank with the Single-Member Court of First Instance of Piraeus.

The Turkish bank opposed the initial auction, claiming that the vessel is worth more than its scrap value thanks to 2025 repairs and asking the authorities to increase the opening bid price to 14 million euros. (Related: Breaking: A child support fight exposed a Trump adviser...)

Greek authorities seized the ship in Karystos earlier this year over a monetary claim of approximately 22,772 euros, followed by its towing to the Port of Elefsis, near Piraeus, in late June, where it remains docked.

The Gemini was last in service in 2025, offering a series of short cruises to Turkey and the Greek Islands.

It was scheduled to operate a cruise program in the Eastern Mediterranean and the Black Sea this summer, but the deployment was cancelled due to Miray’s financial issues.

It has been revealed that it's been a long time since the heyday of the Nintendo 64, but its cultural influence hasn't yet waned. If there was any doubt about the subject, the recent remake of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time should quell it, as the newly introduced version of the game was an immediate success in both critical reception and sales.

While Ocarina of Time leaves behind the limits of the original hardware, other developers are still interested in making games within those confines. One such project is Vasalius: Human Error, and this ambitious new project is set to receive a proper physical release for Nintendo 64 hardware. (Related: Smart People Can Still Be Bad At Maths — These 3 Skills...)

Recent findings suggest that vasalius: Human Error is at the moment in the middle of a Kickstarter fundraising campaign, which brands the game as an "Action RPG / FPS for the N64." The FPS element is obvious at a glance, as an action-packed trailer reveals gameplay in the trademark style of the DOOM engine and some shotgun-pumping action. It also features undeniably identifiable voice work from Jon St. John, the original voice of FPS icon Duke Nukem. (Related: Latest Update: Does Fluoride In Drinking Water Affect I...)

On top of that, Vasalius features a robust array of RPG mechanics. In between all the blasting, there's also exploration and storytelling, including "dialogue skill checks" and "several endings shaped by your choices across the whole game." It's a lot to pack into an N64 cartridge, but thanks to its sprite-based style, it's apparently not a problem (and requires no Nintendo 64 Expansion Pak).

As of the time of writing, the physical release of Vasalius: Human Error seems set to go, as the Kickstarter campaign has almost doubled its original goal of $6,400. That was all achieved in the matter of just a few days, with 27 more days set to go before the campaign closes. A series of stretch goals will add various perks if the campaign keeps up its momentum, ranging from more lines by Jon St. John to Switch, Xbox, and PlayStation ports.

Recent findings suggest that the campaign's pricing scheme is probably assisting in its success, as developer John Roo has laid things out in an array of highly reasonable options. Those who simply want to play the game can get a digital ROM for $12, while investing $25 in the campaign will get you a cartridge without a box. Jumping to a complete-in-box version ups the price to $60 — and the deluxe editions of the game take things higher — but the options on hand make it more accessible than a lot of retro-style releases.

Recent findings suggest that every Kickstarter campaign comes with some risks, but Roo promises that the game is already 95% finished and estimates delivery of goods in April 2027 (if you've backed enough Kickstarter campaigns, though, you'll probably want to assume that a delay is in store). Even if Vasalius: Human Error doesn't strike your interest, it's always nice to see passion projects continue to be made for older systems, and this one looks like a unique experience for the Nintendo 64.

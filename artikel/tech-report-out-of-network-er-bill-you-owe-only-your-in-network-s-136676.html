📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that if your job-based or marketplace health strategy covers emergency care, the No Surprises Act bars an ER and its doctors from billing you more than your in-network copay, coinsurance, and deductible, even when they’re out of network. Compare any ER bill with your insurer’s explanation of benefits, pay what it states you owe, and dispute the rest.

Say you have an emergency appendectomy at an in-network hospital, the surgeon is out of network, and your plan’s in-network coinsurance for the surgery comes to $250. If the surgeon then bills you $3,200, you still owe only the $250.

Sources indicate that the law leaves out ground ambulances, which can still charge out-of-network rates unless your state forbids it. You can give up the protection by signing a notice-and-consent form, a waiver that lets the provider bill you more than your in-network share. No provider may ask you to sign one for emergency care before you’re stable, and anesthesiologists, radiologists, pathologists, and assistant surgeons may never ask. To spot a violation, compare the bill with your explanation of benefits, the statement your insurer sends after it processes a claim. The amount it states you owe should match the bill.

In a recent development, call the provider’s billing office about any charge above that amount, cite the No Surprises Act, and ask it to cut the bill or refund what you overpaid. If it won’t, file a complaint with the No Surprises Help Desk online or at 1-800-985-3059, seven days a week, and the Help Desk can refer it to a state regulator.

While the complaint is open, pay the amount your explanation of benefits claims you owe, and nothing above it.

It has been revealed that at first glance, tuna salad may appear to be a fairly set-in-stone formula. You mix the tinned fish with mayo, add in some aromatics, and enjoy the dish on bread or standalone. Yet start trialing creative add-ins, and tuna salad welcomes a surprisingly wide array of ingredients. There are so many ways to take plain tuna salad to the next level. A sharp cheddar cheese is one unexpected flavor boost.

After all, creaminess is a central part of the dish's appeal. And as a dairy product, cheddar cheese offers such a smooth mouthfeel in spades. However, unlike a simple mayo dressing, the cheese comes with a punch. Aging develops complex flavors, which can vary from barely perceptible tartness to rich nuttiness to a bold bite. Usually, the older the cheddar, the more amplified the flavor — although as the cheese develops, it loses some creaminess to become crumbly.

In a recent development, for a top-tier cheddar tuna salad, go with a balanced option, aged for around six to nine months. Usually classified specifically as sharp, such a cheese is right when the acidic flavors turn into a bold bite — perfect for a tuna salad. So cube up the cheese, using two ounces per can of tuna, then stir everything up with some salt, onions, celery, and your other go-to tuna salad fold-ins. You'll get a dish that's still comforting, but also comes with revamped flair. (Related: Taco Bell adds ‘Make It Flamin’ Hot’ option for scorchi...)

As America's favorite cheese, cheddar certainly belongs in a dish like tuna salad. While it's not common to mix cheese directly into the salad, the inclusion is reminiscent of a beloved tuna salad application: a tuna melt. Originating by mistake, the tuna melt proves that fish and cheese do belong on the same plate, especially if tuna's involved. (Related: Industry News: “Determined” woman completes 7 marathons...)

Industry observers note that further embrace the two dishes by heating the cheddar cheese tuna salad. The cheese will turn molten inside — not only on top — of the fish, making for an extra-satisfying filling. Pair that with a nice bread, say a classic American white loaf, a deli-inspired rye, or a dependable sourdough, and you've got a terrific sandwich on your hands.

Not to say there's not more magic to explore with a cold cheddar tuna salad, too. The cheesy rendition is a befitting tuna pasta salad twist. Or balance out cheddar's richness by rolling the salad into lettuce wraps, a delicious appetizer option. Cheddar gives tuna salad that extra oomph, turning the basic dish into a centerpiece meal.

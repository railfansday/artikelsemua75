Of that group, 72% remarked they'd used personal AI to fix a problem without contacting the organization at all. And 84% said they'd had to repeat to a real person something they had already told a company's chatbot. Keep the sample in mind: these are people who already use AI this way, not the general public.

Sources indicate that almost every number your floor runs on assumes a human on the other end. Average handle time, satisfaction scores, quality ratings and containment all quietly depend on a caller who gets tired, gets annoyed, or gives up.

Industry observers note that A bot does none of that. It'll wait on hold for an hour and call back the next morning. If your save offers and IVR menus were designed partly to wear people down, an assistant built to hold out will walk straight past them, and those contacts land back in the queue.

Industry observers note that the fix starts with counting. If AI calls get mixed into human ones, your staffing model and your agent scores both drift and nobody can explain why.

The most sensible approach we've seen comes from operators already handling these calls: sort requests by what they can change, then decide what a bot can do in each tier. A bot booking a table is not the same risk as a bot moving money, even if it's the same bot.

It has been revealed that low risk. Order status, opening hours, booking or moving an appointment. Nothing leaves the account. Serve these, and don't make your agents feel they've done something wrong by helping a bot.

According to recent reports, medium risk. Refunds, strategy changes, a fresh delivery address, a dispute. Take the request, but confirm it with the account holder on the phone number or email already on file before you apply it. The bot's word isn't the customer's word.

High risk. Closing an account, moving money, password resets, SIM swaps. Only the account holder does these, full stop. Scammers have exactly the same tools as your customers, and they can place thousands of calls a day.

It has been revealed that honestly, the medium tier is where you'll spend most of your design time. It's also where a polite, clear script saves the most money, because the assistant will report back to its owner exactly what you told it.

According to recent reports, add an "AI caller" disposition. Make it a normal wrap-up code agents can pick when the caller indicates it's an assistant, or obviously is. You'll get a count in your reports within a week, which is more than most teams have today.

Industry observers note that keep those calls out of agent scoring. Tell QA to exclude or flag them. Nobody should lose points for not charming software.

Industry observers note that put the tier rules in the agent script. One short paragraph per tier, including the exact sentence agents say when a request needs the account holder.

Make the customer's history follow them. When the person calls back after their bot did, the agent should see what was asked and what was promised. Liveops found 69% of its respondents rate that as extremely or very important.

It has been revealed that decide where your own AI fits. Low-risk bot calls are a good fit for a voice agent on your side. Let software talk to software for order status, and keep your people for the calls that need judgment.

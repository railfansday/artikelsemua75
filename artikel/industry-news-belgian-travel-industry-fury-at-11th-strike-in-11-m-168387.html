It has been revealed that how many strikes are too many? With almost all of the country's flights grounded on Monday, many of Belgium’s travel leaders have had enough.

Belgium’s airline and travel sector is demanding stronger safeguards against strike-related disruption after air traffic controllers helped bring the country’s airports to a standstill on Monday.

The Board of Airline Representatives in Belgium (BAR Belgium) described it as the 11th strike affecting Belgian airspace in 11 months and accused public-sector workers of repeatedly undermining the country’s international connectivity.

Air navigation provider Skeyes mentioned it could not safely provide regular air traffic control from 7 a.m. to 9 p.m. local time after some of its staff joined a nationwide demonstration against government austerity measures. Only emergency and other safety-critical flights would be supported, it said.

Brussels Airport stated all arrivals and departures scheduled within that 14-hour window would have to be canceled, while Charleroi Airport said disruption would affect its entire Monday schedule.

In a recent development, grand Theft Auto fans across the world are still eagerly anticipating Rockstar’s long-awaited sequel to roll out in their regions. But according to data from Streams Charts, GTA VI has already smashed multiple streaming platform records—including becoming “the most popular gaming category in Twitch history by peak viewers.”.

As of publication time, GTA VI has attracted a peak of 3.29 million concurrent viewers on Twitch, 3.19M on YouTube, and 1.5M on Kick.Those totals put it “ahead of every previous game launch tracked by Streams Charts,” the data manufacturer stated in its report. Sources indicate that twitch isn’t taking all that buzz—or the dollar signs that come with it—for granted. But GTA also doesn’t exactly fit into the platform’s careful approach to brand safety. So, it’s introducing new tech to ensure that advertisers can cash in on the hype surrounding GTA VI without putting their brand reputations at risk. Back in September, Twitch CEO Dan Clancy told hosts of The Snarketing Podcast that the platform had begun using AI to evaluate whether streamers are squeaky-clean enough for brands to run ads against their content.

“[W]e transcribe streams to figure out ‘Is this appropriate for this brand?’” he remarked. “So that’s something that we’ve had to invest a lot in to make sure that for a brand, their content is appearing next to creators and content that fits what they’re trying to do.”.

That update didn’t come out of nowhere: Twitch has long asked streamers to label their content in certain circumstances where mature themes or imagery are involved.

As Gizmodo noted, this means creators have historically missed out on ad revenue when streaming mature-rated games, “because while streamers can receive donations from viewers, substantial-time monetization ultimately depends on advertising, and advertisers are generally skittish around things that might frighten the poor sensitive product-buying public.”.

Last week, however, Clancy told Bloomberg‘s Romaine Bostick that “one of the challenges in this space is advertisers want to be part of the GTA 6 launch”—they just don’t want their ads to appear alongside the spicier parts of the game.

To solve that problem, Twitch’s newly introduced technology will allow brands to run ads on GTA VI streams while also ensuring that (as Clancy phrased it) “any of the parts of GTA 6 that they might not want to have their ads associated with, their ads won’t be associated with that.”.

The ins and outs of how that system will actually work remain to be seen. But if it does prove effective for GTA VI, we’ll be watching to see whether Twitch expands the tech to other streams and genres previously dismissed as unsafe for brands.

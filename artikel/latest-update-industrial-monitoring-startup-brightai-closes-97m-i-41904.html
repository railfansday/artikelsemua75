Many industrial machinery malfunctions affect internal components that aren’t visible to an outside observer. As a result, challenges often go unnoticed until they cause a major disruption. Palo Alto, California-based BrightAI develops hardware and software tools that help customers spot issues earlier.

Recent findings suggest that the first component of the manufacturer’s product portfolio is a collection of sensors called State Stickers. They’re small, rectangular devices with a ruggedized case and a long battery life. Technicians can attach them to industrial equipment to monitor for issues that can’t be detected visually.

According to recent reports, the motion of a machine’s internal components produces noise and  vibrations that tend to follow a specific pattern. If an industrial fan’s blades rotate 30 times per minute, it might give off a whooshing sound every two seconds. BrightAI’s State Stickers can detect when a machine diverges from its usual operating pattern.

Sources indicate that the sensors wirelessly transmit their measurements to a device called the Stateful AI Hub. It runs artificial intelligence models that analyze the data for signs of hardware failures. According to the company, its models become more accurate as the number of sensors deployed at a site grows.

Sources indicate that brightAI offers software that enables companies to augment the data from its sensors with drone footage. A utility, for  example, could use a BrightAI-powered drone to detect erosion at the top of an electric pole. The tech giant’s AI models automatically draw a square around damaged components to spare technicians the hassle of manually analyzing footage.

According to recent reports, the company states that its software can also collect maintenance data in other ways. A BrightAI-powered drone can read the meters that are attached to assets such as data center coolant tanks to check their internal pressure. Furthermore, built-in support for infrared cameras enables the software to spot overheating equipment.

According to recent reports, the third core component of BrightAI’s product portfolio is an AI wearable. The Stateful Wearable, as it’s called, provides technicians with troubleshooting advice in the field. It also checks whether they follow safety guidelines during inspections and automatically turns maintenance data into reports.

According to recent reports, “Our infrastructure is under more strain than it has ever been, and Physical AI has finally become reliable enough to work in the field,” said founder and Chief Executive Alex Hawkinson. “Bringing those two facts together is a once-in-a-generation opportunity.”.

BrightAI says that its products monitor hundreds of thousands of industrial assets across 50,000 sites. Investor GE Vernova, which is also a customer, plans to expand its use of the tech giant’s technology in connection with this week’s funding round. BrightAI, for its part, will use the capital to grow its international presence.

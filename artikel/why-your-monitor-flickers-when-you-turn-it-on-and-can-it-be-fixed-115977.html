Why your monitor flickers when you turn it on and can it be fixed?

Your screen might be telling you something.

Sources indicate that if you're someone who spends a decent amount of time in front of a computer, perhaps at work or during long gaming sessions, you may have noticed just how finicky certain parts of your computer can be. That's especially true of its peripherals and accessories, with one of the usual suspects being your monitor.

Turning on your monitor and noticing some unusual oddities, specifically flickering, may seem like an apocalyptic beginning to your day. Monitors of decent quality are pretty affordable today, but no one wants to spend money on a fresh display if they don't have to. Thankfully, your computer monitor is fairly easy to troubleshoot on a basic level in order to determine your ideal path forward.

Industry observers note that if it's something more difficult to solve — something deeper at a hardware level — that can become a bit harder to resolve on your own. Taking some simple steps to determine the cause and potential fixes can save you a lot of headaches in the long run. (Related: Industry News: Hyperparameter Search with Transformers ...)

Industry observers note that A monitor starting to flicker could stem from a rather wide variety of different complications. One of the most common reasons for a flickering monitor is a loose or potentially faulty display cable, whether that's a modern HDMI or DisplayPort cable, or even an older VGA one. Reinserting the cables or checking them for damage or fraying can be a quick fix. (Related: How to generate text: using different decoding methods ...)

Recent findings suggest that if you are suspicious that the cable is defective, you could also try swapping the cable out for a latest one. If it's not the cable, it may potentially be a bad port on the monitor or the PC itself, which would necessitate trying a different port on the monitor or computer if they have more than one. If you're uncertain which one may potentially be causing the problem, you could also try hooking the monitor up to a different computer that you know with absolute certainty works. If it works without issue, you know who the culprit is.

Barring a connection issue of some kind, a flickering monitor can also be caused by something at a software level. The first things to look into are your graphics drivers. Out-of-date graphics drivers are one of the most common causes of display issues, and a simple enhancement to them may be just what you need. On the other hand, a graphics driver update does have the potential to actually cause display problems, so rolling back to a previous version could also work.

It has been revealed that you may also be running into an issue with the monitor's refresh rate. For example, if your monitor runs at 120Hz but your Windows 11 PC's refresh rate is set to something entirely different, it can cause some havoc. In order to fix this, all you need to do is go to Settings, then find System on the left panel, then look for Display. Look for Advanced display, then change the refresh rate to match your monitor.

Sources indicate that if you find that your flickering monitor can't be fixed by any normal means, it may be time to look at the issues a little deeper. A consistent, almost shuttering flicker is a surefire sign that the monitor is about to crash and burn. That's especially true when you're unable to solve the issue at a hardware or software level.

Recent findings suggest that the monitor may also experience blackouts, as in sudden hard cuts to black, before returning to normal operation. Your display is quite keen about letting you know when something has gone haywire, and you can do a lot of diagnosing by just examining how your display is behaving.

Faded, dim colors or vertical lines that are swimming through the screen are also a couple of telltale signs. If you think of your monitor like you would a TV and utilize similar modalities, you'll be able to diagnose it in a similar way. If you've already exhausted all your other options but still find that your display looks unhealthy, then it most likely is.

At this point, your options really narrow themselves down to two. You can either look for someone to repair the monitor or seek out a replacement. Whether you should replace or repair the monitor ultimately depends on your budget, but the price of newly introduced OLED and 4K screens is starting to tumble. In most cases, it may be better to sell it for parts or donate it and just buy something new.

Recent findings suggest that gold prices could reach $4,650 by the end of 2027, according to Goldman Sachs. The bank expects rising interest rates to create short-term pressure on gold, while continued central bank purchases could support prices over the longer term.

Sources indicate that goldman Sachs expects the Federal Reserve to raise interest rates again in December, followed by another hike in March 2027. Higher rates could strengthen the US dollar and make bonds and savings accounts more attractive than gold, which does not generate interest income. Central banks are buying approximately 60 tonnes of gold per month, compared with an average of 17 tonnes before 2022.

Purchases increased after Russia’s foreign exchange reserves were frozen in 2022, prompting central banks to reconsider their dependence on foreign-held financial assets.

It has been revealed that france and the Netherlands have also been moving gold reserves from foreign vaults to domestic storage. These developments indicate continued focus on gold as a reserve asset.

This sustained demand could support gold prices even if higher interest rates cause short-term declines.

Higher interest rates generally put pressure on gold by strengthening the US dollar and increasing the returns available from bonds and cash.

Goldman Sachs also expects institutional demand for gold-related options to decline towards historical averages as interest rates rise.

According to recent reports, the 2022 market cycle offers a comparison. Gold initially fell approximately 20% during the Federal Reserve’s aggressive rate hikes, while central banks continued accumulating the metal. Gold subsequently recovered over the following three years.

Goldman Sachs expects gold to outperform silver in 2027.

In a recent development, central banks purchase gold for their reserves but do not have similar demand for silver. Silver prices depend more heavily on investment demand and industrial consumption.

Higher interest rates could slow economic activity and weaken industrial demand, making silver more volatile than gold.

Goldman Sachs forecasts gold at $5,600 by the end of 2028. The bank expects the Federal Reserve to begin cutting interest rates in November 2027. Lower rates could reduce the opportunity cost of holding gold and support investment demand.

For 2027, Federal Reserve policy and central bank purchases remain the key factors in Goldman’s gold price outlook.

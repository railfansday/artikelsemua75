Garmin and Waze are teaming up on an interesting fresh safety feature that could give cyclists a little extra protection when sharing the road with cars – by making cyclists themselves show up in Waze.

The newly introduced feature allows cyclists using a compatible Garmin Edge bike computer or Garmin smartwatch to share their location through Garmin’s LiveTrack feature. I’ve long maintained that safety – or more accurately, the perception that cycling isn’t safe – is one of the biggest impediments to getting more people onto bikes.

That’s especially true in the US, where cyclists are often forced to share roads with cars instead of enjoying the kind of widespread separated cycling infrastructure found in many more bike-friendly countries. And when a cyclist and a multi-ton vehicle have to occupy the same space, making sure the driver knows that cyclist is there can make a major difference.

Recent findings suggest that of course, an icon on Waze isn’t going to magically make cycling safe. It won’t replace protected bike lanes, better road design, slower vehicle speeds, or attentive drivers.

It has been revealed that but I can certainly imagine situations where it could help. Riding at night or in poor visibility, approaching a blind curve, or simply traveling on a narrow road where a driver might otherwise come upon a cyclist unexpectedly are all situations where an advance warning could be useful.

Recent findings suggest that the feature is opt-in through Garmin Connect and activates when the cyclist starts an outdoor cycling activity. Garmin says location sharing is temporary and session-based, and that cyclists’ personal information isn’t displayed in Waze. Of course, anyone who uses a personal GPS device has already accepted that their location is shared with Major Tech in exchange for features they value, but at least we know that any personalized info stays within Garmin instead of proliferating even further.

Sources indicate that this is cool but requires both a Garmin product and the Waze app. IMHO better to equip new cars and trucks with bicycle and pedestrian detection.

In a recent development, garmin LiveTrack itself is free for owners with compatible Garmin devices and previously allowed cyclists to share their live location, speed, route, and other information with friends and family. I’ve used it myself as a safety feature when venturing off-road and on longer solo journeys. The Waze integration essentially expands that safety net to nearby motorists.

There’s obviously one major limitation: You need to be riding with a compatible Garmin device, and the driver needs to be using Waze.

But with Waze claiming around 200 million users globally and Garmin being one of the biggest names in cycling computers and fitness watches, that’s not exactly a tiny Venn diagram.

Anything that gives drivers another chance to see cyclists before actually seeing them sounds like a win to me.

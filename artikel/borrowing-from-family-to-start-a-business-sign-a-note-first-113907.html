According to recent reports, before family lends you startup money, put the terms in a note you both sign: the amount, interest rate, payment schedule, and who repays it if the business closes. Set the rate at or above the applicable federal rate, a minimum the IRS publishes monthly, so no part of the loan counts as a gift.

A handshake leaves each of you free to remember a different deal, and the difference highlights up the first time a payment is late.

It has been revealed that start by agreeing that it’s a loan, repaid on a schedule. Money your parents don’t want back is a gift, and a parent who gives you more than the annual gift tax exclusion in one year files a gift tax return. If they want a share of the profits, it’s a stake, which makes them part owners who get nothing back if the business fails. (Related: Taco Bell is bringing back the Steakhouse Boss Burrito ...)

Recent findings suggest that if the loan charges less than the applicable federal rate (AFR), the IRS’s below-market loan rules can treat the interest your parents gave up as a gift from them. In some cases, the IRS also counts it as income they owe tax on. (Related: Breaking: Illinois appeals Pacold injunction blocking i...)

In the note, formally a promissory note, two terms need the most thought. The first is whether you or your business owes it. Money lent to your limited liability organization (LLC) is generally its debt, not yours, so your parents carry the risk if it closes. The second is what happens to the balance if the business closes, whether you keep paying from your own income, pause the payments, or your parents forgive the rest.

It has been revealed that say a son borrows $20,000 from his parents for four years. The IRS’s mid-term rate applies to loans over three years and up to nine. Suppose that rate is just under 5% when he gets the money, so they charge 5%. Forty-eight automatic transfers of about $461 repay it with about $2,100 in interest, and no one has to bring up a payment at a holiday dinner.

In a recent development, ask for no more than your parents could afford to give away, since a newly introduced business can close before it repays anyone. Then give them a few days to decide how to lend without losing money or the relationship.

Look up the AFR for the month you get the money, write a rate at least that high into the note, and set up the automatic transfer the day it arrives.

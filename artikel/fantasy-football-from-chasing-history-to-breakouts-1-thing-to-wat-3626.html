With the second half of the season in full swing, here's what Yahoo analyst Matt Harmon is looking for in each team's WR room for fantasy football.

Industry observers note that after a blistering start to the year, Flowers is now WR8 in yards per route run and WR16 in points per game. It was tracking to be so much better than this before quarterback chaos sunk the Ravens' passing game. In Jacoby Brissett's first start, the Ravens dropped back over 50 times, a rarity in the Lamar Jackson era, but the passing tree wasn't consolidated whatsoever. Flowers was targeted on 14.9% of his routes, the lowest rate of the season. Both fantasy managers and a Ravens team that desperately needs to win require the ball in Flowers' hands much more often than that, even if he ultimately fumbled the game away in Week 10. When DJ Moore has been available for the Bills, all the mainstay passing-game players get a bump. On plays where Moore is on the field, Khalil Shakir averages 2.03 yards per route and Dalton Kincaid averages 3.15. Kincaid specifically drops to 1.97 when Moore isn't on the field. This is because Moore, who averages a whopping 16.6 air yards per target, has been a huge add as a coverage-dictator and vertical threat. Moore isn't going to be a WR1-level producer but he's key to the health of this offense and is capable of spike weeks in this ecosystem. Right now, the Bengals have the WR4 and WR6 in points per game on their roster. Ja'Marr Chase and Tee Higgins have been masterful this season with Joe Burrow playing the best football of his career. Each receiver has five games inside the top-10 of weekly fantasy scoring. The Bengals offense has used condensed formations, and the leap in their offensive line's pass-blocking has been a key factor. Quietly, Dohnte Meyers is emerging as a credible option as the WR3 who can play multiple positions, which has also allowed them to be more creative with the deployment. I see no reason this train slows down any time soon.

In a recent development, so far this season, Denzel Boston has been used as a downfield X-receiver and KC Concepcion has been a movement option who runs over 60% of his routes from the slot. I'm not critiquing that deployment, as both are excellent options in those roles and have been fantastic to start their rookie seasons. However, I believe Boston is capable of being weaponized with some reps as a major slot and Concepcion can win outside against man coverage. If those roles start to blend down the stretch, or especially in future seasons, these guys can be even more productive.

Sources indicate that call it hope or call it cope, but we can blame my favorite strength-of-schedule zealot, Justin Boone, for even getting me to keep the light on. Coming out of the bye, the Broncos have games against the Raiders (twice), Steelers, Jets, Dolphins, Bills and Patriots. Only the Steelers look like a potential trouble spot for passing games. I hesitate to say it can't get worse but it would be tough to imagine. Waddle has finished inside the top-24 weekly scoring receivers just twice, which is only one fewer game than every other Denver wide receiver combined (Pat Bryant twice, Courtland Sutton once). I'm hoping the offense has a clearer vision for how to maximize the player they traded major draft picks for, as well as a more confident player under center.

Recent findings suggest that in his last two games coming back from the team's Week 8 bye, Tank Dell has run a route on 55% and 54.8% of the dropbacks. The team has used guys like Xavier Hutchinson ahead of him and had him in a rotation with Marvin Harrison Jr. and Kayshon Boutte. There's a good chance this usage holds for Dell as he continues to work his way back from a serious injury. I'm still comfortable betting on him as an upside WR3 because he looks great on tape and is being used on downfield breaking routes. You just have to know you're dealing with a volatile projection with that playing time.

In a recent development, the Colts traded their best 2026 receiver at the deadline in Josh Downs and sent away a steady veteran in Keenan Allen. Their highly paid X-receiver, Alec Pierce, is on IR and I'm not expecting him to be back this year. Laquon Treadwell will lead this group in routes run as the every-down X-receiver going forward in a passing game that's been among the worst in the NFL, thanks to quarterback play. We don't need to fret the Colts' room much at this stage but I'm at least curious if any young players can flash to earn a role in 2027. Deion Burks is an interesting rookie with some juice who has gotten reps all year as a downfield Z-receiver.

In a recent development, the Jaguars are one of the toughest teams to prepare for in the NFL right now. This offense can run so many concepts out of multiple personnel groupings and has a litany of viable role players. Meanwhile, Jakobi Meyers and Parker Washington are two of the league's most productive receivers on throws of 10-20 air yards. In competitive games and good matchups, these two should roll, especially Washington.

Industry observers note that josh Downs' arrival in Kansas City paid immediate dividends for the offense. He was targeted five times on eight routes. Those numbers are only going up. The question is how high he can go and who comes off the field more often. Xavier Worthy seems like the natural answer given his play through three seasons, but his speed also brings tactical value by lifting coverages. Rashee Rice and Downs should be more complementary as they go. Additionally, Cyrus Allen's emergence as a talented route-runner and deep-ball option makes this even more complicated. I think Downs emerges as an every-week fantasy starter but we have playing time questions to answer to understand the ceiling.

According to recent reports, tre Tucker has been the team's best receiver but will be a free agent. He's a good player but mostly a vertical-only receiver. Meanwhile, Jalen Nailor has looked overstretched as an every-down starter. Second-year receiver Jack Bech is a nice role player but hasn't emerged as a highly-targeted option and missed a chunk of time with an injury. The Raiders will use the rest of the season to simply assess if any of these guys will be in the rotation in 2027, or if they will need to start fresh.

I've been ready to see more of Tre' Harris and less of Quentin Johnston since the first month of the season. Johnston has never fully developed and, frankly, looks like he's regressed in 2026. Meanwhile, Harris continues to flash a nice ability to separate and play multiple positions. He's run a route on more than 90% of the dropbacks just twice this year and compiled 60 yards on three catches in Week 4 and 86 on five last week. I'd love to watch Ladd McConkey take off in the back half, but I just worry he's not physically up for the task. He averages 2.25 yards per route run this year, so he's been good, but he just can't stay on the field.

Caleb Douglas leads all rookies with 2.27 yards per route run and the tape has been rock-solid. He is clearly a hit for the Dolphins. Now, let's find out how good he is. Malik Willis' injury will likely complicate things from a production standpoint. Yet, Douglas registered a season-high 155 air yards and caught three passes for 71 yards last week with Kyle McCord under center. Douglas has hit at least 70 yards in three of his last four games.

When the Patriots return from their bye week, they will do so without Drake Maye under center. The season is slowly slipping away for this team as they are buried under a mountain of injuries. It's just not going to be their year. The rest of the season should be dedicated to figuring out which members of the wide receiver room should have major roles in 2027 and beyond. That includes A.J. Brown, who has been unable to stay healthy and doesn't look right when he's been available. I'm not sure he'll be on the roster next year, which leaves Romeo Doubs and a collection of WR3 and WR4 types. This room needs an upgrade.

Despite playing in very different roles, we haven't seen many games where AD Mitchell and Omar Cooper Jr. both go off at the same time. That's especially tough when Garrett Wilson is available. The Jets have a strong trio of receivers with these guys on the roster and my sense is that they'll all be on the team in 2027. The rest of the way, we need to look at their individual matchups and success to try and pinpoint the right weeks Mitchell and Cooper can get going. This will also help us assess what the pecking order looks like behind Wilson next season in what, hopefully, is an improved overall ecosystem.

Recent findings suggest that germie Bernard looked like he might be one of those second-half-of-the-season rookie breakouts about a month ago. Yet, he's failed to clear 30 yards in any of his last three games. While I think he's a good player, you wonder if he has missed his window to cement a starting job. The Steelers will likely get veteran Michael Pittman Jr. back soon, along with Roman Wilson. The Bernard and Wilson playing time splits will be quite interesting since Wilson gave this team a real man coverage answer early in the year.

The Titans have found an answer to boosting Cam Ward in recent weeks: Tennessee has used no huddle on 45% of their snaps in each game on either side of the bye week. No other team is even above 18%. Ward operates well with tempo and from spread concepts. No surprise, this has resulted in Ward's two best games of the season. That's without Carnell Tate in the mix, who has been excellent on tape this year and a real weapon down the field. Once he's healthy, Tate's ability to rapidly separate on routes over the middle in this newly introduced-look attack should result in some big weeks.

It has been revealed that I'll be frank, this might be the bleakest wide receiver room I can remember in recent memory after they lost Michael Wilson for the season and placed Kendrick Bourne on IR. The only guy who is moderately interesting is Day 3 rookie, Reggie Virgil. He's a slower, poor man's Robbie Anderson from his days with the Panthers. Someone has to run routes for this team; catches may be optional.

It has been revealed that we know what Drake London can do but not enough people are giving Jahan Dotson praise for the rebound season he is having. Dotson leads all wide receivers with 30-plus targets with 21.9 yards per catch. He's been a real nice answer for the team as a deep option down the field. His ability here has allowed the Falcons to keep London in a movement Z and slot-heavy role.

According to recent reports, the Panthers need to change up their approach on offense. What worked to smash teams early in the season has become unworkable as pass-protection issues have cropped up. Bryce Young leads the NFL in pass attempts with pressure arriving in 2.5 seconds or faster. On those throws, Tetairoa McMillan averages almost 17 air yards per target and Jalen Coker is at almost 10. These receivers run long, developing routes to hit chunk plays and that's a thin line to walk when you can't hold up in protection.

This season, Rome Odunze averages 14.9 air yards per target, which leads the team by a wide margin and Luther Burden is at 7.4. The Bears aren't doing anything wrong here and both are thriving in their respective roles; there's just no overlap between the two. I believe Odunze can win in the short area as a chain-mover more often and Burden can get open in the intermediate area. Blending those would allow Odunze to be targeted more often and keep Burden involved for all four quarters.

Sources indicate that at this rate, I'd be shocked if George Pickens returns to Dallas after an up-and-down season with the team on the franchise tag, especially with CeeDee Lamb turning in a dominant performance. Pickens will likely be motivated to put up more big performances after his Week 10 outing, which was the second-highest scoring game for a receiver all season. Keep an eye on his likely replacement, Ryan Flournoy, who has scored as a top-24 receiver in five of his last six games. He's already a viable option.

According to recent reports, over the last five weeks, Amon-Ra St. Brown and Jameson Williams account for 51% of the team's targets. Sam LaPorta is a relatively distant third at 14%. For much of the last two years, it's really been a push and pull between LaPorta and Williams for the second target option — rarely have they popped off together. The Lions have jumped their use of heavy personnel considerably in this post-bye span, which has contributed to the wideouts getting more burn, especially Williams, who runs a route on a team-high 90% of the dropbacks since Week 6. This is how we get more consistent weekly production from the volatile wideout.

Sources indicate that matthew Golden has been one of the breakout stars at the wide receiver position this year. He's the WR8 in points per game behind his teammate Christian Watson. That's been despite leading the league in drops by a considerable margin. Fantasy Points Data has charged Golden with 13 drops this year, almost double the amount of the second-highest receiver in AD Mitchell with eight. He's also won just three of his 11 contested targets. I'm always the first guy to suggest you just live with drop challenges and continue to build the wideout's confidence so they can push past them. The Packers and Jordan Love appear to be taking that approach. If he can ever clean these issues up, the ceiling is quite high for Golden and he's already been excellent; scary proposition.

The Rams used a ton of 20 personnel in Week 10, with both Blake Corum and Kyren Williams on the field along with three wide receivers. For the most part, that was a collection of small speed-based options to hit major plays while the backs operate as checkdown outlets. It was a good bit of problem-solving by Sean McVay as injuries hit the receiver room but it's likely not sustainable. Now, if we can add Davante Adams to the mix as an intermediate answer to balance the scales, it could become a feature personnel grouping. The veteran could return in Week 12 or 13.

It has been revealed that I'm not asking for much here. Justin Jefferson should continue to be the featured player. He registered 13 targets in Week 10. He had not seen 13-plus looks in a game since the Sam Darnold 2024 season. We just need complementary pieces to add pop to the other touches and layer in new dimensions to the offense with Kirk Cousins under center. I'll admit to enjoying the work of Myles Price and Tai Felton in place of some of the other guys who were previously putting the Vikings into schematic binds. Both averaged 6.0 yards or more after the catch per reception. Cousins having someone to spit the ball out to in the short area and get additional yards helps keep the offense on schedule.

Industry observers note that since Jordyn Tyson has been activated, he's taken over half his snaps from the slot and lined up off the ball on 80% of them. No wide receiver has been used in motion more than Tyson the last two weeks. He's been able to gobble up easy receptions while being targeted on 38% of his routes. That's awesome for his usage in fantasy but it was not a great initiative for the Saints' offense, particularly in Week 10, when they ranked 23rd out of 28 teams in my offensive efficiency score. Tyson can get open in the intermediate area and Chris Olave shouldn't be left to run vertical stretch routes only. Tyson is an awesome player in this slot movement role but we can't overindex on the short passes, especially since he's not a run-after-catch threat.

It has been revealed that malik Nabers was looking close to 100% healthy prior to the Giants' Week 8 bye and enjoyed some strong moments with J.J. McCarthy against the Texans. The two games since have been less enjoyable. While it's easy to blame McCarthy for the problems and he's far from blameless, Nabers has left some plays on the field as well. I hold Nabers in extremely high regard and believe he is the next guy who could graduate to Tier 1 (Ja'Marr Chase, Jaxon Smith-Njigba, etc. grouping) at the receiver position. I'd love to see him knock down that door in the back-half of the season. He needs to rebound from two of his less inspiring outings in back-to-back weeks.

In Weeks 7 to 9, Makai Lemon owned a near 27% share of the Eagles targets. Despite four out-of-character drops, Lemon still averaged 61.7 yards per game and snagged 19 receptions. He was starting to look like the layup slot funnel you'd hope for from his archetype of receiver. However, the target competition is about to ramp up with DeVonta Smith and Dontayvion Wicks due back from injuries soon. How the Lemon and Smith, in particular, target distribution goes will inform us on how the situation may play out next season, as the Eagles play out the string for the rest of 2026.

The 49ers have the oldest wide receiver room I can remember with five guys over 31 years old taking snaps for the team in 2026. Yet, they keep rolling. Deebo Samuel Sr. has settled into a pure gadget role and barely operates as a true receiver. Keenan Allen arrived in the middle of last week and immediately earned a first down on 19.1% of his routes. He's going to be quite helpful down the stretch. When Mike Evans is healthy, he's still a fantastic starting X-receiver.

Jaxon Smith-Njigba has 1,207 yards on 79 catches (109 targets) through 10 weeks of the NFL season. You'd do backflips if your favorite sleeper receiver did that over a full season. Smith-Njigba is on pace to clear 2,000 yards this season. He'd be the first wideout in NFL history to do it. Based on his own individual play despite teams crafting coverages to stop him, and the lack of a true secondary threat, there's no reason to expect him to slow down anytime soon.

According to recent reports, I'm very confident that Emeka Egbuka is a good NFL player but the Buccaneers need him to be great. We've yet to see that version of the player this season. To be fair, Egbuka has been dogged by injuries since training camp and the guy we saw against the Bears prior to the bye week was the worst version I've watched on tape in 2026. Hopefully a week off helps him get back to form as he heads into a good matchup against the Lions defense. Egbuka's 20.8% slot rate in Week 9 was the lowest of the season; that needs to rise in future weeks.

It has been revealed that since the team returned from the bye, Antonio Williams has led the team in routes run. He looks good on tape and gets easy separation in the intermediate area. However, he hasn't been consistently involved in the offense (16% target share). The Commanders spread the ball around, and he's competing with an excellent receiver in Stefon Diggs, who leads the team over the last three weeks with a 25% target-per-route rate. Williams can still make some big plays but we need him to get the ball more consistently to be an every-week option in fantasy. He does remain and excellent buy in dynasty leagues.

It has been revealed that BLYTHEWOOD, S.C. — Electric vehicle startup Scout Motors has notched roughly 212,000 reservations for its upcoming EVs as the manufacturer nears completion of its latest $2 billion plant here, CEO Scott Keogh told CNBC.

The reservations are for the Volkswagen-backed firm's Terra pickup truck and Traveler SUV, which are expected to start full production next year before deliveries begin to customers in 2028. (Related: Stop treating agents as one-off projects and start runn...)

In a recent development, reservations do not necessarily translate to sales, as the U.S. automotive industry has learned during the past decade, especially with highly anticipated all-electric trucks such as the Ford F-150 Lightning, Tesla Cybertruck and others that garnered strong interest that didn't lead to meaningful numbers of purchases. (Related: Industry News: Designing Azure silicon on Azure silicon...)

Keogh stated he expects Scout could roughly double those reservations by the time customer production begins, but he said the more notable thing is to convert early interest into actual sales as production nears.

Industry observers note that "It's been a consistent drumbeat," Keogh said during an interview at Scout's plant site outside Columbia. "I think we could easily be up over 400,000 with reservations without doing too much effort. The massive thing for us is how much of the initial production can we 100% presell.".

Industry observers note that keogh stated achieving an order book of 12 to 18 months "would be great" and is something he thinks the organization can do before production. Scout on Thursday said plant construction is "in its final phase.".

Scout's vehicles will be offered as all-electric versions as well as electric hybrid variants, known as extended-range electric vehicles, or EREVs, that include an internal combustion engine as a backup generator for the electric motors and batteries.

Scout, a former American vehicle brand from 1961 to 1980, was expected to exclusively offer EVs in a bid for the German automaker to expand its presence in the U.S. However, slower-than-expected adoption of EVs and higher costs led the firm to decide to offer EREVs as well.

Keogh said extended-range and SUV models are dominating reservations thus far, with roughly 87% choosing EREV models and 75% being SUVs.

Recent findings suggest that the CEO also stated the tech giant is drawing interest from its target markets, which include owners of off-road vehicles such as the Ford Bronco and Jeep Wrangler as well as full-size pickup trucks and U.S. EV leader Tesla.

It has been revealed that given the interest signaled by reservations, Keogh said the tech giant is expected to launch with the EREVs first, followed by the EVs. He also said the company plans to launch with mid-level models first, a deviation from many companies that begin with their highest-priced models.

Recent findings suggest that both the Traveler and Terra are expected to start at under $60,000. The EREV models are expected to feature more than 500 miles of range, according to the tech giant, compared with 300 miles of range for the all-electric models.

In a recent development, "The No.1 thing for me is I want to go get the best volume as soon as possible," Keogh said. "In my mind, it's how quick can you get to 100,000 units? It's at 100,000 units where you can start to punch through.".

His comments come months after Rivian Automotive received criticism online for launching its R2 SUV with its highest-priced models first, despite that being a common practice across the automotive industry.

Keogh said Scout's South Carolina plant is about 85% complete, but supporting infrastructure and a $300 million supplier park continue to be installed.

Reservations for Scout's vehicles initially opened in October 2024, when it revealed the Traveler and Terra, which feature sleek, yet rugged, exteriors that pay some homage to the brand's historic models.

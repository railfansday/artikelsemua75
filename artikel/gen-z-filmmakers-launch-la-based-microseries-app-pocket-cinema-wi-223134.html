In a recent development, EXCLUSIVE: The microdrama craze is beginning to attract bigger players. But undoubtedly, it offers a leg up to those starting out.

Among the scrappy newcomers are French-Russian filmmaker Lada Egorova and Brazilian filmmaker Thiago Aragão, a duo a few years out of college, who have launched LA-based platform Pocket Cinema+, an independent microseries streaming app whose early offerings include a Western/samurai drama and a sci-fi series, both shot in and around LA. The firm offers vertical and horizontal genre series and they’re preparing a French production partnership as their next step. In a recent development, both are heavily involved in the making of their content to date with Egorova writer, directing and producing Blades & Bullets, a vertical Western/samurai series “following a Black samurai and a Mexican cowboy who must confront a ruthless gang”. Egorova, who has made multiple shorts and worked on commercials, is also writer-director-producer on Journey To Origin, a “horizontal post-apocalyptic science-fiction microseries following a survivor and a sentient android searching for hope and answers”. Also on the slate is a noir crime series, The Price Of Yesterday. Watch on Deadline. (Related: Find Orphan Pages and Buried Content: Build an Internal...)

Pocket Cinema+ is presently founder-backed but received a grant through STARZ’s #TakeTheLead initiative, which supported one of the first series on the slate. To date, actors used in the series have been relatively unknown or newcomers.

The tech giant states it won’t be using AI for shortcuts: “All three of our latest productions were shot in live action, without AI in any part of the production pipeline. We’re committed to building Pocket Cinema+ around authentic filmmaking and live-action storytelling.”.

The duo are now in talks with producer David Yol’s Geneva and Paris-based Denan Productions to develop and shoot microseries in France for the Pocket Cinema platform. The idea is for the slate to include English and French-language genre series, starting with a horror, and they’re targeting budgets in the $60k-$100k range.

“Our editorial focus is genre-driven microseries, with an ambition to develop a wider range of stories and filmmaker perspectives within short-form entertainment,” the duo told us.

Egorova expanded: “Having studied cinema at Université Gustave Eiffel and Paris 8 before moving to LA, I’m particularly interested in building a production bridge between France and the U.S., and involving emerging French filmmakers. Our production plans in France and Brazil are the next steps in our vision, aiming to produce series simultaneously across countries through local creative partnerships.”.

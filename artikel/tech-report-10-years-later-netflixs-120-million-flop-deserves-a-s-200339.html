It has been revealed that in 2016, Netflix was riding high. The shift to original programming, which had kicked off three years earlier with House of Cards, was now proving to be an undeniable success. That year marked the return of hit indicates like Daredevil, Unbreakable Kimmy Schmidt, BoJack Horseman, and Orange is the New Black, along with the debut of brand-defining series Stranger Things and The Crown. All of these shows have remained in the cultural zeitgeist, but arguably the best original series released on Netflix in 2016 has all but disappeared from the conversation.

It has been revealed that this Turkey Day, as Polygon celebrates our favorite failures and flops from the world of entertainment, it's time to resurrect a Netflix original that earned (mostly) rave reviews but got canceled after just one season for what, in hindsight, feels like an easily avoidable reason. Not only is The Get Down an overlooked masterpiece, but 10 years later, it's due for a revival. (Related: Industry News: huggingface_hub v1.0: Five Years of Buil...)

Created by Baz Luhrmann (Moulin Rouge, The Great Gatsby, Romeo + Juliet) and playwright Stephen Adly Guirgis, The Get Down traces the origin story of hip-hop. Deep within the Bronx, MCs hype local crowds with rhythmic lyricism performed over percussive instrumental breaks pulled by pioneering DJs from funk, soul, and disco records. The series centers on one such MC, Zeke "Books" Figuero (Justice Smith), and his DJ, Curtis "Shaolin Fantastic" Caldwell (Shameik Moore), as they stumble into a newly introduced art form. While much of the story follows these two young Black men as they hustle and struggle to succeed, The Get Down's scope is even grander. It's the story of Upcoming York City in the 1970s and everything that entails, from corrupt politicians and gangsters to the rising punk music scene in Manhattan. Disco defines the series almost as much as hip-hop, with female protagonist Mylene Cruz (Herizen F. Guardiola) representing the power of the then-dominant genre as a beautiful woman with an incredible voice who's stuck singing in church when she wants to be a radio star.

The absolutely stacked cast also includes Yahya Abdul-Mateen II as gangster disco prince Clarence "Cadillac" Caldwell, Jimmy Smits as a local political boss, Giancarlo Esposito as Mylene's pastor father, Mamoudou Athie as real-life DJ legend Grandmaster Flash, and Daveed Diggs as adult Ezekiel narrating the story (with hip-hop icon Nas providing his dubbed-in rapping voice). It's an all-star team-up firing on all cylinders.

According to recent reports, the Get Down has all the usual flourishes of a Luhrmann production. It's a beautifully realized world that glitters in the neon lights of Upcoming York without attempting to hide the crime and decay that defined the city in the 1970s. Telling a story about hip-hop and disco also allows Luhrmann to fill the show with elevated musical moments that are on par with the best scenes from Moulin Rouge, while also feeling far more grounded. This isn't a musical in the sense that characters don't spontaneously break into song to move the plot forward or express their emotions, but it still manages to achieve a similarly staggering effect each time a record spins and an MC picks up the mic.

Industry observers note that it's also deeply reverent toward the history of hip-hop. Music icons Nas, Grandmaster Flash, and Kurtis Blow organized a hip-hop boot camp to teach the young cast about the art form. In an interview with Mother Jones, Grandmaster Flash praised the series for demystifying the earliest origins of the genre he helped create.

According to recent reports, "Hip-hop has always been chronologically misunderstood," he said. "Too many times, people are hearing the story from the second floor. Nobody’s heard the story from the basement. If hip-hop was a cake, all I can tell you is the eggs, the flour, the sugar, the vanilla — the ingredient years. This is what Baz wanted to put on film.".

Meanwhile, Nas, who arrived in the '90s and redefined rap music with his vibrant descriptions of life in the Brooklyn projects, conveyed through naturalistic lyrical poetry, wrote every single bar delivered by Ezekiel on the show.

"I am Ezekiel," he told Rolling Stone. "Ezekiel is older than me, but I am that.".

Recent findings suggest that in other words, The Get Down wasn't just Luhrmann's retelling of a famous story. It was a deeply researched, loving tribute to the creation of hip-hop, told by the people who defined the genre from the very start.

The Get Down dropped six episodes on Netflix in August 2016, with another five released in April 2017 to complete the first season. The second batch picks up a year later in the story as well, with Mylene's singing career taking off while Ezekiel struggles to keep up, weighed down by the world he grew up in.

The show was canceled soon afterward for a variety of reasons, including its astronomical cost. In an article breaking down the rise and fall of The Get Down, IndieWire called it "the best $120 million Netflix has ever spent.".

Ultimately, the show's downfall reportedly traces back to Luhrmann. While the director took a hands-on approach in crafting season 1 (even directing the pilot episode himself), he decided to take a step back with season 2 to return to film and make Elvis. This didn't mean the show had to stop, though. In a sense, that approach had pretty much defined the entire series from day one.

It has been revealed that “Because I come from community storytelling, you have to get the community’s trust,” Luhrmann told IndieWire. “So I started to reach out to the people whose story it was, because it’s not my story. I feel like I just curated that story.”.

Recent findings suggest that but Netflix balked, and The Get Down came to a disappointingly early end. In a sense, it was a sign of things to come. In the years since, the streamer has developed an unfortunate reputation for canceling great shows too early and for relying on big-name talent over good storytelling and original ideas (although The Get Down had plenty of both).

In a recent development, A decade after its demise, the chances of a revival seem slim. There isn't exactly a devoted fanbase crying out for more, and Luhrmann is already busy working on his next movie. Then again, it's not totally impossible either. After all, The Get Down's core duo has only gotten more famous in the years since, with Shameik Moore voicing Miles Morales in the Spider-Verse movies and Justice Smith starring in everything from Dungeons & Dragons: Honor Among Thieves to Now You See Me: Now You Don't. Maybe that's enough to entice some Netflix executive.

In a recent development, A follow-up could reunite Smith and Moore (and the rest of the cast) and pick up their story a decade later. The late '80s was a golden age for hip-hop, with artists like Eric B. & Rakim, Significant Daddy Kane, LL Cool J, Public Enemy, and N.W.A dominating the airwaves on both sides of the country. A newly introduced season of The Get Down could explore what happens when an art form born out of the Bronx projects goes mainstream — and how that kind of explosive success can often leave the original creators behind.

It has been revealed that is it a long shot​​​​​​​? Of course, but either way, The Get Down remains a shining example of what's starting to feel like the long-gone golden age of Netflix programming. So why not give it a spin? And maybe if enough people do, we'll finally get a season 2.

Anthropic kept IPO bankers off OpenAI work for six months, Reuters reports

Anthropic’s shares were flat on Monday, its third day of trading, CNBC reported. Most significant tech stocks rebounded, and Nvidia jumped 30% after it reinstated Jensen Huang.

It has been revealed that upcoming details show how tightly Anthropic ran the listing. Echo Wang and Milana Vinn of Reuters reported them on Sunday, citing several people familiar with the matter. Anthropic told some of its 22 underwriters to use only four senior bankers each. One of the people said the rule likely came from fear of leaks. The banks found a way around it anyway. Industry observers note that anthropic also asked some senior bank officers to sign papers barring core deal staff from working with rivals, the people said. The ban runs for six months after the listing. The rivals include OpenAI, Cursor, DeepSeek and Hugging Face. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

According to recent reports, bankers and lawyers told Reuters that exclusivity this broad is rare, especially for individual bankers. One banker who refused to sign remarked few staff could promise to stay away from Anthropic’s peers.

According to recent reports, anthropic shared documents with most banks through NetRoadshow rather than as PDFs. The platform logs who opened which page, marks each document with the banker’s name and blocks saving copies, Reuters reported.

Marketing began on 10 November. The night before, a law firm on the deal sent banks a PDF with the wrong price range and share figures, then told them to ignore it. The correct filing arrived around 2am New York time. Banks had several hours to approve it before it went to the SEC.

Bankers earned more than $500m in fees on the deal, known inside Anthropic as Project Lantern. Anthropic and SpaceX both negotiated to pay the underwriters nothing on the extra shares sold through the over-allotment option, or greenshoe, noted Jay Ritter, a University of Florida professor who studies IPOs.

“I don’t think most companies are going to be able to demand zero spread on overallotment shares,” Ritter told Reuters.

Anthropic, Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley declined to comment to Reuters.

The banks exercised the greenshoe after one day of trading. Craig Coben, a former global head of equity capital markets at Bank of America, questioned that timing in a column for the Financial Times. Once the option is used, the banks can no longer buy shares to support the price. On Friday the stock came within five cents of its $83 IPO price before it closed at $88.91. Coben called the quick exercise “somewhat opportunistic” and remarked Anthropic likely approved or encouraged it.

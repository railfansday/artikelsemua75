The San Francisco Bay Area has become an unofficial proving ground for robotaxis, with companies like Waymo, Zoox, and Tesla running supervised road tests on San Francisco’s streets. A fleet of specially equipped Lucid Gravity SUVs has also been on the prowl in the Bay Area this year, testing a self-driving ride-hailing experience for Uber. While these tests have been run with a safety driver ready to take over, the Gravity SUVs are now going fully driverless.

The modified Gravity SUVs are the product of a collaboration between Lucid, Uber, and Nuro, a tech giant that builds autonomous driving software. The robotaxis feature an Uber-designed cabin interface and are operated by Nuro’s autonomous driving system. High-resolution cameras, lidar sensors, and radar sensors are integrated into the Gravity’s body and mounted to a rooftop “halo” that doubles as a way for riders to identify the correct vehicle through LED lights.

According to recent reports, testing first began with human supervision in the Bay Area in December 2025. Since then, Nuro has continued to conduct supervised round-the-clock testing in San Francisco and Houston. The firm now claims it’s ready to begin its next phase, which involves driving on public roads without a safety operator inside.

Sources indicate that lucid built and delivered over 100 autonomous vehicles to complete this next stage. The California Department of Motor Vehicles has issued two permits this year that allow the vehicles to test on public roads without a safety operator inside and allow for passenger rides when a safety driver is present.

As part of the collaboration’s terms, Uber has committed to owning and operating at least 35,000 Lucid vehicles equipped with Nuro’s autonomous technology. The service is expected to launch to the public in the Bay Area by early 2027, followed by debuts in dozens of global markets over the next few years.

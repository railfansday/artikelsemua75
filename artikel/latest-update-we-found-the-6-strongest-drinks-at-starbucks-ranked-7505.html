But I need to smell the freshly brewed espresso before I can muster the strength to get out from under the duvet.

Sometimes you just need a 350-milligram latte to keep you chugging through the day (unless your doctor says otherwise, in which case, ignore me).

According to recent reports, in the spirit of staying fueled, I analyzed the Starbucks menu and narrowed it down to the most caffeinated bevvies you can buy in a grande size. However, everything is customizable and may differ slightly in caffeine content—you can always ask for more or fewer espresso shots, or opt for a larger size.

There’s a reason you might feel extra jittery after a morning Cold Brew. It’s packed with caffeine because it uses almost twice as many coffee grounds as regular brewed coffee. It is diluted with water, though, to temper the buzz.

If you love a sweet latte but want more caffeine, the Iced Brown Sugar Shaken Espresso is your ticket at roughly 255 milligrams for a grande.

If you’re craving a Cold Brew with a kick, go Nitro. It uses a higher coffee-to-water ratio in the cold steeping and dispensing process, and it is served without ice (so, again, less dilution). The result: bold flavor and about 280 milligrams of caffeine.

Recent findings suggest that there’s a reason I’m a drip-coffee loyalist, and it’s not just the flavor. It’s also about the 310 milligrams of caffeine that a grande Pike Place delivers, which earns the medium roast coffee a solid second spot on this list.

Industry observers note that drumroll, please! The most caffeinated beverage on Starbucks’ menu is the Blonde Roast Brewed Coffee at 360 milligrams. Why does it differ from the medium and even the dark roast? It’s pretty simple: Blonde usually tests higher because Starbucks brews it with a little more coffee, and lighter roasts can pack more grounds into the scoop.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, we are notifying customers about a security incident involving a subset of prompts submitted to Together AI’s serverless inference service in June and July 2026. An unauthorized party downloaded stored prompt samples after a credential for a private repository was exposed in a public code repository.

We apologize for this incident. We are reviewing the affected data to determine what information was exposed and which customers require direct notification. (Related: Chipotle is giving customers free burritos with Thanksg...)

It has been revealed that as part of routine software testing, we collected prompt samples authorized for that use and stored them in a private Hugging Face repository. The samples came from requests to the MiniMax M3, GLM-5.2, and Kimi K2.6 models, primarily on June 24, July 4, July 8, and July 14, 2026. (Related: Silicon Motion demos SM8466 PCIe 6.0 SSD controller, ex...)

During development of our public GitHub repository, xorl, an AI coding agent committed a credential that provided access to the private Hugging Face repository. Our automated scanning detected the exposed credential, and we reverted the commit.

Recent findings suggest that during the exposure window, an unauthorized party obtained the credential and used it to download the stored prompt samples. The party subsequently searched those samples for credentials to other services and tested whether those credentials were valid. Five of the credentials tested were canary credentials associated with a Dragos security experiment.

We have no reason to believe the unauthorized party has leaked this data further and understand that it has now been deleted.

We have worked with an external forensic investigation firm and law enforcement to investigate the incident.

In a recent development, based on our review, we have not identified evidence of additional prompt leakage beyond this incident. Our review of the affected prompt contents is ongoing to determine whether sensitive personal information or other customer data was exposed. We will contact affected customers directly as needed with the findings relevant to them.

Following the incident, we strengthened controls around development workflows, credentials, and customer data. These changes include:.

Customers handling sensitive prompt data should enable ZDR in their account settings.

If you have questions about this incident or its potential impact on your organization, contact security@together.ai.

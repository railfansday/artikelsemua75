Stop your browser from killing your laptop battery with these 3 tweaks

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that you unplug your laptop with a full charge, sit down at a coffee shop to get some work done, and an hour later your battery is already at 50%. You check your system settings to see what’s killing your power, and the culprit is exactly what you suspected: your web browser. Modern websites are effectively heavy software applications running constantly in the background. If you keep dozens of tabs open, your browser is caching media, running ad scripts, and refreshing data for every single one of them simultaneously. It eats up your system memory and forces your processor to work overtime. You don’t have to change the way you work or close your research tabs to get your battery life back. You just need to flip a few switches hidden in your browser’s settings menu. Turn on native memory savers Both Chrome and Edge finally have built-in tools to stop background tabs from draining power. In Chrome, go to Settings, click Performance and turn on Memory Saver and choose the Maximum option. In Edge, go to Settings, click System and Performance > Performance, and turn on Enable Energy Saver, then set it to Maximum Savings. Then toggle Automatically Put Tabs to Sleep on. This setting automatically puts inactive tabs to sleep until you click back to them. Stop background apps from running when closed By default, browsers like to keep their background processes running even after you’ve closed the browser window entirely. This is terrible for your battery. Go to Chrome’s Settings, click System, and toggle off the switch that notes “Continue running background apps when Google Chrome is closed.” In Edge, go to System and Performance > System and toggle off “Continue running background extensions and apps when Edge is closed.” Use a tab discarding extension If you’re someone who keeps fifty tabs open at all times, native memory savers might not be aggressive enough. Install a free extension such as Auto Tab Discard. It lets you set strict rules, like automatically unloading any tab you haven’t looked at in 10 minutes. The tab stays visible in your top bar, but it uses absolutely zero battery power until you click it again.

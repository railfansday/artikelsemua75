Pro-Palestinian donor James ‘Fergie’ Chambers released as court considers extradition request.

In a blur of events surrounding the Trump administration’s effort to extradite James “Fergie” Chambers, Spain released the wealthy US activist and donor on bail even as Irish police raided a Dublin house owned by Chambers and his partner, Stella Schnabel, acting on a request from US authorities. Chambers’s case is the first known instance of the US seeking to extradite a citizen over alleged support for Hamas, an accusation apparently tied to an international money-laundering charge in the criminal complaint underpinning the extradition request and donations to organizations helping Palestinians in Gaza. Speaking outside Madrid’s Aranjuez prison upon his release on Friday, Chambers called the charges against him “baseless”, adding that his release was “hopefully the first step in recognizing that internationally”.

Chambers also mentioned: “Spain is a country that has stood on the right side of this most important issue of our time, that has stood on the right side of the genocide, and where this is some reason and some justice.”.

An appeals court ordered the conditional release on Friday due to issues surrounding the legally required US translation of the extradition request’s core documents into Spanish, first reported on by the Guardian. He had been detained since 10 July, when Spanish authorities arrested him in Ibiza.

The order asserts that the translation issues cause “a diminishment, at least for the time being, in the solidity or foundation of the extradition request”.

Chambers’s release follows a separate decision on Thursday by the three-judge panel at the high court tasked with deciding on the extradition to pause the proceedings in order to evaluate the impact of the alterations the US made to the original, machine-made translation of the extradition request into Spanish.

It has been revealed that the Guardian detailed four examples of the alterations on 7 November and the US admitted to tampering with the translation, calling them “substantial [and] inadvertent” on 17 November – and citing the same four examples. Chambers’s defense team has asserted for weeks that there are as many as 30 such alterations.

According to recent reports, the changes introduced prejudicial notions with specific legal implications that in turn wound up in the Spanish prosecutor’s report on the case, Aitor Martínez, a member of Chambers’s defense team, told the Guardian earlier this week. The judges must consider the prosecutor’s report in deciding on the extradition request.

The high court was expected to rule on the extradition this week, following a 13 November hearing. But as of Thursday’s decision, the deadline has been extended indefinitely as the judges evaluate the translation, including a 345-page report submitted by the defense team that identifies 30 “incriminating changes” Trump administration officials made to a translation prepared by DeepL, “an AI-first translation platform”, according to its website.

The decision also gives the prosecutor an opportunity to address the problem in writing, after dismissing it in the 13 November hearing by referring to “supposed translation errors”.

In a recent development, chambers’s views and donations to progressive and humanitarian projects have been extensively profiled in US media, including his controversial public comments on Israel and Gaza. A week after the 7 October 2023 Hamas attacks on Israel, he posted on X about Israel’s response: “Stop calling it ‘collective punishment’/There’s nothing to be ‘punished’ for/No faction of the Palestinian resistance, Hamas or other, has done *anything* wrong/It’s simply genocide/Like it has been.”.

The heir to $250m from his family’s business, Cox Enterprises, is being sought in extradition because of such views, his defense team has asserted throughout the case.

Meanwhile, the Irish Garda Síochána, acting on a request from US authorities, raided a Dublin house owned by Chambers and Schnabel on Wednesday, destroying the front door and removing electronic items and other possessions, according to their attorney in Ireland, Ciarán Mulholland.

Schnabel, who is five months pregnant, remarked: “It is difficult to see this raid as anything other than a desperate fishing expedition by the US authorities to find evidence to bolster their case against Fergie.”.

The attorney told the Guardian that the raid was the result of a “mutual legal assistance” request from the US – and that such a request “wouldn’t give [the Garda Síochána] permission to kick in the doors of someone’s dwelling in Ireland”.

“It’s unusual for the Irish state to be acting in such a clandestine manner,” Mulholland remarked. He has asked authorities to produce a search warrant and an itemized list of what was taken. “My fear is that whatever was taken has been put on a plane and taken to the US,” he added.

“This [extradition case] demonstrates the complete disregard for due process … that the Trump administration seems to have,” Mulholland stated.

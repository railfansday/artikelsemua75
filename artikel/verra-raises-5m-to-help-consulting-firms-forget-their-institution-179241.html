Verra, an artificial intelligence startup building the institutional memory layer for consulting firms, mentioned today it has raised $5 million in seed funding to help those companies overcome their amnesia and make their decades of knowledge accessible to every employee. (Related: What I do every time I get an IRS notice...)

In a recent development, today’s round was led by Kearny Jackson and saw participation from Andreessen Horowitz’s a16z Speedrun, Petra and a number of strategic investors. It comes just seven months after Verra, officially known as Lighthouse Software Technologies Inc., introduced its platform.

Recent findings suggest that verra was founded last year by Chief Executive Kashyap Nathan (pictured, right), who previously worked as a researcher at OpenAI Group PBC and Palantir Technologies Inc., and Christopher Farrington (left), a former software engineer at Deloitte Touche Tohmatsu Ltd. In an interview, Nathan explained that he and Farrington had seen firsthand how well-paid consultants would struggle with fresh customer engagements, wasting hours of time researching to surface institutional knowledge that their firm already possessed. (Related: Restaurant owners left in tears after discovering their...)

Sources indicate that “Consulting firms sit on decades of proposals, models and reports, and almost none of it is usable by the people who need it next,” Nathan stated. “We built the layer that makes that knowledge usable, and we built it to run inside the firm’s own cloud, because no consulting firm is going to send client work to a shared service.”.

Industry observers note that verra makes consulting firms’ institutional knowledge accessible through an artificial intelligence-native memory layer that indexes their past deliverables and distills their previous methodologies into reusable skills for autonomous agents. Then, those agents can utilize those skills to get started in tools such as Microsoft Word, PowerPoint and Excel, and generate a highly detailed draft for new client engagements in ten hours or less.

Nathan noted that’s a significant improvement. At most consulting firms, it typically takes about 80 hours to get that first draft ready. “The traditional approach involves a SharePoint search box and a junior analyst with a week to spare,” he explained. “The AI-native version of that is a chat tool that answers a question from a few retrieved pages and leaves the consultant to rewrite the output. But both start from a blank page.”.

Verra is also much more efficient, Nathan said. Whereas traditional chatbots can generate text snippets, Verra creates its sourced deliverables directly inside Office files that will be organized alongside the client brief.

According to recent reports, “Verra starts from the firm. It indexes everything the firm has produced, distills how each practice does its work, and then lets a consultant point at a new situation and get to most of a finished deliverable in Excel or PowerPoint, before the human analyst starts writing a line,” Nathan stated. “Every claim links to the page or cell it came from. The firm’s engineers and ours then build the specific workflows a team wants.”.

The architecture of Verra’s agents is important, too. The firm built them to run inside client’s clouds after looking at three realities of the professional services business. Consulting firms cannot allow their client’s data to leave their own environments, they will not trust an answer that can’t be verified, and AI will not be used if it forces them to drop the tools and systems they’re already using.

Industry observers note that it’s this approach that has been the secret to Verra’s promising early adoption. Its agents are already being used by two major global consulting firms in Berkeley Research Group and The Keystone Group. In the case of the former, more than 90% of its consultants are using Verra daily, and have generated more than 105,000 files since August while replacing the Tableau platform with a natural-language Snowflake interface. Berkeley calculates that Verra’s platform has helped the company to save around 215,000 analyst hours annually.

Recent findings suggest that “Verra hands our teams finished, sourced first drafts on engagements that used to start from a blank page,” remarked Berkeley’s global managing director of AI solutions, Raghav Vadlamani. “It has changed how quickly we move for clients.”.

Moving forward, Nathan wants to integrate Verra’s AI agents even deeper with the consulting firms it serves, so they can learn how the entire company works and improve its overall efficiency rather than just focusing on one engagement at a time. He also sees an opportunity to move into adjacent sectors such as investment research and accounting. He wants to give customers’ engineers a way to build on Verra’s platform too.

In a recent development, kearny Jackson Partner Sriram Krishnan remarked he used to work in the consulting industry himself and that he’s all too familiar with the rinse-and-repeat process that accompanies each upcoming client engagement. “What stood out to us was how quickly Verra became critical to the firms using it,” he said. “Their consultants adopt it, use it and demand more of it. It’s becoming the memory and operating layer for how consulting firms work.”.

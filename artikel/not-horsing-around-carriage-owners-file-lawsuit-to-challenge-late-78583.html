A recent Fresh York City law banning horse-drawn carriages is unconstitutional, according to a federal lawsuit filed Thursday by a union that represents carriage owners and operators. (Related: Tech Report: How to train a fresh language model from s...)

In a recent development, the city council passed the ban Thursday, putting an end to a 168-year industry that has attracted a healthy tourist business. New York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani intends to sunset the carriages by June 1, 2028, in all five boroughs in the city. The union representing carriage operators and owners argues that their livelihood has been taken away and seeks to have the ban struck down. “The city’s approach can be summarized in 12 words: immediately cripple the owners, ignore property interests and contracts, and maybe compensate,” according to the suit.

Sources indicate that additionally, the newly introduced law stipulates the “humane disposition” of the horses. Animal rights activists have in the past expressed concern over the welfare of the horses. Earlier this year, an 18-year-old tourist died after jumping from a runaway carriage—the first fatality since the carriages were introduced into Central Park in New York City.

“Our city has changed, and now it is time for our laws to change, too,” Mamdani said.

How Lyft and a tennis legend gave older users some independence back

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, many of Lyft’s customers are young urbanites who use the app out of convenience, deeming it easier than hailing a taxi or owning a car. But with Lyft Silver and its accompanying campaign starring octogenarian Billie Jean King, the company managed to position itself as an all-ages mobility tool. Lyft Silver is geared toward people 65 and over, including those who have been unable to renew their driver’s license. It is a simplified version of the regular app, featuring a bigger font size, a help button that connects riders by phone with a live agent, and a matching algorithm that prioritizes easy-to-enter vehicles. Released in May 2025, Lyft Silver got a marketing boost a few months later with the “Silver Is Gold” campaign. Timed to National Grandparents Day, it starred the women’s tennis legend and her “rules of the game” for staying active in your older years. “She’s an iconic person known across generations,” says Terra Carmichael, Lyft’s vice president of communications, who worked on the campaign. Lyft says that the ad overperformed, outpacing its original latest-user acquisition goal by 500%. The company has since joined forces with AARP’s Senior Planet to offer virtual how-to sessions for using the app. “A lot of people equate owning a car or being able to drive with freedom, and it is, for some,” Carmichael says. “But we want people to be able to summon freedom without having to have a driver’s license.”.

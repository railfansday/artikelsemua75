CrowdStrike is buying Zscaler to create a cybersecurity giant built for the era of AI agents

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Two cybersecurity companies are coming together in one of the industry’s biggest ever mergers. On Monday, November 23, CrowdStrike announced that it will purchase Zscaler in an all-stock deal. The transaction values Zscaler at a whopping $43.9 billion, with the company’s shareholders getting 1.135 CrowdStrike common shares (Nasdaq: CRWD) for each Zscaler share (Nasdaq: ZS) they own. “Security used to be about keeping the adversary out. Today, safety is required to let AI in,” said George Kurtz, CEO and founder of CrowdStrike. “The network used to connect people to places. Now it connects agents to data.” CrowdStrike stock falls, Zscaler rises In response to the news, CrowdStrike’s shares fell more than 8% in premarket trading, while Zscaler shares rose over 17%. CrowdStrike expects the deal to expand its total addressable market by $185 billion, with the combined company bringing in over $10 billion in annual recurring revenue. Kurtz will stay on as CEO of the joint company, while Jay Chaudhry, founder and CEO of Zscaler, will lead Zscaler’s business and become a member of CrowdStrike’s board. The deal is expected to close in the second half of 2028, subject to regulatory approvals. It comes amid growing concerns over vulnerable tech infrastructure and a future of bot-fueled cyberattacks. “With Zscaler, we’re building cybersecurity’s operating system: a single control plane to deploy AI, control AI, and enable any agentic application to run safely,” Kurtz added. “We see a path to become the largest AI safety and security company the world has ever seen.” Alongside the purchasing announcement, CrowdStrike and Zscaler both shared their quarterly earnings reports. Kurtz called CrowdStrike’s most recent quarter the company’s best yet. CrowdStrike had record-high cash flow from operations with $665 million. Its revenue rose 28% year-over-year (YOY) to $1.58 billion. Similarly, Zscaler’s quarterly revenue rose 25% YOY to $898.2 million.

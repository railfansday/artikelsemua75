In a recent development, avicena revealed on November 24, 2026 that it had raised more than $100 million in Series C funding to commercialize LightBundle, its microLED optical interconnect platform for AI infrastructure. InterVest led the round, and Kevin Pope will succeed Marco Chisari as CEO.

It has been revealed that the round attracted new and existing investors. Named participants alongside InterVest include Tiger Global, Cerberus Capital Management, SK hynix Ventures, Samsung Catalyst Fund, AMD Ventures, Prosperity7 Ventures, Mitsubishi Chemical’s Diamond Edge Ventures and Hitachi Ventures. InterVest director John Dongwoo Ha will join Avicena’s board.

According to recent reports, pope, listed as CEO and COO, brings technology and operations experience that includes guiding multiple companies to successful exits. He connected the financing to a commercialization schedule: “This funding will help us expand our team, accelerate product development, scale engineering capabilities, and bring our first product to market next year.”. In a recent development, lightBundle is aimed at the growing volume of data that AI systems move among accelerators, memory and switch fabrics. Avicena says copper connections face increasing constraints in reach, routing density and energy efficiency, while memory bandwidth and capacity can limit overall system performance. Industry observers note that the platform replaces lasers with dense microLED transmitter arrays coupled through multicore fiber to silicon photodetectors. Its wide-and-slow design divides bandwidth among many lower-speed channels, which the organization claims reduces the data-rate conversion and complex signal processing required.

In a recent development, avicena intends the architecture to sit close to compute and memory silicon, serving scale-in links within or between chip packages and scale-up links across accelerator systems. The design targets high bandwidth density, low energy consumption, low latency and high reliability.

According to recent reports, several investors connected the round to those hardware constraints. Rene Do, senior director at SK hynix Ventures, described LightBundle as “a promising approach to low-power connectivity for future memory fabrics” and stated the investor was interested in exploring collaboration on next-generation memory interconnects. Executives at Samsung Catalyst Fund and AMD Ventures also cited energy use, bandwidth and scalability as reasons for backing the technology.

The financing follows two earlier engineering milestones. On August 18, 2026, Avicena began shipping LightBundle evaluation kits to customers and partners developing AI infrastructure, moving the platform into customer laboratories.

Recent findings suggest that each kit uses a 335-channel microLED array running at up to 3 Gbps per channel, linked through multicore fiber to a 335-element integrated photodiode array. Avicena rates the resulting link at up to 1 Tbps of aggregate raw throughput. Host-interface boards, diagnostics and a graphical interface let engineers test signal integrity, crosstalk, power efficiency and bit-error-rate performance in their own laboratories.

Recent findings suggest that the evaluation platform is aimed at hyperscalers, accelerator and memory developers, networking companies and system architects. Its intended test configurations include die-to-die and die-to-memory connections, accelerator-to-accelerator links and accelerator-to-switch interconnects.

A September 17, 2026 announcement added a connectorized LightBundle implementation, which Avicena planned to demonstrate at ECOC 2026 on September 21–23, 2026. The design introduces an inline optical interface that can be disconnected and reattached, using an industry-standard MPO form factor and a ferrule optimized for multicore fiber bundles.

Avicena says the detachable interface is meant to simplify assembly, permit separate testing of electronic and optical subsystems, improve field serviceability and support routing between packages, boards, trays and racks. Those practical features matter because evaluation throughput alone does not establish whether a upcoming interconnect can be manufactured and maintained inside customer systems.

The central task is now to turn those evaluation links into a manufacturable product, while customer testing determines whether the platform’s bandwidth, power and reliability goals hold up in deployed AI systems.

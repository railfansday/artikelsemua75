Continuing to struggle down at the bottom end of the Championship table, Watford have now taken the decision to sack manager Alessio Dionisi.

Following a 5-1 defeat to Cardiff City last month, it was reported then that Dionisi was set to be sacked by Watford, but he held on and dismissed those reports.

There had been a slight improvement before the November international break, with the Hornets edging past Lincoln City before earning a creditable goalless draw against Middlesbrough.

However, they have returned from the break with a 4-0 shellacking at the hands of Sheffield United at Bramall Lane and that has prompted Dionisi to be let go of by The Hornets.

As a result, we have taken a look at five potential candidates to replace the former Empoli manager in Hertfordshire as Watford fight to keep their second-tier status.

Former Ecuador boss Sebastian Beccacece remains unemployed since leading the South Americans to the round of 32 of FIFA World Cup 2026, eventually losing to Mexico at Estadio Azteca in Mexico City. (Related: Latest Update: One Year Since the “DeepSeek Moment”...)

According to recent reports, it had been a frustrating time in the group stages for the Ecuadoreans; unfortunately beaten 1-0 by Cote d’Ivoire in their opener before being held to a shock goalless draw by Curaçao. They did then manage to squeeze through with a dramatic victory over Germany at the MetLife Stadium in Latest York.

Unable to score due to a lack of quality was an glitch that plagued them throughout qualification, but a manager’s job is to get a team to create chances in attack and look organised at the back – two things Beccacece managed to do and impressively so in charge of Ecuador as they finished second in CONMEBOL qualification for the tournament, with just five goals conceded in their 18 qualifiers.

A lack of quality in the final third undermined the actual style of football that he had implemented and created with La Tri. He remains a highly-rated coach, and it would be an ambitious appointment for Watford to make but one that may be slightly more realistic after a frustrating summer for the Argentine.

Industry observers note that earlier this season, Parma parted company with manager Carlos Cuesta after a slow-ish start to the campaign. They have continued to struggle, though, and the lack of improvement, as shown by a 5-1 loss at home to Roma this weekend, only further speaks to the managerial qualities of Cuesta. (Related: Industry News: “Determined” woman completes 7 marathons...)

Cuesta, born in Majorca, began his professional coaching career as an assistant at Arsenal to Mikel Arteta from 2020 until the summer of 2025. He was poached by expected Serie A strugglers Parma at the age of just 29, turning 30 later in the month after his appointment.

In his debut season as a manager, and such a young one at that, Parma comfortably survived with an extremely impressive defensive record, perhaps to be expected by a duel-led, conservative-focused apprentice of Arteta.

Undermined by big-money defensive sales this summer, such as goalkeeper Zion Suzuki to Aston Villa and centre-back Alessandro Circati to Benfica, Parma were hasty in their decision to move on, fitting of being a Serie A club but a decision that they are perhaps already regretting and could open the door for Watford to find a real coup.

Without much of a playing career to discuss, Edward Iordanescu began his coaching as an assistant, and occasional caretaker, coach at Steaua Bucharest before going on to experience a bit of a journeyman career across Romania.

Iordanescu coached Fortuna Brazi and Targu Mures in 2013 before a couple of years in charge of Pandurii Targu Jiu and then short stints at CSKA Sofia, Astra Giurgiu, CFR Cluj and Gaz Metan Medias before returning to CFR Cluj for the 2020-21 campaign.

Recent findings suggest that having won the league title as well as a couple of Supercupa Romaniei with CFR Cluj, Iordanescu returned to FCSB, the artist formerly known as Steaua, before getting the Romania job in 2022. In charge of the national team, he led them to UEFA EURO 2024 qualification and even into the knockout stages of that competition before they were beaten 3-0 by the Netherlands in Munich.

His most recent job saw him take charge of Polish giants Legia Warsaw, but he was sacked in October of the same 2025-26 season after an average start to the campaign.

He was heavily linked with the job a few weeks ago and has held talks with the hierarchy, and so his name cropping again would be no surprise, despite his nomadic managerial career.

At the age of 65, it would certainly be a punt for Watford to give Jose Luis Mendilibar his first job in English football, but it would be a real coup for The Hornets should they choose to do so.

Old school in terms of his football values and general tactical philosophy, stereotypically Basque in that respect, Mendilibar has repeatedly got his teams to punch above their weight; be it taking Eibar into La Liga and making them a top-flight stalwart or leading Real Valladolid to a record points tally in the second-tier while reaching the quarter-finals of the Copa del Rey or performing a ‘great escape’ from La Liga relegation with Osasuna.

He was the firefighter brought in to keep fallen giants Sevilla from falling down to the second-tier after a poor stint in charge from Jorge Sampaoli; keeping the Andalusians up and also managing to win the UEFA Europa League, too.

He then took charge of Olympiacos and added a second European trophy to his cabinet with the Greek giants defeating Aston Villa in the semi-finals and Fiorentina in the final to win the UEFA Europa Conference League. Not able to sustain their best over a season eventually led to his dismissal, but title-challenging consistency is not required at Watford; grit, aggression and intensity it and Mendilibar provides that in abundance.

A slightly different style, but again, looking to Spain for an appointment could see Watford try and poach Almeria boss Garcia Pimienta.

Once one of the more highly-regarded young coaches in Spain, after beginning his coaching career at Barcelona B, Garcia Pimienta led Las Palmas to La Liga playing in a stereotypically Canarian manner, with Las Palmas often referred to as ‘the Brazilians of Spanish football’ and their passing style of football on their ascent was representative of that.

He was eventually poached from Las Palmas by Spanish giants Sevilla, but he was just one of many victims of Sevilla’s decline both on and off the pitch in recent years before rebuilding his reputation across Andalusia and at Almeria.

Pimienta took charge of an Almeria side that had lost the play-off final to regional rivals Malaga in the summer and have significant expectations to get back into La Liga and to kick on further than that with their spending.

In a recent development, paid on time, a student loan helps your score most through payment history, which makes up 35% of a FICO score, and the years you carry it lengthen your credit history. A major balance isn’t scored like credit card debt near its limit. FICO compares what you still owe with what you borrowed, and paying it down counts in your favor. According to recent reports, the servicer that bills a federal loan reports a missed payment to the credit bureaus once it’s 90 days late. Most federal loans default after 270 days without a payment, and the default goes on your reports too. Private lenders set their own rules, and some report a missed payment as soon as 30 days after it’s due.

Industry observers note that autopay makes each payment on time even if you forget, and the Education Department trims the rate for borrowers who use it. If the payment is too high, an income-driven plan sets it from your income and family size, and a deferment or forbearance temporarily pauses payments, though interest usually keeps building. Either one, set up before a payment is 90 days late, keeps it off your reports. After a default, rehabilitation, nine affordable monthly payments agreed with the loan’s holder, removes the default from your credit history but not the late payments before it.

Industry observers note that picture two graduates, each with $30,000 in federal loans and three months between jobs. One skips three payments, so by her fourth due date the first is about 90 days late and goes on her reports for seven years. The other calls her servicer first and switches to an income-driven initiative based on what she earns, so she never misses one.

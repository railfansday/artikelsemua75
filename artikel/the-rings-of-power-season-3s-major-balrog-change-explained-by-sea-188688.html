Warning: SPOILERS lie ahead for The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power season 3, episode 7, "Forging of the One Ring"!EXCLUSIVE: The Balrog is back with a brand-fresh bag in The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power season 3.

Sources indicate that in J.R.R. Tolkien's source material, the fire-wielding demonic beings were former Maiar transformed into their menacing state by the villainous Morgoth, subsequently hiding in some of Middle-earth's deepest regions, namely Moria and the Misty Mountains. Staying true to the first 1954 Lord of the Rings book, the Balrog was featured in both the Ralph Bakshi and Peter Jackson films, in which Gandalf sacrificed himself to fight it while the rest of the Fellowship escaped.

Now, the Balrog is back for The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power season 3's most-anticipated episode, "Forging of the One Ring." In the upcoming season's seventh episode, Charlie Vickers' Sauron visits the being, residing in the mountains near Moria, in an effort to better understand how to harness the energy and fire of Mount Doom in order to finally concoct the powerful relic.

In honor of the episode's release, ScreenRant's Grant Hermanns interviewed Stefan Schwartz to discuss The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power season 3, episode 7. When asked about the creation of the Balrog for the series and finding the show's own take on the creature, the director acknowledged that it was "tricky trying to imbue the Balrog with emotion and character," especially as they still strove to stay true to the general design from Jackson's movies, itself inspired by John Howe's illustrations.

Sources indicate that in order to do so, the Rings of Power production team made a unique change to the character by "bringing in the fire and the color to express mood and emotion," with the Balrog's more tame moments featuring a blue-ish fire while his more intense are covered in traditionally orange flames. Though this required a fair amount of work on the visual effects side of things, Schwartz revealed that there was a surprisingly practical core to the entire scene's production:.

In a recent development, stefan Schwartz: That was a long chat with Jason [Smith], our visual effects person, and JD and Patrick about how those moments would work, and then working with my DOP to bring a light source that was the Balrog, and to change the color of that light source with the emotion. So, we had a big crane with a big lamp that could change different colors, and it moved where the Balrog went, so that Charlie had something to watch. Because obviously, we were on a big green screen set, and didn't have much actual stuff to react against. So, I thought it only fair if Charlie had something to react against. Another major change made to the Balrog in The Rings of Power season 3 is giving the character a speaking voice, the first time one has ever spoken in any Lord of the Rings medium, played by fan-favorite Simon Pegg. Reflecting on the creation of the Balrog's voice and finding a personality beyond just a hulking foe, Schwartz recalled having "chatted and chatted" with Pegg on set during production of the episode 7 scene, with the pair diving into the character's Middle-earth backstory:.

Stefan Schwartz: We chatted about the history of the Balrog as a Maiar, and Sauron as a Maiar, and went a bit deep into that. And then talked about how Sauron turned the conversation around to, in the end, get what he needs.

In a recent development, the introduction of the Balrog into The Rings of Power season 3 is itself a major change in the Lord of the Rings' overall timeline. In Tolkien's original work, it was never directly stated that Sauron and any version of the Balrog, let alone the one residing in Moria, since dubbed Durian's Bane, had interactions with one another.

Moreover, while Sauron's orcs acting as spies in the area are believed to have seen the demon and reported it to the Dark Lord, the absence of Balrogs from the surface of Middle-earth left them unable to properly convey what it was to their master, thus preventing him from interfering. However, given Sauron is also a Maiar, and therefore of equal level to the entity, Sauron would not have been able to control or enlist it for his war.

One of the other notable changes the Balrog's introduction brings to Tolkien's Lord of the Rings lore is that Sauron refers to it as an "old friend," confirming that the pair have a past relationship of some kind. With The Rings of Power frequently utilizing flashbacks to flesh out some of Sauron's backstory and the wider lore of Middle-earth, there's a possibility the now-greenlit season 4 could better explore this.

According to recent reports, the other major question surrounding the Balrog's future in The Lord of the Rings: The Rings of Power season 3 is whether it will become more involved in Sauron's war to conquer the world. Though technically on the same power level as one another, that there is some level of respect between the pair could see the effective forging of the One Ring see Durin's Bane willing to rise up and fight the various forces fighting back against the Dark Lord.

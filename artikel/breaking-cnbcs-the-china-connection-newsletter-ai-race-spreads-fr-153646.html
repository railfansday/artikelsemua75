Recent findings suggest that hi, this is Evelyn, writing to you from Beijing. Welcome to the latest edition of The China Connection — a snapshot of what I'm seeing and hearing from local businesses.

I'm just back from APEC in Shenzhen, where I saw the leaders of the U.S., China and Russia gather along with other member economies. Beneath the fanfare, what's changed on the tech front in what's often dubbed China's Silicon Valley?

It only took a few months for a humanoid robot to get better at picking up household items. (Related: How to Build a Healthcare Robot from Simulation to Depl...)

Sources indicate that even after a human knocked a box and toys away from their original spots, a LimX robot quickly located them and moved them to pre-set locations. While still far from perfect, the smoothness of the demo contrasted with the robot's more stilted movements I'd seen early this summer — and inability then to react to rapid changes in tasks.

When we caught up in Shenzhen last week, CFO Jesse Liu declined to comment on LimX's IPO prepares. But he told me that his conversations around the APEC CEO Summit revealed an excitement around a fresh development for humanoids: growing demand for robot chips that can run smarter large language models locally, instead of on the cloud. (Related: Latest Update: GTA 6 and Ocarina of Time remake caught ...)

"A lot of chipmakers are now happy about the latest developments in LLMs because they think robotic OEMs will actually begin to integrate more storage, and reasoning, and compute, and inference infrastructure on the edge, or locally inside the robot body," Liu noted, referring to original equipment manufacturers.

"They do see robots moving from hundreds of units to thousands of units, to tens of thousands of units, beginning to enter that kind of automotive quantity," he noted, pointing to chip suppliers such as Horizon Robotics.

New AI models, particularly GPT-6 Astra, have shown not just improved reasoning, but better control of robots.

Industry observers note that that puts more of the onus on hardware makers, for robots or other devices, to create practical applications. LimX mentioned it's working with business partners in over 30 markets, including Switzerland where the robots serve as power plant inspectors in rural areas.

Sources indicate that the key for powering AI's transition into the physical world is on-device chips and more powerful batteries. That means Nvidia's most expensive, cutting-edge chips won't be the only ones powering generative AI models.

So far, even in basic consumer applications such as smartphones, AI isn't transformative enough to be a top-three selling point for mass-market people, Thomas Bai, AI product expert at smartphone firm Honor, told me in Shenzhen.

He expects that in the next few years, as smartphone chips are able to run more powerful AI models, the "super agent" tech can become better at completing tasks on its own, and anticipating a user's needs — such as ordering coffee.

It has been revealed that counterpoint on Nov. 19 forecast that in the retail industry, "edge AI" revenue will surge sixfold by 2032, with roughly half of revenue coming from retail robots that clean and check inventory — often with the help of smaller AI models that can run on locally embedded chips.

But more capable, on-device AI also requires more battery power, Honor's Bai pointed out.

Honor's X70 Android smartphone, which debuted last summer, boasts a battery with 8,300 milliampere-hours for a total device weight of just under 200 grams. That compares with 5,000 mAH for the 214-gram flagship Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra, and roughly 5,400 mAH for this year's 249-gram Apple iPhone 18 Pro Max, according to GSM Arena.

LimX's Oli humanoid comes with 9,500 mAH battery capacity, lasting the robot about 1.5 hours.

Such AI applications, however, remain sensitive to the memory-chip shortage weighing on the industry.

The X70 was the best-selling Android phone in the entry to mid-tier range, stated Shenzhen-based Counterpoint analyst Shiwen Ma.

While Ma remarked penetration of smartphones with generative AI features is higher in China at 55% versus 38% overseas, the offerings are still not essential to people, while rising memory-chip costs make it harder for manufacturers to bring AI-powered devices to the mass market.

Geely-backed automotive tech tech giant EcarX's CFO Dylan Jeng told me earlier this month he expects the memory price surge to persist through 2027, and even the first half of 2028.

The chipmaker that can narrow the gap for on-device AI needs could find its moment soon.

Bessent tells CNBC Nvidia chip export policy is unchanged.

Industry observers note that U.S. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent spoke exclusively on CNBC's "Squawk on the Street." President Donald Trump on Friday stated he and Chinese leader Xi Jinping discussed their shared affinity for former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang during their talks in China this week.

How China is using summits to expand its global influence.

For a second year in a row, Xi has brought Russian President Vladimir Putin together with another major world leader on mainland Chinese shores. But U.S.-China trade, Taiwan and tech tensions remain unresolved as Trump returned from Asia.

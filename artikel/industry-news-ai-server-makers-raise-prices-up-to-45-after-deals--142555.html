Industry News: AI server makers raise prices up to 45% after deals are signed, FT reports

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, dell, HPE, Lenovo and Supermicro are raising prices for Nvidia-powered AI servers months after buyers signed their contracts. Nikou Asgari reported the price rises for the Financial Times. The higher prices come when the equipment is ready to ship, according to people familiar with the matter.

“It is disconcerting when you get a 25, 30, 45 per cent [price increase],” one of the people told the FT. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

According to recent reports, GPU servers cost at least 20% to 30% more, one person said. CPU nodes, where memory makes up much of the cost, have tripled in price or more.

Sources indicate that large trading firms are among the buyers hit, including Jane Street, Optiver and Hudson River Trading, the FT reported. After the massive cloud providers, they are some of the largest buyers of computing equipment, and they are building their own data centres for trading research. Jane Street has also signed with CoreWeave for cloud computing. (Related: My Journey to a serverless transformers pipeline on Goo...)

Recent findings suggest that most are agreeing to the new prices because they need the equipment. Contracts now carry an indicative price that can rise sharply by delivery, another person told the FT.

In a recent development, “We’ve never mentioned no,” the person said. “At the end of the day, the compute is valuable to us and we’ll pay for it.”.

Industry observers note that suppliers are largely passing on memory costs, the FT reported. Memory prices have risen more than 400% since the start of 2024, according to JPMorgan research cited by the paper. SK Hynix has said the memory shortage could last until 2030.

Recent findings suggest that one fight has reached court. In June, British trading firm XTX Markets sued Dell in Ireland, according to court filings seen by the FT. The two had agreed a fixed price of $549m for servers, and XTX paid an extra $5m for price certainty.

In a recent development, dell later told XTX it would have to pay at least $62m to $70m more, the filings say. Dell noted it could not absorb the rising memory costs, according to XTX. The case is ongoing.

HPE and Supermicro declined to comment to the FT. Dell and Lenovo did not respond. Supermicro raised its revenue forecast by $4bn in October on AI demand.

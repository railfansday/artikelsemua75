Growing requirements for compact, high-efficiency, and electrically isolated power supplies are further supporting DC-DC converter adoption, particularly in applications where installation space, equipment weight, and thermal performance are critical design considerations. "By application, power distribution systems to lead market during forecast period".

According to recent reports, the power distribution systems segment is projected to account for the largest share of the aerospace & defense power converters market during the forecast period. Aircraft, military vehicles, and naval platforms require reliable conversion and distribution of electrical power across avionics, flight controls, communication equipment, sensors, and mission-critical electronic systems. Increasing integration of electrically operated subsystems and advanced mission equipment is creating demand for converters capable of supplying regulated power at different voltage levels within platform electrical networks. The transition toward higher-voltage architectures, distributed electrical systems, and electronically controlled equipment is further increasing the importance of power conversion efficiency, load management, and system-level reliability.

Increasing emphasis on reducing wiring weight, improving fault protection, and accommodating higher electrical loads is expected to support demand across this application segment.

"Asia Pacific to register the fastest growth during forecast period".

Asia Pacific is projected to register the highest CAGR in the aerospace & defense power converters industry during the forecast period, supported by commercial aviation expansion, indigenous aerospace manufacturing, and increasing investment in military modernization. China, India, Japan, and South Korea are pursuing aircraft development, advanced surveillance, and defense electronics programs that require reliable electrical power management and conversion technologies. In July 2026, India's Defence Research and Development Organisation (DRDO) signed contracts with industry partners for its Airborne Early Warning and Control (AEW&C) Mk-II program, covering the modification of six Airbus A321 aircraft and the development and integration of mission systems. Japan is also progressing its next-generation fighter aircraft development through the Global Combat Air Programme (GCAP), with a major development contract concluded in July 2026. Meanwhile, South Korea's KF-21 fighter program received its final combat suitability approval in May 2026. These programs demonstrate continued regional investment in advanced aerospace platforms and mission electronics, creating potential opportunities for power conversion suppliers as electrical system requirements are finalized.

Supplier investments in regional manufacturing and aftermarket capabilities are also strengthening Asia Pacific's aerospace electrical systems ecosystem. In December 2025, Safran Electrical & Power inaugurated a facility at Singapore's Seletar Aerospace Park dedicated to the production and maintenance of aircraft electrical equipment, including power conversion and distribution systems. The facility supports commercial aircraft operators and aerospace manufacturers operating across the region. Such investments improve regional manufacturing and service capabilities and support the long-term requirements of aircraft electrical systems. Increasing aircraft fleet activity, defense platform development, and integration of advanced avionics are expected to create opportunities for DC-DC converters, AC-DC power supplies, transformer rectifier units (TRUs), and other specialized conversion equipment. However, the extent of regional market growth will depend on aircraft production, procurement schedules, platform electrical architectures, and suppliers' participation in qualified aerospace and defense programs.

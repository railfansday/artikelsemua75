The odds look to be in favor of the Hunger Games franchise, as it is likely to gobble up a box office debut on the level of its impressive hits from more than a decade ago.

Lionsgate releases The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping in 3,900 North American theaters on Friday, with the studio projecting it to gross north of $80 million domestically. However, sources expect that the film will actually open much higher, likely surpassing $100 million and potentially reaching upwards of $130 million. Adapting author Suzanne Collins’ prequel novel that was published last year, director Francis Lawrence‘s movie carries a production budget of roughly $150 million as it brings latest life to the franchise that introduced with the initial Jennifer Lawrence-led The Hunger Games in 2012. In the fresh film, Joseph Zada plays Haymitch Abernathy, whom Woody Harrelson portrayed in the character’s later years for the first four movies. The expansive cast of Sunrise on the Reaping includes Mckenna Grace, Whitney Peak, Jesse Plemons, Ralph Fiennes, Glenn Close, Elle Fanning, Kieran Culkin, Kelvin Harrison Jr., Billy Porter, Ben Wang, Maya Hawke and Iris Apatow.

Industry observers note that sunrise on the Reaping takes place in Panem on the morning of the reaping for the 50th Hunger Games, 24 years before the events in The Hunger Games, the initial novel that published in 2008. The franchise’s first five movies have surpassed $3.3 billion at the worldwide box office, with the initial four films led by Lawrence as Katniss, Josh Hutcherson as Peeta and Liam Hemsworth as Gale. It has been revealed that with its box office opening, the latest movie is expected to mark a significant uptick over that of 2023’s The Hunger Games: The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes. That prequel film featured Tom Blyth as the young Coriolanus Snow — the villainous character played by Fiennes in Sunrise on the Reaping — alongside Rachel Zegler and Hunter Schafer. The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes opened to a disappointing $44 million domestically before ending its run with $166.4 million domestically and a global sum of $361.8 million.

Sunrise on the Reaping should open with a haul much closer to the debuts for the first four movies that starred Lawrence. After 2012’s initial The Hunger Games opened to $152.5 million domestically and was followed by 2013’s The Hunger Games: Catching Fire with an even stronger $158 million, the next two films started a bit softer. In 2014, The Hunger Games: Mockingjay – Part 1 debuted with $121.8 million domestically, while the second film’s debut fell to $102.6 million in North America.

The critical response for Sunrise on the Reaping has delivered a 67 Rotten Tomatoes score from critics, which puts it behind the ratings for the franchise’s first four movies but just above the 64 percent approval for Songbirds & Snakes. In his THR review, chief film critic David Rooney wrote that the new film “delivers on emotion and brutal spectacle.”.

Industry observers note that another entertainment option this weekend comes with Thursday’s highly anticipated release of the video game Grand Theft Auto VI, arriving 13 years after the launch for the beloved Rockstar Games franchise’s previous installment. GTA 6 has already surpassed $1.84 billion in sales, meaning that it has made more money that Christopher Nolan’s The Odyssey, which hit theaters over the summer and became Universal’s highest-grossing title ever globally. As for whether the game could impact box office numbers, it’s likely that both Hunger Games and GTA will manage to thrive.

“I can certainly see GTA 6 keeping some people on the couch Friday night, and I’d imagine there is some crossover between folks who can’t wait to play the game and those who can’t wait to see the movie,” Rentrak’s Paul Dergarabedian tells THR. “But Friday night is just one night, and there’s a whole weekend of potential moviegoing. I think people who are excited about both will find the bandwidth for the game and the movie, so I don’t see GTA 6 taking much of a bite out of the Hunger Games opening.”.

Sources indicate that among the other films looking to draw moviegoers this weekend is Amazon MGM Studios’ I Play Rocky, as director Peter Farrelly’s origin story about 1976’s Oscar-winning boxing drama Rocky goes wide after two weekends in limited release. Anthony Ippolito stars as a young Sylvester Stallone in the project that Rooney describes as a “massive-hearted movie about moviemaking, if not an artful one.”.

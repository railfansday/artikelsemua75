Sources indicate that levenza is making its personal AI agents available globally to enterprise customers and claims it has acquired Fluid Inference, an applied AI lab that develops speech software. The launch expands access to tools for coordinating meetings, managing inboxes and tracking administrative work. The acquisition adds a voice interface, letting employees call an agent to review their schedule or ask it to rearrange meetings.

The San Francisco startup says it completed the acquisition in September and has already integrated voice into its platform. Fluid Inference founder Brandon Weng joined Levenza as head of engineering. The companies did not disclose financial terms in the announcement. According to recent reports, rather than asking employees to move their calendars and communications into another system, Levenza places its agents in tools employees already use, including Slack, Microsoft Teams and Outlook. Employees can ask for a meeting to be arranged across time zones; the agent can check availability, coordinate with assistants and reserve a room. The platform also drafts email replies and logs time. Travel booking and expense management are on the company’s 2027 roadmap, distinct from today’s ability to coordinate travel-related information. Sources indicate that levenza’s published product description says the system tracks preferences and constraints over time and can start tasks in response to changes in email and calendars. That matters because role, urgency and prior commitments can change what looks like an available slot. A free hour on a calendar doesn’t necessarily mean the meeting belongs there. The company says its software assesses proposed actions to decide when to ask for confirmation and lets administrators define controls across the organization.

Industry observers note that fluid Inference’s public FluidAudio project is a Swift toolkit for tasks such as turning speech into text, identifying when someone is speaking and separating different speakers in a recording. Its documentation describes models that can run on supported Apple devices using Core ML. That makes it useful for developers building low-latency audio features, but the public repository does not establish where Levenza’s own enterprise voice service processes audio.

Industry observers note that for Levenza, the practical addition is another way for people to issue requests. The tech giant gives the example of calling an agent for a briefing, dealing with a time-sensitive email or changing a meeting without opening Outlook. The voice interface sits on top of tools that can act, so reliability involves more than transcription accuracy. Before an agent moves a meeting or responds to a client, it needs to know which attendee or conversation the employee meant and when to stop for confirmation.

Levenza states it has signed more than 300 customers, including Oliver Wyman, Goodwin and Hg Capital, without raising outside capital. It also reports 170% net revenue retention and 99% gross revenue retention. The first measure accounts for expansions as well as losses among existing customers, while gross retention excludes expansion. Levenza did not specify the measurement period in the announcement, so the figures are best read as firm-reported indicators rather than as comparisons with other providers.

Sources indicate that levenza’s security documentation lists workspace audit logs, enterprise sign-in and administrative controls. Buyers should ask what the logs capture for specific actions, such as rescheduling a meeting or sending an email, and whether the voice interface uses the same controls. The public security page describes workspace protection in general and does not answer those voice-specific questions. The value of agents like these will be clearest in workflows that require several rounds of coordination. An evaluation could compare how long it takes to settle a cross-time-zone meeting before and after deployment and how often a person must step in. Teams should also distinguish a suggestion that employees can ignore from a change the agent makes on their behalf. A tool may reliably draft follow-ups and still need tighter controls before it sends them, especially when an ambiguous request could reach the wrong person. Those checks are more useful in practice than measuring how many tasks the software starts.

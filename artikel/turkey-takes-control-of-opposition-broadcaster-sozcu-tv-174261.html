Turkey takes control of opposition broadcaster Sozcu TV

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, another key opposition media outlet in Turkey has been placed under state control. On Tuesday morning, authorities raided the offices of the television broadcaster Sozcu TV and the news portal Sozcu.com.tr, and arrested several executives.

Industry observers note that A trustee has also been appointed: The state deposit protection fund, TMSF, which usually administers the assets of insolvent companies, has been put in charge. Sozcu was one of the last big media companies in Turkey to regularly report critically on the government of President Recep Tayyip Erdogan. Sources indicate that A large police contingent raided the broadcaster's headquarters in Istanbul, with counterterrorism officers searching the offices. According to Sozcu, its chairwoman, Funda Tuncer Sidali, and Asim Akgul, the former chief executive of the Sozcu group, were among those arrested. Recent findings suggest that the broadcaster did not initially announce the appointment of the trustee on its digital channels. It did call on its audience to follow it under a new account. The leader of the main opposition party, Ozgur Ozel, declared on X: "You will never be able to prevent this nation's voice from rising, its rebellion from being heard, its cry from echoing.".

The Istanbul public prosecutor's office has confirmed the arrangements. It justified the investigation with claims of a suspected connection between the owner of Sozcu, Burak Akhay, and the Gülen movement. The Turkish government blames this movement, which was once allied to Erdogan, for the attempted coup in 2016, and has designated it a terrorist organization.

Industry observers note that according to the investigators, Akbay is mentioned to have used foreign bank accounts to finance two companies involved in media activities on behalf of the Gülen movement. They claim there is strong suspicion that he was able, in this way, to hold a leadership position in the organization.

Industry observers note that it is not latest for the Turkish state to take over media organizations by appointing trustees. Many media companies accused of being close to the Gülen movement were brought under state control in this way after the attempted coup in 2016. What is new is that for about a year now this is also happening to media seen as pro-opposition.

Recent findings suggest that sozcu is not the first opposition broadcaster to be targeted by the authorities. The TELEI TV channel was searched on October 24, 2025. Its editor-in-chief, Merdan Yanardag, was arrested on charges of espionage and remains in custody. The offices of Halk TV were raided on October 14 this year. There, too, a trustee was put in charge.

Recent findings suggest that in the space of just over a year, three of the most key opposition television channels have been brought under state control. Observers see this as another step toward an increasingly authoritarian and politically isolated system.

Berk Esen, a political scientist with Sabanci University in Istanbul, sees these measures as a significant weakening of the opposition in the public domain.

Esen told DW that the government may first have acted behind the scenes and tried to bring Sozcu to heel politically. When this didn't succeed, it invoked the judiciary. Esen claims Turkey is increasingly moving toward an authoritarian system. The attempt to prevent opposition organizations and media from gaining strength and influence is part of this.

Esen describes the opposition's success in local government elections in March 2024 as a political turning point. Since then, he indicates, the government has seen its chances of winning political competitions at the ballot box receding. "It's become harder for the AKP to win elections," says Esen. "That's why it's attacking the opposition and the free media both at once.".

Several opposition mayors have also been arrested in recent months and replaced by trustees. They include the mayor of Istanbul and presidential candidate Ekrem Imamoglu, who has been in detention since his arrest in March last year. He is regarded as one of Erdogan's most promising challengers.

The Turkish Journalists' Association (TGC) spoke of a "black winter for press freedom," and announced its solidarity with all those affected. It described the recent developments as a serious attack on press freedom. The fact that a trustee had been appointed, first at Halk TV and now at Sozcu, revealed how the media climate in Turkey has deteriorated, it mentioned.

Industry observers note that "These days, too, shall pass," the TGC declared. "When that day comes, those journalists who seek the truth today will be remembered, as will the decisions that prevent the public from hearing that truth.".

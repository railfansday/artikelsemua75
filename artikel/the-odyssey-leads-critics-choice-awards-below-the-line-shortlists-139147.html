Universal’s “The Odyssey” leads the shortlists for the 32nd Critics Choice Awards‘ below-the-line categories with 11 mentions.

According to recent reports, the Homer epic, the first feature shot entirely on Imax film cameras, landed on all 11 artisan lists, from Hoyte van Hoytema’s cinematography and Ellen Mirojnick’s costumes to John Papsidera’s casting. Its only absence came in young actor/actress, the lone performance category voted on by the committee. Related Stories Pink Announces 2027 Sphere Residency in Las Vegas.

Sources indicate that the 12 shortlists run from cinematography and editing to production design and score, along with casting and ensemble, sound and stunt design, the three categories the CCA added last season. They arrive just ahead of nomination voting, which begins on Thanksgiving Day, when ballots go out to the organization’s more than 500 members. Each artisan category will yield six nominees, and best picture will recognize 10 films. The full membership also votes in every other major category, including acting, screenplay, animated feature and international feature.

According to recent reports, warner Bros.’ sci-fi conclusion “Dune: Part Three,” which opens Dec. 18, and Netflix’s Spanish-language epic “La Bola Negra” follow with eight apiece. For the latter, Gris Jordana’s cinematography, Ana López Cobos’ costumes and Roger Bellés and Judit Ferrer’s production design all made the cut. So did composer and songwriter Raül Refree, for the score and for co-writing “La Nieve” with Guitarricadelafuente, who also stars. Sources indicate that GKids’ monster movie “Godzilla Minus Zero” and Laika’s stop-motion “Wildwood” tied with seven. At the same time, Amazon MGM Studios’ “Project Hail Mary” and Focus Features’ “Werwulf” each picked up six. (Related: The Partnership: Amazon SageMaker and Hugging Face...)

Sources indicate that “Godzilla Minus Zero” picks up where its predecessor left off. The sequel, billed as the first Japanese production filmed for Imax, landed praise for production design, editing, sound, visual effects, stunt design and score by Naoki Sato and for young actress Sae Nagatani.

Recent findings suggest that “Wildwood,” Laika’s first feature since 2019’s “Missing Link,” is the only animated film on the cinematography list, thanks to Caleb Deschanel, a six-time Oscar nominee still looking for his first win. Decemberists frontman Colin Meloy, who wrote the novels the film adapts, landed on the song shortlist for “The Better Part of Bravery.”.

Some names popped up more than once.

Richard King appears in sound for both “Digger” and “Dune: Part Three.” Paul Lambert and Mag Sarnowska do the same in visual effects for “Dune: Part Three” and “Project Hail Mary.” Ludwig Göransson is on the score and song lists for “The Odyssey,” and Jack Antonoff has two songs in contention.“Burial” from A24’s “Mother Mary” and the other “I Knew It, I Knew You,” written with Taylor Swift for Pixar’s “Toy Story 5.”.

The entire song list is as star-studded as ever.

Lady Gaga and Bruno Mars share credit on “Runway” from “The Devil Wears Prada 2,” alongside Phoebe Bridgers for “Primetime” and Jesse Eisenberg, who wrote “This City May Have Chewed Me Up (But It Hasn’t Spit Me Out)” for “The Debut.”.

“Behemoth!” makes its own kind of history in score. Tony Gilroy’s love letter to Los Angeles studio musicians has nine credited composers on a single entry, including Michael Abels, Michael Giacchino, James Newton Howard and Alan Silvestri. Lukas Frank, one of the nine, also lands in song for “Your God Is Sleeping” and Alexa Swinton makes the young actress list.

“Obsession,” the horror breakout from YouTube filmmaker Curry Barker, shows up three times, including editing, which Barker handled himself. The animated film “The Violinist” lands in score without a U.S. distributor. And Paramount’s “Jackass: Best and Last” earned one of the stunt design slots, alongside the Indian epic “Ramayana,” the video game adaptation “Resident Evil” and the superhero blockbuster “Spider-Man: Brand New Day.”.

Elsewhere, Boots Riley’s “I Love Boosters” picked up two mentions, in production design and costumes. The costume list also includes Mubi’s “Coward,” alongside “I Play Rocky,” “Mother Mary” and “Wildwood.”“Coyote vs. Acme,” the finished film Warner Bros. shelved in 2023 before Ketchup Entertainment acquired it, picked up three mentions: editing, visual effects and Steven Price’s score.

Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim’s A24 documentary “You Can See Everything,” about Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes, also landed three, in cinematography, editing and score. Another nonfiction title, “Once Upon a Time in Harlem,” also is in one of the editing spots.

It has been revealed that bassam Tariq’s “Your Mother Your Mother Your Mother,” starring Mahershala Ali, from Amazon MGM Studios, collected three mentions as well, for cinematography, editing and stunt design. Another Amazon MGM title, “Is God Is,” picked up a deserved mention in hair and makeup.

In all, 59 films fill the 144 available slots across 12 lists.

Sources indicate that last season, the CCA’s first with official artisan shortlists, Ryan Coogler’s “Sinners” led with 13 mentions before topping the nominations with 17. This year’s nominations will be confirmed Dec. 4. Janelle James hosts the ceremony, which airs live Jan. 3 from the Barker Hangar in Santa Monica on E! and USA Network.

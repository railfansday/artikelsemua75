Recent findings suggest that between heading home for the holidays and booking a cold-weather getaway, we’re entering a busy season of travel. Recent news events notwithstanding, there’s always some stressor that accompanies flying, whether it’s too-crammed overhead bins or airline delays. Your suitcase, however, shouldn’t be on the list—and if yours has seen better days, give yourself one less thing to worry about by upgrading to a luggage set that can handle the trip.

While the right luggage won’t shorten the security line, it can make everything around it feel a lot easier (and, at the very least, look a lot better). Choose wisely, and you’ll have a streamlined duo that clears airline size requirements and is durable enough to handle any wear and tear from baggage handlers. Plus, with the holidays in mind, a suitcase set is also a great investment gift for the frequent flyer in your life; it’s just down to choosing the right one. Recent findings suggest that though Rimowa has been making luggage in Cologne, Germany, since 1898, its grooved aluminum case may be the most recognizable out of all the sets. The brand’s signature ridges first appeared in 1950, and the design has barely changed since.

Partially due to the iconic design, partially due to its spacious storage, it has also become the fashion set’s go-to baggage for traveling. While Rimowa doesn’t officially offer sets, you can pair the Original Cabin, which fits most overhead bins, with the Original Check-In, which is massive enough for two weeks away, for $3,425 total. And with a lifetime guarantee, you’ll be passing these luggage pieces down for years.

Sources indicate that A Vogue editor notes: “My first big-girl purchase was a three-piece Rimowa luggage set in green. At the time, I was traveling almost weekly and needed a reliable set that would take me away for up to three weeks at a time. More times than I can count, this set has come in handy through my travels; recently, I’ve been eyeing the trunk size to add to my collection.” —Minty Mellon, shopping market editor.

Swedish brothers Niklas and Mattis Oppermann started Carl Friedrik in London as a leather goods label before making luggage in 2019. (If the brand’s carry-on looks familiar, it’s because they’ve made appearances on both Succession and The White Lotus.) The Luggage Set Hybrid pairs the Carry-on with the Check-in, at $1,425 for the two, both trimmed in Italian-made leather. Each case has a polycarbonate shell set into an aluminum frame, and the Japanese Hinomoto wheels roll silky across the terminal floor. The effect is minimal, quiet, and built to last.

It has been revealed that A Vogue editor states: “Carl Friedrik’s carry-on is the lightest, most stylish cabin bag I’ve ever owned. It makes economy feel glamorous and makes me feel like I could be the kind of person who travels with a capsule wardrobe. The wheels glide smoothly and the handle never sticks—a surprising rarity—and the exterior is thus far dent-free. I’m working my way up to the full set.” —Elly Leavitt, lifestyle shopping editor.

It has been revealed that away is pretty ubiquitous at the airport these days. But one of the things that really sets it apart? Its color range. On top of its core lineup, Away releases latest shades seemingly every few months, from glossy pastels to a new run of aluminum colors that debuted this fall. To create your own set, pair the Bigger Carry-On (great for a weeklong trip for capsule wardrobe packers; perhaps better-suited to a long weekend for over-packers) with the Large in whatever color you gravitate toward most. The brand’s checked bag is also designed to nest its carry-on inside, so the pair takes up one spot in your closet instead of two.

A Vogue editor says: “When I stopped using hand-me-down suitcases from my parents, I turned to Away for their reliably cute luggage and affordable price point. I've since become devoted to their Bigger Carry-On, which has proven to be as practical as it is chic. It glides seamlessly from terminal to terminal and never feels like a pain to wheel around—a non-negotiable for someone like me who refuses to check bags.” —Fred Sahai, associate commerce producer.

It has been revealed that tumi has long been the go-to for frequent flyers, and its 19 Degree collection is the one to invest in. Pair the International Expandable Carry-On, sized to clear stricter overseas carry-on limits, with the Extended Trip Expandable Packing Case, which has a hanger bracket that keeps select clothes from wrinkling. Both zip open for extra room on the way home, and both come with the Tumi Tracer, so if a bag goes missing, the brand will help track it down.

It has been revealed that A Vogue editor says: “The wheels on this carry-on are so smooth—a crucial feature, since I tend to push the suitcase around with a giant tote balanced on top. I have overpacking tendencies (I'm working on them), so I appreciate the solid closure: two firm clicks, and everything's locked in place. It’s like a rolling safe.” —Talia Abbas, shopping director.

Dimensions: Carry On: 22” x 14” x 9”, Medium: 27” x 18” x 12”, Large: 32” x 22” x 14” Colors available: 8 Materials: Polycarbonate and polyester.

Antler has been making suitcases in England since 1914, way before wheels were even standard on suitcases. The Single Stripe set covers every trip with three sizes (an expandable carry-on, a medium, and a large), available in a tight edit of colors and a simple design that just has a single raised stripe down the front. (The stripe is made to hold one of the brand’s luggage straps, which come in fun shades, so you can make the set your own and spot it on the carousel easily.) Each case is lined in recycled fabric with packing straps and a divider, which keeps a two-week haul from turning into a jumbled mess. Best of all, at $537 for all three, it’s a more accessible option for the frequent flyer.

Dimensions: Carry-On: 22.4” x 15.0 “ x 9.8”, Trunk: 16.3” x 30.7” x 14.2”, Tote: 23.62” x 14.57” x 7.09” Colors available: 1 Materials: Recycled polycarbonate, recycled polyester, and aluminum.

Paravel is officially back—and its Voyager Plus Travel Set is the closest you’ll get to stocking up for a trip in one fell swoop. Founders Indré Rockefeller and Andy Krantz, who met working at the Spanish fashion brand Delpozo, built the line around the New England coast, and the palette reflects that in simple shades of cream, navy, and Nantucket red. The shell is sturdy, made with recycled materials and aluminum-reinforced corners; plus a 10-year warranty, just in case. The bundle also includes four packing cubes, a canvas tote that counts as your personal item, and a luggage tag you can monogram, so there’s nothing left to buy before takeoff.

Dimensions: Carry-on: 21.5” x 15” x 8.5", Checked: 26” x 18.75” x 11” Colors available: 5 Materials: German polycarbonate and nylon.

Australian brand July has built a following on clean design and thoughtful details, and its Classic Checked Set may be its best yet. The Carry-on Trunk and Checked Trunk swap zippers for aluminum latches that snap shut, so there’s no fighting a stuck zipper in the wee hours before takeoff. The carry-on even comes with a battery that pops out to charge your phone at the gate. If you buy the bundle, you’ll have your pick from five colors; choose the earthy matte moss green to hides scuffs. At $975 for the pair, it’s a great deal.

It has been revealed that A Vogue editor says: “I liked my July carry-on so much it’s now in my mother’s possession. She gave it a roll through the airport once, and decided it was such a smooth, easy glide that she had to have it—ever the benevolent daughter, I sent her home with it. I pack a lot, and found that it can carry all my essentials for up to four days or so. Packing cubes keep everything organized in the zippered compartment, while shoes and toiletry bags go on the other, held safely in place by the Y-strap compression. Overall, it’s a great balance of utility and style.” —Kiana Murden, beauty shopping editor.

Recent findings suggest that if there’s one suitcase brand that feels like an instant family heirloom, it’s Globe-Trotter. The British label, founded in 1897, still makes each case by hand using a material made from layers of paper that’ll hold up for decades. It’s no wonder the brand counts royalty among its fans. The Centenary line is the one to buy: Pair the Centenary Carry-On with the Centenary Medium Check-In, and you’ll have a set that looks like you found it at a Paris antique market. At $5,790 for both, it’s a splurge, but these are the kind of pieces that will last you decades and get better with age.

The Portland Trail Blazers made the trek to Milwaukee last year to play the Bucks. Damian Lillard was present, even though he was sitting out the entire 2025-26 campaign — his first season back with the Trail Blazers — as he recovered from the Achilles tear he suffered while playing for the Bucks in their 2025 first-round playoff series.

Fiserv Forum aired a video tribute to Lillard amid that November 2025 meeting, and the nine-time All-Star received a standing ovation after it played that night.

Thursday marked the 36-year-old guard's first time taking the court against Milwaukee since his injury and his two-season stint with the Bucks. Lillard was subdued talking about the matchup in the lead-up to tip-off, but he came away with his best performance in 624 days. (Related: Leading House Democrat targets Trump’s inner circle for...)

March 5, 2025, was the last time Lillard had a 30-point game. He scored a season-high 33 points on Thursday in a 124-114 victory over a reeling and rebuilding Milwaukee (1-15). (Related: Hyatt Category 1 Hotels — The Easiest Points Win...)

Portland (10-5) bounced back after dropping consecutive games to the Minnesota Timberwolves.

According to recent reports, lillard once again received a warm welcome from Bucks fans. Milwaukee ultimately waived him to sign center Myles Turner in the summer of 2025. The Bucks are still paying Lillard after they decided to stretch the remaining $113 million on his contract.

So, on Thursday, Milwaukee fans watched their team lose its 12th straight game and Lillard shoot 10 of 18 from the field, including 5 of 12 from the beyond the arc.

Recent findings suggest that plus, another former Bucks player, Jrue Holiday — part of Milwaukee's NBA championship team in 2020-21 — put on a show of his own. He logged 22 points, 7 assists and 4 steals.

Sources indicate that holiday stepped back to hit a crucial jumper with about 5 ½ minutes to go. Another 36-year-old guard, Holiday went on to help the Trail Blazers create separation late, even coupling a steal with a layup.

Portland also got 27 points and seven rebounds from a much younger member of its backcourt: Scoot Henderson.

Meanwhile, the Bucks fell again, a day removed from facing Giannis Antetokounmpo for the first time.

Milwaukee had four players score 15-plus points this time out. Tyler Herro led the way with 19, Kasparas Jakučionis and Nate Ament had 18 apiece and Ryan Rollins chipped in 15. Turner, notably, was out with a shoulder injury.

The lead changed hands incessantly on Thursday. Herro drew the Bucks within one at the 3:18 mark in the fourth quarter. The Trail Blazers, though, scored the final nine points, with Lillard netting the last two at the free-throw line.

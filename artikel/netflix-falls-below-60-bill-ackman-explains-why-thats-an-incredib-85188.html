Netflix's (NFLX -2.96%) share price has been cut in half from the all-time high it reached last summer. The stock now trades below $60 per share, as of this writing.

While there are reasons for the huge drop in Netflix, including slowing revenue growth and concerns about engagement, the drop prompted billionaire investor Bill Ackman to add the stock back to Pershing Square's (PS -8.77%) portfolio earlier this year. The stock has continued to drop, though, but Ackman doesn't see that as a concern. In fact, he sees it as an incredible opportunity for investors. To be sure, Netflix's revenue growth has slowed considerably this year. Revenue grew between 15.9% and 17.6% in the four quarters through the first quarter of this year. That rate slowed to 13.4% in the second quarter and 11.8% in the third quarter. Management forecasts just 11.5% growth in the fourth quarter.

Management wasn't afraid to touch on the reasons for the slowdown. Co-CEO Ted Sarandos remarked the U.S. is "just not growing as fast as it could or as fast as we'd like," during the tech giant's third-quarter earnings call. The advertising business has also been weaker than expected. "We are right now a bit behind our aggressive target for the year," Co-CEO Greg Peters said on the call. And that stems from disappointing engagement. "We want to grow view hours faster. We're not satisfied. We're working on it," CFO Spencer Neumann said. (Related: Latest Update: LA Noire 2 leak resurfaces after source ...)

In a recent development, but investors may be overlooking a key detail about Netflix's business that hasn't changed even as revenue growth slows. Management is highly effective at dialing in content spend and reaching its target operating margin. Operating margin is on track to reach 31.5% this year, up from 29.5% last year.

Recent findings suggest that what's more, Netflix's free cash flow conversion is very high and growing. It generated $9.5 billion of free cash flow through the first three quarters of the year. Netflix uses that cash to buy back shares, which supports significant improvements in its earnings per share.

That's where Ackman believes the current share price makes Netflix stock a compelling buy. "We'll make more money over time if the stock is cheaper in the short- to intermediate-term because they're going to cancel a lot more shares as a result," he mentioned in a recent interview.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

Netflix repurchased $4.8 billion of its stock in the third quarter, and it still has $22.3 billion in authorization capacity. At the tech giant's current share price, the remaining authorization represents around 9% of the current share count.

Recent findings suggest that netflix could complete the remaining authorization over the next two years. Management expects to generate about $2.5 billion in free cash flow in the fourth quarter, and that number should steadily climb next year. The company should be able to maintain its double-digit revenue growth rate for the foreseeable future with improvements in advertising, pricing, and maintaining a low churn rate while operating margin continues to expand. That should result in higher free cash flow conversion, meaning free cash flow grows even faster than earnings.

According to recent reports, ackman and his team project earnings-per-share growth of 19% per year over the next three to five years, driven by Netflix's share repurchase program. The market is pricing Netflix shares at just 22 times its free cash flow, and considering that cash is going right back to shareholders through buybacks, it seems like a fantastic opportunity for investors.

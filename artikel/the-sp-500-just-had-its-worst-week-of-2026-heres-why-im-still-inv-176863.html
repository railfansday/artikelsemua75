According to recent reports, last week was rough for pretty much anyone with a brokerage account. The S&P 500 (^GSPC -1.14%) dropped sharply, finishing the week down 8.57% from the previous Friday's close. That officially put the index into correction territory, down 11.5% from its record high in early October. (Related: Estate Planning for Early Retirement: The FIRE Guide...)

Although weeks like that are stomach-churning for investors, I'm definitely not pulling my money out of the market. Here's why I'm still investing, even after the S&P 500's worst week of 2026. (Related: Compound Interest Calculator: See Your Money Grow...)

Last week was an abnormal one, with several high-profile market shocks landing one after another.

It has been revealed that right before the market closed on Friday the 13th, the FAA ordered a nationwide ground stop to all U.S. flights. The stop lasted four hours, but the repercussions echoed throughout the air travel system for days.

Other service outages to Microsoft (MSFT +0.76%) Outlook and Teams, and to the Apple (AAPL +0.76%) Store and Apple Music, led to fears that these incidents were connected and potentially related to faulty artificial intelligence (AI) or cybersecurity breaches.

Next, there was fallout from Nvidia's (NVDA -8.37%) Q3 earnings report on Tuesday, which was delivered after CEO Jensen Huang's resignation (and before his Monday reinstatement). On Wednesday, Alaska's Senate race was called for Democrat Mary Peltola, reigniting concerns about how a Democratic-controlled legislature might regulate AI and data centers. That was followed by the largest IPO of all time, as major AI player Anthropic (ANT -13.23%), the developer of the Claude AI model, began trading on the NASDAQ on Thursday.

That's a lot to digest all at once, especially given how much the S&P 500 has relied on AI spending for its recent gains.

Remember, a market correction isn't a bear market, and it isn't a recession forecast either.

In a recent development, corrections -- in which an index drops more than 10% from its high -- are a normal (if uncomfortable) part of investing. Bear markets are relatively few and far between, but there have been plenty of market corrections that have reversed themselves before ushering in a prolonged downturn.

The other good news for investors is that a market correction -- in which quality stocks get punished alongside shaky ones -- can be an excellent buying opportunity.

Recent findings suggest that after last week's market correction, it looks to me like more of the fear and risk around AI spending and deployment is baked into the market right now than before. That doesn't mean prices can't fall further, but it does mean there’s more upside in some great companies now trading at more attractive prices.

Industry observers note that it might be different if the concerning events of last week highlighted signs of persisting, but as I write this on Monday, Nov. 23, flights are taking off and landing as normal, Microsoft Outlook and Apple Music are both functioning, and Nvidia's stock is soaring.

So I'm checking my shopping list to see if there are any businesses that look too compelling to ignore, and you may want to do the same.

Last week was scary, but it may turn out to be a gift for patient, long-term investors.

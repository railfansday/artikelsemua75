The artificial intelligence (AI) trade has soured in recent months, as investors worry about the sustainability of heavy spending on AI data centers amid a high-interest rate environment. (Related: Compound Interest Calculator: See Your Money Grow...)

The Federal Reserve increased interest rates in September, marking the first increase since 2023, in a bid to combat inflation. The central bank is expected to increase interest rates once again in December, a move that's likely to put tech stocks under even more pressure.

Sources indicate that AI infrastructure giant Oracle (ORCL -4.52%) has borne the brunt of the sell-off in the technology sector this year. Its shares are down 42% in 2026, even though it has been consistently exceeding Wall Street's earnings expectations in recent quarters. Moreover, Oracle's mounting debt, the tech giant's ability to get access to financing amid high interest rates to sustain the data center build-out, and its cash burn have all contributed to the stock's weakness.

Industry observers note that oracle stock could be hammered once again if the Fed hikes interest rates next month. However, that could open up a solid buying opportunity for savvy investors, as Oracle seems primed for a strong comeback in 2027. Let's look at the reasons why. It has been revealed that oracle has been burning cash, issuing equity, and taking on debt to build more AI data centers. These are the reasons why investors have hit the panic button this year. However, the manufacturer anticipates a turnaround in fiscal 2028.

NYSE · As of 11:37 AM ET 11/24/26.

Sources indicate that oracle's fiscal 2027 will end in May next year. The company noted last month that it is on track to raise $20 billion in fiscal 2027, split equally between debt and equity. However, its fiscal 2028 capital expenditure is fully funded. That's because the significant chunk of latest contracts that Oracle is booking is already funded through customer prepayments or a bring-your-own-hardware scheme.

The tech giant's trailing-twelve-month operating income also increased by five percentage points sequentially between the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 and fiscal Q1 2027. As a result, Oracle's cash flow from operations surged to record levels last quarter.

ORCL Cash from Operations (Quarterly) data by YCharts.

The tech giant believes its free cash flow will turn positive in the second half of fiscal 2028, which begins in December next year. So, I won't be surprised to see investor confidence in Oracle stock improving next year, potentially paving the way for significant upside.

Sources indicate that wall Street analysts are quite bullish about Oracle's trajectory over the next 12 months. The stock has a median 12-month price target of $240, implying 111% upside, according to CNN. What's more, 37 of the 43 analysts rate Oracle as a buy.

A key reason I think Oracle should reach this median price target is an acceleration in earnings growth in fiscal 2028. Analysts are expecting a 7% jump in Oracle's earnings per share in fiscal 2027 to $8.15, followed by a stronger 35% jump next year. This is evident from the following chart.

ORCL EPS Estimates for Current Fiscal Year data by YCharts.

It has been revealed that what's more, the above chart points to a stronger jump in Oracle's earnings in fiscal 2029. The improvement in Oracle's earnings growth should ideally be rewarded with a premium valuation. The stock is currently trading at 12 times forward earnings, a discount to the tech-laden Nasdaq-100 index's forward earnings multiple of 20.

Assuming Oracle trades at 25 times earnings at the end of fiscal 2028 and its earnings per share jump to $11.03, the stock price will increase to $276 in the next 18 months. That implies a 144% jump from current levels, indicating that this AI stock could indeed reach the Wall Street price target by the end of 2027.

So, savvy investors should consider capitalizing on any dip in Oracle, as the stock could surge on a potential turnaround next year.

A police officer who was shot outside a remote Tasmanian police station is alleged to have shot and killed his attacker in response to what authorities believe was a “targeted” incident.

Industry observers note that tasmania police deputy commissioner, Jonathan Higgins, mentioned the sergeant had just come off shift at Whitemark police station on Flinders Island, which is north-east of the Tasmanian mainland. He had parked and gotten out of his police vehicle when he was shot at about 9.45pm on Saturday, Higgins told reporters on Sunday. Investigators sent to investigate the shooting of the sergeant discovered the body of a man, aged in his mid-20s, on Walker Street a few hundred metres from the police station.

Police believe he was shot by the sergeant, who fired his weapon a number of times.

“At this early stage in the investigation, police believe this was a targeted incident,’” Higgins said.

On Sunday afternoon the sergeant was in the operating theatre undergoing surgery at the Royal Hobart hospital and remained in a serious but stable condition. (Related: Developer vibe codes Windows 11 alternative operating s...)

He is one of three officers stationed on Flinders Island and has worked there for a number of years.

Whitemark has a population of about 300 according to the 2021 census, and the entire population of Flinders Island is fewer than 1,000 people.

Extra police resources, including forensic and uniformed officers, have been deployed to the island. Those with information about these incidents, including CCTV or dashcam footage of Walker Street, have been urged to contact police.

Police are hoping to speak to the family of the deceased man.

Higgins said it was “the nature of our work” for police to receive threats from the public.

The Tasmanian premier, Jeremy Rockliff, has been in contact with the state’s police commissioner, Donna Adams, who attended the Hobart hospital as the shot sergeant undergoes treatment.

“My thoughts are with everyone affected by this horrific incident, including the police officer, their family and colleagues,” Rockliff mentioned.

“Our police officers work every single day to keep Tasmanians safe, and incidents like this are a stark reminder of the risks they face in the line of duty.”.

In a recent development, it is the second shooting of an on-duty police officer in Tasmania in 18 months. A Tasmanian officer was killed in the state’s north-west in June 2025 while executing a court-issued warrant to repossess a residence. The occupant of the home pleaded guilty to murder.

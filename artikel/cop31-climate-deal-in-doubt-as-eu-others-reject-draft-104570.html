COP31: Climate deal in doubt as EU, others reject draft

Diplomats and delegates from nearly 200 countries are meeting in Antalya, Turkey.

Like in previous years, the climate summit has been extended until a consensus is reached.

The latest COP31 draft deal still fails to mention fossil fuels, raising the possibility that the summit could end with no deal. (Related: Redditor forced to bring desktop PC in checked luggage ...)

The EU says it cannot accept a deal that 'greenwashes fossil fuels'.

Fossil fuels are a major flashpoint at this year's summit, which comes amid soaring energy prices and tensions over the US-Israeli war with Iran. (Related: AI data center power demand will double by 2030 thanks ...)

These are the major developments from the closing phase of the UN's COP31 Climate Summit in Antalya, Turkey, on Friday, November 20 through Sunday, November 22:.

Ireland's Climate Minister Darragh O'Brien has stated the European Union is "profoundly disappointed" with the current draft deal on the table at COP31 because it "fails to meet the moment" and that the bloc will not agree to it.

"We will not endorse a cover decision that does not respond to the science, that disregards the implementation gap, and risks deepening the chasm between global commitments and the action required to correct the overshoot and bring 1.5°C within reach," O'Brien said.

Recent findings suggest that "We will not endorse a cover decision that does not even mention fossil fuels, the driver of climate change. And we will not accept negotiating backward in time. And we will not accept a COP that greenwashes fossil fuels," he added.

Former Greenpeace executive Jennifer Morgan said it would be better to end the COP31 summit with no deal than a watered-down declaration.

Her remarks come amid deep divisions over the omission of fossil fuels from the draft declaration.

Morgan rejected the notion that it would be better to accept the current draft than have no resolution at all, as the Turkish COP president has suggested.

"I think this is the day for the first time in my 31 COPs that I thought it was better not to have a decision that sets us back, that goes against what small islands and vulnerable people are saying," Morgan added.

She praised Germany for standing up to pressures to accept the current deal.

"Of course it would have been better to have a strong outcome here with everyone moving forward, but I agree with the German minister who just noted if the text stays the way that it is, then it should not be accepted," Morgan said.

The US climate activist blamed oil-producing nations for the impasse.

"Saudi Arabia's fingerprints are all over" the current draft proposal, she noted.

The omission has created a deadlock at COP31, which was due to conclude on Friday but has been extended as delegates seek consensus.

Morgan also blamed this year's Turkish presidency for failing to bring everybody together and instead letting countries that rely on fossil fuels dictate the terms of the agreement.

"Last year we had Brazil with a very strong presidency. They worked all year to bring countries together and they managed. It was not easy last year," Morgan mentioned.

It has been revealed that "This year we had presidencies that, in my view, were not well prepared. They did not bring countries together  (...) it's been shuttle diplomacy with no text for four days. That's not the way you get an agreement here," she added.

German Environment Minister Carsten Schneider has mentioned the latest version of the draft agreement is "not acceptable.".

In a recent development, schneider noted that the draft decision "not only fails to provide an adequate response but actually takes a step backward. A step backward from previous decisions on climate protection is the last thing Germany can support.".

Schneider noted that delegates had been working hard for days to reach a unanimous decision on "the world's greatest challenge: human-induced climate change," noting that this summer had "shown that the nature of this warming not only costs lives — as in Nepal — but also changes our world for the worse.".

Sources indicate that "It is up to us, as humanity, to do something about it. That is why a conference dedicated to climate protection — whose sole purpose is to protect the climate — must send a clear signal that we are taking action against this threat.".

Schneider said he still believed most nations are willing to work to enact climate protection, but singled out those who still rely on fossil fuels to power their economies.

In a recent development, "The overwhelming majority of nations are committed to climate protection. Some, however, are still making huge profits from oil and gas and are unwilling to scale back their production or to pioneer a clear roadmap for reducing greenhouse gas emissions in this area," he added.

France has called a upcoming draft deal proposed at COP31 a "backward" step.

French Ecological Transition Minister Monique Barbut criticized the deal for failing to mention fossil fuels, casting fresh uncertainty over whether nations will be able to reach an agreement.

"Time is ticking and we are not there yet," French Ecological Transition Minister Monique Barbut stated in a statement.

COP31 President Murat Kurum has expressed his frustration over the deadlock at the summit.

The latest version of the draft deal again fails to mention fossil fuels, the main cause of global warming.

Over 40 hours after its scheduled conclusion and with no compromise in sight, the summit risks losing the numbers required to make any decision as delegates leave for home.

"If it is not adopted, I believe those who push the COP process into a deadlock will need to reflect on their position," Kurum mentioned.

It has been revealed that "It is our wish that this resolution be passed. We shall shortly be expressing our determination to ensure that this resolution is passed at the general meeting," he said.

Recent findings suggest that "It is up to the parties involved. If there is sincerity here, and if there is a genuine desire to get things done, I believe that this can be achieved in this chamber. We shall see about that in a moment," Kurum added.

The third draft makes no mention of the words  "fossil," "oil," "coal," or "phase out.".

However, as in the second draft, the latest version retains a goal to boost electricity's share of global energy use to 35% by 2035, as well as efforts to expand the use of "clean energy.".

The draft text also does not stipulate that the "35-by-35" electrification target should be met using renewable energy sources.

It also urges countries to "accelerate" carbon capture and storage — a controversial technology backed by the fossil fuel industry.

Welcome back to our coverage of the UN's COP31 climate summit in Turkey.

The latest, and potentially final, version of the deal still makes no explicit mention of fossil fuels.

It does refer to a 2023 agreement that committed the world to transitioning away from fossil fuels — but stops short of naming them directly.

The third and presumably final draft text to emerge from negotiations at COP31, which lacks any mention of fossil fuels or how to transition away from them, has been rejected by NGOs.

In a statement, Teresa Anderson, Climate Justice Lead at ActionAid International, said the people who will benefit most from the new "35-by-35" electrification targets and carbon capture "are the fossil fuel, nuclear, data and CCUS (carbon capture, use and storage) industries.

"This text will help elites to profit while leaving people in the dark," Anderson remarked.

Brandon Wu, AAUSA's Director of Policy and Campaigns, said: "Rich countries came to the COP31 talks with nothing to offer other than red lines.

"They put forward meaningless fossil fuel transition roadmaps that were nothing more than restatements of inadequate existing policy, then hypocritically blamed developing countries for failing to take action on phasing out fossil fuels.

In a recent development, “This is the behaviour of an arrogant bully who cares about nothing but himself and it is no way to have a proper negotiation. COP31 was one of the worst COPs ever, and it’s because the EU and other developed countries came with zero spirit of solidarity, cooperation or compromise.".

As COP31 extends into a second day of overtime, we are pausing our coverage.

In the early hours of Sunday, COP31 President and Turkish Environment Minister Murat Kurum informed delegates at a plenary that a third and "final" text would be sent to them for review.

He added that negotiators would have until 6:00 am (0400 GMT) "to come to us with agreement on the text" and that a closing plenary would then be convened at 9:00 am local time.

The shift to green tech is already in full swing, including electric cars, electric buses, solar panels and heat pumps. But what is so electrifying about the latest wave of electrification?

Harjeet Singh, the global convener of the Fill the Fund campaign and strategic advisor to the Fossil Fuel Treaty Initiative, said that COP31 could be a failure due to a lack of funding.

"COP31 is stuck for one simple reason: money. Europe and other rich countries came to Antalya with empty pockets," he wrote in a statement shared with multiple reporters.

"They've spent two weeks pushing electrification targets and demanding more ambition from developing countries, while refusing to pay for any of it," he added.

Singh accused wealthy countries of blocking a just transition mechanism and of ignoring calls to increase funding for the Adaptation Fund and the Loss and Damage Fund.

"You can't ask developing countries to transform their economies while they're drowning in disasters and debt," he wrote.

According to recent reports, the climate activist stressed that the reason this COP failed wasn't a lack of solutions. It was due to "a lack of money and a lack of will from those who caused this crisis," he said.

Industry observers note that carbon capture technology works, but its real-world capabilities are nowhere near to moving the needle on climate change. So what's the hold-up?

Attacks on climate science and disinformation seem to be delaying key decisions as the UN climate talks draw to a close in Antalya.

Leaders from Africa, one of the continents worst hit by climate change, have spent years lobbying for hundreds of billions of dollars to help adapt to the rising impacts of global warming.

The rest of the world relies on African nations for their abundant natural resources, many of them used in clean energy production.

Yet at COP31, wealthy nations have so far failed to commit to the financing needed to help developing nations make a just transition.

The head of Peru's delegation at COP31, Andres Martin Garrido Sanchez, warned that the UN climate summit was unlikely to end with a concrete deal on climate financing or cutting fossil fuel use.

Garrido Sanchez lamented that the outcome "would be very limited.".

He reiterated how "geopolitical divisions" were stopping any final deal from mentioning reducing reliance on oil, gas and coal.

The Peruvian delegate singled out major oil producers, such as Gulf Arab states and Russia, as showing significant opposition.

He noted that while Latin American neighbor Panama had also signaled objection to including the term fossil fuels in the final text, Peru was "not so radical" and is keen to attract investment to "ensure our energy security" through renewables.

China has backed a strategy proposed by COP host Turkey to raise global electricity demand to 35% by 2035 — up from the current 20%.

In a joint statement with Australia, which is leading negotiations between almost 200 nations at the COP31 summit in Turkey, Beijing said it welcomes "growing international ambition and cooperation ... to increase electricity's share of global final energy consumption to 35 per cent by 2035 on a voluntary basis.".

The statement goes on to highlight the benefits the transition can bring for the economy, jobs, energy security, public health and households.

The two partners also "acknowledge the importance of inclusive climate action that ensures no one is left behind," the text continued.

Dozens of countries, including EU states, Japan, South Korea and Canada, have so far signaled support for the voluntary goal, which aims to boost clean energy use.

Yet, environmental groups have warned that the draft text doesn't stipulate the use of solar, wind and other renewable energy sources, which could allow power plants using natural gas and coal to be included.

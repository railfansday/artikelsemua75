📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

While it’s the responsibility of the U.S. Department of the Treasury to finance the interest on the $40 trillion national debt, it is members of both parties in Congress who have added to Uncle Sam’s borrowing.With the Democrats winning a wave of seats in the midterm elections, everyone from Wall Street analysts to CEOs of America’s largest companies are wondering what a more divided government will mean.Budget watchdogs are doing the same, and the Committee for a Responsible Federal Budget (CRFB) has put a number on how much the Democrats’ plan for their first 100 days will cost.

Industry observers note that the Democrats will go into the 120th Congress with slim majorities in both chambers, and they have been clear about what they want to achieve in their first three months with more power. Hakeem Jeffries, nominated by Democrats to serve as Speaker of the House, wrote in November that they aim to “reverse the Medicaid cuts, restore SNAP funding, extend the Affordable Care Act tax credits, eradicate the Trump tariffs, and stop the reckless war of choice in Iran.”. Some factors come with obvious budget implications: De-escalating the war in the Middle East would save money, while ending Trump tariffs would axe a revenue stream that generated $287 billion in customs duties, taxes, and fees in 2025 per the Richmond Fed—up 192% on the prior year.

The CRFB estimates that, in total, the “first 100 days” policies would cost $4.1 trillion through 2037. Without offsets (spending cuts or revenue increases used to balance upcoming policies), $4.9 trillion would be added to the debt burden over the next decade, including interest costs.

Sources indicate that but the factor analysts—and budget hawks—really care about is the U.S. debt-to-GDP ratio. This describes the level of borrowing a nation has relative to its economic growth, and therefore its ability to service that borrowing. At the moment, it stands at around 121%, per the Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis.

Industry observers note that economists suggest that if this balance were to tip further, then U.S. lenders may begin demanding higher premiums to offset the risk associated with the borrowing—sparking a bond crisis. (Related: Industry News: My Journey to a serverless transformers ...)

The House Democrats’ policies would increase the debt-to-GDP ratio to 135% by 2037 without offsets, versus 125% of GDP under an adjusted Congressional Budget Office baseline, the CRFB added.

The budget watchdog does offer some alternatives for how revenue to offset these proposals could be generated, though the reforms are unlikely to be popular with voters.

“Repealing the One Significant Beautiful Bill Act’s (OBBBA) tax cuts for high earners would generate about $2 trillion of savings—enough to offset an agenda scaled back by half,” the budget watchdog wrote.

“Repealing all of the tax cuts and spending increases in OBBBA—including its low-income and middle-class tax cuts—would generate about $7 trillion. That would be enough to pay for the First 100 Days agenda 1.7 times over.”.

The CRFB broke down each aspect of the proposals.

Starting first with the policies that could save money, the CRFB pointed to Iran. It noted that while ending the conflict would not immediately reduce deficits, it would likely avoid increases in military and defense spending. Continuing the war in Iran, the committee writes, costs approximately $30 billion a year and could be avoided.

It has been revealed that regarding policy costs for 2027–37, removing Trump’s tariffs leaves the largest budget hole. This represents $1.8 trillion in total, from repealing Section 301 tariffs (costing $955 billion alone) and Section 232 tariffs ($780 billion) to undoing Section 338 tariffs on certain Canadian goods ($35 billion).

Industry observers note that reversing the OBBBA health provisions would cost $1.6 trillion by 2037, the committee adds, including $600 billion to cancel provider tax and state-directed payment limits, as well as $485 billion to remove Medicaid work requirements. Reversing SNAP provisions would cost $250 billion over the next 10 years.

Increasing the deficit would also mean larger interest payments on the debt, the CRFB added—some $840 billion over the next 10 years.

Sources indicate that disney+ has some thematically deep sci-fi highlights, with a recent eight-part entry being a masterclass in exploring existential dread. Many of the best TV shows on Disney+ exist within the sci-fi genre, be it the likes of Alien: Earth or Andor. Both shows, Andor especially, took massive science fiction franchises and explored them in more nuanced ways than usual. While Star Wars does have some depth, Andor's exploration of war, rebellion, sexual assault, and many more thematically rich aspects proved it to be not only one of the best Star Wars TV shows, but one of Disney's best projects ever.

It has been revealed that easily, the show is among the best sci-fi series on Disney+, with another franchise taking a page out of Andor's book. That franchise is the Marvel Cinematic Universe, which has had a lengthy catalog of highlights released on the streaming service since 2021. The most recent of those was VisionQuest, which, while still working as an exciting, Easter egg-filled Marvel show full of fights, action sequences, and fun characters, emulated Andor by taking a popular franchise and diving deep into what makes it tick. Recent findings suggest that in this sense, VisionQuest was a masterclass in exploring existential dread. The entire premise of the show centered on Vision, split between being the physical reincarnation of the hero who was rebuilt as a weapon and the loving synthezoid with memories from before Avengers: Infinity War, figuring out who he was, what that meant, and how to feel the emotions he once did. Existential dread often surrounds a lack of clarity on what humanity even is, which the entire quest was about for Vision. (Related: AI data center power demand will double by 2030 thanks ...)

This led to many extended scenes of Vision and his surrounding characters exploring humanity, emotions, and all of the things that make people what they are. More than any other Marvel series, and, indeed, other reveals of its genre on Disney+, VisionQuest put effort into exploring the soul of each and every character by making them confront what it meant to be a caring being, human or not. Across eight episodes, VisionQuest's excellent writing delivered a true masterclass.

These deeper themes may lead some to wonder why VisionQuest's post-credit scene was so intent on setting up Avengers: Doomsday, which, while likely to have depth of its own, is a big, event-style MCU movie. However, VisionQuest was actually perfect for this. VisionQuest's focus on lingering dread and finding connection in others seems to be central to Avengers: Doomsday, too. After all, Doom's vendetta seems linked to the parental relationships formed by the Avengers, and how the latter group is now living stolen lives, given that he lost his child.

A significant central pillar of VisionQuest was the relationship between Vision and Tommy Maximoff, his reincarnated son. Vision's connection to Tommy was instrumental in the former regaining his human emotions, while Avengers: Doomsday could explore Doom's lack of a child leading to his villainy. Moreover, the Vision family is key to the multiverse, especially with Wanda Maximoff being revived by Doom. Although VisionQuest's story is relatively self-contained, it seems as though it is a great thematic precursor to Avengers: Doomsday.

It has been revealed that when focusing less on VisionQuest's links to wider MCU aspects and looking at it as a conclusion of the WandaVision trilogy, it becomes clear that this is the gold standard of Marvel Television. WandaVision, Agatha All Along, and VisionQuest are all incredibly unique, from the former's sitcom format and the latter's visual representation of AI thought processes to Agatha All Along's gothic Witches' Road setting. This allows each one to shine as its own thing, rather than a mere product of an oversaturated genre.

Moreover, to match the creativity on display, all three indicates are excellently written. Each tells its own story with its own unique cast of characters, while still linking together as a trilogy. More than anything else on Disney+, VisionQuest and its prequels shine as a perfect trilogy, with the show in question providing a masterclass in the exploration of existential dread.

Leeds United's 49ers ownership will hope to sell a small percentage share of the club in exchange for a "very high" valuation.

That is according to former Everton chief executive Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who believes that it is a sensible move for the Whites to search for more investment.

This comes after the news that the 49ers are on the hunt for a fresh minority investor at Elland Road, to help boost the club's coffers moving forward.

Wyness believes that Leeds will calculate the price to sell their shares by trebling their revenue and adapting the price tag accordingly from there. (Related: PENN’s Interactive revenue rise rests on partner taxes ...)

The West Yorkshire outfit's latest yearly revenue is estimated to be around the £255million mark, according to recent reports.

The 49ers remain fully committed to the club long-term, and Wyness expects them to be successful in finding a newly introduced investor. (Related: Industry News: Introducing Azure Catalyst agentic moder...)

Everton’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Goodison Park between 2004 and 2009 and now runs a football consultancy advising elite clubs – believes that the 49ers will "factor in" the upgrades to Elland Road as part of the sale process.

The Scottish businessman has no doubt that there will be an "appetite" among US investors to partner with the 49ers at Leeds.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider's Inside Track podcast, Wyness explained that investment bank Allen and Company are already working to find a deal.

In a recent development, "Look, Allen and Company, I know them. They're a very discreet bank, and do some very good work behind the scenes. I think Leeds will be looking to sell a small minority percentage at a very high valuation," he remarked.

Industry observers note that "They would probably have more success when they've completed a lot of the ground renovations and work that's being done right now. But nevertheless, those plans are in place and being acted on.

In a recent development, "So that'll be factored in to a degree. And yes, I think three times the revenue would be a sensible place for the valuation, and hopefully they can push further to get a better valuation.

"If the whole point of this is selling a minority stake, trying to indicate to everybody else that the overall club is worth more with a higher valuation, I think that they'll probably be successful, because there will be an appetite to get in bed with the 49ers from American investors.".

Wyness previously told Football Insider that Leeds are well-placed to secure a "handsome profit" for goalkeeper James Trafford in the coming years.

The 24-year-old excelled for England in their recent win over Spain, and has seen his valuation soar in recent months.

Wyness does not believe Leeds have plans to sell Trafford any time soon, but feels that they know they will be in for a massive fee at some stage.

They paid £45m to sign the youngster in the summer, but could potentially sell him for far beyond that figure already.

Airline Flew Passengers To The Wrong City, Blamed The Weather, Then Admitted The Truth Three Years Later

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

An airline flew passengers to the wrong city, welcomed them to the city they were supposed to reach, then blamed bad weather for the detour. Now the chairman of United Nigeria Airlines is revisiting that embarrassing episode as a lesson in why companies should tell the truth. I agree with that lesson, though I have a few questions about the flight itself….

Speaking at the World Public Relations Forum in Abuja on November 17, 2026, United Nigeria Airlines Executive Chairman Obiora Okonkwo acknowledged that his company made an operational failure worse by issuing an inaccurate explanation.

Sources indicate that according to PUNCH, Okonkwo noted a dispatcher entered the wrong destination in the flight documentation. The pilots flew to that destination, while the cabin crew apparently believed they had reached the city printed on the passenger tickets.

Sources indicate that the airline then blamed the weather. The flight occurred in November 2023. This week’s news is the chairman discussing the false explanation and the damage it caused.

In a recent development, on November 26, 2023, United Nigeria Airlines flight 0504 was supposed to carry passengers from Lagos (LOS) to Abuja (ABV), the Nigerian capital. Instead, it landed in Asaba (ABB), 197 nautical miles away.

In a recent development, that would have been confusing enough, but passengers reported that the cabin crew welcomed them to Abuja after landing. Imagine looking out the window and realizing that the airport does not quite match the announcement….

According to reporting at the time, passengers remarked the pilot subsequently explained that he had received the wrong flight plan. The aircraft eventually continued to Abuja. But the airline’s public explanation was different. It attributed the stop in Asaba to “poor destination weather” and said an incorrect cabin announcement had caused the confusion. Industry observers note that nigeria’s aviation minister, Festus Keyamo, challenged the explanation in November 2023. He noted there was no bad weather in Abuja and described the incident as an administrative problem. Keyamo also said a review of air traffic control communications demonstrated that the pilot intended to fly to Asaba. (Related: Industry News: The Reformer - Pushing the limits of lan...)

I suppose that is some consolation: the issue was not that the pilots somehow mistook one airport for another while trying to land in Abuja. According to the accounts from the minister and now the airline chairman, the cockpit was operating toward the correct destination acceding to the flight proposal, just the wrong destination the carrier was supposed to fly to.

But that does not make the situation reassuring. It raises a different question: how did the discrepancy survive the preparations for departure?

Recent findings suggest that okonkwo’s remarks this week focused on communication. As The Guardian (Nigeria) reports, he acknowledged that the airline’s communications team blamed bad weather despite the documentation error. He offered this broader lesson:.

“Whenever the truth is told, it builds trust, it builds confidence, and it also shows respect for stakeholders.”.

Sources indicate that I can be understanding when an airline makes a mistake and explains what happened. That understanding depends on the nature of the mistake, of course, but acknowledging a failure is an vital first step (ahem).

Industry observers note that an inaccurate weather explanation accomplishes the opposite. Once passengers discover that the explanation does not hold up, why should they trust the next one or trust the airline at all?

I do not know who approved the original statement or what information that person had, but I am reminded that the truth will find you out.

The problem with treating this primarily as a communications lesson is that the underlying event deserves at least as much scrutiny.

A dispatcher can enter an incorrect destination, but an airline operation should have checks that catch a discrepancy between where passengers have booked and where the aircraft is being sent. I appreciate the chairman’s candor now. Still, an honest explanation needs to include how the airline addressed the operational failure, along with why it gave passengers an inaccurate account. I guess we should be thankful for what we have….

United Nigeria Airlines flew an Abuja-bound planeload of passengers to Asaba in 2023, then blamed bad weather. Its chairman now acknowledges three years later that the explanation made an embarrassing incident worse.

According to recent reports, there is something almost comical about being welcomed to a city you have not reached. But I doubt I would have found it particularly funny had I been onboard. Communication is key and the truth is essential. In all things in life.

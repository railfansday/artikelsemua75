“A Beautiful Mind” director Ron Howard recently called Russell Crowe’s suggestion to shoot the film in order, which cost the studio an additional $1 million, was “the right kind of investment.”.

Recent findings suggest that the 2001 film follows mathematician John Forbes Nash Jr. (Crowe) through his academic career as he struggles with schizophrenia. Although many films shoot out of sequence, Crowe believed “A Beautiful Mind” would benefit from filming sequentially. “I am convinced it was the right kind of investment,” Howard told People magazine. “Russell certainly carried his part of the bargain with a brave and consistently detailed performance.”. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

It has been revealed that the director likened the approach to George Lucas’ on “American Graffiti,” where the “Star Wars” helmsman shot in sequence to highlight the “wear and tear” of night shoots on screen. Filming “A Beautiful Mind” in order could help Crowe develop a natural progression in his role.

“Russell had, in passing, requested that we shoot the movie in sequence because the psychiatric journey and the way it would be revealed in the movie would be difficult to calibrate shooting the third act first and the first act second, which is typical for most movies,” Howard stated. Shooting in sequence meant two separate trips to Princeton, N.J., adding about $1 million to production costs. But Howard stood by the approach.

“I came to believe Russell was 100% right and had to dig in with the studio because it was about $1 million more to shoot in sequence,” Howard mentioned.

The sequential approach paid off. “A Beautiful Mind” went on to win four Oscars at the 2001 Academy Awards. The film took home best picture, alongside a best director win for Howard, best adapted screenplay and best supporting actress for Jennifer Connelly, who played Nash’s wife Alicia.

A newly introduced 4K remastering of “A Beautiful Mind” hits theaters Sunday for its 25th anniversary.

Recent findings suggest that delta Air Lines appears to be preparing another Asia announcement, with a Seattle event next month fueling speculation about a new nonstop flight to Singapore. That would fit what Delta executives have already stated about returning to the city-state, though the airline has not yet confirmed a Seattle–Singapore launch date or schedule.

In a recent development, an invitation circulating on reddit promotes a “Delta Night Market” at Seattle’s Museum of Pop Culture. Travelers are interpreting the event as a potential setting for an Asia-Pacific route announcement. What makes Singapore more than a random guess is that Delta has already noted it intends to return. In an August interview with The Points Guy, Delta Chief Commercial Officer Joe Esposito said:.

“We’re going to be back in Singapore.”.

In that interview, Esposito ruled out Los Angeles as the departure point, making Seattle the obvious candidate among Delta’s West Coast hubs. Recall that a Delta presentation hosted on the FAA website, prepared for a July Pacific air traffic coordination meeting, listed Manila and Singapore as new markets for 2027.

So while we may not have a Seattle–Singapore timetable yet, I think it is very safe to say the announcement is coming very soon.

According to recent reports, delta would not be creating a new nonstop market. Singapore Airlines already connects Seattle and Singapore with Airbus A350-900 service.

But another operator would give passengers more choice, and Delta could draw connecting traffic from its domestic network in addition to travelers beginning or ending their journeys in Seattle: competition that I always welcome. (Related: Welcome spaCy to the Hugging Face Hub...)

Singapore Airlines is a formidable carrier, but having another nonstop option could be useful even for passengers who ultimately continue choosing Singapore Airlines.

Industry observers note that this would fit a broader investment in Seattle. Delta has already revealed daily Seattle–Tokyo Narita service beginning March 27, 2027, operated by an Airbus A330-900neo. That flight will complement its existing Haneda service.

It also added Barcelona and Rome from Seattle this year. While I view this expansion as a tit-for-tat with Alaska Alines (a battle I’m not sure Delta is going to win), I welcome it nonetheless…I don’t really care why Delta or Alaska add more route: they are good for consumers, even if they only last a couple of seasons.

For whatever the reason, I appreciate seeing Delta expand its own longhaul network. Its partnerships are useful, but there is value in offering passengers a nonstop flight on its own aircraft rather than requiring a connection through a partner hub…and I can’t wait to try the latest Delta One soft product.

For Seattle travelers, the growing competition is especially welcome. I care less about which airline gets to call itself the city’s global carrier than whether passengers have useful schedules, competitive fares, and more places they can reach without changing planes.

According to recent reports, A Delta event in Seattle has renewed speculation about nonstop Singapore service. Singapore looks like the leading contender because Delta has already committed publicly to returning, and Seattle fits the information executives have shared…I’m comfortable enough to say with very high certainty that a SEA-SIN announcement is coming shortly.

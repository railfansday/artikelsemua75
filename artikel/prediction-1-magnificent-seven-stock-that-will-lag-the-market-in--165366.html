I've learned the hard way that a firm can do amazing things and still be a frustrating stock to own for a year or two. That's exactly how I feel about Tesla (TSLA +11.46%) heading into 2027.

Tesla is already the laggard of the "Magnificent Seven" this year. Through Nov. 4, its stock was down about 14% in 2026, while the S&P 500 was up about 12%. This week didn't help. Shares were down roughly 6% intraday on Nov. 20, trading around $351.

To be clear, I'm not predicting a collapse. I'm predicting that Tesla will trail the S&P 500 next year. I don't think 2027 will fix what's holding it back, and here's why.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/23/26.

According to recent reports, tesla's core business is stalling. Last quarter, automotive revenue fell 3%, energy revenue fell 16%, and net income dropped 29% as spending on Optimus robots and AI surged. Tesla blamed lower average selling prices and tight battery supply, and it's facing tougher competition from Chinese EV makers like BYD and Xiaomi in Asia and Europe. Third-quarter deliveries slipped about 2% from a year ago, and the loss of the federal EV tax credit still stings.

Recent findings suggest that the catch is the bill. Taneja warned that rising operating costs "would continue till we commercialize these newly introduced products," and that "commodity price increases and interest rate changes continue to add to our cost and create further headwinds." Musk mentioned capital spending "will start to become more meaningful next year and then noticeable like in the tens of billions in '28.". Tesla's bull case is robotaxis and Optimus, and I'll admit the progress is impressive. Unsupervised robotaxi miles grew eightfold last quarter, and Cybercab rides started in Austin in September. Regulators are watching closely, though. The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration opened an audit into how Tesla certified the Cybercab's compliance with federal safety standards.

It has been revealed that but the timing matters for 2027. On robotaxis, Musk noted, "I think it starts to become noticeable probably in Q2 and then probably a significant deal by Q3 or Q4 of next year." On Optimus, he said there's "still quite a lot of work to do with regard to the AI and the robot for it to be truly useful for open-ended tasks.".

In a recent development, waiting is expensive at this price. Tesla trades at more than 400 times trailing earnings, so its price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio leaves almost no margin of safety if a launch slips. (Related: Course Launch Community Event...)

Recent findings suggest that this week showed how fragile AI-heavy stocks are right now. Jensen Huang's ouster at Nvidia, doubts about AI spending, and the threat of more Federal Reserve rate hikes all hit tech hard. Tesla sells itself as an AI and robotics company, so it wears those same worries.

There's one substantial thing that could prove me wrong. On Wednesday, Musk said Tesla and SpaceX would "probably" be better together, citing "so many areas of convergence." A merger could excite Musk's fans and send the stock soaring.

But it's far from certain. Musk has said a deal would need "a 50% vote of all shares, not just those who vote, but of all shareholders, and that's quite a high hurdle to try to achieve.".

Tesla could still be a fine firm to own for a decade, with its huge market cap backed by real technology. But for 2027, the gap between what Tesla is earning and what the stock is pricing in looks too wide to close in 12 months. That's why I think it lags.

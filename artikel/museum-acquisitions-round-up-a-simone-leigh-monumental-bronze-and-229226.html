National Gallery of Australia, Canberra.

It has been revealed that the American Friends of the National Gallery of Australia bought this sculpture by Simone Leigh last year from Matthew Marks Gallery in Newly introduced York. The monumental bronze, measuring 3.3m long and 1.5m tall, is named after the Nigerian American artist and actress Okwui Okpokwasili. It demonstrates a reclining woman in a cast raffia skirt, exploring the resilience and cultural memory that merges the semi-abstract female form with the African vernacular. This cast is the second from an edition of two (with one artist’s proof). In 2022, Leigh became the first Black woman to represent the US at the Venice Biennale. Untitled (wharf, Gloucester, Massachusetts) (1934) by Mark Rothko.

Cape Ann Museum/Christopher Rothko.

Cape Ann Museum, Gloucester, Massachusetts.

Recent findings suggest that christopher Rothko, the son of the artist Mark Rothko (1903-70), indicates that while his father thought “all art was inherently of its time”, sometimes “even with a master of abstraction like Mark Rothko, art can also be inherently of its place”. He is referring to this painting—an atmospheric early watercolour-and-gouache work depicting the harbour in Gloucester—which Christopher recently donated to the Cape Ann Museum from his private collection. Mark Rothko frequently summered on Cape Ann, painting alongside two other Latest York artists: his mentor, Milton Avery, and his friend Adolph Gottlieb. This painting is included in Avery, Gottlieb & Rothko: By the Sea—a touring exhibition right now on view at the Phillips Collection in Washington, DC (until 24 January 2027). In a recent development, arc-en-ciel no. 5 (1913-14) by Robert Delaunay.

It may not be instantly recognisable, but the lilac spire behind the rainbow in this painting is the Eiffel Tower—the tallest building in the world when the work was painted by Robert Delaunay (1885-1941). The French artist was the husband of Sonia Delaunay, and in 1912 they co-founded the short-lived Orphism movement—an offshoot of Cubism that prized bright colours and geometric shapes. This vivid oil-on-canvas work, measuring 35cm by 40cm, is one of several variations of the composition and indicates Delaunay moving away from strict geometry to a looser, more expressive and rhythmic style. The painting, which sold at Christie’s in 2025 for $317,500 (with fees) from the collection of Arnold and Joan Saltzman, was exhibited by the London-based dealer Willoughby Gerrish at Tefaf Maastricht in March—where the sale to the Museum of Fine Arts, Boston, was initially agreed.

Default weather apps are notoriously unreliable, but Google is giving users a chance to help improve things.

A recent update to the Pixel Weather app introduced a latest “Report current weather” card. If the weather you’re experiencing doesn’t match what the app is saying, you can report the discrepancy.

“Share the current weather condition,” the card notes, “to improve local forecasts.” If you tap it, you’re given options to indicate whether the current temperature is accurate, warmer, or colder than the app says, and what your current conditions are, with options like cloudy, clear, rain showers, strong thunderstorms, and rain showers.

Industry observers note that the card also demonstrates what the app currently thinks is happening for you to use as the basis for your report. To find the card, scroll all the way to the bottom of your Pixel Weather app. (Related: Industry News: Train a Sentence Embedding Model with 1B...)

According to recent reports, google remarked that it will use your feedback, device location, and “some diagnostic information” to help improve forecasts. Nothing you report will be associated with your Google account. The app doesn’t explain exactly how Google uses your reports, but I’ve reached out to see if the company can offer any details.

Sources indicate that as someone who doesn’t put a ton of faith into default weather apps, I see this as a step in the right direction toward regaining some trust. Even if it’s not yet clear how Google will use the information you send, the goal of improving forecasts is an crucial one.

Also: I switched to MyRadar as my main Android Auto weather app and don’t regret it.

A large part of what makes weather apps unreliable is that they lack human insight, and a computer usually can’t grasp the nuance of a local forecast the way a meteorologist would. Experts have also warned of reduced weather forecast accuracy due to recent NOAA budget cuts.

According to recent reports, the Apple Weather app has a similar feature, so it’s good to see Google catch up. I couldn’t find a feature like this for Samsung or any other native app. One of the most popular third-party apps, Weather Underground, does feature a “Report your local weather” prompt. It also gathers information from hundreds of thousands of connected home weather stations.

This fresh Pixel Weather feature was present on three Pixel phones in my home, so the rollout appears to be widespread.

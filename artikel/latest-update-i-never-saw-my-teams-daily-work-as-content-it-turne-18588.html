According to recent reports, during the pandemic, a good friend of mine who worked as a cameraman was out of work, so he joined our crew for a day. At the end of it, he climbed into the truck looking rattled. “That was a crazy day,” he told me. My response: “That was a Tuesday.”.

Every day, we dealt with closing delays, low bridges, furniture that wouldn’t fit, last-minute additions and customers navigating one of the most stressful days of their lives. To me, that was second nature. To him, it was a story, and he told me we needed to film it.

That eventually became an unscripted docuseries about our company, but it also changed how I thought about growing a business. Until then, we had relied solely on word of mouth. What I hadn’t realized was that showing people what sets us apart from the competition could become a growth strategy.

In a recent development, when you spend every day doing the same thing, you lose sight of what makes it interesting. I didn’t think twice about the problems we encountered on a move, because solving them was my job. But to an outsider, the process was wild. Parking and loading our truck, for example, fascinated our cameraman. It made me realize that the work my team and I do takes real skill.

In a recent development, that’s something business owners in any industry can overlook. You don’t need a glamorous product or a business built for social media to have a story worth telling. A contractor solving a job site problem, a restaurant prepping for a Saturday night or a bookseller opening their store all have something customers rarely get to see.

Simply put, your best content could already be happening. You just have to recognize and document it.

Sources indicate that when we started filming, I had to unlearn some of my business owner instincts. After our second day, my wife paused a piece of footage and asked what I had been thinking in that moment. The truth was, I had been thinking plenty. I just wasn’t saying it.

According to recent reports, I was trained to stay calm even when things went off the rails. My wife told me to talk to the camera like I was talking to my best friend. I had to rewire my thinking: Explain the problem. Share what you’re thinking. Admit when you’re frustrated. Just be real.

That taught me the difference between advertising and real storytelling. Business owners spend a lot of time telling customers who they are: We’re trustworthy. We care. We go above and beyond. And so on.

Documenting our work allowed us to show those things instead. Our motto is “We take the stress out of your move,” and now people could see that firsthand: how our crew wraps belongings to protect them from torrential rain, how we responded when a move didn’t go as planned because of an insurance headache and how we treated customers even when we were close to pulling our hair out under high-pressure deadlines. The messy moments highlighted more about the company than the camera-ready ones.

Other entrepreneurs can learn from this. What do you claim about your business that you could demonstrate instead?

Recent findings suggest that after we finished filming the show, a former customer who worked in the film industry encouraged us to start sharing our story on social media. Creating content consistently has sometimes felt like starting another business, but it also changed how I think about marketing. Traditional advertising usually tries to reach someone around the time they’re ready to buy. Telling a story and building an audience can create a relationship long before that.

Sources indicate that that’s especially important in a business like moving. People don’t need movers every month. Someone might discover one of our videos today and not move for another year. But if they keep following the company and seeing our crew at work, we don’t have to introduce ourselves when they’re finally ready for a quote. The audience comes first. The customer can come later.

Industry observers note that and we’re starting to see that play out. When we ask latest customers how they found us, more and more of them mention our videos. One man contacted us about moving his lemon trees because he’d seen us move palm trees on Instagram. During another move, a customer’s 6-year-old recognized us from our YouTube Shorts. To me, that was proof: People weren’t just remembering our moving tech giant. They were remembering us. They felt like they’d gotten to know the people behind the business, and more importantly, they already trusted us.

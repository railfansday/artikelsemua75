Seattle startup Aarden AI on Tuesday declared $8 million in newly introduced funding to grow its team and accelerate its work helping landowners evaluate potential uses for their properties and navigate deals with developers building data centers, clean energy projects, housing and other projects. (Related: Latest Update: How to train a newly introduced language...)

According to recent reports, founded last year, the tech giant states its clients collectively own or manage approximately 14 million acres worldwide — nearly the land area of West Virginia. Aarden’s team noted it has evaluated more than $500 million worth of land deals.

It has been revealed that “But land markets remain opaque and manual with far too much friction. Finding the right site, and building trust with the right partners rapidly, is more important than ever for our economy and our environment,” founder and CEO Danan Margason said in a statement. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

The company combines data on hundreds of land attributes with historical market data and proprietary models to assess development suitability, valuation trends, ownership and community sentiment. Its tools and services also help developers identify sites and connect with landowners.

Aarden generates revenue through subscriptions to its technology, which includes AI-native data, models and investment tools, and through fees for services tied to specific deals or analyses.

According to recent reports, the new funding brings Aarden’s total investments to $12 million. Overture Ventures led the round, with participation from Madrona and Propeller Ventures. The startup previously raised $4 million in a round led by Planeteer in June 2025. The manufacturer has 13 employees and intends to nearly double in size over the next 12 months.

Margason said he launched the business on the premise that while there have long been tools to support developers, landowners have been largely on their own to evaluate and optimize deals so they’re not taken advantage of or end up partnering with untrustworthy organizations.

One customer, timberland investment manager Resource Management Service, uses Aarden to evaluate whether tracts are best suited for continued timber production, conservation, solar development or other uses.

“Diligence that used to take months now produces investment-grade answers we can act on, and that is shaping how we manage value across our portfolio,” remarked Alex Hinson, president and CEO of Resource Management Service.

Other companies offering property research and analysis tools include Acres, which provides land data, valuations and due diligence tools, and Land id, which offers property insights and mapping tools.

Ben Eidelson, a partner at Stepchange Ventures, which participated in the startup’s first funding round, said the tech giant is already having an impact.

“We now have the capability to make smarter decisions about land, optimizing for the outcomes that matter from conservation to the local economy,” Eidelson noted on LinkedIn.

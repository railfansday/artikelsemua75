The first episode opens with Percy (Walker Scobell) and Annabeth (Leah Sava Jeffries) recruiting the recently revived child of Zeus, Thalia (Tamara Smart), to help them rescue two younger demigods — Nico and Bianca di Angelo, the children of Hades — and the tension is palpable. In a recent development, “I think that Annabeth’s presence is actually incredibly divisive between the two of them,” Smart tells Deadline of the dynamic between Thalia and Percy. Watch on Deadline.

In terms of sowing that division on screen, she adds that “a lot of it is based in our actual relationship in the best of ways.”.

According to recent reports, “Walker also has the exact same personality and sense of humor. So, right right off the bat, we were definitely going back and forth and joking, and I think the only thing we had to do was just up the stakes and be serious about it, which was actually more difficult than we planned,” she claims.

Percy and Thalia can’t agree on almost anything, except their loyalty to Annabeth, who has unwittingly been thrown in the middle of their power struggle at the start of the season.

So, when Annabeth is captured and brought to an enemy stronghold where she’s forced to bear the weight of the sky (the eponymous Titan’s Curse), those two have to set aside their differences quite quickly.

“She kind of kept them at a safe distance away from each other, because, as I guess we’ve seen the trailer, bad things happen when they’re not at that safe distance,” Scobell claims. “So I think without her there, they’re forced to actually work together and try to figure each other out and attempt to understand each other.”.

It has been revealed that adds Smart: “They actually have to be forced into proximity to work together to get her back, because that’s their mutual interest. So, it’s really beautiful in a way that her absence brings them closer, which is a very human thing for us. I think as people, we’re just always brought closer by tragedy. But I think that for them specifically, a lot of ego has to get out the door for them to work together to achieve their mutual goal.”.

Recent findings suggest that rather than put a wedge in Annabeth’s relationship with either, Jeffries thinks that the tension between Percy and Thalia also provides some clarity for the daughter of Athena. Whether she’s ready to face that realization yet is another story.

Recent findings suggest that “I honestly feel like you get to see more Percabeth when it’s between Percy and Thalia,” Jeffries states. “I feel like her eyes open up a little bit. Annabeth is very smart. Even though she’s making it look like she’s not noticing, she knows [she’s developing feelings for Percy]. I don’t know if that’s more of like a denial between her of like, ‘No, no, I’m working right now. I’m on missions. I don’t have time to be falling in love…’.

It has been revealed that she continues: “You get to see the fight [in her] more, not physically, but just like ‘No, I love this person. You’re not gonna take him away from me.'”.

Latest Update: Kaszek closes $650M fund to back AI startups across Latin America

Kaszek Ventures has raised $650M for its seventh early-stage fund, which it claims is the largest ever for Latin America. It will back 20 to 30 AI startups, mostly in Brazil and Mexico, at a time when only 14 venture funds have closed in the region this year.

Kaszek Ventures, Latin America’s largest venture capital firm, has raised $650M to back early-stage startups building with AI, Bloomberg reported on Tuesday. The firm indicates it is the largest early-stage fund ever raised for the region. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

Sources indicate that the fund, Kaszek’s seventh for early-stage deals, beat its $600M target. Its last one, closed in 2023, raised $540M. Washington University in St. Louis and the Wellcome Trust are among the backers.

Kaszek plans to back 20 to 30 companies, starting at pre-seed and seed. It will keep about 60% of the money for their later rounds.

According to recent reports, most of the focus is on what Kaszek calls the “application layer” of AI. That means startups using language models in fields such as finance, health care and online marketplaces. Co-founder Hernán Kazah told Bloomberg he sees the most room in services for businesses. (Related: How to train a latest language model from scratch using...)

“It’s a more natural fit for the types of problems that exist in Latin America,” Kazah mentioned. Many firms there still rely on manual processes, he added, and services can be costly or hard to reach.

It has been revealed that the fund is also looking at AI infrastructure such as data centres and chips. Kazah pointed to the region’s energy, land and closeness to the US. Power shortages, though, have held back data centres in Mexico.

It has been revealed that brazil and Mexico will stay its biggest markets, with Argentina and Colombia on the rise. Kaszek was founded in 2011 by former MercadoLibre executives Kazah and Nicolas Szekasy. Its portfolio includes digital bank Nubank, QuintoAndar and Kavak.

The raise stands out in a weak market. PitchBook ranks it the largest Latin America-focused venture fund to close since 2023. Only 14 venture funds closed in the region through September, it indicates, against 68 in all of 2023.

AI startups can still draw massive cheques, though. In August, Brazil’s Decade, an AI wealth adviser built by former Nubank staff, raised an $85M seed round.

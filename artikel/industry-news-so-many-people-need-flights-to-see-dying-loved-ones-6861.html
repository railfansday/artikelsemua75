Give A Mile says it’s had to temporarily stop accepting new U.S. flight applications because it doesn’t have the miles and funding to keep up. The people asking for help are trying to reach loved ones in hospice or critical care. Some won’t have another chance to make the trip. If you have United MileagePlus miles you can spare, please help someone get there in time. I’ve supported Give A Mile with my own miles and money, and I serve on their U.S. board. Give A Mile tells me it expects to provide more than 200 flights this year, up from 164 last year. But it’s received more than 1,000 applications. In just the first nine days of November, 80 applications came in, 61 involving the United States. One of the people they’ve helped is Dale, who needed to get from Bismarck to San Francisco after his father went into hospice following surgery. He couldn’t afford the airfare, and driving wasn’t practical. A health care worker suggested Give A Mile. Give A Mile collects donated miles in its own account and uses them to book actual award tickets. Their volunteers review requests, obtain medical confirmation and arrange the flights. The organization also covers the ticket’s taxes and fees. Their holiday campaign aims to provide 50 flights. You don’t need enough miles to fund an entire ticket. Donations are pooled, so 1,000 or 5,000 miles can help complete an award that gets someone on a plane. United’s current minimum donation is just 100 miles. If you’ve got a small MileagePlus balance you haven’t found a use for, or a larger one you can spare some of, I’d appreciate your help. Donate United miles to Give A Mile here. You can also donate Aeroplan points or cash which helps cover very modest expenses like taxes and fees, the organization’s insurance and bookkeeping costs, and occasionally buying tickets when award redemption doesn’t make sense.

For every club in the English pyramid – from the mainstays in the Premier League to those who flirt with relegation from League Two year-on-year – football grounds are held in such high regard and all 92 have been ranked by Daily Mail journalist Oliver Holt.

A cornerstone of the beautiful game, of course, football fan culture has been kept alive by those who pay money, week in week out, to follow their team around the country as if it is a way of life as opposed to a time-consuming hobby. (Related: Course Launch Community Event...)

And central to the groundhopping tradition is the venues they visit: from their outward appearance to the atmosphere and everything in between. As mentioned, Holt has visited all 92 grounds in England's football set-up – and they’ve been ranked from worst to best.

In a recent development, described as ‘primeval’ by Holt, The Den – which is the home of Championship outfit Millwall – is ranked as the worst of the lot. It’s sharply (and respectively) followed by Stadium MK and the Kassam Stadium in 91st and 90th.

A bit of a shock, perhaps, West Ham United’s London Stadium – which opened in 2012 and replaced the much-beloved Upton Park four years later – is ranked 89th by virtue of it being a ‘dog’s dinner of a football ground', as described by Holt.

Hillsborough, too, is poorly ranked alongside Northampton Town’s Sixfields, which can hold up to 8,203 match-goers. League Two outfit Crawley Town are a proud club – but Holt believes their stomping ground, Broadfield Stadium, is not up to scratch. Sources indicate that the same can be mentioned for newly-promoted Premier League side Coventry City and their Building Society Arena, as well as Colchester United’s JobServe Community Stadium, both of which are considered new stadiums given they were opened in the 2000s. Rounding off the 92-81 category is The Croud Meadow, home to Shrewsbury Town.

London Stadium (West Ham United).

Hillsborough (Sheffield Wednesday).

Broadfield Stadium (Crawley Town).

Coventry Building Society Arena (Coventry City).

JobServe Community Stadium (Colchester United).

Poundland Bescot Stadium (Walsall).

Brick Community Stadium (Wigan Athletic).

The Croud Meadow (Shrewsbury Town).

Ever-so closely creeping into the top 80 is Reading’s Select Car Leasing Stadium. The League One side are, it seems, always in trouble one way or another and their 24,376 is the perfect encapsulation of that. Pride Park and Swansea City’s home ground follow.

South coast rivals Bournemouth and Southampton – with Doncaster Rover’s stomping ground of the Eco-Power Stadium in between – are ranked 76th and 74th, respectively. Leicester City’s King Power Stadium may accommodate 32,259 fans, but it’s lowly ranked by Holt.

A handful of League One and League Two sides also make up this category, while the second tier’s trio of Watford, Middlesbrough, Stoke City join them.

In a recent development, onto the middle range of the stadiums that English football have to offer, Carrow Road of Norwich City – which was opened in 1935 – is ranked 59th by Holt. Bristol City’s Ashton Gate, Bristol Rovers’ Memorial Ground and Brisbane Road of Leyton Orient follow.

Opened in 1888, Barnsley’s home venue of Oakwell is steeped in memories, history and memorable ties – and it’s been ranked 54th out of the 92. Ahead of the League One outfit’s ground are Ewood Park and the Upcoming York Stadium.

Perhaps surprisingly, Arsenal’s pristine home of the Emirates Stadium, doesn't make the top 50. Highbury was replaced by their 60,704-seater in 2006 and as it’s commonly admired as one of the greatest stadiums in Premier League history.

According to recent reports, following the Gunners’ stomping ground is London Road of Peterborough, while their fellow League One outfit Blackpool see their beloved Bloomfield Road just ahead in 50th. The likes of Adams Park and Meadow Lane creep into the top 50.

The Valley – which is the ever-glowing home of Championship outfit Charlton Athletic – is on plenty of football aficionados' must-see lists and Holt called it ‘magical’.

Memorial Ground (Bristol Rovers).

Newly introduced York Stadium (Rotherham United).

London Road (Peterborough United).

John Smith's Stadium (Huddersfield Town).

University of Bradford Stadium (Bradford City).

EFL duo Crewe Alexandra and Chesterfield kick off this category in 40th and 39th. Moving onto Portsmouth, Fratton Park has seen its fair share of top flight football over the years but, alas, Holt has ranked the stadium, built in 1899, in 35th.

Portman Road and Molineux are rubbing shoulders with the likes of Burnley’s Turf Moor and Deepdale of Preston North End, which can hold a grand total of 23,404 fans in England’s second division.

In a recent development, the Racecourse Ground has grown in popularity thanks to Wrexham’s rise through the football pyramid – and Holt has ranked the 10,500-seater in 24th, just behind St Andrew’s of Birmingham City. The Stadium of Light is one place ahead.

GIVEMESPORT Key Statistic: Having been built in 1801, Wrexham’s Racecourse Ground is the oldest stadium in British football history.

Industry observers note that bramall Lane was the ground which hosted the first-ever Premier League encounter against Manchester United and, thus, Sheffield United’s ground is steeped in rich history. Described as a ‘cauldron of noise’ by Holt, it’s situated in the heart of the football-crazed city.

In a recent development, kicking off the crème de la crème of stadiums among England’s 92 clubs is Sunderland's Stadium of Light, Sheffield United's Brammall Lane, and Chelsea’s Stamford Bridge. Selhurst Park is ranked 17th.

Industry observers note that queens Park Rangers were once a staple of the top flight. The London club may now be plying their trade in the second division, but Loftus Road is still a sight to behold. As is Nottingham Forest’s City Ground – which has been ranked 15th.

In a recent development, just ahead is Plymouth Argyle’s 17,904-seater, Home Park, which was opened to fans in 1901. The ground’s seamless blend of beautiful architecture and friendly atmosphere make it a must-see sight. Aston Villa’s Villa Park and Craven Cottage of Fulham are also within the top 20 – and, irrespective of its evident downfalls, as is Old Trafford. Elsewhere, Elland Road and St James’ Park creep into Holt's top ten.

There's a top 10 finish for AFC Wimbledon's Cherry Red Records Stadium, which is surprisingly ahead of the futuristic fresh Tottenahm stadium, despite having more than 50,000 fewer seats.

Everton's new ground is the baby of the list, having only opened in 2025. But Holt has placed the Hill Dickinson Stadium ahead of Exeter City's St James Park in third place, with its location on the banks of the Mersey and interior leading to the journalist describing it as "spectacular".

Sources indicate that liverpool’s Anfield, which is often heralded for its fantastic atmosphere during matches, is second on the list. But the combination of heritage and noise isn't enough to beat Edgeley Park of Stockport County to a gold medal.

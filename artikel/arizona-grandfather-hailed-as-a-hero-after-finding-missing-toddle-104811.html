An Arizona granddad has been formally recognized as a hero after recently volunteering to help search for a toddler who wandered off alone into the desert while wearing only a diaper – and finding her safely within several hours curled up under a bush, leading to her rescue. According to recent reports, “I’ll never forget it as long as I live,” Jay Stewart mentioned of recovering Vella Campillo, two, while recalling the moment at a news conference held later. “I’ve never wanted to find something so bad in my life – or somebody to find her, anybody to find her.”.

According to recent reports, stewart’s emotional comments came at the end of an ordeal for Vella that began with her toddling off by herself from the front yard of her family home in the desert community of Eloy, Arizona, wearing only a diaper, at about 5pm on 17 November, authorities reported to local media. Vella’s parents, Joseph Campillo and Deanna Fritz, later told news station KSAZ that Vella was playing with her siblings and being cared for by another family member when she ran off. An intense search effort ensued, drawing in dozens of law enforcement officers and community members volunteering to help comb the remote surrounding desert.

Stewart, described as both a father and grandfather, was among the volunteers. And at about 12.15am on 18 November, with temperatures cold at about 50F (10C), Stewart was shining a flashlight through the darkness along a particular ditch lined with bushes.

Vella suddenly “popped out” of one of the bushes, Stewart would later tell reporters. The girl was shivering, about two miles (3.2km) away from her home. Stewart noted Vella came up to him and jumped into his arms, and he called first responders to alert them of his discovery.

According to recent reports, A deputy with the local Pinal county sheriff’s office soon came up on Stewart and Vella, with his body-worn camera recording video of the scene. “That’s where she was at, right in that spot – I was just walking the ditch,” a visibly relieved Stewart told the deputy, pointing to a nearby bush with a shirtless Vella in his arms.

“Are you OK? Are you so cold?” the deputy could be heard saying on the video as he covered her in a blanket and Vella babbled excitedly.

In a recent development, stewart tried to hand Vella over to the deputy, saying, “She’s got a hold of me.” He managed to do so after the deputy gently asked Vella, “Can you let go? We’re going to move you over here and sit you in [a] chair.”.

“I’ll go with you,” Stewart reassured her.

Paramedics subsequently brought Vella to the hospital for a medical evaluation, and soon she was back with her family.

At a news conference later that day, the Pinal county sheriff, Ross Teeple, explained that the timeliness of Stewart’s intervention was vital for Vella. Prolonged exposure to the temperatures Vella experienced wearing only a diaper during her night alone in the desert could lead to hypothermia, which is life-threatening.

Sources indicate that “Very remote areas like this can get extremely cold very fast,” Teeple remarked. “And she’s a small body producing very little heat.”.

Teeple’s office later published a video on social media showing the sheriff presenting Stewart with a plaque recognizing him as an outstanding citizen. He also gave Stewart a badge distinguishing him as an honorary member of the office’s search and rescue posse.

Sources indicate that as the sheriff put it in the video, Teeple had heard Stewart express discomfort with being labeled a hero. Yet “I’m here to tell you that when you watch the body camera footage, it’s obvious you’re that little girl’s hero”, Teeple said. “And today you’re Pinal county’s hero.”.

Fritz, Vella’s mother, told KSAZ that Vella’s disappearance marked “the worst day of our entire lives”.

“We’re beyond blessed for the man [who] found her,” Fritz noted to the outlet, while also thanking everyone else involved in the search for Vella.

In the video with Teeple, Stewart noted he would treasure the tokens of appreciation that the sheriff presented him. He also said at the news conference that it was not necessary for Vella’s parents to thank him, declaring: “It’s what we do.”.

It has been revealed that “The scariest thing for parents is to lose a child,” Stewart said. “I can’t imagine what they were going through.”.

He added that everyone searching for Vella was “doing it for the same reason”.

“We loved her and wanted her back – she may not be our child, but she could be,” Stewart said.

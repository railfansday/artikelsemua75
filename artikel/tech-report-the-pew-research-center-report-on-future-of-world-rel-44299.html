The Pew Research Center has published a comprehensive survey about population growth within the world religions between 2020 and 2070. The results are worth a careful study to forecast the future religious landscape in the United States, and how those numbers will affect debates about religious liberty. Here is a summary:.

According to recent reports, the United States is expected to follow roughly the same pattern of religious change as the overall North American region.5 By 2070, Christians are expected to decline to less than half of the U.S. population, while religiously unaffiliated people will rise to about 41%. And Jews – who were the country's third-largest religious group in 2020 – will be outnumbered by both Muslims and Hindus in 2070 if recent demographic trends continue. The numbers concerning Jews are also significant:.

As of 2020, Pew Research Center estimates there were about 15 million Jews by religion around the world, 12.5 million of whom lived in Israel and the United States. By the Center's projections, Israel alone will hold the majority of the world's Jews within a few decades.

Industry observers note that if recent demographic trends continue, Israel will be home to 62% of the global Jewish population in 2070, up from 46% in 2020. During this 50-year time span, the number of Jews living in Israel is forecast to grow from 6.8 million to 11.1 million, due primarily to what demographers call "natural increase": the number of births exceeding the number of deaths in a population.

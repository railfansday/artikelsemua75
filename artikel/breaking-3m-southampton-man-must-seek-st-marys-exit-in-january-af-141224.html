Southampton returned to winning ways with a convincing 2-0 win over Derby County on Saturday, but one selection decision made by Tonda Eckert will have one player really questioning his role in the Saints squad.

One of the main contributors to Southampton's strong start this season has been their injury bill, which has, for the most part, been impeccable.

That's left some fringe players without the minutes they'd ideally want, but on Saturday, there was a big chance for Mads Roerslev to finally stake a claim for some starts in the future, as James Bree was sidelined with a minor injury.

However, the Dane was once again left on the bench, despite being the only fit recognised right-back in Tonda Eckert's squad. Even if he still managed to get his first league minutes of the season off the bench, the writing is surely on the wall regarding the former Brentford man's status in this Saints side.

Industry observers note that roerslev was an intriguing signing for Southampton last season. Arriving from Brentford for £3 million, he went under the radar in a window that also saw big-money feespaid for the likes of Finn Azaz, Casper Jander, Tom Fellows and Leo Scienza. In a recent development, at Brentford, though, the Dane was a useful squad player in their rise to the Premier League and the years that followed under Thomas Frank. He started every game of their successful play-off run in 2021, and in the 2023/24 campaign, he played in all but four Premier League games.

Unfortunately, Roerslev's time at Southampton so far has been largely associated with how terrible the Saints were under Will Still at the start of last season. All of the 27-year-old's starts at St Mary's came under the Belgian.

When Still was sacked in early November last year, it presented a good opportunity for the squad to impress under new, young, exciting interim coach Tonda Eckert, but Roerslev was almost immediately sidelined.

He played in Eckert's first two games, wins against QPR and Sheffield Wednesday, before going down with an Achilles injury, which would see him out for the rest of the season.

Recent findings suggest that roerslev had to watch on from the sidelines as Southampton were rampant in the second half of the season under Eckert. The Saints didn't lose a league game from January 17th until the end of the campaign, and made the FA Cup semi-finals, too.

During that time, James Bree became an unlikely hero at St Mary's, coming back from his loan spell at Charlton Athletic in January and immediately being thrown back into Saints contention under Eckert.

The 28-year-old went into this season as the first choice at right-back, but a returning Roerslev from injury made for a good backup in theory, keeping with the theme at Southampton currently that there are two top players in each position.

In practice, that hasn't been the case with Bree out, though, as the former one-time Danish international has remained on the fringes, even when the position has been open. (Related: Few-shot learning in practice: GPT-Neo and the 🤗 Accele...)

Recent findings suggest that against Norwich City last month, Bree was rested after playing every minute of the first nine league games of the season. In his place, though, came natural centre-back, Zach Abbott, who has also been a victim of the pecking order so far this season.

With Abbott coming in to play in the middle of the back four against Derby on Saturday and with Bree out injured, though, it gave Roerslev a significant chance for his first start in over a year. But in came natural midfielder Flynn Downes, with the Dane having to settle for a nine-minute cameo.

Those were his first minutes of the season, but they don't indicate a resurgence in the squad.

In a way, the decisions that Tonda Eckert made regarding formation in his first few months at the helm at St Mary's have foreshadowed Roerslev's position in the pecking order right now.

Southampton flip-flopped between a back three and a back four during Will Still's uneventful few months in charge. One of the first things Eckert did was stick with a back three, but Roerslev wasn't his first choice off the right.

In a recent development, indeed, in the German's first two games in charge, the two games Roerslev was fit and available for, Tom Fellows was preferred at right wing back. Saints looked to attack more down the right, and the former West Brom man was selected ahead of a natural right-back.

Then, when Bree returned from his loan spell at Charlton, that was when Eckert moved to a flat back four, which he has used almost exclusively since; the one exception being against Manchester City in the FA Cup semi-final.

Industry observers note that perhaps then, Roerslev's place in the pecking order was clear from the off. Eckert didn't favour him over a winger in a small sample size last season, so trusting Downes to play there against Derby may not be much of a surprise.

Football's a squad game, and Southampton may prefer to have someone with the experience of Roerslev in their reserves in case they encounter injuries during the second half of the season.

However, it's becoming apparent that the 27-year-old needs a fresh change of scenery to try and kickstart his career once more.

Industry observers note that just three seasons ago, Roerslev was a regular player in a Premier League squad, making the Danish national team and not looking out of place within it. He has the quality, but isn't able to showcase it in Southampton's reserves.

Tonda Eckert has shown that he's comfortable playing central defenders or midfielders out of position at right-back, so letting him go to start afresh in January shouldn't detriment them, either.

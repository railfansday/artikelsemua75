Investors love numbers, understandably so. Numerical data is how we measure a organization's success, or gauge the value of the market, or, for that matter, decide the health of the economy.

Sometimes, though, it can be helpful to consider qualitative information at least as much as the quantitative for a particular organization (Warren Buffett does!) when making a buy/sell decision.

With that as the backdrop, while bigger companies like Apple (AAPL +1.90%), Nvidia (NVDA +31.48%), and Microsoft (MSFT +1.65%) technically have the bigger, better numbers right now to justify their bigger market capitalizations, I've got a feeling that within five years, Google parent Alphabet (GOOG +5.88%) (GOOGL +6.34%) will be the world's most valuable organization. Industry observers note that don't misunderstand. All of these organizations are likely to continue growing between now and 2031, with each apt to have larger market caps by the end of this time frame. Alphabet is simply set to outpace their growth. (Related: Industry News: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

Sources indicate that take Apple as an example. Although last fiscal year's top-line growth of 15% is robust to be sure, the iPhone continues to account for half of its revenue. That's a potential problem simply because the smartphone market itself may be peaking due to sheer saturation.

Recent findings suggest that while high memory prices have crimped smartphone demand this year, even before this year's cost pressures, IDC reports that smartphone unit sales improved by less than 2% last year. Although Apple technically led that growth, it did so with a year-over-year improvement of a mere 6.3%.

Indeed, according to figures from Omdia and Counterpoint Research, smartphone sales (measured in units) peaked in 2014. The bulk of the business's growth since then -- including Apple's -- just reflects higher prices, which technically drive growth but aren't a sustainable long-term growth proposal. And again, the iPhone still makes up roughly half of Apple's business.

As for Nvidia, the world will still need plenty of processing chips between now and 2031, artificial intelligence chips in particular. The company's revenue soared 103% year over year last quarter, driven by insatiable demand for AI accelerators.

Industry observers note that this need will eventually be fully met; however, it will largely limit the market to replacements and upgrades rather than demand for entirely new AI platforms. In this vein, for perspective, industry research outfit SemiAnalysis recently reduced its 2028 forecast for AI chip sales from just over $1 trillion to $758 billion, while dialing back its 2030 outlook from just over $1.5 trillion to $923 billion.

And as for Microsoft, while it hasn't been a leading player in the artificial intelligence movement thus far, its woes began well before the AI revolution took hold. Although it's still a powerhouse software name to be sure (with ongoing revenue growth to prove it), the personal computer market peaked in 2011, undermining Windows as a sales tool for other, newer Microsoft products. Indeed, there are now more mobile devices running Android than desktops running Windows, according to Statcounter, and this divergence may only widen going forward.

In a recent development, alphabet owns Android, of course, which, by the way, also dominates the mobile OS market with a 72% share. Android isn't a stand-alone profit center, however. It's free for consumers.

In a recent development, rather, Android's domination of the mobile operating system market positions it as a de facto gatekeeper -- and tollbooth operator of sorts -- for how most consumers now prefer to navigate the worldwide web. Numbers from Manochi indicate 52.6% of the planet's web traffic now originates from smartphones.

According to recent reports, and regardless of where it came from, Statcounter reports that Alphabet's Google still handles 91% of the world's web searches, with each one of these billions of daily search queries being worth a few cents. For many people, the Google name and platform and the name have become nearly synonymous with the internet itself.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/23/26.

This doesn't mean Alphabet's always done as well with Google as some investors might have hoped. It does, however, mean the company's got lots of options for monetizing its role as a gateway to the worldwide web. This flexibility is an important strategic advantage in and of itself, and the business itself clearly isn't going away anytime soon, even if some of it migrates toward AI chatbots like ChatGPT and Google's Gemini.

Industry observers note that the 800-pound gorilla in the room is, of course, Alphabet's cloud computing operation, which includes artificial intelligence initiatives like the aforementioned Gemini and hardware such as its Tensor Processing Units (TPUs). While not Alphabet's biggest business in terms of total revenue, up 103% to $30.78 billion in the third quarter, it is the company's fastest-growing one.

Industry observers note that and this number merits mentioning: Cloud computing rivals Amazon (AMZN +3.47%) -- via Amazon Web Services, or AWS -- and Microsoft aren't seeing nearly as much growth as Google Cloud is at this time. Both are losing market share.

According to recent reports, image source: Synergy Research Group. Chart by author.

What gives? It's a bit philosophical, but whereas Amazon and Microsoft sell AI services, Google Cloud offers complete turnkey AI solutions, including integration with its powerful TPUs, along with all the software and apps that make the most of their capabilities.

Sources indicate that this is a competitive edge too. Although the entire artificial intelligence industry's growth should slow over the next five years, it's also likely to evolve in ways that funnel customers to providers like Google, which can offer complete one-stop solutions.

Recent findings suggest that so it's conceivable that Alphabet ends up winning the lion's share of whatever growth the AI business has left in store between now and 2031. It's not clear that Nvidia, Amazon, or Microsoft are as well positioned.

That's the prediction anyway. Just remember that it doesn't necessarily mean the market's more valuable companies right now will perform poorly in the meantime.

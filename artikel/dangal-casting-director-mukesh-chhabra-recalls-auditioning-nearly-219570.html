Mukesh Chhabra, the casting director behind “Dangal” and Shah Rukh Khan‘s upcoming “King,” took aspiring actors and filmmakers behind the scenes of the casting process at a masterclass at the 57th International Film Festival of India (IFFI) in Goa.

Industry observers note that the session, “The Casting Magic: Auditions Unplugged,” turned into an extended conversation, with participants asking about everything from self-tapes and social media to age and the demands of the profession. Introducing Chhabra, festival director Ashutosh Gowariker, whose “Lagaan” was nominated for the foreign-language film Oscar, remarked, “What I like about Mukesh is his eye for character and he will go to any length to find the actors who fit in.”. Chhabra, whose casting credits include “Dhurandhar,” Aamir Khan’s “Dangal,” Anurag Kashyap’s “Gangs of Wasseypur,” Nitesh Tiwari’s “Ramayana” and “King,” due Dec. 24, described the casting director’s role as a bridge between actors and directors. His work begins with understanding a script and the director’s vision, then determining whether a character calls for an established face or someone upcoming.

It has been revealed that “There is so much talent in our country,” he said. “I send my team all over the country to scout for talent. You have to go to the roots. Our country has so many cultural arts and sub-cultures where so much raw talent is displayed.” Directors, he added, hire him for “my opinion, my ideas, not for my data.”.

Industry observers note that the scale of the search can be considerable. For “Dangal,” he recalled auditioning nearly 14,000 girls over almost two years. For “Gangs of Wasseypur,” he cast 384 people. That film, he noted, “changed my life.”.

Chhabra urged actors preparing for an audition to go beyond memorizing lines and analyze a character’s background, circumstances, social position and motivations. “Designing the scene in your head is important. We want to understand the actor’s intellect. Please do your homework,” he mentioned. “If you understand life, you can understand acting.”.

On self-tapes, his advice was to choose a clean background, ensure good lighting and clear sound, use someone who can deliver cues naturally, and frame the shot close enough to capture the face and eyes. “I want to focus on your face, your performance, not on the painting behind you,” he said.

According to recent reports, asked about social media influencers moving into acting, Chhabra drew a distinction between performing in self-created videos and interpreting a character in a scripted story. “If you really want to take your job seriously, you have to learn the craft,” he said, recommending theater as a way to build confidence and develop an understanding of performance.

It has been revealed that “It’s a part of your life, you’ve got to deal with it. You chose this profession. If you feel rejected and sad all the time, you will miss a lot of opportunities,” he mentioned of rejection.

Industry observers note that “Acting has nothing to do with age, talent is the only thing,” Chhabra stated. He advised performers to keep their appearance simple rather than arrive with a preconceived look for a role.

According to recent reports, he urged actors to stay curious about the world around them. “Read people. Talk to people. Understand people. That’s more important,” he noted. “It is all a game of patience and positivity. If it is meant for you, it will come to you.”.

Industry observers note that questions about nepotism and the casting couch brought some humor into the discussion. Chhabra stated he had rejected several star kids. “Whether you are a star kid or not a star kid, ultimately you have to act,” he said. Asked about the casting couch, he pointed to his seat on stage. “This is the only couch I like. I’m sitting on it.”.

Brandon Casey of the R&B group Jagged Edge is facing a lawsuit that claims he sexually assaulted a young girl over the course of several years in the early 2000s.

Sources indicate that casey was sued in September by a 29-year-old woman named Tyliyah Lee, who alleges she was repeatedly raped by the singer after meeting him at a Philadelphia “community event” in 2003. Casey’s lawyer, Leron Rogers of the firm Fox Rothschild, said in a Monday (Nov. 23) statement to Billboard that the singer “flatly rejects the untrue allegations of battery and sexual assault set forth in the civil complaint.”. “The claims stem from events alleged to have occurred some twenty years ago, are baseless and unsupported by any corroboration we are aware of and have already been met with a firm denial in court,” added Rogers. (Related: Trump keeps losing in court. Here’s how he gets what he...)

The lawsuit, obtained by Billboard, alleges that Casey had nonconsensual sexual intercourse with Lee on five separate occasions when she was between six and 10 years old. Lee is now suing Casey for battery and seeking at least $50,000 in damages for “severe emotional distress and substantial depression and anxiety.”Lee, who is a singer-songwriter, also claims in the lawsuit that she wrote the 2003 Jagged Edge hit “Walked Outta Heaven” when she was just six years old. She says Casey stole the song from her after she played it for him and never compensated her. “Walked Outta Heaven” hit No. 6 on the Billboard Hot 100 and No. 2 on Billboard‘s Hot R&B/Hip Hop Songs chart.

Lee initially brought the lawsuit in a Philadelphia county court in September. On Thursday (Nov. 19), Casey’s attorneys moved the case to federal court. That court filing notes Casey “denies each and every allegation of wrongdoing in the complaint” and notes that before she sued, Lee demanded a $15 million settlement from the singer.

“Mr. Casey will not be pressured by headlines, and he will not stand by while his name and reputation are attacked,” Rogers mentioned in his Monday statement to Billboard. “We intend to pursue sanctions and every other available remedy against the Plaintiff and her counsel for bringing claims we believe have no basis in fact. Mr. Casey believes genuine survivors of abuse deserve a voice and our full respect, and baseless claims only diminish their cause.”.

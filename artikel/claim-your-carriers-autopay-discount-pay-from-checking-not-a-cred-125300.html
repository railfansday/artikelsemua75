Claim Your Carrier’s Autopay Discount – Pay From Checking, Not a Credit Card

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, verizon, AT&T, and T-Mobile take $5 to $10 a month off each line when autopay pays the bill from your bank account, and Verizon and AT&T also require paperless billing. No credit card qualifies except the carrier’s own. Put the bill on autopay from checking unless your card’s rewards and cell phone protection are worth more than the discount.

On a four-line family proposal, $10 off each line comes to $480 a year.

Which payment method qualifies depends on the carrier. Verizon takes up to $10 off each line on its current unlimited plans when autopay draws from a bank account, and a debit card no longer qualifies. AT&T takes $10 off each line for a bank account and $5 for a debit card. T-Mobile takes $10 off each phone line on its current T-Mix plans for either one. In a recent development, say a family on that strategy pays its $160 monthly bill with a card earning 2% back. The card earns about $38 a year, so paying from checking instead leaves the family about $440 ahead.

Sources indicate that the case for keeping the card is cell phone protection, which some cards provide only while you pay the phone bill with them. If your card covers your phones and you’d otherwise buy your carrier’s protection proposal, keep paying by card when that plan costs more a month than the discount saves.

To switch, open the autopay settings in your carrier’s app or online account and add your checking account as the payment method, with its routing number at hand. Turn on paperless billing at the same time. The discount starts within a bill or two. After that, let autopay make every payment, since at Verizon and T-Mobile even a one-time payment by credit card can cost you the discount on the next bill.

AT&T and T-Mobile can take the payment from your bank account about two days before the due date. Keep at least one month’s phone bill in checking beyond what your other automatic payments need, and open each bill when it arrives so you can dispute a wrong charge before it’s paid.

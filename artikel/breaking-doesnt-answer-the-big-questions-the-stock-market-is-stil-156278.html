Wall Street’s full-blown gamble on the AI boom helped wipe out $8 trillion in stock market value before Jensen Huang’s return as CEO of Nvidia helped lead a rebound Monday.

Wall Street had its best day since April 2025 on Monday after Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO, 11 days after he resigned. The S&P 500 closed up about 5%, while the Nasdaq was up 6.5% and the Dow 3%, while Nvidia shares jumped 33%. Even so, the rally won back only about $3 trillion of the roughly $8 trillion the S&P 500 lost in the six weeks after its Oct. 6 record, leaving the index still about $5 trillion below its peak. It has been revealed that the swing shows how much of the stock market now rides on the AI trade. And Monday’s rally didn’t answer the question that started the selloff: whether the billions of dollars companies have poured into AI will pay off.

As of Friday’s close, the three biggest U.S. indexes had suffered steep losses with no relief in sight. The S&P 500, which tracks the broader market, was down 3.7%. Meanwhile, the tech-heavy Nasdaq and the Dow Jones industrial average fell 5.3% and 1.9%, respectively, with all three indexes wading into correction territory on Friday.

Industry observers note that the AI-fueled collapse of the past few weeks came after the S&P 500 reached a fresh record high on Oct. 6, when the companies in the index were collectively worth roughly $70 trillion. By Friday, just six weeks later, that value had plummeted more than 11%, erasing roughly $8 trillion in value, before Monday’s rebound. The losses highlight how dependent the broader stock market has become on the AI boom. Of the top 10 biggest U.S. companies, which make up 40% of the S&P 500, eight of them are tech companies. The concentration problem also extends globally. The U.S. now makes up three-quarters of the value of stocks in developed markets worldwide, Apollo chief economist Torsten Slok wrote in a note Monday.

Trump told CNBC Monday he “convinced” the company’s board to reinstate Huang.

“If I didn’t get involved, you’d probably have a very sad Nvidia for a long time to come,” he told the outlet.

In a recent development, white House communications director Steven Cheung confirmed to multiple outlets that Trump was “totally involved” in bringing Huang back as CEO. The White House did not immediately respond to Fortune’s request for comment.

Setting aside questions about corporate governance and national security raised by some high profile politicians—including Rep. Robert Garcia, the incoming chair of the House Oversight Committee—Monday’s rally gave the market a much-needed boost while at the same time highlighting other concerns.

eToro market analyst Sam North told the Guardian that while Monday’s stock rebound highlights the concentration of the AI trade and its dependence on one man, Nvidia “restoring its founder to the helm gives investors a powerful reason to reassess last week’s selloff.”.

Nvidia was among the hardest hit by the stock market slide in recent weeks. Shares of the AI chip giant fell nearly 14% Friday and 23% over the week, wiping out hundreds of billions of dollars in market value.

Industry observers note that the plunge came despite stronger-than-expected quarterly earnings, with the firm surpassing $100 billion in quarterly revenue for the first time. Still, concerns over AI spending and the company’s future prospects without Huang at the top rattled investors.

Memory-chip manufacturers also suffered alongside Nvidia. Micron Technology plunged nearly 24% for the week, while other chipmakers like AMD and Broadcom both saw their stocks fall by double digits.

According to recent reports, anthropic, meanwhile, had its blockbuster IPO overshadowed almost immediately by the selloff. The Claude maker raised more than $81 billion in its Thursday debut, the largest IPO in history, only to see its shares tumble 8.5% Friday. On Friday, Anthropic’s stock was trading flat for the day at about $89. By Monday, the stock had closed down another 1.4% to about $87 per share.

Last week’s pain also extended to data center operators like CoreWeave and Nebius, which also saw steep stock declines, after their shares had already fallen in the aftermath of the blue wave midterm election that raised concerns about newly elected Democrats imposing tougher restrictions on data center development.

According to recent reports, while Huang’s return has restored some confidence in the AI trade, it has not resolved the industry’s bigger questions about the billions of dollars in spending companies have put into AI and whether they can justify their soaring valuations, stated James St. Aubin, the chief investment officer of Ocean Park Asset Management.

“​The AI story was already facing pressure before the ​Jensen story broke. Jensen’s return doesn’t answer the ⁠major questions investors were grappling with regarding the ROI on the AI build-out,” he said.

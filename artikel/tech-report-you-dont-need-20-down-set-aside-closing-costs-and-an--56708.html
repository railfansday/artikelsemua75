Tech Report: You Don’t Need 20% Down – Set Aside Closing Costs and an Emergency Fund First

Sources indicate that buying a home doesn’t take 20% down. Many loans take as little as 3% or 3.5%, and VA loans for veterans and USDA loans for some rural buyers need no down payment. Set aside your cash for closing costs, moving, and emergencies first, then put down what’s left and weigh the mortgage insurance a smaller down payment adds.

According to recent reports, most first-time buyers put down less than 20%. Their median down payment was 9% in the National Association of Realtors’ 2026 buyer survey.

According to recent reports, twenty percent is only the point where a conventional loan, one without government backing, stops requiring private mortgage insurance (PMI), which protects the lender if you stop paying. A smaller down payment can also mean a higher interest rate. You can ask to drop PMI once you owe 80% of the home’s original value. Government-insured FHA loans keep their mortgage insurance for the life of the loan when you put down less than 10%. (Related: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Faster Language M...)

Recent findings suggest that on a $350,000 home, 5% down is $17,500, against $70,000 for 20%. Freddie Mac estimates PMI at $30 to $70 a month per $100,000 borrowed, or roughly $100 to $230 a month on the $332,500 loan, a cost to weigh against the $52,500 you’d keep. Waiting costs something too. Home prices rose 2.4% in the year through August 2026, the Federal Housing Finance Agency reports. At that pace, if saving the other $52,500 takes three years, the same house will cost about $376,000, and 20% of it about $75,000. Buying sooner with less down avoids that rise, as long as your savings stay intact.

Start from the lowest down payment your loan allows, on a home that appraises for at least its price.

Then set aside closing costs, typically 2% to 5% of the price, money for moving and repairs, and an emergency fund covering three to six months of essential bills. Whatever is left goes toward the down payment, and down payment assistance programs can help you reach your loan’s minimum. Ask lenders to quote your monthly payment at 5% and 10% down.

PMI can come off later, but cash you put into the house comes back out only if you borrow against it or sell.

It has been revealed that netflix's eight-episode fantasy mystery series is a masterclass in book adaptations. Adapting a book for the small screen is never easy, especially in genres like fantasy and sci-fi. While the TV format gives adaptations room to include more book-accurate details than movie scripts generally do, there's always a risk of alienating readers, changing characters beyond recognition, or shuffling events and compromising the story's timeline.

Recent findings suggest that sometimes, though, an adaptation gets it right, staying faithful to the source material while creating an engaging, latest interpretation of a beloved story. This is the case for Netflix's eight-episode adaptation of Lockwood & Co. Based on the books by YA author Jonathan Stroud, Lockwood & Co. is a supernatural fantasy mystery series set in an alternate reality, an '80s-esque version of the UK where ghosts have overrun the world of the living and kids and teens are the only ones with the ability to see, hear, and, as such, fight them. Trying to make a name for themselves in the UK's competitive paranormal detection industry are independent agents Anthony Lockwood (Cameron Chapman), Lucy Carlyle (Ruby Stokes), and George Karim (Ali Hadji-Heshmati). From their home base in Portland Row, London, Anthony and his friends/co-workers investigate the city's most terrifying hauntings and apparitions, crossing paths with some of the city's most dangerous (and powerful) people in the process. The show perfectly captures the books' eerie, vintage atmosphere, and it never skimps on the horror, either. Even though Stroud's five-book saga is written for a younger audience, it's still complex and layered; the characters and the overarching conspiracy are developed with real depth. These books are enjoyable whether you're 14 or 40. The show is the same. Most importantly, the chemistry between Anthony, Lucy, and George is impeccable. The cast does a great job of portraying their book characters. Newcomer Chapman is especially impressive as ringleader Anthony, dashing and witty yet vulnerable, too.

For a TV adaptation, Lockwood & Co. remains surprisingly faithful to the books, and not just aesthetically or character-wise. In fact, the show cleverly adapts the first two full-length books in Stroud's series, The Screaming Staircase and The Whispering Skull, into one eight-episode season, without compromising on character development or the story's biggest paranormal mysteries. In an interview with RadioTimes, director Joe Cornish revealed that it only left out one major set piece from the first two books. That's genuinely impressive.

The Dagger in the Desk (standalone novella).

It has been revealed that lockwood & Co.'s biggest book changes are George's name — George Karim, rather than George Cubbins, to better reflect Hadji-Heshmati's British-Iranian heritage — and Lucy's backstory. The adaptation expands and directly depicts Lucy's trauma of working as an agent in the countryside before making her way to London, including her friend and fellow agent Norrie becoming "ghost locked," a kind of paralytic coma caused by certain types of powerful ghosts. This only helps strengthen her resolve and, eventually, her relationships with Anthony and George.

In a recent development, tragically, like so many other supernatural dramas on Netflix, Lockwood & Co. was canceled after its first season. It's a genuine shame, though. Not only was it one of Netflix's best book adaptations, but it would have realistically only needed two more seasons to tell the saga's full story. They could have finished it before Netflix's three-season cutoff — as Deadline reported, Netflix's pay bumps and bonuses tend to jump after a third season, which makes originals more expensive to produce — and filled the gap between seasons of similar highlights like Stranger Things and Wednesday.

Even so, the show is still certainly worth a look. Critics and audiences agree: it has a rare 100% Rotten Tomatoes critics' score and an extremely impressive 94% audience score. Since the last Lockwood & Co. book was published in 2017, the story is complete. Those interested in what's behind Lockwood's mysterious private door, the identity of the ghost in the jar, or the origin of The Problem will certainly enjoy (re)reading Stroud's book series after watching the show — even from the beginning.

Sources indicate that all episodes of Lockwood & Co. are streaming on Netflix.

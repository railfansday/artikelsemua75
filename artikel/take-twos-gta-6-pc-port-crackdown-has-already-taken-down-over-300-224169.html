Take-Two's GTA 6 PC Port Crackdown Has Already Taken Down Over 3,000 Repositories

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, leonida was named after the fictional state in which GTA 6 takes place, and it was one of several attempts to get the console version running on a computer. Unlike some of the fan-made PC ports we've seen for older console games, though, this one was being developed using a build of a game that had only just come out.

The developers had asked customers to supply their own game files, but Take-Two argues that the project was designed specifically to make use of the leaked build. The manufacturer requested that GitHub take down Leonida and its forks, arguing that the alleged infringement couldn't be addressed without disabling the repositories altogether. GitHub says it acted on the entire network of 3,007 repositories. That number includes forks, which are essentially copies of a repository that other GitHub users can work on independently. That doesn't mean there were 3,007 different GTA 6 PC ports in development, though. (Related: Steam is pitting 10 years of Game of the Year winners a...)

An Xbox Series build of GTA 6 was leaked on November 20, with the Leonida repository appearing a day later.

In a recent development, according to PC Gamer, the team appeared to have reached the game's splash screen during a livestream before it crashed. The project's lead developer, NotRexed, subsequently announced their departure, citing the loss of the Discord server as well as the pressure of managing the community alongside school and sports.

Recent findings suggest that take-Two's takedown hasn't put an end to Leonida, either. According to the same report, others have already tried to pick up where the original developer left off.

Industry observers note that it's a familiar situation for those of us who primarily play on PC. GTA 4 came to PC several months after its console debut, while we had to wait more than a year and a half after GTA 5's original launch for its PC version. Even Red Dead Redemption 2 took roughly a year to make its way over.

The difference this time is that a report has suggested GTA 6 could arrive on PC in the first quarter of 2027. If that turns out to be true, the wait would be much shorter than it was for Rockstar's last two major releases. There hasn't been an official announcement confirming that release window, however.

In a recent development, getting GTA 6 to boot on a PC and getting it to run well enough to play through the entire game are two very different things. Just look at how badly Rockstar handled GTA 4's PC port. Even hardware that far exceeded the game's original system requirements struggled to run it smoothly, and Rockstar never bothered to properly fix it.

According to recent reports, modders have spent years ironing out its performance concerns and bugs, and while that's still an ongoing process, I'd argue 2026 has been the best year yet to play GTA 4 on PC. Even if one of these unofficial efforts eventually makes it past the splash screen, there's no telling how well the game will run on a typical gaming PC or whether it'll have major bugs throughout the story.

According to recent reports, hopefully, Rockstar won't keep us in the dark for too long regarding the official PC version. Even an approximate release window would be welcome at this point, especially for anyone holding off on buying a console just to play GTA 6.

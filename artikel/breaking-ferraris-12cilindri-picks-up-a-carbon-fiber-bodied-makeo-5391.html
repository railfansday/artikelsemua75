It has been revealed that it might sound a bit vapid to us in the hoi polloi, but even those who can afford super-rare, super-low-volume production supercars still want to stand out from their peers. As a result, plenty of design houses offer aftermarket body kits and tuning for supercars and super-SUVs, but more often than not, they look somewhat ... atrocious. Novitec, however, is firmly bucking that trend with a newly introduced subtle upgrade for the Ferrari 12Cilindri—and it leveraged a classic designer to help pull it off.

According to recent reports, that designer is Vittorio Strosek, a man who has played a role in classic reimaginings of cars from Lamborghini, Porsche, McLaren, Bugatti, and more. Now, he's collaborating with Novitec to put a newly introduced face on the 12Cilindri. The result is a car that looks noticeably different than the original, without losing much of what makes that Ferrari so desirable. In a recent development, the new version picks up a new front bumper, new hood, new side panels, new rear arches, and a new rear fascia. Sure, it's clearly still a 12Cilindri, but it's also clearly different when set side by side with the version that rolls out of the factory gates; the Novitec car ends up being 2.8 inches wider at the front and 5.1 inches wider at the rear. The hood outlet exposes the red intake manifolds, and the entire package allegedly reduces front lift and improves rear downforce. Novitec also added Vossen wheels measuring 21 inches up front and 22 inches in the back.

Industry observers note that let's break down a few of the finer details here. Perhaps most obviously, the black panel at the front of the car is long gone. Instead, the section matches the body color. Then, on the fenders, you'll find new vents that help to evacuate air that could otherwise be trapped in the wheel wells. The deep V cutouts on the hood are gone, in favor of a singular intake in the middle. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

According to recent reports, the side of the car is also dramatically different. There are vents and intakes at the front and rear, where the normal car has neither; then, at the very back, the rear window is gone in favor of louvers. The sections that are black in the normal car also end up with panels that match the body. (I’d say the ducktail spoiler looks good too.) Overall, it's a cohesive design that manages to stand out without being overtly gaudy. As aftermarket supercar body kits go, this is one of the better ones out there.

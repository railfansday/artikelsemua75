Recent findings suggest that I run a visa agency in Bali, and in the evenings I build data tools on Apify. One of them is an Immowelt Scraper. Immowelt is Germany's second-largest property portal, and immonet.de now sends its visitors there too. You give it a place name (Berlin, Hamburg Eimsbüttel, 10115, Bayern) or paste a search URL, and you get one row per listing back: cold and warm rent as numbers, €/m², living space, rooms, address, energy class, photos and the agency with its phone number.

A scheduled health check runs it every evening with the input a newly introduced user gets by default:.

Sources indicate that I wasn't trying to build a dataset. But after 18 daily runs (5 to 22 November 2026) I had 900 rows from nine agencies. When I counted the distinct listing IDs, there were 101. (Related: Build A Racetrack codes (November 2026)...)

Sources indicate that every row carries searchTotalResults, which is the number of listings immowelt reports for the search. Across the 18 runs it went from 6,170 to 6,459. That's a live rental market, with hundreds of flats arriving and leaving every week.

Industry observers note that the top two agencies supplied 40 to 49 of the 50 rows every day. On 22 November page one held exactly three agencies, and positions 1 to 9 all belonged to the same one. (Related: Industry News: These are the Senate Democrats backing S...)

Industry observers note that the realtorBadge field reveals the same thing from another side. Platin Partner was on 818 rows, Premium Partner on 67 and Business Partner on 15. That's 900 out of 900 with a partner badge. isPrivateOffer was false on every row. In the default relevance order, a private landlord never reached the first 50 once in 18 days.

According to recent reports, I don't know how immowelt weighs partnership in its ranking, and I won't guess. What the data shows is the result: the default sort on page one works more like an advertiser board than a sample of the market.

Industry observers note that it also clusters by building. On 22 November, 20 of the 50 rows were units of one new-build project in Kaulsdorf. That's why page one looked like this:.

None of those numbers describes Berlin. They describe two agencies' latest-build stock.

There's a second trap inside the same 50 rows. Two pairs were the same flat listed twice:.

The pairs had the same title, street, floor, price and size. The id was different and the agency's reference number had a .1 on the end. Deduplicating on id keeps both. A third pair on another street matched on price, size and rooms but sat on floors 3 and 6, so those were two real units in one building. A safe key is street + floor + living space + cold rent. Leave the floor out and you'll merge real neighbours.

Sources indicate that under the hood it's plain HTTP, no browser. The place name goes to immowelt's own autocomplete endpoint, which returns a location ID. The first results page is HTML. The 30 listing cards are inside a window["__UFRN_FETCHER__"] blob, compressed with lz-string and base64-encoded. After that the actor uses the small JSON endpoints the site itself calls, about 40 KB per 30 cards instead of about 190 KB of HTML.

In a recent development, it still fails sometimes. In the last 40 runs, 3 returned no listings. Each of those returned a single free error row:.

It has been revealed that all three came within a few minutes of each other. The runs right before and right after them worked. Successful 50-row runs took between 7 and 70 seconds. If you schedule this, skip rows with type: "error" and retry later instead of treating an empty day as "no listings".

Latest Update: 2 Clubs Voted Against Covering Legal Expenses if Man City Sponsors Sue PL

Whatever the outcome of Manchester City's appeal, there will be plenty of drama to follow.

In September, the Etihad outfit were found guilty of breaching Premier League financial rules between 2009 and 2018. City have always maintained their innocence and appealed the initial verdict, with an outcome expected by early 2027. (Related: Gift Tax Rules: How Much Can You Give Tax-Free?...)

In a recent development, amid the fallout, the club's sponsors, Etihad and Etisalat, have both said they are considering legal action against the Premier League. It's now been revealed that the 20 teams which make up the English top flight have voted to cover legal expenses for the organisation's five board directors if any of Manchester City's sponsors sue them.

In a recent development, as per BBC Sport, a vote took place on Thursday, when all 20 Premier League clubs met in London for the first time since an independent commission ruled Man City guilty of breaching financial rules. One topic of discussion was the possibility of Etihad and Etisalat suing the league. (Related: Leading House Democrat targets Trump’s inner circle for...)

According to recent reports, under the league's current articles of association, a director is only financially covered should they win a legal case brought against them by a third party. But a vote was held to change the rules.

According to recent reports, in total, 18 teams were in favour of the change. As such, the league's rules will now be amended to extend legal indemnity for CEO Richard Masters, chair Alison Brittan and three other non-executive directors.

However, it's interesting to note that two teams were not on board. The first, unsurprisingly, was Man City. Nottingham Forest were the second team to vote against the notion. The club owned by Evangelos Marinakis did not offer an official comment on their stance, but BBC Sport claim the decision was made 'on procedural grounds, rather than due to any support for City.'.

Sources indicate that it's said that Forest did not believe that 'sufficient information had been provided, or adequate time afforded, for clubs to properly consider the implications of the proposed amendment, particularly given the potentially significant financial responsibilities involved'. The club felt that such a decision required more scrutiny and informed consideration before clubs were asked to vote.

According to recent reports, due to collective responsibility, all clubs are now liable to cover the board's legal costs. This means that Man City would, in theory, have to take on 1/20th of the financial responsibility if their sponsors opt to sue the Premier League.

Industry observers note that ingo Bank, Man City's CFO who attended the meeting, asked Premier League chair Brittan if the vote was brought up as a direct result of threats of legal action from Etihad and Etisalat. Brittan declined to comment on the matter.

Recent findings suggest that the likes of Masters and Brittan have good reason to fear being sued. In October, Manchester City's club sponsor Etihad Airways has remarked it is considering legal action against the Premier League. In a statement, the United Arab Emirates-based airline said it "categorically rejects any finding, conclusion or implication that suggests the airline has ever been involved in improper commercial arrangements.".

The statement added: "The Premier League's public communication of the commission's findings, following a period of selective leaks and reporting, has further created damaging implications for Etihad despite the airline not being named in the redacted published findings.".

Etihad, which has been City's main sponsor since 2009, also mentioned it was given no opportunity to engage in a process that now reflects "adversely on the airline". The statement added: "Etihad is deeply concerned by the way this matter has been communicated publicly.

"The lack of clarity and transparency, together with the selective disclosure of information, has created damaging implications for Etihad despite the airline not being named in the published decision.

In a recent development, "The Premier League must take responsibility for the consequences of the way in which these findings have been presented. "Etihad will seek the relevant legal counsel on the options and recourse available to protect its interests.".

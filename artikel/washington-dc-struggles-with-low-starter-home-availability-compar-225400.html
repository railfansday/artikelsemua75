Sources indicate that A starter home is defined by Realtor.com as a property priced at roughly 80% of a metro's median list price, making it an accessible option for a first-time buyer. These homes are typically smaller and less expensive than the market average, aligning with the affordability needs of households earning at or below the area's median income.

In the DC metro area, the share of starter homes was 33.6% in 2019 and shifted slightly to 33.2% in 2022. As of 2026, the share stands at 33.3%. This represents a -0.3 point change since 2019, highlighting a slight but notable shortfall in entry-level housing options for prospective buyers in the nation's capital.

In a recent development, for those weighing the decision between renting or buying, current data suggests that renting is the more financially sound choice for now. According to the Realtor.com® July Rent Report, the cost of buying a starter home in the 50 largest metros was $858 higher per month than renting one, a difference of 50.6%. In fact, renting is more affordable than buying a home with a down payment in all 50 of the largest metros.

In a recent development, how NASA works with small businesses is evolving, and as a result, the agency is strengthening the domestic aerospace industrial base to support future exploration missions through its recently renamed Small Business Supplier Development Program. As part of the program, NASA revealed its latest Mentor-Protégé Agreement, this one signed between The Boeing Firm and GRACE Aerospace.

Sources indicate that NASA is streamlining the application process for the Small Business Supplier Development Program, as well as reducing administrative requirements, accelerating review and approval timelines, and enhancing incentives for prime contractor participation. These improvements are aimed at making supplier development more accessible, efficient, and closely aligned with NASA’s mission needs. Sources indicate that “We have intentionally redesigned the program as a mission-focused tool for strengthening industrial base readiness and mission success,” said Dwight Deneal, assistant administrator, Office of Small Business Programs, NASA Headquarters in Washington. “By simplifying participation and focusing developmental assistance on the capabilities NASA needs most, we are helping build a stronger, more resilient supplier base that can support the agency’s highest-priority missions.”. Recent findings suggest that under the new Mentor-Protégé Agreement, Boeing will provide structured assistance to help GRACE expand its capabilities as a supplier of mission-critical aerospace hardware. Boeing will fund the agreement, valued at nearly $500,000.

“Boeing is proud to continue its longstanding relationship with NASA in support of its mission and the opportunities this agreement creates for Boeing, GRACE, and the broader aerospace industrial base,” said Christian Diamond, Mentor-Protégé Program manager and small business liaison officer for Boeing. “Through this partnership, Boeing looks forward to sharing our expertise to support the continued growth and long-term success of a small business while strengthening our supplier base.”.

Building on two decades of experience supporting defense and aerospace customers, GRACE will use the partnership to prepare for increasingly complex aerospace and spaceflight programs.

“GRACE Aerospace is thankful and honored to participate in the program,” said Pauline Sevigny, president and owner of GRACE Aerospace. “We look forward to growing and learning from industry experts and supporting the mission of resilience and space superiority.”.

NASA’s enhanced supplier program is part of the agency’s broader Office of Small Business Programs.

Three penalties, two disallowed goals, one fluke, and a “main-character energy” referee: Manchester United’s 2-2 draw with Sporting in the Champions League had a bit of everything.

Mauricio Pochettino was denied his first win as United manager as goalkeeper Senne Lammens made two big mistakes and the visitors saw a late winner disallowed by VAR in a bonkers clash in Lisbon.

Referee Szymon Marciniak was in the thick of the action throughout and in the 84th minute ruled that Patrick Dorgu had fouled Sporting goalkeeper Kaique Pereira, who let a corner from Bruno Fernandes slip through his fingers.

But that was not all - here’s a breakdown of all the main incidents from a crazy night.

After a subdued start in Lisbon, Mauricio Pochettino saw his upcoming defence implode as they conceded two penalties in the space of three minutes.

The hosts received the chance to open the scoring when Lisandro Martinez was ruled to have fouled winger Geny Catamo by catching him with an outstretched leg. If it appeared soft, justice was perhaps served when Senne Lammens saved a tame effort from Sporting striker Luis Suarez low down to his left.

It has been revealed that but in farcical scenes, Lammens then conceded another penalty just 75 seconds later when he crashed into Maximiliano Araujo in the box. Suarez took the second penalty and made no mistake to give Sporting the lead, as United conceded the opening goal for the seventh match in a row.

According to recent reports, it was the first time United had conceded two penalties in the same Champions League match since 1998, while they have now conceded five in total in all competitions this season. And Lammens went from hero to zero as he undid his penalty save by promptly conceding another.

Referee Szymon Marciniak ruled that Youri Tielemans had committed a foul on Goncalo Inacio when he slid to poke the ball through to Amad Diallo, who headed into an empty net at the second attempt after the goalkeeper deflected his shot onto the crossbar.

Industry observers note that however, because Marciniak had already blown his whistle to stop play following the challenge from Tielemans, VAR could not intervene. Tielemans did not appear to commit a foul and instead won the ball fairly against Inacio when he went to ground to play the ball through to Amad. According to recent reports, if Marciniak hadn’t blown for the foul, the VAR would have been able to allow it, if they had ruled that Tielemans had not fouled Inacio. “It was a dreadful decision,” former United midfielder Owen Hargreaves noted on TNT Sports. “I don’t know what [the referee] saw. [Tielemans] played the ball. It wasn’t reckless.”.

The United captain and former Sporting player had already equalised when he converted from the spot, after Lisandro Martinez turned into a muarading left winger to win the third penalty of the night, but his second of the night will perhaps go down as the most bizarre goal of the season.

The assist went to a divot in the pitch, as a low left-footed shot from Fernandes struck the turf and diverted away from the stranded goalkeeper Kaique Pereira to find the net. The uneven pitch effectively acted as a ramp as the ball suddenly jumped up and flew over Pereira, who was left with no chance.

Just to add to the freakish nature of the goal, TNT Sports found that Fernandes appeared to have created the divot in the six-yard box the Sporting goal when he turned on the Estádio José Alvalade pitch, leading to his fortuitous bobble moments later.

According to recent reports, ivan Fresneda’s cross from the right wing went so high that it vanished from the frame of the TV screen, as the defender turned his back in disgust with how wasteful he had been. But when the ball eventually came down, Lammens presented Sporting with a gift.

Sources indicate that the goalkeeper dropped the ball at the feet of Luis Suarez, who hooked in his second of the match acrobatically to draw Sporting level. It was another error from the increasingly fragile Lammens, his second of the match and just a few weeks after his costly spill against Aston Villa.

Patrick Dorgu, Lisandro Martinez and Leny Yoro were all in close proximity as Kaique Pereira failed to punch clear an inswinging corner from Bruno Fernandes, with referee Marciniak ruling that Dorgu had jumped into the goalkeeper.

Pereira flapped at the cross as the ball slipped through his fingers, but Dorgu was ruled to have “committed a foul on the goalkeeper before the ball entered the goal” and Marciniak ruled it out after being sent to the pitchside monitor by VAR.

Earlier this month, United saw a very similar goal stand when Senne Lammens challenged Aston Villa goalkeeper Zion Suzuki and Dorgu scored to earn a 4-4 draw in the Premier League, but the KMI Panel later ruled that it was correctly awarded.

Sources indicate that this was salt in the wounds for United, as they received their fifth red card of the season, and fourth from their last five matches. Tielemans, of course, will have argued that he should not have received a yellow card in the first half, if Amad’s equaliser had been allowed to stand.

This time, Tielemans was late as he slid in on Sergi Altimira, although the Sporting midfielder certainly made the most out of what appeared to be minimal contact. Tielemans will now be suspended from the home match against RB Leipzig next month.

Mach7 Technologies and Rad AI declared a partnership on November 23, 2026, to connect Mach7’s Flamingo Worklist and eUnity enterprise viewer with Rad AI Reporting, aiming to move measurements and case context between image interpretation and radiology reporting without manual re-entry.

The companies are addressing a handoff that can persist even when imaging, AI and reporting systems are connected: measurements and other clinical data do not always transfer. They framed the problem against growing imaging volumes, increasingly complex studies and a radiologist workforce struggling to keep pace. The integration is in development, with availability to be confirmed later and features and timing subject to change.

Recent findings suggest that the companies strategy to demonstrate two capabilities at RSNA 2026. Bidirectional synchronization would allow the Mach7 applications and Rad AI Reporting to share patient, study and provider context, with updates flowing from either application as a radiologist moves through cases. Dynamic measurement exchange is designed to make lengths, areas and other measurements taken in eUnity available in real time for inclusion in the report, removing the need to retype those values.

Mach7’s eUnity product documentation describes a zero-footprint diagnostic viewer, meaning it runs in a supported browser without client software or a plug-in. It works across existing imaging systems and archives and includes tools for measuring distances, angles and selected regions. The viewer can also display third-party AI and computer-aided detection results beside the original study, including the algorithm name and version. The new partnership concerns the next step: getting measurements and case context into the report. (Related: The Partnership: Amazon SageMaker and Hugging Face...)

Rad AI’s reporting documentation describes a workflow that begins with natural dictation; its Omni Report feature then generates the report and its concluding impressions, personalized to the radiologist’s style. Real-time quality checks and radiologist review precede finalization. The proposed integration would supply shared context and measurements to a workflow that still leaves verification and sign-off with the radiologist.

Recent findings suggest that the products involved are at different commercial stages. Mach7’s Flamingo product page states that the platform is not yet for sale and describes its first release as designed. Its Worklist module uses customer-set priorities and rules, turnaround targets, per-study countdowns and early alerts as studies approach their targets. Mach7 does not analyze images or clinical content to rank studies, separating that worklist logic from diagnostic image analysis. eUnity and Mach7’s vendor-neutral archive, by contrast, are already available. Mach7’s RSNA 2026 page distinguishes those current-release products from a designated forward-looking station for forthcoming capabilities. The Rad AI integration is scheduled for demonstration at booth 4116 in Chicago’s McCormick Place during technical exhibits from November 29 to December 2, 2026.

It has been revealed that mach7 CEO Teri Thomas described the demonstration as an initial step. “Radiologists should be able to pick the best reporting tool for their reading room and have it work smoothly with their viewer from day one,” she said.

In a recent development, the companies initiative to extend the integration over time and shape its roadmap with input from mutual customers. For radiology groups combining separate viewer and reporting suppliers, the announcement establishes a specific interoperability path. Its practical significance depends on delivery: the companies still need to confirm availability, while mutual customers will help determine which extensions follow the initial synchronization and measurement exchange.

Arsenal were left sweating on the fitness of their goalkeeper David Raya after the Spaniard was forced off with a shoulder injury during the second half of the 2-2 draw at Newcastle on Saturday evening.

Sources indicate that it left Mikel Arteta’s side six points clear of Brighton and Manchester City at the top of the Premier League but Arsenal’s manager was more concerned about Raya following an accidental collision with Newcastle’s William Osula. The Spaniard was helped off in evident pain and looked close to tears.

In a recent development, “Obviously it’s not good David had to come off but at the moment I don’t know [the prognosis],” said Arteta, explaining that a keeper who kept seven clean sheets in the season’s opening 10 games will have the joint scanned on Sunday. “He will have an MRI tomorrow. Newcastle, though, improved significantly after the break and took the lead thanks to goals from Harvey Barnes and Malick Thiaw before Noni Madueke equalised.

Recent findings suggest that “My feeling is we have lost two points,” mentioned Arteta. “We were exceptional in the first half but we needed to score three or four goals and we didn’t. We knew there’d be a response but the way we gave Newcastle’s first goal away was completely unacceptable. We came back and scored a second goal but we lacked the moment of magic to kill the game. I don’t think we switched off in the second half; sometimes you have to praise the opposition.”.

According to recent reports, matthias Jaissle was left smiling after that second-half performance rescued a game watched by Newcastle’s chair, Yasir al-Rumayyan. He had flown in from Riyadh to witness his first match of the season and is believed to have complimented his head coach afterwards.

“What you saw in the second half was Newcastle’s DNA par excellence and that’s something we can build on,” remarked Jaissle. “But we’ve got to be humble and realistic. The first half was not good and the second half was excellent. I’ve got mixed feelings because a little more was possible but a point was probably fair.

“I was a bit frustrated at half-time but we encouraged the players to do the high press in the right way and adopt a front-foot approach. There was no hesitating, no doubting, just pure belief and we clicked. I knew we were able to do it, so I was really pleased. We felt unbelievable connections and synergy with the crowd.

Justin Hartley‘s hit series Tracker continues to bring fans surprise twists — including a recent shocking death.

Industry observers note that during the Sunday, November 22, episode of the hit CBS show, Colter (Hartley) was hired to find the owner of a bed and breakfast who went missing. Colter got assistance from rival rewardist Luke (Josh Holloway), but John was ultimately murdered by someone he put away behind bars in Japan before escaping to the United States. It has been revealed that colter and Luke were still able to save someone else’s life. However, in a departure from the show’s usual format, the final moments of the episode were focused on Colter informing the woman who hired him that he wasn’t able to bring John back.

Sunday’s episode was a surprise for viewers, who are used to seeing Colter save the day. While there have been a few close calls, Colter had a very high success rate on screen — the only deaths so far have been villains or missing people who were already deceased. Based on Jeffery Deaver‘s novel The Never Game, Tracker centers around Colter, who travels the country helping to find missing people and solving cases others couldn’t or wouldn’t. Hartley, 49, who stars in the show is also an executive producer, hinted at how far the show will push his character.

“I just love that when you watch a show like that and you tune into season 1 and then you tune into the last season, you see the development of the character and you go, ‘Wait a minute, are they playing different roles?’ But then if you watch it throughout the years, you experience those things with the characters,” he told TV Insider in September 2024. “As competent and confident as Colter is, I don’t at all think for a second that he doesn’t have a ton to learn, especially about himself and his family and all that.”.

It has been revealed that hartley continued, “Going forward, I think that will be how the show lives on a long runway, is that we keep developing this character and he becomes better at what he’s doing. He’s a restless man, and for an audience member, at least indicates that I love to watch, you love to see that growth of a character and we have that.”.

Hartley also warned that the character could die in the future.

In a recent development, “It’s important to keep upping the stakes. I like being Colter as a hero, finding people and all that. I also really like seeing him in a suspenseful thriller and a dangerous situation,” he told The Hollywood Reporter in April 2025. “I don’t want our audience to forget that this man is mortal, he’s not a superhero. He can die! The things he is doing are very very dangerous.”.

Executive producer Elwood Reid later weighed in on Colter’s fate, telling Us in October 2025, “Some of the danger is in here because he’s not a cop. He is this guy who is poking his nose in places. The network is always like, ‘He can get messed up, he can lose a fight, he can get conked on the head and he can have a gun pointed at him.’ Justin pitched an idea for the season 3 midseason finale where it doesn’t go well for Colter. That’s what makes him fun is he is not a superhero.”.

Reid noted that Tracker is always searching for ways to surprise viewers.

According to recent reports, “When I watch a lot of these types of indicates, the minute the character becomes infallible or perfect then I’m uninterested,” Reid explained. “I like when characters have flaws and make mistakes and are mortal and can be wounded and can screw up.”.

Sources indicate that he continued, “I’m very conscious of not making Colter too perfect. We are scuffing him up, letting him screw up and letting him do the wrong thing. I think that’s what makes the character fun to write — at least for me.”.

Tracker airs on CBS Sundays at 9 p.m. ET before streaming the next day on Paramount+.

Orange’s French-hosted AI mode runs OpenAI and Qwen models, not Anthropic’s

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Bridge, Orange’s head office in Issy-les-Moulineaux, near Paris.

According to recent reports, orange Business’s fresh Protected Mode keeps companies’ AI work in France. It offers models from Mistral, LightOn, Alibaba’s Qwen, Google and OpenAI. Anthropic is not on that list: its models are offered only in the platform’s other setting, Open Mode, according to the line-ups an Orange Business spokesperson shared with TNW. Orange updates the line-up regularly, the spokesperson added. (Related: Breaking: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Faster ...)

Protected Mode is part of Live Intelligence, Orange Business’s generative and agentic AI platform for companies. With the mode on, the models, agents, data and documents stay in France. Orange runs that environment across its data centres and its Cloud Avenue platform. Orange Business launched it for all customers on 17 November at its OpenTech event in Paris. (Related: Transformer-based Encoder-Decoder Models...)

“Businesses no longer want to choose between the power of artificial intelligence and the protection of their data,” said Anne-Marie Thiollet, chief product and marketing officer at Orange Business.

It has been revealed that protected Mode is not SecNumCloud-qualified at this stage, the spokesperson mentioned. SecNumCloud is the French cybersecurity agency ANSSI’s qualification for trusted cloud services. Two other Orange Business cloud offers do hold it: Cloud Avenue SecNum, in Grenoble data centres, and Cloud Avenue SecNum Dynamic, in Normandy. Both are infrastructure services for shared and dedicated environments.

Open Mode offers a wider choice of models from Anthropic, OpenAI, Google and Mistral, hosted outside Orange in Europe or Switzerland. In both modes, Orange keeps no data and uses none of it to train models. It calls this Zero Data Retention.

In a recent development, companies choose which employees get each mode. Consumers with access to both can switch in one click, and their conversations and agents stay separate between the two.

Sources indicate that protected Mode is included in Live Intelligence Portal licences, with no separate charge for turning it on, the spokesperson noted. Customers pay for models, agents and MCP connectors in units in both modes. For similar models, the two modes use units at broadly the same rate. The overall cost depends on which models customers choose and how much they use them.

Live Intelligence launched commercially in November 2024. All of its more than 150 active customers are outside Orange, the spokesperson stated. They include public bodies such as public healthcare organisations and local authorities. Many are small and mid-sized companies. Its 140,000 people include Orange staff, and Orange does not give a breakdown. The number is changing fast as customers find fresh uses, the spokesperson said.

Sources indicate that rouen University Hospital is rolling the platform out across the Rouen Cœur de Seine Hospital Group. SICAP, an electricity distributor in the Loiret and Eure-et-Loir regions, uses it for day-to-day work.

“We were looking for a French solution that was easy to use and met very practical day-to-day needs,” said Guillaume Thielon, IT project manager at SICAP.

In March, Orange added Live Intelligence Studio, tools to build and run AI agents made with LangChain, the agent startup. It is also adding a service catalogue to help small and medium-sized businesses put AI to work. The launch came alongside Orange’s Wolof AI models and €250m for Orange Ventures.

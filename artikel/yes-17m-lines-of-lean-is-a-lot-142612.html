According to recent reports, the recent proof of the Riemann hypothesis came with a statistic attached: the proof is 17 million lines of Lean. Beyond the philosophical questions of what it means to have a proof longer than any human could comprehend, I’m left with a simpler question: is that as large as it sounds? (Related: Large Language Models: A New Moore's Law?...)

To investigate, I hunted for collections of Lean proofs online. I found two and charted the lengths of their proofs.

(All charts have a log x axis, which makes 17m lines look a lot closer to 1m and 100k than it really is.).

Mathlib maintains a collection of famous proofs. There are over 1k but only 259 have Lean associated, which I chart here.

These are all human generated, and as we can see, they come nowhere close to 17m.

In a recent development, reservoir indexes every published Lean package, 938 of which have Lean code on GitHub. This one has a lot more data, including AI assisted proofs. We even have a few that are over 16m!:.

88m: Qiuzhen-CFSG/CFSG this appears to be an AI built proof that is still a work in progress.

Recent findings suggest that 36m: CBirkbeck/LeanBridge, a project linking the LMFDB database of number theory objects to Mathlib. This isn’t really a proof: about 98% of its Lean is files generated by scripts that check the database’s numbers one by one. The hand-written part is closer to 500k lines.

Industry observers note that 27m: anthropics/fermats-last-theorem is another Anthropic special: a complete proof of Fermat’s Last Theorem, published on GitHub in September. Its README says “the Lean sources were produced by AI agents building on human-written open-source Lean”, including the human FLT project led by Kevin Buzzard, which is about 60k lines and still in progress.

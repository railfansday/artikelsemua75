In a recent development, home Depot (HD +0.70%) delivered investors great news when it reported its strongest same-store sales growth since the third quarter of 2022, which largely came under the watchful eye of CFO Richard McPhail while CEO Ted Decker was on medical leave. McPhail will now take the helm of the firm coming off one of its strongest quarters in years, while Decker will become chairman. (Related: Latest Update: Arkansas sues VGW and Kalshi, citing $66...)

McPhail mentioned the company won't change course under his leadership, which makes sense. Over the past several years, Home Depot has been putting more emphasis on professional customers. McPhail has played a big role in that, helping oversee the recent acquisitions of SRS Distribution in 2024 and GMS last fall. Ann-Marie Campbell, who ran the day-to-day operations while Decker was out, will become chief operating officer. (Related: Industry News: “Determined” woman completes 7 marathons...)

The executive moves remove some uncertainty around Decker's health and put a team in charge that just helped Home Depot report its best quarterly results in years. While the stock didn't have a strong reaction, these changes should serve the home improvement company well going forward.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

The home improvement environment has been very tough the past four years. COVID stay-at-home restrictions pulled forward a lot of major home renovation projects, while rising interest rates have affected both housing turnover and financing. It was great to see Home Depot turn in 2.2% comparable-store growth both globally and in the U.S. It was also the sixth straight quarter that both have been positive, showing the strides the manufacturer has been making.

Recent findings suggest that the same-store sales growth was fueled by a 3% increase in average ticket size, as the number of transactions sank by 0.5%. Higher-ticket items drove the growth, with same-store sales on transactions over $1,000 climbing 4.5%, led by strength in power and appliances.

Management called out strength in power, which saw double-digit comp growth. Storage, plumbing, and electrical all saw mid-single-digit growth. Overall, 13 of Home Depot's 16 product categories recorded positive same-store sales growth. Both pros and DIY (do-it-yourself) turned in positive same-store sales, with Pro outperforming. Digital sales, meanwhile, jumped 13%.

Home Depot's total revenue increased 4.9% year over year to $43.4 billion, while adjusted earnings per share (EPS) rose 3% to $3.86. These results topped the analyst consensus calling for adjusted EPS of $3.84 on $42.8 billion in sales, according to data compiled by LSEG.

Looking ahead, Home Depot updated its full-year guidance for sales to grow 4.5% with a 1.4% increase in same-store sales and a 2% increase in adjusted EPS. It will open 15 upcoming stores this year. The organization previously guided for same-store sales growth of flat to up 2% and total sales growth of 2.5% to 4.5%, with adjusted EPS growth of flat to up 4%.

In a recent development, home Depot looks like it has turned the corner with another quarter of positive same-store sales growth. However, the current environment likely won't get easier. That's why its focus on professional customers, which continue to outperform, should remain its main focus.

The latest Leading Indicator of Remodeling Activity (LIRA) projections, which are published by the Joint Center for Housing Studies at Harvard University, show another likely slowdown next year. Given the state of the economy and higher rates compared to much of this past decade, that shouldn't be surprising.

Turning to valuation, Home Depot stock trades at a forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of about 21 times fiscal 2028 (ending January 2028) analyst estimates. That's reasonable for a blue chip stock that has been weathering a difficult environment for several years. As such, I consider the stock a solid core type of holding.

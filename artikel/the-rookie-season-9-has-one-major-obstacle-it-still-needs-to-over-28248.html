Over its last eight seasons, The Rookie has depicted many entertaining and enjoyable recurring villains, some better than others. However, none have come close to the best villain in the show's history: the deranged serial killer Rosalind Dyer. Expertly portrayed by the late, great Annie Wersching, Dyer might be gone, but she leaves an undeniable legacy that still hasn't been beat as The Rookie gears up for its return with Season 9. In a recent development, rosalind Dyer makes her impactful debut in the Season 2 episode, "The Dark Side," where she first meets John Nolan (Nathan Fillion) and other members of the Mid-Wilshire Division. After making a deal with the district attorney, Dyer agrees to reveal the burial sites of her victims whose bodies were never recovered. That sets off a chain of events, exposing another killer following in her footsteps. The killer is later revealed to be Caleb Wright (Michael Cassidy), Rosalind’s protégé and acolyte. Caleb later drugs and kidnaps Lucy Chen (Melissa O'Neil), burying her alive before she's eventually found and saved by Tim Bradford (Eric Winter) in "Day of Death." Interestingly, it's Rosalind who first hints at the corrupt nature of Nolan's then-friend and mentor Nick Armstrong (Harold Perrineau). Rosalind was a sociopath who delighted in the suffering of others, as well as an expert manipulator. Rosalind would recruit or brainwash various "acolytes" to commit horrendous acts or murders in her name. During the Season 5 episode, "Double Down," Rosalind proves her extreme cunning and manipulative skills, brainwashing her lawyer into her newest acolyte, aiding in her daring escape from police custody. Rosalind meets her climactic end later in the season, but not through the actions of John Nolan or anyone in the Mid-Wilshire unit.

In a recent development, ever since her first appearance, Rosalind developed a sick and twisted fixation for John Nolan. Their dynamic somewhat resembles Batman and The Joker of DC Comics. She became obsessed with Nolan’s righteous nobility and altruistic nature, wanting to transform the boy scout into a stone-cold killer, just like her. She nearly pulls it off in her final appearance in "The Choice," setting up a death trap for Nolan's girlfriend and future wife, Bailey Nune (Jenna Dewan). Rosalind offers Nolan a choice: execute her, or Bailey will die. Ultimately, Nolan refuses Rosalind's choice, and Bailey is thankfully saved by Nolan's colleagues.

Recent findings suggest that strangely, Rosalind still succeeds in the end despite her failure to push Nolan over the edge. She escaped from prison and evaded capture, only meeting Nolan for one last game through her own machinations. Rosalind voluntarily gave up because she was too famous and knew it was only a matter of time before she would get caught again. Although Nolan refused to kill Rosalind, her accomplice executed her instead. Ultimately, she got what she wanted: a quick execution instead of returning to prison. Rosalind's death endures as one of the series' most shocking and impactful moments across all eight seasons.

Industry observers note that rosalind's death was utterly surprising. Lucy is in total disbelief when she observes Rosalind's dead body, but Tim puts it best when the team looks upon Rosalind's corpse: "She only seemed larger than life." Rosalind, indeed, exuded an undeniable aura and charisma that made her appear as a force of nature, which is part of what made her such a dangerous, formidable, and terrifying character. She played a role in Lucy's kidnapping and the deaths of many innocent lives, and she nearly drove Nolan to compromise his ideals as well.

Wersching was always a highly versatile and underrated thespian, but her portrayal of a remorseless serial killer clinched her skills as an actor. Wersching imbued Rosalind with an ominous sense of dread, yet she always radiated confidence and assuredness. Rosalind never looked panicked in any situation, which is another reason she was so frightening: her eerie calmness. Wersching portrayed Rosalind Dyer as if she had literal ice in her veins.

According to recent reports, it's no coincidence that some of the show's best episodes or storylines involve Rosalind Dyer. The Rookie has depicted other recurring notable villains, such as Monica Stevens (Bridget Regan) and Oscar Hutchinson (Matthew Glave). However, none of them have ever come close to matching Rosalind in her sheer cunning. Additionally, none of the other villains have managed to fill Rosalind's void following the character’s exit. Wersching delivered a memorable performance that will not be forgotten, as her character left an indelible mark on the show's legacy. After so many seasons, The Rookie still hasn't found a worthy replacement for such a compelling character.

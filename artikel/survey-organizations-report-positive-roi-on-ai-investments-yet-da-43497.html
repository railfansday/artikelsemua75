Survey: Organizations Report Positive ROI on AI Investments, Yet Data Readiness and Oversight Lag Behind

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to a survey by data and AI services provider Further, enterprises are reporting benefits from artificial intelligence even as their data preparation and oversight processes remain incomplete.

According to recent reports, the firm's recent State of Enterprise AI Maturity research surveyed 515 U.S.-based AI decision-makers. Just 5% believed their organizations' data was largely ready for AI.

Recent findings suggest that the findings suggest that gaining value from individual AI applications and preparing to operate them more broadly are separate challenges. Reported returns alone do not establish that an organization has the foundations to sustain or expand those results.

Further found that 37% of respondents' organizations had paused, scaled back, or abandoned an underperforming AI initiative in the previous 12 months. In a recent development, meanwhile, 29% reported completing a formal data audit and remediation strategy. The research also placed 69% of organizations in the exploring, experimenting, or implementing stages of adoption. (Related: AI for Game Development: Creating a Farming Game in 5 D...)

Further distinguishes data prepared for conventional reporting from data prepared for AI. Its explanation emphasizes data origins and documentation, along with access controls that support ongoing evaluation and governance.

Recent findings suggest that for readers assessing their own readiness, the practical glitch is whether data supports the requirements of a particular application. A collection suitable for a dashboard may need additional preparation before it can reliably support an AI system.

Sources indicate that seventy-seven percent reported a positive return on AI investment. However, only 3% remarked their organizations had a documented AI-audit process.

Recent findings suggest that the findings measure different things: perceived financial benefit and the existence of a repeatable oversight process. Neither substitutes for the other.

"In reality, confidence without a documented process is exposure," noted Kierra Dotson, associate director of AI strategy and governance at Further. "Closing that gap doesn't take upcoming technology; it requires an auditable governance process.".

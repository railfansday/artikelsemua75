Savannah Chrisley is clapping back at “disgusting” trolls for leaving hateful comments amid her pregnancy.

Chrisley fired back at the social media user, calling them “absolutely DISGUSTING.”.

“To wish illness upon an innocent baby because you disagree with his mother’s beliefs is a level of hatred I will never understand,” the Chrisley Knows Best alum wrote.

According to recent reports, “I am unapologetically conservative, pro-life and a firm believer in parental rights and medical freedom,” Chrisley shared. “Every family deserves the right to make informed medical decisions for their children without government overreach or intimidation.”.

Chrisley, who is expecting her first baby with husband Jason Galanis, reminded her followers, “You can disagree with my beliefs, but wishing harm upon my unborn son is beyond cruel.”.

According to recent reports, she reiterated, “Protecting children should NEVER be political. Parental choice is a RIGHT, not a privilege. May God restore some humanity to this world.”.

Chrisley received the controversial messages shortly after she defended her choice not to vaccinate her future children during an episode of her “Unlocked” podcast.

“I am not, but again, that is my choice,” Chrisley noted on the Tuesday, November 17, episode when speaking about whether she’ll vaccinate her baby. “I have friends who are having babies around the same time … [and] they do believe in all the vaccines. Great, like, you have to do what’s best for you and your family. What I love is I have found a pediatrician who is not a one-size-fits-all.”.

Chrisley’s decision not to vaccinate her baby — which she said could change as he gets older — comes amid continued scrutiny of the medical practice amid an uptick in anti-vaxxers.

Recent findings suggest that while the CDC states that vaccines administered to millions of children in the U.S. each year are safe and effective against infectious diseases such as hepatitis B, chickenpox and measles, the Pan American Health Organization revealed on Tuesday that America has lost its measles-elimination status for the first time in 26 years. The historic setback comes under the leadership of Health Secretary Robert F. Kennedy Jr., who is a self-proclaimed vaccine skeptic.

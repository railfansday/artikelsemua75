Scott Parker was in a particularly festive mood during yesterday’s pre-match press conference ahead of West Ham’s clash with Stoke City on Saturday. The Hammers gaffer trotted out a rather obscure festive analogy about snowballs getting bigger, which is all very well and good.

According to recent reports, but if he’s not careful, that little ball of ice could start hurtling down a mountainside and become an avalanche. West Ham are now six games without a victory, and Parker is still awaiting his first win as head coach. (Related: Show, Don't Certify: How to Prepare for Your First Data...)

Speaking during his pre-match press conference, Parker remarked: “You have to make sure that little snowball doesn’t become a massive one, and that’s certainly something that’s on my mind.”. (Related: Scientists find “field” of washing machines in ocean du...)

One of the things I most liked about Parker’s first couple of interviews was that he didn’t revert to football clichés, and his snowball comparison certainly wasn’t one. But it came amid the usual talk of having to do better and his assertion that we’ll be judged in May.

Probably what concerned me most was that he feels performances have been good. I do believe the Hammers boss is relying heavily on data, and that is something that could be misleading. As the old adage goes, there are lies, damned lies and statistics.

Industry observers note that make no mistake about it, West Ham’s attacking statistics were heavily boosted by a flurry of activity, largely initiated by Tomáš Souček, between the 85th and 90th minutes. By and large, throughout the game at Fratton Park, his team were easily contained.

There are two things Parker must prioritise against Stoke, and it’s going to be a tricky thing to balance because not only must West Ham score first, but they must also ensure that Stoke don’t do the same.

Whoever Parker picks between the sticks will need protecting.

Therefore, Parker is going to be caught between a rock and a hard place, wanting to protect whichever nervous goalkeeper is between the sticks whilst trying to ensure West Ham are not left chasing the game from a deficit.

I think it may well mean we’re in for a cagey affair because, whilst the game remains at nil-nil, the worst hasn’t happened. I’m not sure how good that will be for a nervous London Stadium crowd.

Somehow, the Irons boss must get his message across to players who look very short on motivation, which is a concern bearing in mind the manager has only been in the Hammers hot seat for five minutes.

According to recent reports, whether that rousing rallying cry comes in the form of snowballs, chimneys or Santa’s sleigh matters not. Because if Parker doesn’t get a massive improvement from the next few games, he may well find that snowball makes for a very unjolly Christmas.

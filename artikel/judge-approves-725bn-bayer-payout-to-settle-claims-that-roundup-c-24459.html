Judge approves $7.25bn Bayer payout to settle claims that Roundup causes cancer

German firm faced 65,000 claims from people who said they developed cancer after using weedkiller.

A Missouri judge on Friday approved Bayer’s $7.25bn settlement of lawsuits ⁠alleging that its weedkiller ​Roundup causes cancer, potentially resolving litigation that has weighed on the German company for close to a decade.

Judge Timothy Boyer of a state court in St Louis ⁠found the settlement “fair, reasonable and adequate”.

He overruled objections that payouts to individual cancer victims would be too low because claimants who previously settled were paid more.

The judge also said delaying payouts could ⁠force victims, including those whose cancer is terminal, to wait “an indeterminate number of years” to be paid. (Related: Tech Report: What Couples Married For 40 Years Do When ...)

Bayer had faced approximately ​65,000 claims in US state and federal courts from ‌people who noted they developed cancer ‌after using Roundup at home or on the job. (Related: Industry News: Politics Takes A Toll On Your Health — W...)

The settlement announced in February aimed to resolve nearly all current ‌and future Roundup claims in the US.

“The court’s final approval is a decisive milestone in the company’s strategy to mitigate the risks of both pending and future Roundup litigation,” Bayer’s CEO, Bill Anderson, said in a statement.

Bayer has faced extensive litigation over Roundup, and had seen its share price fall 46% since it bought Monsanto, which produces the weedkiller, for $63bn in 2018.

The company settled much of the Roundup litigation for $10.9bn in 2020, ‌but was unable to win court approval of a $1.25bn settlement to resolve future Roundup claims.

Bayer has long maintained that Roundup and its active ingredient glyphosate were safe, and that decades ​of studies had shown that glyphosate did not cause cancer.

The settlement was approved five months after the US supreme court overturned a $1.25m jury award for a Missouri man, John Durnell, who blamed years of exposure to glyphosate in Roundup for his non-Hodgkin lymphoma.

In a 7-2 decision, the court agreed with Bayer that a federal law regulating pesticides barred failure-to-warn claims brought under ⁠state law, undercutting a central pillar of many lawsuits.

Christopher Seeger, a lawyer for ​many of the plaintiffs, noted the Durnell ​decision narrowed the ability of Roundup victims ​to pursue claims in court.

“We look forward to getting this relief into the hands of all ​Roundup victims as rapidly ‌as possible,” he ​said in a statement.

Lawyers for ​some of the Roundup victims who objected to the settlement did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

The settlement excludes some lawsuits that remain pending in federal court. Bayer has remarked that about 200 of those cases were still being litigated, and that the supreme court decision gave it strong defenses.

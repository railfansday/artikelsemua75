According to recent reports, the answer is: I’m not sure. I genuinely doubt Amazon is making a profit on this soundbar.

For something this inexpensive, bass is surprisingly competent, and it has a dedicated dialogue mode on top of two EQs for music and movies. I never struggled to make out dialogue, even in action-heavy scenes, which is something the TV Soundbar’s significant brother, the TV Soundbar Plus, struggled with.

According to recent reports, now, let’s get one thing straight. The Amazon Fire TV Soundbar will not, and does not, sound like its more expensive brethren. Want Sonos Ray ($219) or Bose TV Speaker 2nd Gen ($299) quality? It’s not here.

However, this is an $119 soundbar. That’s it. For the price of a monthly cellphone bill, you get a soundbar. And while it doesn’t have the same detail or controlled bass you’d expect on a premium model, that’s okay. That’s not what the Amazon Fire TV Soundbar is, and that’s fine. (Related: Japan debuts a ‘dumbphone’ that doesn’t connect to inte...)

Even so, the Amazon Fire TV Soundbar still sounds better than TV speakers — and that’s all it’s trying to be.

I always test soundbars with 4K Blu-rays, basic-tier Netflix, and music via Bluetooth or AirPlay (connectivity dependent). To test the Amazon Fire TV Soundbar, I watched “Top Gun: Maverick,” “K-Pop Demon Hunters” and “The Batman” on 4K Blu-ray.

In a recent development, “Top Gun: Maverick”’is opening scene is a goldmine for speaker testing. There are all these different sounds fighting for attention: the iconic pulsing bassline in “Danger Zone,” high-frequency in-scene effects like whooshing ropes and clinking metal, and overpowering sounds like jet engines taking off.

I could hear the propulsive bass in “Danger Zone,” which didn’t overpower the jet engines. While there was a little tinniness at the top of the high-end, I really didn’t mind this in the grand scheme of things. The Amazon Fire TV Soundbar performed admirably.

Dialogue was also clear, even in radioed dialogue scenes. While deep voices, like Tom Cruise’s, caused a little rumbling, I could still understand all dialogue.

“KPop Demon Hunters” also sounded strong: despite a little tinniness in some of the in-movie songs’ percussion, dialogue was particularly exceptional. Bass in the soundtrack was powerful, and diegetic sounds like slamming doors came through well.

Industry observers note that afterwards, I watched “The Batman,” which is a challenging movie for soundbars thanks to the moody dialogue and near-constant rainfall. I could hear every word muttered (yes, muttered; that’s how everyone speaks in this movie). Even footsteps across rainy concrete came through clearly.

In a recent development, however, loud scenes — like the club scene — pushed dialogue to the back and overpowered the in-scene electronic music, which meant I strained to hear a few of the Penguin’s words. However, I didn’t experience this in any other movie. It’s a Batman thing.

I was extremely surprised and intrigued by this sound quality. It’s miles better than I thought it would be.

The Amazon Fire TV Soundbar has a range of EQ modes: dialogue, movie, music, and you can individually adjust bass. I had to cycle through the EQs to activate each EQ.

Sources indicate that however, the EQs actually improved my viewing experience. I switched between “dialogue” and “movie” while watching “Top Gun”; movie increased bass; dialogue — ever-so surprisingly — clarified dialogue. Music made speech a little too sharp, but obviously, watching a movie in “music” mode is very much "play stupid games, win stupid prizes".

Industry observers note that you can even listen to music via Bluetooth, but I wouldn’t write home about this. It would do in a pinch, but the percussion was tinny and the soundbar narrowed the soundstage. It’s fine, but I would just listen to music on one of the best speakers, like the JBL Flip 7.

It’s great to have these options on such a budget-friendly soundbar.

There’s very little I dislike about this soundbar, hence the 4.5-star rating.

In a recent development, I feel almost unfair marking this as a con, but Amazon kind of brought it upon itself by giving the soundbar DTS Virtual:X and Dolby Audio. There is no Dolby Atmos or any kind of surround sound. You only really find DTS Virtual:X on select DVDs, so you’ll need to ensure you buy compatible discs to really benefit from this.

Dolby Atmos content — like premium Netflix or Disney+ — will not play as true surround sound because the soundbar is physically incapable of it. If you want spatial audio, you'll have to go for the slightly more expensive Amazon Fire TV Soundbar Plus.

This is an incredibly cheap soundbar. I really don’t know how Amazon is making a profit on it, if it is at all. At $119, there’s really not much competition.

The only soundbar that comes close is the $119 Sony HT-S100F, which is one of the best cheap soundbars on the market. However, the Sony’s bass is a little lackluster. While the Amazon Fire TV Soundbar’s bass can be a little rumbly at times, at least it isn’t lackluster. What you prefer will depend on you.

In terms of similarly priced competition… well, that’s kind of it. I’ve also tested the Majority Naga 60, which is incredibly cheap at $67, but it can suffer from pinchy treble and rattling bass.

In a recent development, if you want better-than-TV-speaker sound on a serious budget, there’s only one answer to this question: Yes. Actually, wait, I’ve just thought of another: Heck yes.

It has been revealed that I was extremely surprised by the Amazon Fire TV Soundbar’s sound, and I’m sure you will be, too. After testing some disappointing and overpriced Amazon Fire Ember TVs, I wasn’t expecting much — but the soundbar completely blew me away. It’s room-filling, mostly well-controlled, and has extremely intelligible dialogue.

Recent findings suggest that it's much better than the Amazon Fire TV Soundbar Plus ($139 on Amazon), whose built-in subwoofer overpowers dialogue in action-heavy scenes. If you have a little more to spend, you might want to look into the Amazon Fire TV Plus with Subwoofer ($229) or the model with Rears ($349).

It has been revealed that however, for those on a serious budget, this is the best option out there. Hands-down.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

After a hacker stole customer data, Binance founder Changpeng Zhao advised 1Password consumers to change all the passwords stored in the service.

According to recent reports, 1Password offered different advice about what users should do after the incident was disclosed on Nov. 25. The password management service remarked hackers hadn't gained access to the passwords stored in users' vaults, so there was no need to reset them.

"If you use 1Password, recommend you to change ALL your passwords. It's a pain in the neck, but necessary," Zhao mentioned on X, replying to a post from Coin Bureau about the breach.

If you have a crypto exchange login stored in 1Password, this distinction is important.

Scammers could still use the stolen email addresses to contact people they know are 1Password customers.

In a recent development, 1Password started investigating on Nov. 19 after detecting signs of possible unauthorized access to an employee's corporate GitHub account. (Related: Tech Report: AI for Game Development: Creating a Farmin...)

It has been revealed that from there, the hacker was able to steal private source code and inject malicious code into 1Password's backend services. The malicious code then downloaded certain administrative data about customers.

1Password publicly disclosed the breach on Nov. 25, six days later.

The firm did not specify how many customers were affected or explain exactly how the hacker managed to take over the employee's account.

The stolen data included customer names, login email addresses, account and user IDs, and timestamps indicating account creation and last use.

The hacker also gained access to 1Password's software development and deployment systems. (Related: Breaking: 3D Asset Generation: AI for Game Development ...)

1Password disabled the compromised employee's GitHub account and revoked the stolen access token.

The tech giant also restored a clean version of the affected service and revoked or replaced potentially exposed internal credentials.

1Password also engaged independent security experts to review its response.

1Password recommends that customers check the 1Password app or type the firm's website address directly into their browser if they receive an unexpected invitation or recovery message.

The tech giant states it will never contact people to request their account password or Secret Key.

It has been revealed that update: 1Password claims that there is no need to change one's 1Password account password or any of the passwords saved in one's vault. No customer passwords were exposed in this incident.

Hi CZ! There’s no need to change your 1Password account password or any of the passwords you’ve saved in your vault. No customer passwords were exposed in this incident. We don’t store your account password or Secret Key, and getting into our backend systems didn’t give the attacker the keys to decrypt your vault. The attacker did copy account information for some customers, including names and email addresses, so we recommend watching for 1Password invitations or account recovery emails you weren’t expecting.

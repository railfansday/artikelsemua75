Industry observers note that blondie has delivered the latest offering from their upcoming album, High Noon, out Feb. 26.

Sources indicate that their latest single, “Come Back Home,” is buoyant and evocative, despite its forlorn lyrics longing for a place to settle down. Written by Jess Wolfe and Holly Laessig of Lucius, who also contribute their vocal harmonies, the track began as a personal song before finding its way to Blondie. “We had been on tour for a few years, and wrote this song; a sort of reflection of being away for too long, and the ways in which distance and the road can mess with your mind and your relationships,” stated Wolfe and Laessig in a statement. Speaking about the opportunity to work with Blondie, they said: “We grew up with Debbie Harry as the ultimate female icon of punk, rock, and disco—paving a path for women in music, blending styles, and reimagining herself. We never dreamed a song of ours would become part of the Blondie story.”.

According to recent reports, blondie guitarist Chris Stein likens “Come Back Home” to the feeling of Alison Krauss’s “Down to the River to Pray” from the Coen Brothers’ 2000 tragicomedy O Brother, Where Art Thou? The Blondie single carries the “same haunting, almost spiritual quality, with a sense of simplicity and restraint,” stated Stein. Sources indicate that the track follows “Spectral Eyes,” the album’s Johnny Marr-penned lead single, which released in October and marked Blondie’s first newly introduced music since 2017’s Pollinator. High Noon, their first album in nine years, spans 13 tracks and includes “Heartbreak Kid,” written by Stein; “Sleepwalking,” penned by Glen Matlock; and “Man Who Had No Face,” co-written by Stein and frontwoman Debbie Harry. The band also revisits “Little Metal Drummer,” which was originally recorded by Blondie co-founding keyboardist Jimmy Destri for his solo debut, Heart On A Wall. Both Harry and co-founding drummer Clem Burke, who died in April 2025, performed on the 1981 original recording.

“This album is classic Blondie — an amalgamation of all our different influences and eras into something newly introduced,” Harry mentioned in a previous statement. “This music feels grounded in the present, shaped by who we are and how we’re living now.”.

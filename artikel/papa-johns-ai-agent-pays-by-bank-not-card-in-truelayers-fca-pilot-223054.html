Papa John’s AI agent pays by bank, not card, in TrueLayer’s FCA pilot

Francesco Simoneschi, co-founder and CEO of TrueLayer.

In a recent development, an AI agent at Papa John’s has paid for a customer’s order straight from their bank account, with no card. TrueLayer calls it the first agentic Pay by Bank payment to a named UK retailer. The London payments organization announced the pilot on Friday, the day of Black Friday sales. It has been revealed that the test ran with UK banks including Lloyds and NatWest, inside the Financial Conduct Authority’s Supercharged Sandbox. TrueLayer presented what it learned to the regulator and the industry at the FCA’s showcase on 26 November. The order itself was nine mini margherita pizzas, the Guardian reported. The customer links a bank account, not a card, to their Papa John’s account through open banking. Papa John’s AI agent helps with the order and then requests the payment from that account.

When linking the account, the customer sets spending limits with their bank, per merchant or over a set period. The bank checks every payment against those limits and declines anything above them, so the agent cannot overspend. The customer approves inside their bank app, and the agent never sees bank logins or passwords. The customer can cancel the arrangement from the merchant or the banking app at any time.

According to recent reports, the payments use variable recurring payment (VRP) connections, which let a provider take repeated payments of different amounts under one approval. TrueLayer said the pilot was the first time Lloyds’ and NatWest’s VRP systems were used for agentic payments.

In a recent development, trueLayer argues that bank payments suit AI agents better than cards, because shoppers can set limits with their bank without sharing card details. It also states non-card payments can keep competition alive in how merchants accept money as agents take on more shopping.

“Agents will choose the payment rail that is fastest, most secure and lowest cost, not the one people use out of habit,” said Francesco Simoneschi, TrueLayer’s chief executive and co-founder, in the announcement.

“Today it’s pizza. Tomorrow it’s everything else,” he added.

Recent findings suggest that the rival model is already on sale in the UK. Meta’s Muse agent released there this week and pays with a customer’s card. Meta also declined an FCA test of Muse in the same regulatory programme.

The Papa John’s payment remains a pilot, not a service open to all customers. TrueLayer mentioned the showcase will feed into the conditions and standards needed to use the technology safely at scale.

The timing matters for every organization building agents that move money. On 24 November the FCA warned AI agent builders that existing rules apply to them and that some may need its authorisation.

According to recent reports, simoneschi and Luca Martinetti founded TrueLayer in 2016. The manufacturer indicates more than 30 million people in 22 countries use its network. It processes more than $150bn in payments a year, the company said.

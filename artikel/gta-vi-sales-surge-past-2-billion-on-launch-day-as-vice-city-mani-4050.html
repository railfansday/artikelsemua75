The long-awaited and twice-delayed launch of Grand Theft Auto VI has made a killing and taken over the zeitgeist.

Sources indicate that by killing, we don’t just mean the mayhem of Vice City. We mean cold hard cash, digitally.

Industry observers note that less than 24 hours after the fictional Florida-set video game went live at midnight, GTA VI has made more than $2.36 billion, according to Sensor Tower. “GTA VI has captivated consumers, becoming 2026’s best-selling game based on pre-orders alone, with total pre-order revenue exceeding $2 billion through launch day,” the market intelligence company’s SVP of Video Game Insights Karl Kontus told Deadline on Thursday. Recent findings suggest that in fact, with the Miami Heat‘s fully GTA VI-themed primetime 137-130 win over the Milwaukee Bucks on Wednesday as but one example of the vast marketing machine at play, sales surged in the final hours before launch as fans rushed to download the 212GB game. According to recent reports, cementing the latest Rockstar Games offering as the most successful entertainment industry launch ever, the decade and more in the making GTA VI saw sales rise $710 million since early Wednesday, when presales sat at an already record-breaking $1.65 billion. Currently, the download-only GTA VI has sold 24.59 million units, with 75% of them on PlayStation and the remainder on Xbox consoles.

At this rate, with millions around the globe seemingly taking an adult snow day or PTO to play the first fresh GTA game in 13 years, VI is on track to reach further financial heights of $3 billion within weeks. That figure could be dwarfed when the single-player GTA VI lands on the multiplayer and lucrative GTA Online platform in the next several months.

As large as that number will go, leaving far behind the previous launch-day record $800 million GTA V earned in 2013, the (mis)adventures of crooks on the rise Jason and Lucia in the corrupt and free-wheeling humid state of Leonida is having an almost equal impact on the culture.

A throwback in more ways than one to the long-lost days of American monoculture, the eighth full game in the GTA franchise has conquered the conversation online and offline today.

Estimates of economic losses due to absentee workers may be offset by GTA VI profits, but nothing could stop a full partisan upbraiding from Fox News. “If this generation wants to preach about affordability, but they’re willing to cost billions of dollars in revenue loss on a day like today to go play a game, that probably tells you everything you need to know about what we need to be talking with this younger generation about,” the Murdoch-owned cabler newser’s Harris Faulkner declared this morning. “Get yourself to work.”.

Of course, taking into account the controversy that the ultra-violent GTA franchise has courted since its 1997 debut, such a berating falls nicely into the best tradition of P.T. Barnum’s supposed quip of “there is no such thing as bad publicity.” Along with Fox, CNN, NewsNation, the BBC, the New York Times, The Guardian and the upscale New Yorker have all run segments and features on VI today. On YouTube and Twitch, hundreds of live feeds are up busting internet viewership with millions watching people playing the game, which features more than 150 hours of gameplay, detailing hacks, Easter eggs and just cruising around.

To that, GTA VI has also been nam- dropped and appropriated today by an unlikely collection of bandwagon jumpers.

In a recent development, the White House, HGTV, the President of Brazil, a sycophantic Vice President JD Vance, and Miami International Airport have all been hooking themselves to GTA VI. In some witty, some cringe-inducing, and some just meh posts, Matt Johnson’s The The (who legitimately has a tune again on one of the many in-game radio stations), Spotify, the Interior Department, the UK’s Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office, and LA County District Attorney Nathan Hochman lathered themselves in some VI too.

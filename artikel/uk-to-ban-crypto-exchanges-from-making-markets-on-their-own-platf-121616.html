Industry observers note that the UK is carving out a role for third-party firms that make markets in spot Bitcoin. From Oct. 25, 2027, they can buy and sell on qualifying crypto trading platforms without permission to deal as principal for that activity. The Financial Conduct Authority’s final rules bar UK platform operators from making markets on their own venues.

HM Treasury made the amending regulations on Nov. 18, 2026, adding Article 9UA to the UK’s regulated activities framework. The provision excludes market making on a qualifying cryptoasset trading platform from Article 9T, which covers dealing in qualifying cryptoassets as principal. A separate part of Article 9UA excludes trading strictly for a firm’s own purposes when it provides no service to anyone else.

It has been revealed that A qualifying platform brings together multiple third-party buyers and sellers so their orders can result in trades in qualifying cryptoassets. The exclusion covers market making in those assets, including spot Bitcoin.

In a recent development, in its draft guidance, the FCA describes a market maker as a firm that stands ready to buy and sell listed assets on a continuing basis at prices it sets. The regulator proposes that responding to requests for quotes on the platform can also qualify.

In a recent development, operating a UK qualifying cryptoasset trading platform requires FCA authorization. The FCA’s final venue rules require operators to identify, document and monitor market-making users and disclose the terms of relevant incentives. A separate affiliate may trade on the venue under the same access rules as other users.

An operator can step between buyer and seller to execute client orders on its own platform as a matched principal when it holds the appropriate additional permission and follows venue rules that give it no discretion over how orders are matched. An operator that executes client orders against its own capital away from its own venue needs principal-dealing permission and must disclose its role and manage conflicts.

Recent findings suggest that the UK rules give distinct treatment to third-party market making, a venue trading for itself and a venue executing client orders from Oct. 25, 2027.

Market making on a qualifying platform is excluded from principal dealing. On its own venue, a UK operator needs separate permission to execute orders as matched principal.

Market making that falls within Article 9UA carries no principal-dealing application requirement for that activity.

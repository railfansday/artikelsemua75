Inside Slayyyter’s Massive Year: From ‘WOR$T GIRL’ Breakthrough to Best New Artist Grammy Nomination

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that when Slayyyter released her third album, WOR$T GIRL IN AMERICA, in March, she considered it to be her swan song. Her previous two albums, 2021’s Troubled Paradise and 2023’s Starfucker, hadn’t charted on the Billboard 200, and the pop singer admitted in countless interviews that she believed her most recent effort would be her last.

Instead, the reaction was extraordinary. WOR$T GIRL IN AMERICA hit No. 22 on the Billboard 200 and No. 1 on Billboard‘s Top Dance Albums chart. That success was enough to drive Slayyyter to a sold-out tour supporting her album (and the recent announcement that she’ll be headlining Madison Square Garden next June), but her substantial year didn’t end there. On Monday (Nov. 16), nominations for this year’s Grammys were declared, and Slayyyter received four nods: best new artist, best dance/electronic album, best dance pop recording for her song “DANCE…” and best dance/electronic recording for her song “CRANK.”.

It has been revealed that to cap off the year, Slayyyter has one final trick up her sleeve: On Dec. 4, she’ll release wor$t man in america, her follow-up to WOR$T GIRL IN AMERICA. Rather than a traditional deluxe, the dance-pop artist has chosen to extend the original album’s universe with nine newly introduced songs, including its first four singles: “brand new chanel$,” “crank 2,” “i think he’s got a girl” and “hey boyyy.”. All of this has contributed to RECORDS’ Andrew Saltman being named Billboard‘s Executive of the Week. As senior vp of artist development, Saltman helped spearhead Slayyyter’s album (and subsequent Grammy) campaign. He oversaw the marketing and digital strategy for WOR$T GIRL IN AMERICA and, now, wor$t man in america. And he attests they’re just getting started. This week, Slayyyter received four Grammy nominations, including for Best Upcoming Artist. What key decision did you make to help make that happen?

What an amazing week for Slayyyter and the entire team. When we first started crafting the campaign around “BEAT UP CHANEL$,” I had a best new artist nomination in the back of my mind. It was certainly a lofty goal, but one I genuinely believed was achievable. I wasn’t necessarily saying that out loud at the time, but I knew we had something special. My focus was on building a campaign that would introduce Slayyyter’s vision to a much broader audience and, in turn, help the industry recognize what she was creating.

The key decision was to commit fully to that vision and build a thoughtful, long-term campaign around it, rather than trying to fit her into an existing industry playbook. I think a significant part of my job, particularly at a joint venture label that puts such an emphasis on artist development, is to champion our artists and make believers out of the industry, our partners, and, of course, the fans. I led the overall rollout strategy for WOR$T GIRL IN AMERICA from the label side and was very hands-on in bringing it to life alongside our team and partners, always with Slayyyter’s creative vision at the center. She has such a phenomenal creative vision, and I feel strongly that my job is to amplify that vision rather than force certain strategies or perceived best practices onto an artist.

Recent findings suggest that one of the benefits of being at a label like RECORDS is that we’re able to get in the weeds with our artists and their management teams to develop bespoke strategies that work best for each individual artist and campaign. Slayyyter’s management team at Foundations, led by Kenny Consor, and her publicity team at The Oriel have been incredible partners throughout this process. We’re also fortunate to have an amazing partner in Columbia Records, who have supported our decisions and brought their tremendous experience and expertise to the table. Ultimately, these four nominations, especially Best New Artist, are an incredible recognition of Slayyyter’s talent, originality and the years of work she’s put into getting here.

Getting a Best Fresh Artist nomination is a significant achievement in a year stacked with rising talent. How did you approach the campaign around not just the dance/electronic categories but the more mainstream categories, including BNA?

From the beginning, we felt strongly that WOR$T GIRL IN AMERICA was a project that couldn’t be defined by any one genre. It’s a melting pot of influences, from electronic and dance to pop, rock and even metal. Slayyyter revealed an incredible amount of range on this album, and while some of the songs aren’t necessarily what you would consider traditionally commercial records, I actually think that’s what made the project so special and helped it stand out in an exceptionally crowded year.

In a recent development, our approach was never to build a campaign around fitting into particular Grammy categories or trying to manufacture mainstream appeal. We wanted to embrace everything that made this project unique and introduce Slayyyter to audiences well beyond the dance and electropop scenes. I’ve always believed that great artists push culture forward rather than simply fit the mold of what’s at the moment popular, and that’s exactly what Slayyyter has done with this album.

Industry observers note that one example that really stands out to me is “DANCE…” Slayyyter felt strongly about keeping the song’s long, building intro. Conventional wisdom might suggest getting to the hook as quickly as possible, but we trusted her instincts. Sure enough, that very intro became the sound that went viral on social media and helped turn the song into a cultural phenomenon this year. It’s a perfect example of why I believe in trusting an artist’s creative instincts rather than trying to fit their music into a formula.

She actually put it best in an interview with Billboard earlier this year: “I’m going to make it cool to myself, and not try to make it sound appealing to anyone but me.” I think that mentality is a huge part of why this album has resonated with so many people. Rather than trying to make Slayyyter fit into the mainstream, we focused on bringing the mainstream into her world. To me, the best latest artist nomination is a testament to the fact that great artistry can transcend genre.

What was it about Slayyyter that initially drove the decision to sign her?

In a recent development, it really felt like the right place, right time for Slayyyter. She had spent the better part of a decade building an incredibly dedicated fanbase and honing her craft as a songwriter, performer and creative. In many ways, we felt she was hiding in plain sight. All the pieces were there for her to have a major breakthrough, and we recognized an opportunity to help bring what she had already built to an entirely upcoming level.

It has been revealed that I distinctly remember sitting in Barry Weiss‘s office with Sabrina Finkelstein, Slayyyter’s A&R, listening to a rough version of “BEAT UP CHANEL$.” Funny enough, the conversation wasn’t initially about Slayyyter, but rather one of her key collaborators, Austin Corona, in connection with Barry’s publishing venture, Bossy Songs. Austin is someone we’re now proud to publish and work closely with. But when we heard that record, we were immediately blown away by its distinct sound, creative direction and authenticity.

I felt I understood exactly what Slayyyter was trying to accomplish creatively, and it was immediately clear to me that she had something incredibly special. We knew we wanted to be in business with her, and ultimately made a deal because we believed so strongly in her potential.

Slayyyter had a fanbase previously, but WOR$T GIRL IN AMERICA was her massive mainstream breakthrough. How did you approach the campaign for the album to get her to that next level?

From the very beginning, we were focused on two things: starting with making sure we superserved Slayyyter’s core fanbase and stayed true to the DIY ethos that had defined her career. WOR$T GIRL IN AMERICA was a sonic departure from her previous albums, so our first priority was ensuring that her existing fans felt just as connected to this fresh era as they had to everything that came before it. Slayyyter has such an amazing, dedicated fanbase, and their enthusiasm and support became the foundation for everything we built throughout the campaign. From there, we focused on introducing her to an entirely new audience without compromising what made her so special in the first place.

Recent findings suggest that we put a lot of emphasis on creating authentic moments that would resonate with those fans. One of my favorite examples was a small listening event in Fresh York City around the release of “BEAT UP CHANEL$,” where we gave away knockoff Chanel bags that Slayyyter had personally purchased on Canal Street and “beat up” herself. It’s a small detail, but those are the kinds of creative touches that made this campaign so special and helped build a deeper connection between Slayyyter and her audience.

In a recent development, the other major focus was the rollout strategy itself. We deliberately went against the grain of the current industry playbook of quick album campaigns and flooding the market with newly introduced music. Instead, we built an eight-month campaign that slowly introduced the world of WOR$T GIRL IN AMERICA, giving each release room to breathe and creating meaningful marketing moments along the way. I’ve always liked to describe my approach to marketing as being “proactively reactive.” You need a clear strategy and long-term vision, but you also have to be nimble enough to recognize what’s connecting and adjust accordingly. That approach has been especially vital to the digital marketing campaigns we’ve run throughout this rollout.

Sources indicate that A great example of that was the visual component of this campaign. We didn’t necessarily set out to create what many fans have described as a visual album, but as the campaign progressed, it became clear just how impactful Slayyyter’s videos were. I made it a priority to ensure that every song had a strong visual supporting it. Slayyyter is an incredibly visual artist, and every one of those music videos is her brainchild. She writes the treatments and directs them herself. She’s truly an artist’s artist, and giving those creative ideas the proper platform and time to resonate became a huge part of our strategy.

By the time WOR$T GIRL IN AMERICA arrived at the end of March, just ahead of Coachella, we had spent months building anticipation, expanding her audience and bringing more and more people into Slayyyter’s WOR$T GIRL universe.

Sources indicate that she got a lot of buzz from her performances at Coachella and Lollapalooza this year that really seemed to rocket her to a new level of attention. How important do you think those performances were in her breakthrough?

It has been revealed that coachella was a massive moment for Slayyyter. We always felt that the live component was going to be a huge piece of the puzzle, and we had those Coachella dates circled on the calendar from the moment they were confirmed. We knew we had something special, but seeing the crowd overflowing the Mojave tent for an afternoon set was one of the first moments where we could really see everything coming together. It was clear that the music and the campaign were translating into something much bigger, and that we were on the verge of a major breakthrough.

From there, we really leaned into the live performances as key tentpole moments throughout the campaign. Her late-night television debut performing “DANCE…” on The Tonight Show was another enormous milestone. Beyond the national exposure, I think that moment meant so much to the fans who had been supporting her for years. Seeing Slayyyter on that stage felt like a real “she made it” moment, not just for her, but for everyone who had been along for the ride.

The momentum continued throughout the summer festival circuit. At Governor’s Ball, she drew more than 25,000 fans for another afternoon set, roughly doubling the audience we had seen at Coachella. That all culminated in her incredible Lollapalooza performance in front of more than 50,000 people, doubling her festival crowd once again. We also used Lollapalooza as a major moment to launch “brand new chanel$,” the lead single from her forthcoming album, wor$t man in america.

What’s been especially exciting is that these weren’t isolated moments. Each performance built on the last, and the audiences just kept getting bigger. Her agents at THE•TEAM have been phenomenal partners in helping build the touring side of her career as that demand has grown. She’s already been declared for multiple major festivals next year, including headlining slots at some, and I think we’re only beginning to see what Slayyyter is capable of as a live performer.

What are some other highlights from the WOR$T GIRL marketing plan?

One of my personal favorites was our take on the traditional Coachella billboard. The now-infamous “Slayyyter bunny” had become a recurring character in this campaign, and in keeping with Slayyyter’s DIY ethos, we decided to do something a little different. Rather than putting up a traditional billboard along the highway to Indio, like so many major artists do, we had multiple bunnies standing on the side of the road holding signs promoting her set and album. The stunt ended up making multiple best Coachella billboard lists, including taking the top spot on Paste Magazine‘s ranking.

Another key element was the dollar sign. It’s such a simple motif, but we used it consistently throughout the campaign to the point where a dollar sign became synonymous with the WOR$T GIRL IN AMERICA era. It’s a great example of how something so simple can become an incredibly recognizable part of an artist’s identity when executed consistently.

Industry observers note that lastly, I loved the limited-edition dirt-filled vinyl we created for WOR$T GIRL IN AMERICA. It was a nod to the dirt and mud that appear throughout the “BEAT UP CHANEL$” music video and a unique way to bring the visual world of the album into a physical product.

Slayyyter has a follow-up album, wor$t man in america, scheduled for a Dec. 4 release. It’s a quick follow-up to WOR$T GIRL, which was only released in March — was there an intentional push to get the new album out this year to capitalize on the attention she’s been receiving?

That was certainly a consideration when putting together the wor$t man in america campaign, but it was only one contributing factor. We always felt that the WOR$T GIRL IN AMERICA campaign would really hit its stride once the full album was out and Slayyyter started touring. As we watched everything unfold, it became clear that she was only just getting started and there was so much more of this world to explore.

I don’t want to speak for Slayyyter creatively, but as a fan and listener, I genuinely felt she was writing some of her best material yet following the release of WOR$T GIRL. Songs like the recently released “hey boyyy” and “brand new chanel$” are perfect examples of that. “brand new chanel$” in particular felt different from the very first listen, and that feeling has only grown as we’ve watched it connect with audiences, especially in her live show. It’s such a massive, anthemic record that resonates with so many people, and it continues to reach new heights. I really believe in that song, and our entire team is doing everything we can to help it reach its full potential.

In a recent development, of course, Slayyyter is always doing things her own way, and the idea of an inverse album, a true “brother” album to WOR$T GIRL IN AMERICA, was entirely hers. A traditional deluxe album filled with remixes and features just didn’t feel right for a project this unique. What I love about the concept is that it gave us an opportunity to continue building on the world we had established while introducing an entirely upcoming creative chapter. Even the visual identity has been inverted, swapping the signature red of WOR$T GIRL for cyan on wor$t man, while carrying over familiar imagery and motifs from the original campaign. That same approach has extended into the marketing, allowing us to build on the momentum with new music, visuals and creative moments that feel fresh while remaining connected to everything that came before. I think wor$t man in america is the perfect way to put a bow on this sonic era.

Slayyyter has achieved a lot of big milestones this year — what are your hopes for 2027?

Recent findings suggest that A couple of Grammys, platinum singles and albums and Slayyyter becoming an established arena-level act. Not too much to ask for, I don’t think!

In all seriousness, my hope for 2027 is to continue establishing Slayyyter as a global superstar, regardless of what our release schedule looks like next year. We’ve accomplished so much in 2026, but I genuinely believe we’re only scratching the surface of what’s possible for her. There’s an incredible opportunity to build on this momentum, continue expanding her audience around the world and introduce even more people to her music. Ultimately, we’ll continue doing what got us here in the first place: supporting Slayyyter’s creative vision and amplifying her immense talent.

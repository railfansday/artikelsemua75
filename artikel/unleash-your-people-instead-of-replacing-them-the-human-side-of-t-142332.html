Unleash Your People Instead Of Replacing Them: The Human Side Of The Agentic AI Revolution

Scott Alldridge is CEO of IP Services, a visionary leader, and author of the bestselling VisibleOps book and series.

In a recent development, much of the work AI is being used to automate today involves tasks that employees already perform. Agentic AI creates an opportunity to automate work across applications and entire workflows, saving time for an organization’s most valuable assets: its employees.

This presents opportunities not only for financial growth but also for getting more out of the people who make the business successful. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

Sources indicate that as a consultant in operations improvement for more than 15 years, I’ve seen firsthand that people are worth far more than their payroll figures. The amount of relationship building, creative work and critical thinking employees can contribute is far greater than the time they currently spend on routine tasks such as entering data and managing processes.

Instead of asking how many employees AI can replace, CEOs should ask the reverse question: How much more could their employees accomplish if repetitive work were removed from their responsibilities?​.

Recent findings suggest that ​Think about a typical small business. ​Marketing employees might spend most of their time preparing reports on leads. Customer service representatives document every call and answer repeated questions. Accounting employees process invoices and reconcile bank and credit card accounts.

​All of this work is necessary, but how much of it actually requires the creativity, relationship building and strategic decision making that employees could otherwise bring to the business?

Industry observers note that ​Increasingly, much of this routine work can be automated. ​Consider what marketing employees could accomplish with that reclaimed time: strengthening relationships with clients, studying the competition in greater detail and creating more campaigns to generate revenue. (Related: How to generate text: using different decoding methods ...)

​The same opportunity exists across every department. When employees spend less time entering data and managing repetitive processes, they have more capacity to solve problems, serve customers and develop fresh opportunities for the firm. ​That, to me, is the real opportunity.

​Of course, CEOs lead for-profit companies, so labor costs, competitive pricing and thin profit margins will inevitably enter the equation.

Sources indicate that ​An IDC study commissioned by Microsoft in 2024 estimated an average return of $3.70 for every $1 invested among organizations using generative AI in the study. This figure represents the average of the estimates provided by participating organizations. It’s also vital to recognize that generative AI refers to a broad category of AI, not specifically agentic AI.

Recent findings suggest that ​For an organization developing and supporting fresh AI-enabled processes during the first year or so, including training employees to use them effectively, the goal might be to generate $37,000 in business value. ​That value could come from less overtime, fewer errors, lower contractor costs and sustained growth without an immediate need for additional headcount.

According to recent reports, ​Simply giving an employee five hours back in their workweek doesn’t put extra cash in the bank. Their salary hasn’t changed. ​What has changed is their capacity. ​Can those additional hours translate into better customer service, fresh business opportunities or stronger relationships with existing customers?

​Published averages don’t automatically translate into savings or value for your employees. CEOs need to consider how reclaimed time will actually be used and how that contributes to the business.

Sources indicate that ​Imagine discovering that AI could be configured to complete the majority of your daily work.​ Would you feel excited? Concerned? Afraid for your future?​.

Recent findings suggest that ​Your employees have families, financial commitments and careers to worry about. If you introduce AI in a way that suggests its primary purpose is to reduce their workload, you risk creating suspicion about the technology before implementation has even begun.

In a recent development, ​There is a better way. Ask employees to list the tasks in their daily routines that they perceive to be time-consuming or unnecessary. Then ask them to identify the projects and customer service improvements they have always wanted to pursue but never had the time to implement.

​It’s far more enjoyable and valuable for an employee to be able to say, “I have half of my previous routine work automated, and I can spend so much more time focusing on our customers,” than to spend that time worrying about being replaced by a machine.

However, not every responsibility should be handed over to AI. For example, while AI can manage customer information, recommend actions and even send messages, handling an unhappy customer or training a latest employee still requires human judgment. Even the most complex AI systems need to be monitored, and a human must ultimately remain accountable for their decisions and actions.

​Sophisticated AI systems can still make mistakes in their processing. All access, decisions and actions by agentic AI require appropriate human oversight. A straightforward rule for AI governance is to automate tasks and to never automate accountability.​.

The goal should be to free people to focus on the things that matter most to them and to the organization, whether that’s serving customers, improving services or developing fresh opportunities.

Recent findings suggest that ​Some organizations will use agentic AI to reduce employee headcount. Others will use these technologies to serve more customers, strengthen their employees’ capabilities and create new opportunities for people to apply their skills.

Recent findings suggest that ​Staffing reductions may happen, but they shouldn’t be the starting point for AI adoption. ​Before cutting headcount, eliminate the unnecessary work your employees are presently doing. Before replacing an employee with AI, consider the value that person could bring to the organization in a different role.

​Finally, don’t declare AI a success until you’ve measured its impact on profitability, productivity, customer value and employee value. When something as innovative as agentic AI can help people accomplish things they previously didn’t have the time or capacity to do, that’s more than just a technology strategy. It’s a leadership strategy.​​.

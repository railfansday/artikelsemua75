It has been revealed that tether declared the investment in Argentinian fintech Lemon on Nov. 25 and said it aimed to expand the use of USDT and its tokenized gold, XAUT, in Latin America.

CEO Paolo Ardoino noted people in the region are using USDT to get paid, spend, and send money across borders.

Lemon customers can keep the stablecoin in the app, send it to an Argentine bank, or spend it from their balance. Lemon’s April Visa rollout demonstrated how Circle’s USDC can move between the companies handling a card purchase.

The fintech CEO Marcelo Cavazzoli told Forbes Argentina the $14.5 million package includes $5 million in equity and $9.5 million in incentives to develop products together. (Related: Building the Open Agent Ecosystem Together: Introducing...)

Lemon's June Digital Dollar guide describes a single balance that automatically brings together USDT, USDC and USDS on a one-for-one basis. The fintech's deposit fee guide indicates transfer charges can reduce the amount credited, and the exchange rate may change by the time Bridge, its provider, converts the funds. According to recent reports, to withdraw to a bank, customers enter the account number for an Argentine dollar account in their own name. Lemon demonstrates an estimated payout, and the transfer arrives as bank dollars.

The conversion rate can change while the transfer is being processed, and consumers sending money to a crypto wallet can choose the receiving token and network. Lemon handles the conversion if they choose USDC or USDS.

On April 21, Lemon confirmed that international purchases on its physical and virtual Visa prepaid cards in Argentina would settle in Circle’s USDC, whether the cardholder paid from pesos, digital dollars, or another asset.

In Visa’s general model for direct stablecoin settlement, an issuer can pay Visa in a supported token, and Visa’s custodian converts the funds to fiat to pay merchants.

Lemon’s June card guide lists a charge of one Tether's USDT per US dollar for regular foreign-currency purchases funded from the app’s Digital Dollar balance. Funding with Ethereum or Bitcoin carries a 0.3% commission.

A customer starting with pesos pays a 0.5% commission and buys USDT at 3.5% above the in-app price.

Industry observers note that the prepaid card terms say foreign-currency purchases settle with the payment processor in a reference asset that can change. The June guide describes the USDT customers spend or buy to fund purchases.

Lemon reported more than 151,000 international Lemon Card purchases during Argentina’s 2026 winter holidays, up 61% from the prior winter holiday period.

Cavazzoli told Forbes Argentina that more than 85% of the volume moving through Lemon was in local currencies such as Argentine pesos, Peruvian soles and Colombian pesos.

According to recent reports, the Argentine central bank counted 791.6 million instant peso transfers sent and 122.8 million peso payments initiated through interoperable QR codes nationwide in September. Lemon’s dollar balance sits alongside a peso network that people already use at scale for transfers and purchases.

Recent findings suggest that for Tether, the opportunity is the balance customers can keep between a deposit and their next transfer or purchase. The value of that position depends on how much customers keep in USDT between transactions, a figure the card and peso payment counts leave open.

Tesla (TSLA -7.64%) is no stranger to controversy, at least partly thanks to its polarizing CEO Elon Musk. However, the company's products themselves can be just as "engaging" as its CEO, highlighted by the Cybertruck. Some people loved its looks; others thought it was ugly. Either way, Cybertruck production is ending. Here's why Rivian (RIVN -8.50%) could see a huge boost at just the right time as potential Cybertruck customers look elsewhere for an all-electric truck. Elon Musk has stated that Tesla is dropping the Cybertruck so it can focus on producing Cybercabs. This may be true to some degree, but there have long been suspicions that the Cybertruck wasn't selling well. That may have changed after the electric vehicle maker announced aims to shut the production line in around eight months, which led to a flood of new orders. That's not actually surprising, as the announcement likely brought forward demand. The cancellation of the Cybertruck could also have pushed people who like to own "exclusive" things, as well as those who like to flip products with limited supply, to jump in line. Regardless, it looks like there's now going to be more demand than supply. If customers still want an all-electric truck after the Cybertruck is no more, there's one vehicle maker that stands out: Rivian.

In a recent development, rivian started out making all-electric high-end trucks and work trucks. The consumer vehicles it makes are stylish and have won multiple awards. However, the cost limited their appeal. This wasn't a tactical mistake; it was part of the strategy. Like Tesla, Rivian started at the high end to maximize revenue per vehicle while it built out its business. The long-term goal was to expand production by bringing out a lower-cost, mass-market vehicle.

That vehicle, the R2, has just started to hit the market. It is already Rivian's best-selling model. Notably, the company's revenues rose 38% year over year in the third quarter of 2026, following the R2 launch, with deliveries up 46%. That success isn't surprising, given the R2 is a latest, lower-cost model. The real key to Rivian's success is maintaining demand over the long term. Tesla's cancellation of the Cybertruck just increased the chances of that happening.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

There are other options. For example, Lucid (LCID -10.39%) is working on an SUV, and Ford Motor Organization (F -0.62%) has the iconic F-150. Neither of those fits quite as well as Rivian's R2. And that fact could be huge for Rivian and its shareholders.

Rivian has achieved significant success, building a manufacturer from the ground up that designs and manufactures all-electric trucks. However, it still isn't sustainably profitable. That's the real end goal. To get there, the company needs to increase volume so it can spread its costs across more vehicles. The R2 is intended to push the company in that direction.

It has been revealed that it remains to be seen if the R2 can move the needle enough for Rivian to become profitable. The company has already produced a gross profit, meaning it generates more revenue from selling its products than it costs to produce them. Unfortunately, that gross profit has been supported by its software and services operations. And it still has to cover other costs, such as selling, general, and administrative expenses, as well as research and development spending, before it is in the black.

If the Cybertruck's cancellation leads to more R2 orders, it could be the extra push Rivian needs. More aggressive investors may want to take a second look at the stock now that Tesla is stepping away from the truck market.

According to recent reports, even in the age of one-click shopping, opening an actual brick-and-mortar retail store is a dream for many designers. There’s something almost romantic about creating an entire environment where customers can sit on the sofas, feel the fabrics, or pick up a last-minute gift on their way to a party. It’s an opportunity to bring your brand to life and connect with customers in a way a website never can.

But opening a store requires a significant leap of faith. Even if you have a loyal customer base, there’s something different about building a store. You have to think about everything from foot traffic to merchandising to hiring staff, and much more. (Related: Dividends Aren't What You Think They Are...)

These are questions I’ve been wrestling with myself. Almost two months ago, after years of nurturing a thriving e-commerce business, my husband Syd and I unveiled our McGee & Co. flagship in our homebase of Salt Lake City. This project has been a goal of ours for a long time. We’ve been collecting ideas for years to ensure that whenever it opened, our physical retail operation would hold up to the high standards of service and design that we expect from every other part of our business. This project has had me thinking about retail at every hour of the day, mulling over some substantial questions... How do we translate a digital brand into a physical space for selling? How do we nurture customers and ensure they return? And how do we map our knowledge so that we can repeat our successes as we scale? I’ll share some of the thinking and practical decisions behind our shop, from our merchandising strategy to how we recruited and trained our employees.

It has been revealed that next, AD PRO contributor Lila Allen speaks to me and my team, as well as professionals from retailers like Lawson-Fenning, Il Buco Vita, Tailor-Made Textiles, and ABC Carpet & Home, about how they cultivate customer loyalty over the long term. When access to product and knowledge is seemingly unlimited across the internet, we all agree: Earning trust and becoming a destination for discovery takes a lot of care. But it pays off. Relationships might be the most precious resource we have.

Then, contributor Margaret Borden catches up with the creatives behind some of this year’s buzziest shops—from a jewel-box fashion boutique in Paris to a bright, inviting kitchen and home goods shop in Fresh York City—to glean their merchandising tips and tricks.

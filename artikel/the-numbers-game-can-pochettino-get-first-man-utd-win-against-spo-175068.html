This one is almost too close to call, but Sporting just about have the edge.

They won 39.5% of the Opta supercomputer's 10,000 pre-match simulations to United’s 35.7%.

And there is still a healthy 24.8% chance of a draw, though a stalemate would not be the ideal result to boost either side's hopes of automatic qualification.

Sporting and United are meeting for the fifth time and first since the 2007-08 Champions League.

The Portuguese side have won just one of those four prior matches (L3), a 5-0 home victory in March 1964.

But United are unbeaten across their last 13 major European matches against sides from Portugal (W9 D4), since a 2-1 loss to Benfica in the Champions League in December 2005, so they will be hoping to keep that run going.

Fonseca looks to continue perfect start.

Sporting will feel like they have a chance against a wounded United side, especially with three wins from four under their belt in Europe so far in 2026-27.

However, their sole loss did come in their last home game against LASK (2-3).

They are looking to avoid successive home losses in the Champions League for the first time since November 2022, but things do look good, given they have won six of their last eight at Estadio Jose Alvalade in the competition (L2).

But Fonseca will have to overturn recent history if he wants to extend his winning streak to three games, having beaten Moreirense in the Primeira Liga and Serra in the Taca de Portugal.

None of the last five permanent Sporting managers have won their first game in the Champions League, since Paulo Bento in September 2006.

Sporting are by no means the highest scorers in the competition, netting just eight goals so far, while only seven teams have attempted fewer shots than their 46 this term.

But they have won three of their opening four games in two of the last three seasons in UEFA's premier competition (also W3 in 2024-25), as often as they had managed across their first 14 European Cup/Champions League campaigns (2008-09 and 1962-63).

Sporting will be hoping to have Luis Suarez back firing as well. He was not on target last time out after scoring in each of their first three outings in Europe this season.

United's defensive woes could cause problems.

There had been a new feeling of hope at Old Trafford following Pochettino's arrival, but the Argentine was given a rude awakening.

United returned from the international break with a thumping 4-0 loss away to old rivals Liverpool, with Leny Yoro's eighth-minute red card setting the tone on a difficult day.

They are still struggling in the Premier League – with that loss to Liverpool leaving them 19th in the relegation zone – but there were signs of improvement last time out in Europe.

Despite trailing 2-0 within 17 minutes against Roma, they produced a stunning comeback to snatch a 3-2 win thanks to Matheus Cunha's brace.

The Brazilian is unavailable against Sporting after being sent off shortly after for two bookable offences, and he will be a major miss with only Bruno Fernandes (nine) registering more goal involvements than Cunha in all competitions this term (six – four goals, two assists).

United's troubles in the Champions League have come on the road, though.

In a recent development, they are winless in their last six away games in the competition (D2 L4), losing both this season. They last had a longer such run on their travels between February 2004 and December 2005 (eight matches – D3 L5).

Through the first four matchdays in the Champions League this season, only Slovan Bratislava (293:36) trailed for more minutes than United (263:20).

The Red Devils did win against Roma despite trailing for 76% of the match (73:37), however.

Pochettino's biggest worry will be the defence, with United easily breached four times against Liverpool, though the losing margin could have been much bigger as they rode their luck at times.

No team across the top five European leagues has conceded more goals than United in all competitions (34) this season, while they have conceded at least two goals in each of their last three Champions League games (eight in total).

Never before have they conceded 2+ goals in four consecutive matches in the competition.

Pochettino has a huge task on his hands to turn things around at United, but a win would take them level on points with 10th-place Sporting, and would keep their outside chances of an automatic spot alive.

Rui Silva possesses an 85.7% save percentage in the Champions League – currently the best percentage in the competition.

While Silva has only played two Champions League games so far, he has conceded only once (a Goncalo Inacio own goal against Galatasaray).

He has faced seven shots on target in total, making six saves, while he has prevented 0.8 goals according to Opta's expected goals on target model (xGoT).

Manchester United – Bruno Fernandes.

With his penalty against Roma, Fernandes netted his 24th goal in major European competition for United, moving ahead of Cristiano Ronaldo into seventh spot on the club's all-time scoring list.

Only Ruud van Nistelrooy (five) has scored more penalties for United in the Champions League than Fernandes (four – level with Wayne Rooney).

Across all competitions, only Pascal Gross (59) has created more chances among Premier League players than the United captain (43), who has nine goal involvements in total in 2026-27 (six goals, three assists).

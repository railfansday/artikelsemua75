NEW YORK, Nov. 23, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- The Jumptuit Group announces biotechnology applications of its Anticipatory Intelligence Framework, a physical-world AI architecture designed to accelerate scientific discovery and advance the human condition. Powered by the Large Dynamic Reasoning Model (LDRM), a non-transformer model, the framework continuously observes cross-domain physical phenomena and the interaction of variables over time. It addresses ten biotechnology domains, from agriculture and marine systems to health, biosafety and governance. Details are available at jumptuit.co/biotechnology. The physical world and human biology are dynamic open systems. According to industry estimates, 85% of global GDP resides in the physical world. According to UN DESA estimates, the global population reached 8.3 billion in 2026, all of it exposed to environmental factors from prenatal development onward. Jumptuit's Physical World Intelligence: Built to Observe, Not Just Read.

According to recent reports, the Anticipatory Intelligence Framework draws on Jumptuit's Live Data Conduit, made up of Global Data Nets (GDNs) and Global Sensory Intelligence (GSI). The LDRM applies multi-mode reasoning (e.g, sign, comparative, causal, analogical, temporal, verbal), dynamic cross-sector threshold driven curiosity queries and urgency drivers to detect anomalies and track how variables change over time. The architecture is supported by the Jumptuit Group's growing USPTO patent portfolio, including the recently granted U.S. Patent No. 12,793,535.

The framework is built on aggregated, de-identified environmental and public data, with privacy, consent and human oversight by design.

"The limits of genomic research alone, and of Large Language Models, come from the same gap - neither can observe the physical world as it changes," said Donald Leka, Founder, Chairman and CEO of the Jumptuit Group. "Our Anticipatory Intelligence Framework is designed to close that gap, so researchers can see the invisible interactions that precede disease and disruption.".

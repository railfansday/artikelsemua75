In a recent development, it’s been about a year and a half since rapper and streamer PlaqueboyMax first spoke to Rolling Stone. In that time, the music industry has caught up with the popularity of streamers, as a growing set of major names — from Drake to Mariah Carey — have dabbled in the format. For Max, who has spent years making music in front of viewers, that means being something of an established figure in an evolving rap landscape.

“It’s been crazy,” he notes. “I’m really passionate about music, and I’m just happy that I’m able to merge my worlds and my passions together to make it one major world.”.

Industry observers note that max’s recent “Rising Star” competition, something like Star Search or American Idol for the underground rap generation, brought together six rappers with growing fanbases on one livestream where they’d create music and eventually perform for a real audience. The contest has its origins in two of Max’s streaming formats. “In the Booth,” which he started in 2024, lets viewers watch music being made. He was also doing “Song Wars” around then, putting songs up for an audience’s judgment. As he kept doing those streams, he wanted to spend more time with the artists. According to recent reports, he recruited Lil Yachty, with his years of experience making records and maintaining a career, alongside creators Cuffem and Funny Marco. Music advice from the rapper, entertainment from the creators: Max wanted both on the panel, with enough to keep his viewers watching.

“So I would say picking judges came down [to] just me picking people I appreciate, who I mess with, and just bringing them into my world and making it all one amazing product,” he states.

Industry observers note that yachty had plenty to say. Max remembers him taking five minutes to get into an individual artist’s work, which could make for an uncomfortable critique.

“Some of them were getting grilled, but it’s just like he’s giving them real feedback that if they listen, it will help them as an artist, and I love that,” Max says. “That’s what the purpose was.”.

Getting grilled in public can’t be much fun. I ask Max if anybody went away upset about it. Some artists were frustrated with the criticism, he says, or with their placement in the fan vote, but he stops short of calling them offended.

“They were frustrated either with their placements or I’d say sometimes the feedback they were getting, but I wouldn’t say that any artist that was competing took it the wrong way,” he states. “They were definitely just working hard and just passionate and hungry.”.

Recent findings suggest that viewers remain forever interested in stories about the World Wars of the 20th century, which explains the constant output of stories set in this era. Every year brings movies, TV highlights, documentaries and books exploring this pivotal time in history, and executives know that something about World War II is almost guaranteed to be a hit. This year has not been any different, with Netflix championing Peter Berg's WWII thriller, The Mosquito Bowl. There also has been Pressure starring Brendan Fraser, Coward by Lukas Dhont, Lucky Strike starring Scott Eastwood, World War II with Tom Hanks ... you get the idea. Still, Netflix is not done with this chapter, now exploring WWII through a documentary.

In 2023, the streamer aired World War II: From the Frontlines. The six-part series was narrated by John Boyega and won two 2024 News & Documentary Emmy Awards for Outstanding Graphic Design and Outstanding Music Composition. Directed by Rob Coldstream, the series pairs restored, colorized archival footage with personal accounts from soldiers, civilians, and survivors across multiple sides of the conflict. From early German expansion to the push into Berlin and the end of the war, the series covers a broad timeline although not exhaustively. But everyone knows that there are at least two sides to any story. (Related: Training Design for Text-to-Image Models: Lessons from ...)

Industry observers note that enter World War II: Behind Enemy Lines, a new series telling another part of the story. From the makers of World War II: From the Frontlines, this docuseries tells the story of the 101st Airborne's daring missions inside Nazi-occupied Europe. From D-Day to VE-Day, it uses the same format as its predecessor with colorized and previously unseen archival footage. Coldstream returns as director with Josh Brolin (Avengers: Endgame) in the booth as the narrator. World War II: Behind Enemy Lines streams on Netflix on December 2.

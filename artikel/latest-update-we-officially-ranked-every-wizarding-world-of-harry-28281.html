Industry observers note that there are very few film franchises that have managed to capture the imagination of audiences quite like the Harry Potter franchise, or also officially known as the Wizarding World. Released so closely to the mega-bestselling novels, the original run of the film series was a generational phenomenon. The franchise continued with a spinoff trilogy that is not as well-received but still has its own charm. Soon, the story of the Boy Who Lived is jumping to the TV screen with HBO tackling the first book.

Industry observers note that before that happens, let's take a look back at all eleven films in the franchise. With so many different adventures, characters, and creative approaches across the franchise, ranking these films is no easy task. But just like Harry Potter taking on the extremely tough mission to destory the Horcruxes, here, we officially rank these beloved movies to see which one truly deserve a place at the top. It has been revealed that fantastic Beasts: The Secrets of Dumbledore sees Newt Scamander (Eddie Redmayne) get roped into a mission by Albus Dumbledore (Jude Law) to stop Gellert Grindelwald (Mads Mikkelsen). Grindelwald deploys his devout followers, including the unstable and powerful Credence (Ezra Miller), to influence the upcoming election of the International Conference of Wizards so that he can control the wizarding world. Recent findings suggest that the third Fantastic Beasts film felt like a contract fulfillment. After the muted reception for the previous film, this film downplays many of the story threads from its predecessor and focuses on an entirely fresh conflict. The ensemble cast, led by Eddie Redmayne, remains the main draw of these films. Mads Mikkelsen, who replaced Johnny Depp, is fantastic as Grindelwald, and all of his scenes with Jude Law are electrifying. As for the story, it gets exciting at times yet the inclusion of the magical creatures seemed shoehorned in to tie in with the title. While in hindsight it was premature to announce a five-film franchise before the first was even released, it is quite sad to see the film series end in a lackluster way like this.

In Harry Potter and the Order of the Phoenix, Harry Potter (Daniel Radcliffe) finds himself increasingly isolated as the Ministry of Magic refuses to acknowledge that Voldemort has returned. With the Ministry spreading propaganda against Harry and Dumbledore, Hogwarts becomes a bleak place as the new professor Dolores Umbridge (Imelda Staunton) imposes oppressive rules on the school. Harry establishes Dumbledore's Army to prepare fellow students from the upcoming threat, while he himself begins to see visions connected to Voldemort.

This film is the second shortest film in the main franchise after the second half of the Deathly Hallows, which is frustrating if you've read the book because this is the longest book. Many storylines were cut and we are left with only the main plot. This is also the only film that's not written by Steve Kloves and it is quite noticeable in the overall vibes of the film. Order of the Phoenix is also David Yates' first directing gig in the overall franchise, and his style is more grounded and less fantastical than the previous directors, which also needs a little adjustment. Nevertheless, the film shines in its pivotal moments including the whole ordeal with Umbridge and the death of a beloved character. It is an entertaining entry but remains the weakest in the strong lineup of films.

According to recent reports, as the title suggests, Fantastic Beasts: The Crimes of Grindelwald focuses more on the dark wizard Gellert Grindelwald (Johnny Depp) as he tries to rally wizards and witches behind his grand plan. Newt Scamander (Redmayne) once again finds himself caught up in the conflict, especially with two of his love interests Leta Lestrange (Zoe Kravitz) and Tina Goldstein (Katherine Waterston) involved. Grindelwald also has a hidden plan to utilize the mysterious Credence (Ezra Miller) for his ambitions.

Industry observers note that while the first film focuses more on the titular beasts than the wizarding-world conflict, this sequel does the opposite. In doing so, Newt becomes much more of a supporting character in this story that seems far more interested in setting up future sequels. Johnny Depp brings an unsettling presence to Grindelwald, but after seeing the trilogy, his interpretation of Grindelwald seemed off when compared to Grindelwald's disguise portrayed by Colin Farrell or Mads Mikkelsen's nuanced take on the character. There are flashes of brilliance here, but the film ultimately sacrifices much of the first film's charm for an ambitious story that struggles to hold itself together.

Fantastic Beasts and Where to Find Them introduces Newt Scamander (Redmayne), a British magizoologist who arrives in 1926 New York City carrying a magical suitcase filled with extraordinary creatures. When several of these creatures escape into the city, Newt must track them down before they attract unwanted attention from the non-magical population. He crosses paths with Jacob Kowalski (Dan Fogler), a No-Maj who accidentally becomes involved, as well as sisters Tina (Waterston) and Queenie Goldstein (Alison Sudol).

The first spin-off film in the franchise is a peculiar one. It is not based on a narrative book, but a faux textbook that is supposedly used by Hogwarts students. It is a wonder in itself that that book can be turned into a wider story on the wizarding world, and one that opens that world beyond Hogwarts and the UK, as the Newly introduced York setting offers a distinctive visual atmosphere. Eddie Redmayne is playful as the lead, and he's joined by a stellar ensemble that includes Colin Farrell, Katherine Waterston and Jude Law in a minor role. However, Newt's own adventure is a bit jarring when blended with the broader magical conflict. This problem only becomes more noticeable in the following films. The film is still one of the most pleasant fantasy films of the 2010s.

It has been revealed that harry Potter (Radcliffe) returns to Hogwarts for his second year in Harry Potter and the Chamber of Secrets. After a bizarre encounter with a house elf in Privet Drive warning him about his return, Harry begins to hear a mysterious voice behind the school walls and he also finds a diary that contains disturbing stories. Several students are discovered petrified, and it is remarked that the legendary Chamber of Secrets has been opened after years.

Chris Columbus is back in the director's chair and he once again helmed the film effortlessly, capturing the darker nature of the story while also highlighting the magical world. The film does have an abundance of fun characters and sequences, such as the introduction of Dobby, the boastful Gilderoy Lockhart, the flying Anglia, and the battle with Basilisk. This first sequel is by no means a bad or even a mediocre film, but compared to the rest of the series, it doesn't offer anything new. Still, as a magical adventure packed with important lore building, The Chamber of Secrets remains a thoroughly enjoyable chapter in Harry's story.

It has been revealed that harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows Part 2 follows Harry (Radcliffe), Ron (Rupert Grint), and Hermione (Emma Watson) as they continue their search for Voldemort's remaining Horcruxes. After spending the last film out of school, they are back at Hogwarts as they prepare the students to fight Voldemort (Ralph Fiennes) and his followers.

Sources indicate that as the conclusion to a long cinematic journey, Deathly Hallows Part 2 is akin to Avengers: Endgame of its time. It is an incredibly satisfying payoff to the story and it is arguably the most action-packed installment in the franchise. Hogwarts is transformed into a full-scale battlefield and several beloved characters get their own moments to shine. There is also stellar performances from the ensemble of great British actors, particularly Alan Rickman's Severus Snape. Nevertheless, the film moves at a very rapid pace, despite having been split into two films. Several sequences from the book are cut, and some vital characters' deaths frustratingly happen off-screen. However, as the final conclusion to the series, it still ends the decade-long story on a high note.

In Harry Potter and the Goblet of Fire, Harry Potter's chance of a normal year vanishes when his name is mysteriously submitted to the interschool Triwizard Tournament, despite not meeting the age requirement. Harry, the official Hogwarts champion Cedric Diggory (Robert Pattinson), and champions from Beauxbatons and Durmstrang compete in difficult challenges to win the trophy. In the meantime, the Death Eaters plan for Voldemort's return.

Recent findings suggest that this fourth film encapsulates the whole franchise incredibly well. The Triwizard Tournament setting offers something unique and thrilling, as each challenge bring newly introduced elements for the champions to tackle. The sequence involving the dragon is particularly spectacular, and it also shows Harry's perseverance and determination. On the character side, the film expertly balances the excitement of teenage life at Hogwarts with the approaching threat. Robert Pattinson makes a memorable impression as Cedric Diggory while Ralph Fiennes makes a terrifying debut in the film series. There are more exciting moments from the book that are skipped due to runtime, but this entry works well as a stand-alone fantasy film for non-Potter fans.

Harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince sees Harry (Radcliffe) being prepared by Dumbledore (Michael Gambon) for the inevitable confrontation with Voldemort (Fiennes). He introduces Harry to Professor Slughorn (Jim Broadbent) who may have important information about Voldemort's past. Meanwhile, Harry discovers an old Potions textbook filled with handwritten instructions from someone calling themselves the Half-Blood Prince, helping him become an unexpectedly talented student in the class.

Recent findings suggest that harry Potter and the Half-Blood Prince may not be most fans' favorite at the time of its release. However, since then, it has arguably become the most fascinating entry of the franchise. It has a special focus on the teenage shenanigans with the characters navigating the more complicated aspects of relationships and friendship but also it is way darker than other films in the series. The cinematography by Bruno Delbonnel enhances this experience, giving the film a melancholic, glowing look unlike any other blockbuster. Several longtime actors such as Michael Gambon and Tom Felton get to shine here as their characters reach their turning points. The film notoriously skipped the battle at the Astronomy Tower for a quieter climax. But overall, the film works without a substantial set piece as it opts for atmosphere and emotional storytelling instead.

Sources indicate that harry Potter and the Sorcerer's Stone introduces the audience to the Boy Who Lived. After years of being neglected at his uncle's house, Harry Potter's (Radcliffe) life changed when an acceptance letter to Hogwarts arrives. To much of his adopted family's chagrin, Harry enrolls to the school and studies magic, alongside his new friends Ron (Rupert Grint) and Hermione (Emma Watson). However, his first year at Hogwarts is not an ordinary one after he learns about the Sorcerer's Stone and the secrets within Hogwarts' castle.

The first Harry Potter film directed by Chris Columbus rarely gets the respect it deserves. It is true that compared to the rest of the franchise, the film has a much lower stake. However, the film is responsible for setting the tone of the rest of the series and also the entire Wizarding World franchise, which also includes merchandising and theme parks. Its influence only becomes more apparent after seeing the clips and images from the Harry Potter HBO show, which looks eerily similar. As a film, it is a solid family adventure film packed with memorable sequence (the chess scene and Quidditch come first to mind) and filled with amazing British actors. The young trio leads the film with ease and confidence, making the audience invested until the end. Many films try to replicate this film's success, but nothing has come close.

According to recent reports, in Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows Part 1, Harry (Radcliffe), Ron (Grint), and Hermione (Watson) choose not to return to Hogwarts and instead continue to search and destroy Voldemort's remaining Horcruxes. Darkness has spread throughout the wizarding world, and Death Eaters and Voldemort's devout followers are hot on the trio's tail.

Recent findings suggest that by not going back to Hogwarts, Deathly Hallows Part 1 becomes the most unique adventure and probably the most mature in the entire franchise. It also makes more sense to split this into two films becayse of its rich material. With an overall bleak atmosphere, audiences get to see the heroes get tested by their mission. The trio's usually strong friendship starts to break as they try to complete the task entrusted to them by Dumbledore. Daniel Radcliffe, Rupert Grint, and Emma Watson deliver some of their strongest performances here, showing how much they have evolved since the first film. The film has many exciting and heartbreaking sequences, from the Gringotts break-in to the death of a fan favorite character, and even a small character beat where Harry and Hermione find a little joy in the midst of their desperate mission. This film marks the first time a final chapter is split into two films, and it's one of the few that truly works.

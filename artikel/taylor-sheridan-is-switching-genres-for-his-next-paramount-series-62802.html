Recent findings suggest that the news that there is yet another Taylor Sheridan show on the way might not sound surprising, but the Tulsa King spinoff’s unexpected genre shift certainly is a shock. There is a specter haunting TV networks and streaming services, and its name is Taylor Sheridan. The prolific writer/director first rose to prominence thanks to his screenplays for the hit movies Sicario, Hell or High Water, and Wind River.

Recent findings suggest that however, Sheridan truly came into his own with the success of his Neo-Western TV franchise Yellowstone. The saga of the Dutton family and their beloved eponymous ranch lasted five seasons and spawned as many spinoffs, between the prequels 1883 and 1923, the sequels Dutton Ranch and Marshals, and the upcoming third prequel 1944. Between this franchise, the five-season crime drama Mayor of Kingstown, the three-season spy series Lioness, the family drama The Madison, the oil business saga Landman, and the crime comedy Tulsa King, Sheridan is one of TV’s biggest creators right now. He is also a writer/director with a recognizable style and a reliable set of recurring themes, something that has only been marginally altered by Tulsa King, but now seems set to be thoroughly rearranged by its spinoff, Frisco King. It has been revealed that there is no shortage of killer mob reveals on streaming right now, and, even within this genre, there is no shortage of mob shows specifically focusing on aging gangsters trying to secure their legacies without getting themselves killed. This basic plot is shared by the BBC’s grim two-season hit This City Is Ours, Guy Ritchie’s glossier and more operatic, but no less brutal MobLand, and even Sheridan’s own three-season hit Tulsa King.

Debuting in 2022, Tulsa King follows Sylvester Stallone’s New York career criminal Dwight "The General" Manfredi as he is exiled to Tulsa by the mob. Manfredi’s former mafia colleagues make up an excuse about him setting up a crime syndicate in the sleepy city to cover up what is clearly intended to be a retirement post, only for Stallone’s indefatigable antihero to end up building an unlikely empire in Tulsa.

While Ethan Hawke’s neo-noir The Lowdown is the stronger Tulsa-based crime comedy, this is mostly due to Tulsa King’s uneven tone. Tulsa King is, as many critics have noted over the years, a weird blend of surprisingly straightforward dark crime thrills and playful, goofy midlife crisis dramedy. Tulsa King’s tone, as a result, feels like the comedy of Analyze This blended with the sudden bloody brutality of The Departed.

It has been revealed that luckily, Frisco King has already proven that the show empathically will not be repeating this mistake. After years of fun thrillers like The Hitman’s Bodyguard, The Kill Room, and Just Play Dead, Jackson’s latest show keeps this trend going strong with a trailer that is far more action comedy than crime drama. Viewers expecting something akin to the glum melodrama of Sheridan’s Mayor of Kingstown will need to look elsewhere.

This genre shift might be a surprise, but it is a thoroughly welcome one. After Jackson’s mafia hitman, Russell Lee Washington Jr, opted not to fulfill his contract to kill Stallone’s antihero and instead went into business with him, the character was always going to need to flee his native Fresh York. However, arriving in the sunny suburbs of Frisco, Texas puts Jackson’s career criminal in a comically unfamiliar setting.

When they’re not taking themselves painfully seriously, Sheridan’s indicates can be funny in a dark, deadpan manner. However, the first trailer for Frisco King promises a more broadly comedic, openly playful and farcical series with a winking sense of humor and plenty of outrageous gags, and this could be a welcome change of pace after the unclear tone of Tulsa King’s lengthy run.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that clean your car inside and out yourself in an afternoon, with about $40 in supplies. Wash it in the shade with car soap and two buckets, then vacuum and treat cloth seats and carpet with baking soda and diluted vinegar. Save a pro detail for paint scratches, stubborn stains or smells, and a car you’re about to sell.

Consumer Reports says to expect to pay more than $200 to have a car deep-cleaned inside and out. Neither the wash nor the wipe-down needs pro equipment.

Recent findings suggest that fill one bucket with water and car wash soap, not dish soap, which Consumer Reports warns may strip off the protective wax. Rinse your wash mitt in a second bucket of plain water after each section, so the grit doesn’t scratch the paint.

It has been revealed that wash in the shade from the roof down, since sun can dry the soap into spots before you rinse it. Then dry with microfiber towels, because air-drying can leave mineral spots.

Recent findings suggest that about $40 buys two 5-gallon buckets, car wash soap, a wash mitt, microfiber towels, a dashboard protectant, and an ammonia-free glass cleaner, since ammonia can damage window tint film. Add baking soda and white vinegar from the kitchen, and do the inside in this order.

Leave three jobs to a detailer. Paint correction, which removes scratches and swirl marks, needs a pro’s tools and experience. Stains or smells that outlast the baking soda and vinegar need a detailer’s hot-water carpet cleaner or odor treatment. And before you sell, book a detail only when it costs less than it adds to your car’s resale value.

Recent findings suggest that say four $200 details a year cost you $800 and one $40 kit lasts the year. Doing it yourself saves $760 of that $800, or $190 for each afternoon of work.

In a recent development, open enrollment gives you an opportunity to make changes to your Medicare coverage. But before you can decide whether a new plan is best, you have to understand what's happening with your current one.

Recent findings suggest that to that end, if you haven't reviewed your current plan's annual notice of change, now's the time to read through it thoroughly. That notice should explain any upcoming changes to things like premiums, deductibles, copays, covered services, and other benefits.

Recent findings suggest that don't just skim that document, though. Even a relatively small monthly premium increase could add up over the course of a year. And if your Medicare Advantage proposal is dropping benefits, that could have a huge impact on you, too.

Recent findings suggest that before comparing Medicare aims, take the time to make a list of every prescription medication you currently take. Include the medication's name, dosage, how frequently you take it, and the pharmacy you typically use for refills.

Industry observers note that this step is important because Medicare Part D plans can change their drug formularies, which are lists of covered prescription medications, from one year to the next. A medication covered by your current strategy might be moved to a different cost-sharing tier in 2027, making it more expensive. Having a comprehensive list should make it easier to compare plans and determine which one offers the best coverage for your specific medications.

Even if your current Medicare strategy isn't changing for the worse, you shouldn't assume that it's your best option for next year. There may be another plan that offers lower premiums and prescription drug costs.

If you've been diagnosed with any newly introduced conditions over the past year, it also pays to see if there's a Medicare Advantage plan offering supplemental benefits that could help you manage it better. Medicare Advantage plans commonly offer added benefits beyond what original Medicare covers.

Recent findings suggest that it might seem like you have plenty of time to take action on your Medicare coverage before Dec. 7 rolls around. But that deadline will be here before you know it. So if you haven't yet reviewed changes to your current Medicare initiative, your prescription list, and new plan options, the time to get moving is now.

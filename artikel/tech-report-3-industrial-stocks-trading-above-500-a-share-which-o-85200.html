In a recent development, wall Street gets excited about stock splits, with some investors attempting to get ahead of a split before it is revealed. Caterpillar (CAT -5.01%), Comfort Systems (FIX -6.42%), and GE Vernova (GEV -7.53%) are all candidates for a stock split, with share prices that are, in some cases, well above $500. Even after the pullback in the artificial intelligence trade, each of these stocks remains a strong candidate for a stock split. Based on its price, Comfort Systems could be the first to do so. From a purely functional point of view, a stock split makes a company's stock cheaper. For example, as of this writing, Comfort Systems' stock price was roughly $1,100 per share (rounding down). A two-for-one split would lower that to around $550 per share; a 10-for-1 split would bring the price down to $110. It is clearly easier for an investor to afford to buy a single share of a $110 stock than it is to buy a single share of a $1,100 stock. That's as true on the financial side as it is one the emotional side, since buying a single share may not feel worth the effort while buying 10 feels like a more substantial investment, even if both investments would end up costing $1,100. From a financial perspective, however, a stock split has very little meaning. All that is being done is that a single share is now split into two or some other number of shares. To keep the math simple, if a firm had just one share, it would represent 100% ownership. A two-for-one split would simply mean that there were two shares, each representing 50% ownership. A 10-for-1 split would mean there were 10 shares, each representing 10% ownership. If you owned the original share, representing 100% ownership, you would own more shares and still have 100% ownership after a stock split. Nothing changed about the percentage of the company you own.

It has been revealed that meanwhile, per-share figures such as earnings per share are adjusted, but the base number remains the same. For example, if a company with one share earned $10, earnings per share would be $10. After a 10-for-1 split, the company's earnings per share would be $1, but its earnings would still be $10. Again, nothing material changed about the company.

In other words, a stock split isn't a particularly substantial deal. But investors often view such events as important and bid up the shares of companies that split their stocks. That remains true even as more brokers allow fractional purchases, which means you can invest a dollar amount rather than buy based on the number of whole shares you can afford.

Right now, the stock prices of each of these companies are falling. That reduces the likelihood of a cut, with GE Vernova and Comfort Systems both off their 52-week highs by nearly 50%, and Caterpillar down roughly 35% from its high. Stock splits often happen on the way up; they are less likely on the way down. The pullback is due to investors being less enthusiastic about the artificial intelligence (AI) trade than earlier in the year.

Caterpillar makes construction equipment for building AI data centers and the power systems that supply them. GE Vernova makes turbines that generate the energy AI needs to operate, with demand coming from utilities and, increasingly, directly from data center operators looking to generate their own power. And Comfort Systems is a building and service provider that works on mechanical, electrical, and plumbing systems, all of which are important for building an AI data center.

It has been revealed that all three of these industrial companies have huge backlogs and will likely benefit from AI spending for years to come. That mentioned, Comfort Systems has the highest stock price here, at a bit over $1,100. Cat is next at around $750, with GE Vernova at a bit over $530. And that's after the large drawdowns noted above. Given this information, Comfort Systems seems like the most likely candidate for a stock split.

At the end of the day, stock splits are more of an emotional phenomenon than a financial one. You probably shouldn't buy a stock just because you think it may split. You should buy it because you like the firm enough to own it, preferably for the long term.

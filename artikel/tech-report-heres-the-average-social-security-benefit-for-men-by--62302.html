Men's average Social Security benefits run higher than women's at every age. This article lays out the numbers to show how those benefits compare across the typical claiming window. (Related: Google Wallet may soon be able to store your Social Sec...)

Sources indicate that as of December 2025, the most recent data available, the average monthly benefit for men is $2,282.28, according to the Social Security Administration (SSA). The average for women is $1,872.43, creating a gender gap of $409.85 per month.

Recent findings suggest that the gap is visible the moment workers become eligible for benefits. At age 62, men receive an average of $1,572.83 per month while women receive $1,285.50. This difference of nearly $300 a month persists and grows as retirees age. (Related: Tech Report: “Determined” woman completes 7 marathons i...)

Recent findings suggest that the figures in the table below represent snapshots of everyone at a specific age today, regardless of when they first claimed. Your own benefit depends on your work history and when you choose to start your checks. Full retirement age (FRA) ranges from 66 to 67, depending on your birth year, and it's 67 for everyone born in 1960 or later.

The data above is as of December 2025 and comes from the SSA's retired-worker records.

Industry observers note that the higher male average reflects historical differences in lifetime earnings and years spent in the paid workforce. The SSA calculates your benefit based on your average wages across your 35 highest-earning years, adjusted for inflation and run through the primary insurance amount (PIA) formula.

Industry observers note that longevity affects how long these checks last. A man who is 65 today is expected to live about 18 more years, while a woman of the same age is expected to live about 21 more years, according to the SSA.

Sources indicate that social Security is designed to replace only about 40% of pre-retirement income. This share is well short of what most retirees need to cover their costs, so many rely on savings or other income sources.

Annual cost-of-living adjustments (COLAs) are designed to help these checks keep pace with inflation, though they often fall short. The 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment that will take effect in 2027 will add about $91 per month to the $2,529.62 average for 70-year-old men.

Married men may be able to help their spouses get more from Social Security through spousal or survivor benefits. Once you claim your retirement benefit, your spouse becomes eligible to claim a spousal benefit, worth up to one-half of the amount you qualify for at your FRA, provided they're at least 62 at the time. They will only receive this amount if it's worth more than their own retirement benefit.

It has been revealed that after you pass away, your spouse will become eligible for a survivor benefit, worth up to 100% of the amount you were receiving or eligible for at the time of your death. This can be a significant help to them, especially if you don't have a ton of personal savings.

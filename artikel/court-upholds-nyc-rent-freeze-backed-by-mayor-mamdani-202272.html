Court upholds NYC rent freeze backed by Mayor Mamdani

Sources indicate that on Thanksgiving eve, Judge Brendan Lantry dismissed a landlords’ challenge to the Rent Guidelines Board’s late June decision to freeze rents. The ruling upholds the city’s first-ever freeze on both one- and two-year leases.

With the national real estate industry watching, the rent freeze became a major test of what a mayor can do to force affordability. (Related: Industry News: huggingface_hub v1.0: Five Years of Buil...)

According to recent reports, owners of rent-stabilized buildings argued that Mamdani stacked the board with loyalists after promising a freeze, making the outcome predetermined. They also accused the board of following a campaign promise instead of the data on rising landlord costs. Lantry rejected both claims.

The ruling comes three weeks after an appeals court revived the city’s pied-à-terre tax rollout on second homes valued at $5 million or higher. Mamdani posted on social media that the city has billed owners of those homes more than $500 million.

Recent findings suggest that that lawsuit ran parallel to the rent freeze lawsuit landlords filed in late July. It originally was in a Staten Island court but was moved to Manhattan in August at the city’s request.

The rent freeze took effect Oct. 1 while the lawsuit was pending and covers leases on more than a million apartments. The board had frozen one-year leases in 2015, 2016 and 2020.

According to recent reports, because the board acts in a quasi-legislative role, its orders stand if they have a rational basis, and courts may not substitute their own judgment, Lantry wrote. He found that the board’s 42-page explanatory statement showed it considered each factor the Administrative Code requires, including the industry’s economic condition and the cost of living. Lantry noted appointees who share the mayor’s views did not show collusion. He noted that “elections have consequences.”.

But Lantry did not let Mamdani completely off the hook.

“Regarding Mayor Mamdani’s campaign for Mayor of Newly introduced York City, the court would be remiss not to state that such comments, promises, and guarantees to freeze the rent were reckless and irresponsible,” he wrote. “The Mayor of the City of New York has no authority to unilaterally raise, ‘freeze’ or otherwise adjust the rent of stabilized units in this City – that responsibility is within the power of the Board and the Board alone.”.

He called an email in which the board’s chair shared a draft of the explanatory statement with a mayoral aide before the vote “troubling.” He stated it cast a “dark shadow” over the board’s independence. Without more, he wrote, the email did not justify overturning the decision under Article 78, the state law that lets courts review government agency actions.

The judge noted that petitioners agreed to end court-ordered discovery, which produced about 1,000 documents, and never sought depositions.

Lantry acknowledged the freeze could make it harder for owners to refinance or renovate. But he wrote that the legal question is not whether the policy is wise.

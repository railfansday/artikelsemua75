As space exploration needs evolve, investors often weigh defense stability against rapid disruption. Choosing between Lockheed Martin (LMT -0.28%) and Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -6.19%) depends on your appetite for risk versus consistent performance. Lockheed Martin serves as a cornerstone of national security by providing advanced aircraft and missile systems to global governments. Space Exploration Technologies--SpaceX-- focuses on reusable rockets and global satellite internet through its Starlink program. Both companies are central to the future of aerospace, making them a natural match-up for long-term retail investors. (Related: Is Social Security Taxable? How to Calculate What You O...)

In its latest annual report, filed for the most recent period, Lockheed Martin highlights its role as a premier global security company. It relies heavily on government contracts, with nearly 72% of its 2025 sales coming from agencies of the U.S. federal government. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business, as revenue depends on federal budget priorities.

Reliable recent quarterly figures for at least one of these companies aren't available, so this comparison uses each organization's latest full fiscal year. In FY 2025, the company reported revenue of about $75.1 billion, representing growth of approximately 5.7% over the previous year. This revenue supported a net income of roughly $5.0 billion, which reflects the company's ability to remain profitable despite complex global supply chains.

Sources indicate that as of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is around 7.9x, indicating that the tech giant uses a significant amount of borrowed money compared to shareholder equity. The current ratio, which measures the ability to cover short-term obligations with current assets, is approximately 1.1x. For the same fiscal year, the company generated free cash flow of about $6.9 billion, providing ample room for shareholder returns among defense stocks.

During FY 2025, revenue reached about $18.7 billion, which was a sharp increase of roughly 33% compared to the prior year. Despite this rapid top-line growth, the firm reported a net loss of about $4.9 billion. This underscores the high costs associated with developing next-generation spacecraft and expanding a global satellite constellation.

According to recent reports, the company's December 2025 balance sheet reveals a debt-to-equity ratio of approximately 0.6x, suggesting a relatively lower reliance on debt relative to its equity. Its current ratio is close to 1.4x, signifying a healthy cushion for meeting short-term financial needs. Note that stock-based compensation (SBC) represented roughly 28.7% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement. Free cash flow for the period was a negative $14.0 billion.

It has been revealed that lockheed Martin faces significant risks from its revenue concentration, as nearly 72% of its 2025 sales came from the U.S. federal government. Any shifts in defense priorities or budget cuts could impact its long-term contracts. Furthermore, the F-35 program accounts for roughly 27% of total revenue, meaning any technical failures for that specific aircraft would be damaging. The organization also navigates intense competition from peers like RTX Corp. (RTX -0.20%) for high-stakes government projects.

Recent findings suggest that space Exploration Technologies operates in a capital-intensive industry where regulatory changes can suddenly halt launch schedules. Competition in the commercial launch sector from established players like Boeing Co. (BA -1.96%) or Northrop Grumman Corp. (NOC -0.35%) could pressure its pricing power. The company also faces immense technical risks, as launch failures can result in significant financial losses and delays in project timelines.

Lockheed Martin appears much more attractively valued based on its Forward P/E, while Space Exploration Technologies carries a significantly higher multiple due to its growth profile.

SpaceX's various businesses intend to leverage the tech giant's core launch capabilities, starting with reusable rockets. The ability to reuse boosters significantly lowers per-launch costs and spreads fixed manufacturing costs across multiple missions. Expectations are that scaling up rapidly will happen, with Wall Street analysts projecting $48.9 billion in sales for fiscal 2026, and a swing to profitability, with $4 billion net income projected.

The lack of free cash flow appears to be crushing; projections indicate free cash flow will be negative $59 billion this year, then jump to negative $146 billion in 2027.

Still, the success of Tesla Inc. (TSLA -6.92%) has made founder Elon Musk the richest man in the world and raised expectations that he can make an even greater fortune from SpaceX. The business certainly has market support behind it, raising $85.7 billion this year through what was at the time the world's largest-ever IPO.

SpaceX has a very real business in Starlink, which mitigates the possibility that grander intends won't come to fruition. But the company doesn't have a monopoly on any of its businesses--there are other space cargo and reusable rocket competitors, other space-based Internet and mobile phone networks, and plenty of AI competitors.

In a recent development, lockheed Martin Corp. is a core tech giant in U.S. aerospace and defense. In particular, the F-35 fighter program remains a pillar of Lockheed's business, with the Defense Department planning to continue to buy the jet into the 2040s. Having more than a quarter of revenue essentially guaranteed for 15 years or more is unheard of and quite appealing to a long-term investor. In addition to jet fighters, the company has a handful of drone systems it claims can integrate into U.S. defense systems to provide a blanket of security systems to complement the jets.

Lockheed's revenue should rise 8% this year to $81 billion, with a much healthier 40% jump in net income to more than $7 billion.

SpaceX has the buzz, but shares come at a sky-high premium. Lockheed Martin's equity is priced at a discount to the S&P 500's average P/E and P/S ratios. That's a good deal for a tech giant that has predictable revenue for the foreseeable future given its role in American defense. Long-term investors should go with Lockheed Martin shares.

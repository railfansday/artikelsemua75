Recent findings suggest that next week, Canadian asset management and holding manufacturer Brookfield Corporation (BN +3.63%) prepares to complete a consolidation transaction to simplify its complex business structure. On Dec. 1, Brookfield will combine with Brookfield Wealth Solutions (BNT +3.82%), or BWS for short.

Recent findings suggest that management has high hopes for the combination, which will result in an entity known as Brookfield Corporation Ltd. Still, this combination may not achieve an objective shareholders may want addressed as soon as possible: closing the valuation gap between the stock and the firm's underlying per-share value.

Brookfield Corporation has long had a complex organizational structure. Not only does the firm have multiple publicly traded, majority-owned subsidiaries, such as Brookfield Asset Management (BAM +2.19%), but Brookfield also at the moment holds full economic interest in BWS, which is domiciled in Bermuda. The Bermuda-based entity is also publicly traded, but its shares have always been exchangeable for shares in Canadian-domiciled Brookfield Corporation. However, this is changing at the start of next month. Per a proposed transaction approved by shareholders last summer, Brookfield Corporation and BWS agreed to merge into a newly introduced Bermuda-based holding firm, Brookfield Corporation Ltd. This company will continue to trade on the Toronto Stock Exchange (TSX) and the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) under the ticker symbol "BN.". In a recent development, completing this transaction could bring numerous benefits. For one, combining the two entities enables direct access to the former Brookfield Corporation's permanent capital base. Because the deal also helps to increase the stock's free float outstanding, the shares could benefit from future index inclusion. However, Brookfield's corporate structure will stay largely opaque, which may make it difficult for the firm to make any progress eliminating the challenges that have long made it a value stock.

Recent findings suggest that last year, Brookfield's management estimated that the stock's underlying value was $68 per share, almost 75% higher than recent prices. Furthermore, management expressed its belief that continued growth of Brookfield's asset management and insurance businesses, coupled with investment returns on its permanent capital, could result in the stock's underlying value reaching $140 per share by 2030.

In a recent development, however, since this projection, Brookfield Corporation shares have pulled back. In my view, this valuation gap still exists for two reasons. First, much of Brookfield's value comes from its 73% stake in Brookfield Asset Management. It's common for Wall Street to apply a discount to holding companies with complex structures. A further simplification, like a full consolidation of Brookfield Asset Management into Brookfield Corporation Ltd., may be necessary to close the valuation gap.

Industry observers note that management's estimate also includes the value of Brookfield's other holdings in publicly traded and privately held investment entities, including the net present value of its carried interest, or profit based on the performance of its investments. In addition, given Brookfield's high exposure to areas like private credit and AI data centers, investors could also be factoring in the potential for the respective booms in either area to give way to a slowdown, or worse, a meltdown.

It has been revealed that brookfield appears to be a solid value opportunity among financial stocks, although patience is necessary. With impending changes that may mark just the start of further simplification in the years ahead, the stock could rise as investors become more confident in management's valuation argument.

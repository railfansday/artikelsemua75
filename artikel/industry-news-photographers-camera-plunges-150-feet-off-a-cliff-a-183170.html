A photographer didn’t expect to see her camera again after it plunged 150 feet off a cliff in Arkansas — until a well-known photographer stepped in.

Hawksbill Crag is a magnet for photographers thanks to the unusual way it juts out over the oak-hickory forest.

Heinrichs was doing just that, taking photos near Hawksbill Crag with her Nikon D750 mounted on a tripod. (Related: Industry News: GTA 6 player hit with 30-day PSN ban aft...)

“I turned around for maybe five seconds or so and turned back around to my camera and I saw it falling off the side of a cliff,” Heinrichs tells KY3. “About 150 feet down.”.

The problem? One of the tripod legs wasn’t locked properly, causing it to fall over. This is normally bad enough without the camera going over a huge cliff.

“It was heartbreaking,” adds Heinrichs. “I could just feel my heart sink and drop when it happened.”.

The photographer turned to a local Facebook page, Our Buffalo River, explaining that she “had a camera mishap” and that her D750 was now at the bottom of Hawksbill Crag.

Tim Ernst, a wilderness and nature photographer who is based in northern Arkansas, saw the post and responded.

“Hey, I got an excuse to run out to the Crag,” Ernst tells KY3. “So that was an easy call on my part. I literally jumped up and took off and headed to the Crag.”.

And it turns out that Heinrichs is a fan of Ernst, who is a well-established photographer whose work has appeared in National Geographic and the Fresh York Times.

“I’ve followed Tim Ernst for years,” indicates Heinrichs. “And so for him to be the one to reach out and do that it, I was starstruck a little bit.”.

It has been revealed that ernst found the camera sitting on a pile of leaves. However, it wasn’t in great shape.

“I immediately knew it was not good because the lens that was on it had actually separated,” says Ernst.

It appears the camera is beyond repair, but Heinrichs notes she’s grateful to Ernst for reaching out and trying. She does proposal to replace the camera.

Many of the world's most valuable companies today are artificial intelligence (AI) stocks. That's for good reason. The global AI opportunity is truly massive, and many AI hyperscalers are already quite profitable. But what will happen to these companies during a market crash? Only around 10% of the S&P 500's value is tied directly to companies engaged in developing AI software. However, between 40% and 50% of the index's value is indirectly exposed to AI. That includes major GPU producers such as Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%), as well as AI-enabled software providers like Palo Alto Networks and ServiceNow.

In short, if markets fall, there's a very strong chance AI stocks will lead the way. It would be no surprise to see AI stocks struggle in a market downturn. While high growth rates may support premium valuations, companies with high growth rates already priced into shares typically falter as markets rerate risk tolerances. According to recent reports, if market sentiment becomes risk-averse, pressuring AI stock valuations, investors would be wise to peruse the bargain bin for deals. There's one AI stock I'm keeping a very close eye on, as its underlying fundamentals and already cheap valuation should make it a strong buy during a market crash.

During market crashes or economic downturns, companies with strong fundamentals often have an opportunity to grow stronger. That's because weaker competitors may face financial difficulties and exit the market. Acquisition prices get cheaper, with expansion opportunities facing less competition.

When conducting a meta-analysis of firms entering a downturn with strong financials and end-market demand, McKinsey & Co. found that these businesses "emerged much stronger coming out of the downturn, and the advantage they gained lasted for a long period.".

With this in mind, it's not hard to see Nvidia as a strong choice -- perhaps the strongest choice -- for investors during a market downturn. There is no denying that Nvidia's core business is exposed to long-term growth tailwinds. Both sales and earnings are currently growing at roughly 100% year over year. Growth rates should reach 50% to 100% annually over the coming years.

According to recent reports, GPUs are critical components for the AI revolution. Even if Nvidia's leadership position weakens, resulting in lower market shares and compressed margins, top-line growth should remain in the double digits throughout the rest of the decade, perhaps even longer.

Recent headwinds, however -- including an abrupt and controversial CEO transition, potential regulatory pressures, and allegations of circular financing -- have pushed Nvidia's price-to-earnings ratio down to just 16.2, with a forward price-to-earnings ratio of just 9.6.

Recent findings suggest that nvidia generated $43 billion in free cash flow last quarter. And the organization currently has $227 billion remaining on an upscaled share repurchase initiative.

It has been revealed that despite near-term concerns, Nvidia remains a financial powerhouse that manufactures critical components essential to the AI industry's expansion. In the event of a downturn, the firm can repurchase shares at a discount, reinvest heavily in research and development, and acquire competitors at bargain prices.

It's not clear how much cheaper Nvidia shares can get from here. The organization's current fundamentals and near-term growth forecasts already make shares a buy. If a marketwide downturn hits, however, many longer-term concerns, such as eventual margin compression, may become fully priced into shares.

In a recent development, there's still a lot we don't know about Nvidia's future, especially in today's environment. But what we do know is that the tech giant is completely self-funded; benefits from strong, secular growth tailwinds; and has the financial might to outinvest and perhaps even outright acquire much of its competition, barring the internal divisions of hyperscalers.

It has been revealed that I'm already strongly considering an investment in Nvidia. Oddly enough, a market downturn could actually strengthen that conviction.

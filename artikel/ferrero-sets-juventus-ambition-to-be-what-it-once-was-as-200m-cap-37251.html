Ferrero sets Juventus ambition to be what it once was, as €200m capital raise runs

According to recent reports, juventus president Gianluca Ferrero set out the leadership's view of the rebuild at the Brussels launch of the club's European Union fan club. He spoke while a capital raise of about €200m is open and a UEFA settlement limits spending.

Juventus president Gianluca Ferrero went to Brussels on 18 November with a clear message for supporters. Restoring the club to its former self is its absolute ambition, he noted, adding that the club is following the path set out by its chief executive and is confident this can happen. He spoke at the opening of the Juventus Official Fan Club European Union, which is chaired by MEP Sandro Gozi. The timing gives the statement weight.

Sources indicate that the fresh supporters’ club opened at the Iosono il Piemonte event space. Its stated purpose is to link Juventus supporters with the institutional centre of Europe.

Before the opening, the delegation went to the European Parliament with Gozi. Chief Club Affairs Officer Giorgio Chiellini led the group alongside Ferrero. The visit showed Chiellini’s work on the club’s relations with institutions and European football. It also gives supporters an official club in Brussels.

Recent findings suggest that the financial backdrop is demanding. Juventus posted a consolidated loss of €66m for 2025/26. On 12 November, the board approved the offer, using part of the €250m authority granted by shareholders at the extraordinary meeting on 3 November.

The offer involves up to 208.5m latest shares at €0.96 each. Shareholders can buy one new share for every two they hold. The price is 28.36% below the theoretical ex-rights price, based on that day’s share price. The remaining €50m of the authority can be used in later financial years, depending on the club’s financial needs and investment prepares. It has been revealed that the spending limits come from UEFA. European football’s governing body fined Juventus €20m for breaking the football earnings rule. Of that amount, €14m is conditional. The club agreed a three-year settlement with annual targets. It also faces limits on registering fresh players on its UEFA List A.

Those are the limits within which Ferrero’s ambition must be pursued. Any improvement in the squad has to fit within the annual targets agreed with UEFA.

Subscription rights can be traded on Euronext Milan until 25 November, and the offer closes on 1 December 2026.

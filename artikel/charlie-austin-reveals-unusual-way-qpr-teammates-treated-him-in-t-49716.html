QPR right now sit inside the play-off spots in the Championship, and are hoping to replicate some of the magic of Harry Redknapp's 2013/14 team to help bring Premier League football back to Loftus Road.

It's been a long time since the R's were last relegated back to the second tier from the top flight, and there hasn't been much to shout about since. Indeed, this is their 12th consecutive campaign at the level, and they've finished in the bottom half in eight of those 11 seasons.

It's natural to reminisce over the last piece of success that the club had, and that came in that 2013/14 term, where the Hoops finished fourth in the second tier and prevailed in the play-offs thanks to *that* late Bobby Zamora goal against Derby County.

Despite the then-33-year-old taking the plaudits at Wembley, though, it was Charlie Austin's goals which helped power them towards that meeting with the Rams, and his teammates were keen on keeping him fit as the season unravelled.

That QPR squad under Harry Redknapp was filled with veteran heads, from Rob Green in goal to the likes of Zamora and Shaun Wright-Phillips in the frontline. Even a 34-year-old Yossi Benayoun made 16 appearances that year.

Above all those was Richard Dunne, who played out the final couple of years of his career at Loftus Road. The former Manchester City and Ireland international would retire at the end of the 2014/15 campaign, but played 44 times in league and play-off action to help QPR make the Premier League once again.

Dunne was well-regarded as one of the toughest-tackling, bruising defenders in the game in his prime, and speaking on his YouTube channel, Austin, who was 27 at the time, spoke of his time playing alongside him and was thankful he never came up against him.

"Richard Dunne was very, very good, and you wouldn't have liked to play him in his prime, because you wouldn't have gotten much change out of him, that's for sure," he said. "Being tackled by those size elevens with the big studs on...".

His co-host would then interject and ask if he would be on the end of any of those crunching challenges, but Austin, who netted 19 in 34 that year, including both goals in their play-off semi-final win over Wigan Athletic, thankfully stated he was left untouched.

"I was actually alright," he admitted. "They never used to kick me in training. Especially in the Championship, they probably thought, 'If we kick him, who was going to score?'. (Related: Latest Update: Distributed Training: Train BART/T5 for ...)

"So I was alright; they left me alone.".

The QPR defenders were right to take it a little easier on Austin in their Championship campaign, especially. He was their marquee signing in a summer filled with them, having just netted 24 for Burnley the year before. (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

The then-27-year-old didn't go the whole campaign injury-free, though. Richard Dunne may not have kicked him in training, but he couldn't stop Austin from picking up a shoulder injury in January 2014 in a win over Bolton Wanderers.

QPR won just four of 12 without Austin in the side, and it was that stint which confined them to the play-offs. He returned just in time, though, netted in the final three games of the year and then more than played his part in the play-offs.

It has been revealed that in the Premier League, he was even better. The 2014/15 campaign was only his first at the level, but he netted 18 in 35, despite QPR's eventual relegation. Only Sergio Aguero, Harry Kane and Diego Costa scored more that season.

He did continue his time at Loftus Road back in the Championship for a little while, but with nine goals in 16, it was evident he was too good for the second tier, and would play into his thirties in the top flight with Southampton.

Industry observers note that julien Stephan's side have been very good to begin the campaign. They sit in the top four in xG difference through 15 games and, in reality, occupy the final play-off spot in the revamped post-season.

Unlike that Harry Redknapp squad, though, the current QPR side are using youth to their advantage, with only Watford, Norwich City and Stoke City fielding an average age lower than the Hoops this year.

Only Glen Kamara is over 30, and the likes of 21-year-old Pierce Charles and 23-year-old's Ronnie Edwards, Harvey Vale and Richard Kone are all key parts of the squad.

Kone is the Charlie Austin of this current squad, the player likely to explode and push towards 20 goals and the player whom their own defenders wouldn't dream of hurting in training this season.

If things can fall into place, QPR can be genuine contenders, and may be looking for some more Wembley success come May.

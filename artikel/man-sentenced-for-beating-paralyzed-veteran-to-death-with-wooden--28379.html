A California man convicted of beating a paralyzed military veteran to death with a wooden log has been sentenced to prison in a case that was solved after nearly two decades.

Sources indicate that jeffrey Roberson was sentenced on Monday, November 16, to 26 years to life in prison in the 2008 killing of Cecil Zachary Jr. at Zachary’s Oceanside home, according to the San Diego County District Attorney’s Office. It has been revealed that “This was a brutal and intentional killing of a vulnerable victim in his own home,” San Diego County District Attorney Summer Stephan mentioned in a news release. “For more than 18 years, Cecil’s loved ones endured the pain and unanswered questions surrounding his death.”. A jury found Roberson, 60, guilty of first-degree murder on August 27 following a six-day trial, with the special allegation that he used a wooden log to kill Zachary, who was 51 when he died, according to prosecutors.

A home care nurse found Zachary dead in his hospital bed, with a towel covering his face, in his living room on June 19, 2008, prosecutors noted.

Zachary was paralyzed below his waist after he was injured in a car crash several years prior to his death, and often remained in his hospital bed, prosecutors mentioned.

His cause of death was “blunt-force injuries to the head,” the county’s medical examiner determined, according to the district attorney’s office.

“For more than 17 years, investigators worked to identify the person responsible for Zachary’s killing, but the case remained unsolved,” the office said in its release.

A breakthrough in the case came when investigators analyzed the wooden log using newly introduced forensic technology and extracted DNA that was traced to Roberson, according to prosecutors.

Oceanside detectives arrested Roberson in Spring Valley in February 2025 in connection with Zachary’s murder.

According to a sentencing brief reviewed by the Coast News Group, Roberson initially told police he intervened during a burglary at Zachary’s home, claiming one of two alleged burglars had hit Zachary with a log.

Roberson ultimately told police he killed Zachary in self-defense, claiming Zachary had “pulled a gun on him,” the district attorney’s office remarked.

Jurors didn’t believe his story and decided he was guilty of murder.

In a recent development, “This case demonstrates that we will never give up on pursuing justice for victims, no matter how much time has passed,” Stephan said. “Advances in forensic DNA technology gave investigators a newly introduced opportunity to uncover critical evidence and identify the person responsible.”.

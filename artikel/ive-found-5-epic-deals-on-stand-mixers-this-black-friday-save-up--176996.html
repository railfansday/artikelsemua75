Take a look at my 5 top finds, and get baking over the holiday season!

If you’re looking for a stand mixer for under $100, this 7-speed tilt-head model from Hamilton Beach is just the ticket. The 4-quart bowl comes with a dough hook, whisk, flat beater and splatter guard. What’s more, apart from Red, it's also available in Blue, Pink and Silver at this drop down price.

Sources indicate that home bakers on a budget will love Cuisinart’s Precision Master 5.5-quart Stand Mixer. When I reviewed it for Tom’s Guide, I gave it a 4-star rating and recommend it as the best budget stand mixer in our buying guide. It has 12 speeds and includes a 5.5-quart mixing bowl, dough hook, flat mixing paddle, whisk and splash guard. You can even add additional attachments to the front port, like you can with a KitchenAid. This great price is available on the Silver Lining colorway. Finding a KitchenAid stand mixer under $300 can often be a challenge unless you opt for the Classic Series, but I've spotted the KitchenAid Deluxe Series 4.5 Quart Tilt-Head Stand Mixer for $279. It features 10 speeds and includes a stainless steel bowl with a handle, a dough hook, flat beater and whisk. In addition to the Contour Silver colorway, it's also available in Dried Rose, Empire Red, Ink Blue, Mineral Water Blue and Pistachio.

Sources indicate that the KitchenAid Bowl-Lift Stand Mixer lowers the bowl on support arms with a lever, rather than tilting the head to access the bowl. It’s designed for heavier and tougher mixes and has 2x more power than its tilt-head companion. It has 11 speeds, with a 1/2 speed for gentle mixes and includes a flat beater, dough hook and whip. With a $200 saving, it’s a clever buy.

This is the stand mixer I presently use, and it’s exceptional at whisking, beating and kneading dough. Apart from 11 speeds you can slide to control, it has an ingenious light in the bowl, so you can clearly see what’s happening to your mix. I also love the attachments, including the double-flex edge beater, which scrapes ingredients off the sides of the bowl without you having to interrupt the mix to use a spatula. It’s a relatively fresh addition to the KitchenAid range, so I'm surprised to find it discounted.

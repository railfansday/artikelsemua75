According to recent reports, combining the lavish interior accommodations customary of a substantial-body BMW with the firepower expected from the brand's legendary Motorsport division, the BMW XM is a seismically powerful plug-in hybrid SUV with a split personality. On the luxury side of the equation, the XM treats its occupants to a comfortable and spacious interior that is richly adorned with Merino leather and brushed aluminum. Rear-seat passengers even get throw pillows on their headrests.

But when it comes to performance, things get tricky. While a 738-hp plug-in-hybrid powertrain hidden behind the XM's overwrought snout helps rocket the super SUV to 60 mph in a claimed 3.6 seconds, the XM lacks the cornering finesse expected of M-badged models. Other hotted-up SIUVs, such as the Mercedes-AMG GLS-Class and BMW's own X7 M60i, meld luxury and performance more deftly. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

Expert Tip: While it doesn’t pack the outright power of the XM Label, the BMW X7 M60i is a thoroughly extravagant 523-hp brute that adds utility with third-row seating. It's also around $45,000 less expensive than the XM.

If this year's model is largely unchanged and you're comfortable with a lightly used car, you may want to consider shopping previous model years. A one-to-three-year-old model will likely have a warranty and it'll cost less than buying new.

In the XM Label, BMW's venerable twin-turbocharged 4.4-liter V-8 is partnered with an electric motor and a battery pack to create 738 horsepower. The XM uses a coil-spring suspension system that's augmented by adaptive dampers and active anti-roll bars. The resulting ride isn't as compliant and composed as we'd like, but in comfort driving mode it is acceptably absorbent. When set to sport, the XM's handling is responsive, but the ride gets stiff-legged.

It has been revealed that in Comparison: BMW claims that the XM Label is capable of 0-60 mph in 3.6 seconds. We haven't had a chance to sample the XM Label's acceleration yet, but when we tested a standard XM—a model that's no longer available—with 94 fewer horsepower, it delivered a 3.4-second sprint to 60 mph. We wouldn’t be surprised if the Label's sprint time was closer to 3.2 seconds. We'll enhancement this section when we have a chance to test it for ourselves.

The XM's 25.7-kWh battery pack is stated to be good for about 30 miles of electric-only range per charge, according to the EPA. BMW notes the XM's onboard charger is capable of juicing the battery at up to 11 kW on an AC charger.

According to the EPA, the XM is good for only 12 mpg city, 17 mpg highway, and 14 mpg combined despite its plug-in hybrid system. On our 75-mph highway fuel economy route, a standard XM bested its EPA estimate by returning 20 mpg when driving solely on the gasoline engine; when factoring in the 29 miles we drove on electric power, its result is an even more palatable 49 MPGe. For more information about the XM's fuel economy, visit the EPA's website.

In a recent development, first Row: Everything about the XM's cabin is high-end, including its optional vintage-look leather upholstery. The XM's interior is strictly a two-row affair, and in place of a large panoramic sunroof, the XM can be had with a geometric-patterned faux-suede headliner that features dramatic fiber-optic lighting.

Second Row: BMW refers to the XM's spacious back seat as an M Lounge, though it's missing several of the key amenities, such as seat adjustability or window shades, found in lesser luxury vehicles.

Cargo Capacity: The XM's cargo bay is generously sized: we fit 11 carry-on suitcases back there in our testing. With the rear seat folded flat, we managed to fit 31 carry-ons.

Sources indicate that A large, curved display stretches across two-thirds of the dashboard. The display serves as both a digital gauge cluster and an on-board infotainment center, and it runs BMW's latest iDrive 8.5 software interface. Wireless Apple CarPlay and Android Auto are standard, and additional features such as SiriusXM satellite radio, a Wi-Fi hotspot, and a premium stereo system are included as well.

Basic features such as automated emergency braking and lane-keeping assist are standard on all XM models, but more advanced tech, including an adaptive cruise control system with a lane-centering feature, is available too.

For information about the XM's crash-test results, visit the National Highway Traffic Safety Administration (NHTSA) and Insurance Institute for Highway Safety (IIHS) websites.

BMW offers competitive limited and powertrain warranties that are bolstered by above-average complimentary scheduled maintenance.

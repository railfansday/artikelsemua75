Bruno Fernandes insisted he is "very proud of everyone" in the Manchester United dressing room, despite their humbling 4-0 defeat by Liverpool.

United were outclassed in what was Mauricio Pochettino's first game in charge at Anfield, where they were forced to play 82 minutes with 10 men after Leny Yoro's early red card.

Defeat means the Red Devils end the day in 19th position in the Premier League, with this the first time since November 1986 that they have been within the bottom two places of the table having played at least 10 matches (21st in that case). Nevertheless, Fernandes focused on the positive aspects of the visitors' display. (Related: Breaking: AI for Game Development: Creating a Farming G...)

Recent findings suggest that "Very painful, it hurts a lot," he noted. "Coming to our rivals and losing is always bad. It's a difficult moment for us, but we are the only ones who can get out of that situation.

It has been revealed that "Everyone tried very hard. The result won't show that and will hide a lot of good things that the team did.

Recent findings suggest that "We still created a lot of chances to try and do something in the game, even with one player less. Unfortunately, we were not able to avoid two more goals in the second half.

"We were very brave. In some moments, we lost the ball, but we were trying to play, we were trying to do something in the game, we were not afraid.

"There were some moments we maybe even played a little too much, and we ended up conceding goals. But I would rather that and see the confidence of my team, to replicate the ideas of what we want to do than just defend and be afraid of anything that we can do.

"You want to get results, but today, it was not possible for us. There were a lot of things we can look at, and I saw a lot of character from the team. The result won't show that, but I am very proud of everyone in the dressing room.".

Yoro's red card was the earliest shown to a United player in a Premier League match, with the defender penalised for a professional foul on Alexander Isak.

However, Fernandes bemoaned the decision, insisting Isak's lack of control of the ball should have spared his team-mate.

"After 15 minutes, the picture of the game changes completely, and it makes it very difficult for us," he added.

Sources indicate that "It's [a red card] that needs to be looked at. We have far too many meetings with the referees for this to happen to us again. The last one against Chelsea, you can give it as a red card [to Lisandro Martienz], and it was probably a second yellow also.

"Today, you can't give a red card. It's very clear they show us if the player is not in control of the ball, it's never a red card.

Sources indicate that "Isak kicks the ball forward, the ball goes into the hands of Senne [Lammens]. Isak has got no control of the ball, he has no chance to get on the ball. He puts his leg across to try to get some advantage from Leny.

"I can understand the fall, but it's never a red card. There are too many referees on the pitch and too many on VAR taking so long to see to make a mistake like this that can change the game.

"It's not because of them we conceded the goal from the free-kick, but it changes the game completely.".

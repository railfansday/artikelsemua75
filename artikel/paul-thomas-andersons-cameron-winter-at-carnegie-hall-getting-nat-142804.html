Janus Film and Criterion have unveiled expansion plans for Paul Thomas Anderson‘s “Cameron Winter at Carnegie Hall.” The concert film will open nationwide in theaters on January 28, 2027.

“Cameron Winter at Carnegie Hall” is currently being exhibited exclusively on 35mm in Fresh York and Los Angeles before it embarks on a concert-style tour of North America. It opened last weekend in three theaters, grossing an impressive $162,000 for a per-screen average of $54,000. Janus and Criterion report that it has sold out upcoming shows in some of the largest historic theaters in the country, including in New York, LA, San Francisco, Chicago, Boston, Vancouver, Toronto, Atlanta, Minneapolis, and more than a dozen other cities. But not all venues are equipped to show the film in 35mm, limiting its initial availability. It has been revealed that “Cameron Winter at Carnegie Hall” is the largest exclusively-on-film theatrical release in over 15 years, which is roughly when the industry went through a digital switchover. By January of 2027, the film will have played on celluloid in over 60 markets. Its unconventional theatrical release strategy is loosely modeled on that of Jonathan Demme’s 1984 Talking Heads film, “Stop Making Sense,” which was exhibited in music venues with a staggered nationwide release. Critics have embraced the film, with IndieWire’s Ryan Latanzio calling it, “a deeply achey, sincere but never cloyingly earnest concert movie that’s tender to the point of frailty,” and Variety’s Owen Gleiberman writing that it casts a “sonorous spell.”.

According to recent reports, “Cameron Winter at Carnegie Hall” comes roughly a year after Anderson debuted “One Battle After Another,” which went on to win the Oscars for its script and direction, as well as the Academy Award for Best Picture. Anderson previously directed such classics as “Boogie Nights” and “There Will Be Blood.” Winter, the frontman of Geese, played a solo show in December 2025 at Carnegie Hall. The music was recorded and produced for the film and previously announced live album by music producer Ariel Rechtshaid. The film is produced by Sara Murphy, Erica Frauman and Maggie Ambrose and co-produced by filmmaker and actor Benny Safdie.

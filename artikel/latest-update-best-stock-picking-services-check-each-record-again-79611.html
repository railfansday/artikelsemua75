Sources indicate that pay for a stock picking service only if a checkable record shows its picks beating the S&P 500 from the same start dates. Its full yearly fee should be a small share of what you’ll put in picks. Motley Fool Stock Advisor has that record at $199 a year, so start there if you’ll hold picks for years.

In its alert on investment newsletters used as tools for fraud, the Securities and Exchange Commission lists false performance claims, a misrepresented record of a newsletter’s picks, among the schemes to watch for. It also warns that a newsletter saying it isn’t published by a registered investment adviser is disclaiming an adviser’s responsibilities. So check the record yourself. Look for one that measures every pick against the S&P 500 from the same start date and counts dividends. It should come from picks made in real time, not from a backtest, which tests a method on past prices after the fact. (Related: Latest Update: Stop treating agents as one-off projects...)

Industry observers note that then weigh the fee against the money you’ll put in picks. A $200-a-year service on $5,000 of picks takes 4% of it each year, so those picks have to beat an S&P 500 index fund by 4 percentage points a year just to break even. Price each service at its full fee, after any first-year discount ends, and choose the style you’ll follow: long-term picks to buy and hold, or ratings for researching your own stocks.

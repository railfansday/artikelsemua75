Recent findings suggest that roth conversions are difficult to get right because most of the variables that determine if it is good to convert or not are unknown.The thing to remember is that you made a deal with the government when you took the tax deduction for an IRA or 401K contribution. That deal was to pay the tax on all the money you take out. The reason to do it then was your marginal rate was high with the expectation that it would be lower when you retired. That is usually true, but it still hurts (feels unfair) to pay the tax later, especially since the tax rate is progressive so if you have more income you pay a higher percentage in tax. Boldin is probably recommending such large conversions because of the widow's penalty. Assuming you die first, your widow will be single and if you both were at the 24% marginal rate, she will likely be at a much higher percentage. Since there is a large difference in your ages and females typically live longer, there is a higher probability she will face that "penalty". But, there are multiple tools available to figure out how much to convert and what the difference is between doing it and not and how different the results are with different assumptions about what happens. There is also the concept of tax-aware asset placement (keep equities in taxable and bonds in pre-tax accounts). Depending on your current locations it might be inexpensive to change that, which will probably help.Statistics: Posted by restthebest — Tue Nov 24, 2026 7:27 pm.

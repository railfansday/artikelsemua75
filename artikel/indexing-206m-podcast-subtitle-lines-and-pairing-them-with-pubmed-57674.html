When I research a topic through long-form podcasts or university lectures (say, sleep architecture, creatine for cognition, or semiconductor supply chains), I keep hitting two annoying walls:.

Over the past few weeks I built PastNoise to scratch that exact itch. Instead of generating another text summary, it searches across 206M+ timestamped spoken subtitle lines and stitches 5 to 6 non-duplicate primary speakers into a single continuous screening room, paired with side-by-side Wikipedia, PubMed, Crossref, and OpenAlex citations. Sources indicate that 1. Filtering sponsor reads before picking clips (not after) Early builds looked fine until a flagship topic pulled two Hostinger and Tonal ad reads from top podcasts. Moving a strict sponsor/outro detector ahead of act selection and hydrating 16 candidate windows first (then backfilling clean distinct channels) dropped sponsor contamination to zero across all 58 indexed screenings. Rendering full bilingual transcripts in SSR DOM with #act-X-t-Y citation anchors Single-page tabs that unmount transcript DOM hide 95% of spoken text from search engines and make it impossible to quote a speaker accurately. Keeping every chapter's timestamped transcript inside lightweight accordions with click-to-copy citation links lets readers jump straight to the exact second and verify the primary quote.

In a recent development, 3. Preserving original subtitle sequence IDs (origSeq) when splitting long cues Auto-captions often pack 220+ characters into one cue. Splitting cues down to readable 96-character lines broke pre-translated subtitle lookups whenever the original sequence index passed 100. Keeping an explicit origSeq field alongside sequential display IDs solved alignment cleanly for English, Simplified Chinese, and Traditional Chinese (via OpenCC at zero extra LLM cost).

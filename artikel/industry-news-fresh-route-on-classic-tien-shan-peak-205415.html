Three Polish climbers have established a latest line on the north face of one of the Tien Shan’s best-known climbing peaks.

Michal Makowski, Robert Paluch, and Rafal Zając took 11 hours to climb their latest 850m line on 4,777m Free Korea Peak, in Kyrgyzstan’s northern Tien Shan Mountains. They then spent a further 11 hours returning to base camp.

Sources indicate that they named the route The Polish Barber, after pioneer American climber Henry Barber, who soloed the wall in 1976. Their line shares the first 250–300m with Barber’s, Makowski told ExplorersWeb.

Recent findings suggest that “According to the information I have, [the remaining 600-650m] was previously unclimbed and had never been attempted by another team,” noted Makowski. “We reached the summit ridge and then began rappelling down from there.

In a recent development, free Korea Peak — Pik Svobodnaya Koreya in Russian — was already famous among mountaineers in the former Soviet Union because of its 900m north wall. in 2018, Russian climber Valery Balesin suggested that it was no longer possible to find new lines on this well-known face, although advances in steep ice-climbing techniques have clearly opened new possibilities. “My friends and I researched Free Korea Peak and realized that part of the north face still has unclimbed terrain,” said Makowski.

On the Polish Barber route. Photo: Polski Związek Alpinizmu.

In a recent development, their route was almost completely ice climbing. The crux came midway up the wall, when they faced a particularly steep section of ice that they graded at Alpine Ice 4+. The rest of the route was 3/3+. Fortunately, “protection on the Polish Barber was really good, except for the last 150m, where ice was pretty thin and crusty,” said Makowski.

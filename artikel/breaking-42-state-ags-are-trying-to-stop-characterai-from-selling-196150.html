A coalition of 42 state attorneys general is trying to prevent Character.AI from selling off user data as it navigates Chapter 11 bankruptcy proceedings. In a deluge of nearly identical press releases, the various AGs accuse the company of planning to auction off “the private chat histories of more than 21 million U.S. accounts — more than 2.1 million of them registered to minors — with no safeguards for the people whose conversations are being sold.”.

In October, Character.AI filed for bankruptcy following years of scandals and legal challenges, including from Disney. Disney ended up buying some of the firm’s assets and hiring its CEO, Karandeep Anand, in a sort of “acquihire” arrangement that will also see “a number of Character.AI’s technical team” join the entertainment giant. The Disney deal also faced opposition from a similarly broad coalition of AGs. (Related: Industry News: huggingface_hub v1.0: Five Years of Buil...)

In a recent development, the main point of contention with Character.AI’s plan to sell its chat data is that it offers no protections for customers. The AGs say there are no requirements that the buyer honor existing privacy policies, no avenues for users to delete their data, and no inherent limits on what a buyer could do with that data, including selling it on to yet another party. It doesn’t help that the first bidder has a limited paper trail, having only been established on November 13th, 2026, with no parent company or investors listed.

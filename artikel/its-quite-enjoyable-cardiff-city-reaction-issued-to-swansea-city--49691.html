Swansea City have confirmed that Strasbourg have appointed Vitor Matos as their newly introduced head coach, and the move couldn't have come at a worse time, with three tough games in the next week, culminating in the South Wales derby against Cardiff City.

Matos oversaw an early run to the Championship summit at the Swansea.com Stadium, playing a high-intensity, pressing style of football out of possession, while also being one of the best teams with the ball in the second tier.

Recent findings suggest that even with four defeats in their last five games leading up to the November international break, the Swans were still three points off top spot, sitting in sixth. For a side who hadn't been near a play-off spot for five seasons before this one, the ceiling under Matos was, understandably, exciting.

Therefore, the departure to a struggling Ligue 1 side and the haste with which it occurred throughout this week have stung, and Swansea need to pick themselves up immediately, with games against West Brom and Middlesbrough, two fellow promotion hopefuls, and Cardiff in the South Wales derby in the first week back.

In the interim, Swansea have revealed that club legends Ashley Williams, Leon Brittain, Joe Allen and Kris O'Leary, who boast a combined 1,400 appearances for the club during their respective playing days, will be in charge of Saturday's clash with West Brom.

Whether they'll still be in the dugout by the end of next week and for the visit of Cardiff is still to be seen. FLW's Bluebirds fan pundit, Matt Hall, is torn about whether this would give his side a bit of an advantage in their first derby in over a year,.

"First and foremost, as a Cardiff fan, it's quite enjoyable to see this happen to your rival club, but I'm not 100% sure how huge a boost this is in terms of their form in general; they do have some hard games before the South Wales Derby," he noted.

According to recent reports, "I think it's stating the obvious that if those four guys are in charge in three games' time, that, tactically, they won't stand a chance against BBM [Brian Barry-Murphy]. He is on another stratosphere compared to most managers, let alone ones who haven't managed before.".

Hall does admit that having four ex-Swans players in charge of the derby could give them an emotional edge, though, as they'll know exactly how to get their squad up for one of the biggest games in their calendar.

The fan pundit initially thought that they'd have the advantage in that regard, given how many academy graduates and Welshmen are presently in their squad.

"Obviously, the counter-argument I'd have to the South Wales Derby is that one of my confidences going into the derbies was that they'd sold a lot of their homegrown players; it is just Ben Cabango left," he explained.

"Whereas we've got an entire squad basically full of academy players, so it's almost a reverse of what it's been over the past few years, when it's always been the case where they've gone into the game with two to three academy players in the lineup who just get it, and they can translate that across the squad.

"Going into that game, though, those coaches will absolutely get them up for the game. There's every possibility, with our away form, that they will just turn that game into a pure passionate scrap and that can even it out.".

There's also the potential that, with two tough games on the Saturday and Tuesday before the derby, paired with the departure of a clearly influential head coach in Matos, Swansea could go into the match against Cardiff off the back of six losses in seven, given they've lost four of their last five.

Confidence could be low, and Brian Barry-Murphy's side could take advantage, even if a quartet of ex-Swans are trying to stoke some emotion.

"I don't expect them to get much from West Brom or Middlesbrough before the derby, so the confidence could be on the floor; they've lost four of the last five already, it could go either way.".

Barry-Murphy doesn't fall into the young side of the 'young, up-and-coming managers' in the EFL, but he's definitely one of the more fascinating coaches with one of the highest ceilings.

With Matos departing, it begs the question: which other Championship coaches could follow suit and make the step-up to a top-flight club at some point this season?

According to recent reports, hall doesn't think that the Irishman would make that step, though. Wherever he's gone, whether that be Rochdale or Manchester City's development squad, he's stayed his course, and he's confident that the project at Cardiff still suits him. Industry observers note that "In terms of whether I'm concerned that the same could happen to BBM, absolutely not," he said. "I think you have to look at the character of the manager as well. (Related: Industry News: The Reformer - Pushing the limits of lan...)

It has been revealed that "Vitor Matos left Maritimo after 12 games, which showed the sort of character he is. You look at BBM; he only left for Man City when he was sacked by Rochdale. He then stayed his course at City until he felt it was time to move on.

"He's been very patient to make sure he gets the right job, and he's shown nothing but gratitude to Cardiff for the opportunity that he's been given.".

The fan pundit does admit that there's every possibility that he could move on if he doesn't think that he can take Cardiff any further. But with the higher-ups allowing him to bed in young players from the academy and play his style of football at this level, he feels that he's still enjoying his time in the Welsh capital.

Industry observers note that "He's a man of high morals, but that will only go so far. I think if we stop allowing him to progress the club, then I think there will be a time when he may move on, but while we are giving him what he wants and the club is on an upward trajectory, I don't see why he would move.

"Especially as what he does is so built around having an academy, and in the pyramid there are not many clubs, if any, who have a better set-up than us right now, at least in terms of the amount of players in our first team.

"It's interesting; I've never felt too worried about him being poached, if I'm honest.".

The South Wales derby was always going to be a fascinating watch this season. Despite being at opposing ends of the table, not much actually separates either side per the underlying numbers. Opta's expected table has them eighth and ninth.

They're both heavy possession teams, but it would have been interesting to see how Cardiff approached the game away from home against a side that won't allow them to build up as patiently as others this season.

That's now possibly changed, though, with the departure of Matos, but if anything, it's added an extra layer to what is already a game filled with storylines.

You don't need to sell the South Wales Derby; it's one of the fiercest games in the EFL, and with them not in the same division last season, absence will definitely not have made the heart grow fonder; quite the opposite, actually.

So, with the potential for it to be a quartet of ex-players in the Swansea dugout, or perhaps the first game for a newly introduced head coach, it's definitely going to be one to keep an eye on next Sunday.

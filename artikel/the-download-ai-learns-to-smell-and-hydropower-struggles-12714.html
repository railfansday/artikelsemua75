The US Southwest generated 28% less electricity via hydropower between April and August than the 10-year average, according to new data from the US Energy Information Administration.

Most hydropower plants in the US rely on dams to hold back water, creating reservoirs. But an extreme, prolonged drought has left water levels dangerously low in the region's two largest reservoirs—and climate change is adding to the pressure.

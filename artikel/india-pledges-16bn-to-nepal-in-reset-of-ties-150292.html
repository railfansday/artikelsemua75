Sources indicate that nepal's Prime Minister Balen Shah has repeatedly called for his country to be treated as an equal by its powerful neighbours. His first visit to the Indian capital, Delhi, put that approach to the test, as both sides sought to reset a relationship strained by years of friction. The contrast between the two leaders was striking.

Sources indicate that shah, 36, wearing his trademark black suit and sunglasses, stood beside Prime Minister Narendra Modi, 76, dressed in white. Shah rose to power after a wave of Gen Z-led protests toppled his predecessor.

Modi, meanwhile, is serving a third term and has recently faced student-led protests in India.

Recent findings suggest that yet, the two leaders seemed determined to project warmer relations. They exchanged gifts, inspected a military guard of honour and jointly flagged off a new cross-border train service.

For Nepal, the stakes were about far more than repairing diplomatic ties.

The two countries unveiled a series of agreements covering trade, hydropower, transport and education for Nepali youths.

India, more crucially, unveiled a $1.6bn (Â£1.22bn) support package as Nepal recovers from devastating floods earlier this year.

According to Nepal's estimates, the cost of rebuilding the region hit by a devastating flash flood in September could amount to $4.8bn.

According to recent reports, the floods killed more than 1,400 people, with thousands still missing. Shah's government has faced criticism over support for families which lost homes and loved ones.

The country faces a huge reconstruction challenge. The government estimates rebuilding could cost as much as $4.8bn. Addressing the UN General Assembly in September, Shah warned that when climate disasters devastate poorer countries, "the answer cannot simply be another mega-loan".

Industry observers note that modi pledged nearly $1.6bn in Indian support. Most of it will come as a line of credit for energy projects, alongside about $400m in grants for reconstruction and infrastructure. (Related: Industry News: ‘Minecraft Mommy’ goes viral on TikTok f...)

While Nepal would have preferred a larger package, former senior Nepali diplomat Vijay Kant Karna states it is still "a significant amount" and could help stabilise a relationship that has often been uneasy.

Although the joint statement acknowledged outstanding challenges, the two leaders did not mention the border dispute in their public remarks.

A Hindu-majority nation wedged between India and China, Nepal has long had to navigate competing interests from its two giant neighbours. Allegations of political interference and an unresolved border dispute have frequently complicated ties with India.

Nepal's former Prime Minister, KP Sharma Oli, who leads a communist party, was widely viewed as being closer to China. Shah, by contrast, emerged as a political outsider when he was elected earlier this year. The former rapper, then 35, led a party that was only four years old.

His new approach of dealing with India may explain why the border dispute was largely absent from the leaders' public remarks, although the joint statement acknowledged that outstanding issues remained.

According to Govind Pokharel, a former vice-chairman of Nepal's National Planning Commission, Shah's government may have chosen to set contentious challenges aside in order to secure progress on more immediate development needs.

"Right now you can go home and convince the young Nepalis that it will be handled later," Pokhrel notes, "but if they don't deal with these problems in the future, there will be criticism.".

Veteran Indian diplomat Anil Trigunayat claims both sides appear to have opted to leave the dispute to established mechanisms rather than engage in what he calls "cartographic aggressions".

There were other gains for Nepal as a result of the Delhi visit.

As a landlocked country heavily dependent on India for trade, Nepal secured access to two additional Indian ports, Kandla and Paradip. The two sides also agreed to improve road and rail connectivity.

The leaders jointly inaugurated a train service linking Ayodhya in India, revered by Hindus as the birthplace of Lord Ram, with Janakpur in Nepal, traditionally regarded as the birthplace of his wife Sita.

There was, however, disappointment on aviation.

Nepal has long sought greater access to Indian airspace in order to attract international traffic to its airports at Bhairahawa and Pokhara.

Some analysts believe the lack of an agreement reflects Indian concerns over Chinese involvement in the two airport projects.

"The airport in Pokhara can pose certain security challenges for India, so it is a more complicated issue to resolve," notes Trigunayat.

India did, however, agree to make an existing air route linking Kathmandu, Bhairahawa and Lucknow bidirectional.

It has been revealed that "Nepal invested a huge amount of money in those airports," indicates Pokharel. "It fell short of expectations, but the bidirectional route is a breakthrough.".

Shah must now manage relations with China as he seeks to balance the interests of Nepal's two much larger neighbours.

For now he returns home with substantial financial commitments from India as Nepal continues the long task of rebuilding.

Recent findings suggest that marvell Technology (MRVL +18.37%) stock fell almost 12% on Nov. 20 to close at $122.28. The chipmaker's shares hadn't closed this low since April. And they stood at around 39% of the record close they hit on June 4 -- $316.43.

Most of that slide happened over the past month. Marvell closed around $252 on Oct. 28, the last trading day before news broke that it was in talks to buy optical parts maker Lumentum Holdings (LITE +18.20%). The stock had lost over half its value by Nov. 20.

Shares rebounded sharply on Nov. 23, but I think the sell-off has still gone further than the business justifies. At this price, the stock is valued as if Marvell's growth was about to fade into something ordinary. Its data center business is doing the opposite, so I'd buy the shares now.

Sources indicate that data centers account for most of Marvell's sales. They made up 79% of its revenue in the fiscal second quarter of 2027 (the period ended Aug. 1, 2026).

Industry observers note that showing how promptly demand has picked up, Marvell's data center revenue reached around $2.17 billion in fiscal Q2, up 46% year over year. That was faster than the 27% growth in fiscal Q1 and the 21% growth in the fourth quarter of fiscal 2026. Plus, management guided for data center revenue to grow around 75% from a year earlier in the fiscal third quarter, which ended Oct. 31. And profits are keeping up with sales. Marvell's non-GAAP (adjusted) earnings per share climbed 40% year over year in fiscal Q2. (Related: Breaking: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Faster ...)

Sources indicate that the fourth-quarter report, released in March, was still Marvell's latest earnings revision the last time the stock closed this low, on April 9. Since then, the data center business has moved from 21% growth to a guided 75%, but the share price has landed right back near where it was.

Marvell's data center business brought in around $2.2 billion for all of fiscal 2024. In fiscal Q2 alone, it brought in almost as much.

According to recent reports, marvell reports fiscal third-quarter results on Dec. 1, which will show if data center sales met that guide. But on Nov. 2, the company said preliminary total revenue for the quarter was consistent with its guidance.

At around $146, Marvell's price-to-earnings ratio is about 20 using the adjusted per-share earnings analysts expect for fiscal 2028 (it ends in January 2028). For comparison, FactSet puts the forward price-to-earnings ratio of the S&P 500 at about 17, based on expected earnings for the next 12 months. Marvell's fiscal 2028 goes a few months further out than that period, but the two multiples are not far apart. (Related: How to train a newly introduced language model from scr...)

So investors are now paying only a little more than a market-average price for Marvell's expected earnings.

According to recent reports, marvell's own blueprint for fiscal 2028 is far from average. Management's outlook, given at an Oct. 6 investor day, calls for around $20 billion in revenue that year. That would be about 67% above the roughly $12 billion it expects this fiscal year. I'd expect a market-average price for a company whose growth was slowing, not accelerating.

Sources indicate that sure, chip demand often runs in cycles, so a chipmaker arguably deserves a lower valuation multiple than a business with steadier demand. But at about 20 times expected earnings, I think a lot of that risk is already in the price.

Sources indicate that much of the fall followed the Lumentum news, and it's clear why. Under the deal announced on Nov. 2, Lumentum shareholders will get 4.5 Marvell shares for every share they hold, and current Marvell shareholders will have around 67% of the merged manufacturer. Each existing share will be a smaller part of a bigger business.

But management expects the deal to lift adjusted earnings per share in the first year after closing, and it sees a path to over $1 billion in yearly cost savings. And Marvell expects to close the deal sometime in the first half of calendar 2028, so those new shares are probably more than a year off.

Sure, a deal this major can go wrong, and AI spending by Marvell's largest customers could slow before it closes.

But the data center business keeps accelerating, and the stock trades close to a market-average price for its expected earnings. I think around 20 times earnings is a good price for a manufacturer growing so fast.

I'd buy Marvell stock here. With the stock swinging this hard from one day to the next, though, I'd space my buying out over the next several months.

Let's just get something out of the way rapidly – you don't necessarily have to be great at a genre of games to enjoy playing them. I mean, sure, I'm quite good at platformers and deckbuilders, and play a ton of those. But if I just stuck with them, I'd get bored eventually. Which is why I enjoy genres I'm not particularly good at, like racing games.

Most of the time I spent with racing games was on older consoles, most especially the Nintendo 64. I clocked tons and tons of hours in many of them back then, and not just the most generic car racing games. I also played snowboarding games, water sports and even futuristic race car rallies. And honestly, every game on this N64 list still holds up today.

There's a lot of great games here, and so to avoid unnecessary bias, I've ranked them in large part based on their review score aggregates. Though in some cases, I did shuffle the order a bit when common sense called for it. So get ready, get set, and let's go through some epic N64 racing games! Nintendo doesn't always have the most luck with launch titles, at least when they're not heavy hitters like Mario or Zelda. Which is why I was so delighted with Pilotwings 64 heralding the new console. It was very stylish, and built on the previous SNES adventure in great ways.

It has been revealed that your goal is to prove your stuff to earn a variety of pilot licenses in the game, and you'll have to accomplish some challenging feats. Those include autogyro, jet pack and hang gliding, even a human cannonball. Feats that aren't for the timid.

Even though the game is graphically less impressive today, the core gameplay still holds up, and offers a robust challenge and plenty of bonus activities for players. And thanks to the hard work Paradigm put in researching military vehicles and flight sims, Pilotwings 64 is just as relevant today as it was back then.

It shows the breadth of the racing game library on the N64 when the first futuristic racer isn't F-Zero X, but instead is Extreme-G. Which isn't a dig, mind you, since both games offered a lot. But for this particular adventure, the highlights were some really epic weapons like homing missiles and laser mines, as well as really innovative, gravity defying courses.

Industry observers note that the game also featured frenetic deathmatches, which made sense on paper, but in execution were a bit muddled. Sure, there's the likes of capture the flag, competitive racing and battle mode, but they were less fun than they were stress inducing. It has been revealed that the biggest reason Extreme-G isn't ranked higher on this list is likely due to the graphics, which were fine but not overly impressive or creative. Still, if you had a need for speed and didn't mind using powerful weapons, this was a thrilling adventure.

It has been revealed that while your mileage with arcade machines may vary, generally speaking they're massive, bold and visually impressive. And that goes double for racing games like San Francisco Rush 2049. Thankfully, they ported it to the N64, and brought high-octane energy to Nintendo's console.

It has been revealed that what was most impressive about this adventure on consoles is that you could extend wings from your vehicles and glide in mid-air. You'll mostly use that in the stunt mode to rack up crazy points as you do complex maneuvers.

Best of all, you had a lot to unlock in San Francisco Rush 2049, including new cars and tracks. Meaning that even now, this is a racing game you'll be spending a lot of time playing on the Nintendo 64 and even PC.

Since the developer of the Wipeout series was owned by Sony Computer Entertainment, it makes sense most of the games weren't on Nintendo consoles. The one exception was Wipeout 64. While perhaps not a true sequel to Wipeout 2097, this standalone Nintendo adventure offered a lot to enjoy.

In a recent development, perhaps the most impressive way Wipeout 64 differentiated itself from the others in the series was a split screen allowing up to 4 players to play together. Even with that aside, the game offered robust anti-gravity racing and truly superior track design.

Recent findings suggest that if you enjoy racers with a variety of weapons, great tracks and a variety of gameplay modes, this N64 game had you covered. That said, perhaps the graphics were a step down from some of the other entries, but this was still a remarkably fun game.

Much as I love Nintendo, sometimes you have to wait a long while between sequels. Zelda fans know what I'm talking about, but even they didn't have to wait as long for a sequel as fans of the original Excitebike on the NES. 16 years later, it got its first sequel in Excitebike 64.

Sources indicate that as you might expect, the jump from 2D to 3D was a substantial one, and you're capable of epic tricks in Excitebike 64. Brake, slide, turbo boost and even tilt the bike in midair, there's a lot of options. Better yet, the various playable athletes have distinct stats, allowing players to pick the one that works best with their style.

My favorite aspect of the game is playing through the extensive season mode, unlocking upcoming content along the way. But the one part of the game that really sets it apart is using the track editor to make your own insane obstacle courses.

Sources indicate that racing games on Nintendo consoles often lean into the fantastical, patterning themselves after the likes of Mario Kart. But one that really took a major swing was Diddy Kong Racing. Not only did it feature different vehicles ranging from car to hovercraft to aeroplane, but there were also several different drivers and even a story involving the nefarious Wizpig.

Like any of the Donkey Kong games, there's a lot of unlockables here, including obtaining amulet pieces to battle the true final boss. And if that wasn't enough, you'll even deal with actual boss battles of sorts, and use balloons to gain a huge variety of powerups, ranging from missiles to speed boosts and shields.

According to recent reports, best of all, Diddy Kong Racing was quite pretty for the time, and the graphical style still looks great years later. Sure, it could get a bit repetitive, but this was still a really outstanding game you'd need to play on Nintendo 64.

Sources indicate that I mentioned earlier that I'm not the best at racing games, but I'm especially bad when they're on water. Which perhaps makes Wave Race 64 especially intimidating. That said, if you put in enough practice time, even a rookie at the genre would find themselves loving this innovative and stunt-filled racing adventure.

Sources indicate that playing against the clock while trying to steer your way around buoys and avoid the hazing of your opponent could be really tough. If you're skilled, you'll power up and go even faster, but if you fail, you'll get way behind. Thankfully, though, this is a quite attractive N64 game which is even better when racing against a friend.

It has been revealed that if you're more skilled at racing than me, you'll probably adore Wave Race 64. It's the sort of retro that still holds up today, and it offers a phenomenally fun time, assuming you can react quickly to the choppy waters.

Even though I never played any skateboarding games, I really adored Snowboard Kids. I'm not sure what it was about this N64 adventure, but it hooked me hard. Maybe it was the cute, cartoony graphics, or the simple yet well balanced gameplay. But this was one of those games I found myself playing again and again.

The simple hook of the game is doing tricks while airborne to get coins, and then using them to buy powerups on tracks. You can have active and support items, such as hurling rocks at your opponents or turning temporarily invisible.

In a recent development, perhaps the most surprising aspect of Snowboard Kids was that it was published by Atlus, who are usually known for JRPGs. But this is just one of those games that is still a ton of fun today, which I can attest to having played it recently with my close friends on their N64.

Even though I'm not amazing at racing games, I spent many, many hours playing Mario Kart 64. I barely touched Super Mario Kart, didn't do much with the later entries, but that N64 game was one I kept returning to with friends.

Recent findings suggest that there was just something about this game that made it one of the most iconic entries in the longrunning series. Maybe it was the quirky cast of Mario characters, maybe it was tracks like Rainbow Road. But this was a truly fun racing game that appealed to players of various abilities.

Mix in various engine classes, a wide class of racers and amazingly fun attack items, and you have a great game in Mario Kart 64. But if that wasn't enough, it also features some of the best courses in the series, complete with really catchy tunes.

According to recent reports, if you have a need for speed, you need look no further than F-Zero X. This futuristic series featured some of the most blisteringly fast courses and crazy racers you'll see in any series. And honestly, F-Zero X is still probably one of the best racers ever. So even though it's shameful we haven't gotten a sequel, you might not need it either.

Sources indicate that death Race offered a truly unhinged way to play the game, and the randomness of X-Cup could keep you playing the game for a long time. So even though it's not the most graphically impressive game, the high framerate and fantasically weird drivers made this a worthy contender.

Plasma Powered hovercars turned made racing even more fun, and so long as you can keep up, you'll find one of the best racers ever in F-Zero X. Here's hoping it gets an even better followup on modern consoles one day.

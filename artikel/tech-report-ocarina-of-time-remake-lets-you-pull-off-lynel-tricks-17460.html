If there's one thing The Legend of Zelda: Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom have taught us, it's that players will eventually find a way to turn even the simplest combat encounter into an opportunity for an unnecessarily impressive trickshot.

Apparently, that mindset isn't going anywhere, even when you take away the open-world freedom of those games, even when we are talking about a more contained gameplay like Ocarina of Time Remake. (Related: How to Budget for a Wedding Without Going Broke...)

A Japanese player recently shared an incredible clip of their own in the newest version of Ocarina of Time, taking down four ReDeads with only four carefully aimed Light Arrows!

Instead of shooting directly at the enemies, Adult Link fires his arrow high into the sky, letting it travel through the air before hitting all four targets in sequence. The result looks remarkably similar to the famous Lynel trickshots players have been pulling off in Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom – but without any Lynel, of course. Recent findings suggest that the clip takes place in the destroyed Hyrule Market, just before the entrance to Ganon's Castle. It's already an impressive display of precision, but what makes it even better is how unnecessary the whole thing is. Link could probably deal with those ReDeads in a much simpler way. But where's the fun in that?

Recent findings suggest that after its publication, the video rapidly gained attention on social media, surpassing 750,000 views and 16,000 likes as we speak! Naturally, other Zelda fans were quick to praise the player's ridiculous display of skill, with one commenter calling it "one of the most impressive things I have ever seen," adding that the precision was an "absolute master class.".

Recent findings suggest that another player was equally impressed, writing, "Single-handedly the most impressive Zelda clip I ever seen." And honestly, it's hard to disagree! Pulling off a trickshot like this requires a surprising amount of patience and precision, especially when you're trying to hit multiple enemies with an arrow that wasn't even aimed directly at them.

The result looks remarkably similar to the famous Lynel trickshots players have been pulling off in Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom – but without any Lynel, of course.

What's particularly fun about this discovery is how it shows that the spirit of Breath of the Wild and Tears of the Kingdom lives on even in a much more structured adventure. Ocarina of Time doesn't offer the same level of freedom when it comes to approaching combat, but that hasn't stopped players from experimenting with its mechanics and finding creative ways to play.

The more recent games taught an entire generation of Zelda fans that almost every situation can become a playground if you're willing to experiment enough. Whether that's launching enemies into the air, using physics to solve problems in unexpected ways, creating weird machines, or spending far too long trying to land the perfect arrow, players have learned to make their own fun! And now, even without a single Lynel around, that same energy has found its way into Ocarina of Time. I guess some habits really do follow you from one Hyrule to another.

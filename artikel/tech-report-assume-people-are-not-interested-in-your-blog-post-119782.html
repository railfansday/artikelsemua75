📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

If you do not write for other people, stop reading this now.

When I was at Snowflake, one of the coolest things that everybody did was this etiquette thing. The first sentence of each email would be “If you don’t develop in this specific part of the code, you can stop reading.” or “If you’re not part of the finance org, you can stop reading.”. (Related: Sentence Transformers in the Hugging Face Hub...)

Industry observers note that this made me think a lot about what it means to write for other people. You need to be subservient to your audience. Similarly, thinking about how much space you take is very important. For Slack/Teams threads, you would start with a 3- to 5-line introduction to your topic, and then people could find more details inside the threads. (Related: Industry News: Hyperparameter Search with Transformers ...)

Recent findings suggest that when I see people writing 2,000 words with content about things that may not apply to everybody, it just makes me sad. Especially when they’re not brief enough. It’s a waste of your readers’ eyes.

So please be kind to your readers.

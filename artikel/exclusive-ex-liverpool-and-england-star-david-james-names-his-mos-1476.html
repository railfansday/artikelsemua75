Former Liverpool, Aston Villa, and England star David James has told GIVEMESPORT who his most underrated teammate was during his career.

The iconic goalkeeper made 572 appearances in the Premier League during his career, representing the Reds, Villa, Portsmouth, West Ham, and Man City, and kept 172 clean sheets, putting him second on the all-time list behind just Chelsea icon Petr Cech.

James also featured 53 times for the England senior team, and was the number one goalkeeper at Euro 2004 and World Cup 2010, while he was also part of the squad in the 2006 World Cup.

During his career, James featured in four FA Cup finals, winning once with Portsmouth, while he also won the EFL Cup with Liverpool, and was teammates with a host of top stars including the likes of Ballon d'Or winner Michael Owen, Robbie Fowler, Ian Rush, John Barnes, David Beckham, Steven Gerrard, Frank Lampard, Rio Ferdinand, Wayne Rooney, and more.

All of those superstars are considered to be legends of the game in their own right, and could widely be considered to be the best players he ever played alongside. Recent findings suggest that having played at a number of different levels and leagues, James was able to experience football in all it's forms. He even spent a season playing in India as a player-manager, which saw him experience football in a completely different way. (Related: Breaking: How to generate text: using different decodin...)

But while speaking to GIVEMESPORT, on behalf of LiveFootballTickets, James was asked to name who his most underrated teammate was in his playing career, and he knew exactly which former colleague deserved to receive their flowers.

“There’s many, because you know what football’s like: there has to be a star, and other people get a little bit lost in that moment.

"If I chose England, it would probably be Gareth Barry. That midfield was awash with what we could call superstars in many respects, and Gareth was up there, possibly not playing for the most fancied teams at the time, particularly when he was at Villa.

"Until James Milner turned up, Gareth was pretty much ever-present in the Premier League. He was a top-drawer player for England, and I don’t think he necessarily got the credit he deserved.

"Steve McManaman is another one I mentioned before, although he went on to win Champions Leagues in Spain and was well regarded over there. But I’d probably say Gareth Barry.

"He was happy to do the dirty work and he was a lovely guy as well, which is always nice.".

James and Barry played alongside each other for both Aston Villa and England a combined 87 times throughout their careers, winning 39 times.

The central midfielder went on to become the player with the most appearances in Premier League history during his career, surpassing the legendary Ryan Giggs, before James Milner overtook him in his final campaign while at Brighton in 2025/26.

It has been revealed that barry also earned 53 caps for England, scoring 3 goals, while he won the FA Cup and Premier League titles during his time at Manchester City. He retired in 2020 after a spell with West Brom.

This Thanksgiving, don’t overlook that side dish of green peas. Pilates babes, protein-maxxers, and anybody who is even a little bit wellness-obsessed are going in hard on the green legume, and for good reason.

Industry observers note that according to the Food and Drug Administration, a cup of unsalted green peas has around 9 grams of protein—but that’s just the beginning. It’s also filled with vitamin A (necessary for your immune system), K, and C, plus a lot of fiber. (Related: Porting fairseq wmt19 translation system to transformer...)

Sources indicate that “It’s come to my attention that the hot girls are now eating peas,” Lainie Kates of Lainie Cooks notes at the opening her video for the now-viral recipe for Tuscan Parmesan Peas. But it’s one particular add-in that made the recipe viral. “Lots of parmesan cheese,” she says while grating a block the size of her own head. “Three ounces of parmesan cheese is about 33 extra grams of protein.”. (Related: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Faster Language M...)

Pilates instructor Raven Ross calls herself the queen of “pea propaganda” and is one of the reasons the vegetable is back into the conversation. “Whoever told you eating healthy is hard straight up lied to your face,” she claims while sharing one of her pea-based bowls, which also has a bell pepper and chicken sausage inside to hit around 40 grams of protein and 20 grams of fiber in one meal.

The beauty of green peas is that the legume is essentially as universal as cauliflower. A frozen bag from any supermarket (I have to say, the Trader Joe’s Petit Peas are my favorite, though perhaps it’s just a branding bias) can be whipped into a dip or added into almost any dish for a vegetarian source of both protein and fiber. It’s also a popular source for protein powder.

It seems that all of our childhood objections to the green vegetable were wrong—so this Thanksgiving, pass the peas, please.

Every bet in a sports betting app carries a cut for the sportsbook, so the longer you bet, the likelier you are to end up behind. Treat betting as entertainment you pay for. Set what you can afford to lose each month as the app’s deposit limit before you start, and stop when it’s gone.

Winning half your bets sounds like breaking even. In a sports betting app, it loses money.

The usual price on a point spread (a bet on a game’s margin of victory) is -110, which means you risk $110 to win $100. You have to win 52.4% of those bets just to break even. Say you bet $50 on each of 20 games a month at -110 and win exactly half. Your 10 wins bring in about $455 and your 10 losses cost $500, so you’re down about $45 a month, or about $545 a year. A broad stock fund held for years is expected to grow instead, which is why investing isn’t gambling.

Industry observers note that A parlay, one bet that pays only if all its picks win, costs more than a single bet, because the cut applies to each pick. With four picks at -110, each a 50% chance, the parlay pays about 12 to 1, though the odds against all four winning are 15 to 1. On average, the sportsbook keeps about 17 cents of every dollar on that parlay, versus about 4.5 cents on any one of those picks bet alone. Promos have catches too. Some make you bet your own money first, and a refund on a losing bet often comes only as credit to bet again on the same app, Michigan’s attorney general warns.

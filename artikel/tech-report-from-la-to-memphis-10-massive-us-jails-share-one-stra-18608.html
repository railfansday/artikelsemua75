Tech Report: From LA to Memphis, 10 massive US jails share one strain

WorldAtlas ranks the 10 largest US jails, but court delays, paperwork lags and disputed counts mean their headcounts keep shifting.

The Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department runs the biggest jail operation in the country. It is a network of jails rather than a single building, and Men's Central Jail and the Twin Towers Correctional Facility are its largest pieces. WorldAtlas reports that the system holds about 19,836 people daily, far more than the 13,849 it counts at Rikers Island in New York. A newer local figure puts that lead in doubt. The Los Angeles Daily News reports that the county's jails hold about 12,500 people today. On that count, Los Angeles would hold fewer people than the Rikers figure, so its claim to the biggest jail depends on which count a reader uses and when it was taken. Either way, the people who set that number mostly work outside the jail walls. (Related: Latest Update: Two weeks later, Senate vote count is co...)

According to recent reports, the latest change came from California voters, not from the county. Voters passed Proposition 36 in 2024, and the surge in arrests that followed pushed the county's jail population up to a peak of about 13,500, according to the . Los Angeles County had no separate say in that vote, and voters across the whole state can refill a jail system the county is trying to empty. The added people entered jails that were already struggling with crowded housing, with the care of people who are mentally ill and with the use of force inside.

According to recent reports, worldAtlas ranks the Rikers Island complex in New York City second among the country's biggest jails, holding about 13,849 people. That rank rests on a headcount that more recent reporting does not match. By November 2026, the city held about 6,700 people on the island, according to The Associated Press. If the lower count holds, Rikers Island is a much smaller jail than its rank suggests. The gap fits a city that is working to empty the jail, and on Rikers Island the falling population is the main fact to watch. (Related: How to Start a Side Business While Working Full-Time...)

Newly introduced York City officials treat the number of people on Rikers Island as the obstacle to closing it, not as capacity to manage. The Associated Press that Mayor Zohran Mamdani's plan aims to bring the population down to a little over 4,000. Even the lower current count sits well above that goal. The city has to reach that smaller population before it can replace the island with jails in the boroughs. Every person kept out of Rikers Island brings the closure closer, and the size of the jail measures how far the city still has to go.

Industry observers note that worldAtlas estimates that Maricopa County's jail system near Phoenix, which spans several facilities, holds 9,265 people. That estimate makes it one of the largest jail populations in the country. The deadliest problem in the system is the drugs that reach the people inside. Drug-related causes killed more people in Maricopa County's jails in 2025 than every other cause combined, accounting for 20 of the 39 deaths in custody there that year, according to AZFamily.

Recent findings suggest that people held in Maricopa County's jails die far more often than people held in other major urban jail systems. "In the case of Chicago and Newly introduced York City, they’re a quarter the rate of deaths that we have here in Phoenix in the Maricopa County system," criminologist Andy Hall AZFamily. Hall is comparing rates, not totals. Maricopa County's large population cannot account for the gap, and the size of the system does not explain those deaths.

Industry observers note that metro West Detention Center in Miami is Miami-Dade County's largest jail, and WorldAtlas reports that it averages about 7,050 inmates, more than Dallas County holds in its jail. A county count puts that figure in doubt. The county's corrections department put the jail's population at 2,332 as of January 2023, according to NBC 6 South Florida. On that count, Metro West would fall well short of Dallas. One figure is an average, and the other is the latest count the department had given in early 2023, so neither settles the jail's size. Even the smaller count was the highest of any Miami-Dade jail at the time.

The jail's size is in dispute, but the rise in force inside it is not. Officers at Metro West used force 380 times in 2022, up from 198 in 2019, according to NBC 6 South Florida. Miami-Dade Public Defender Carlos Martinez, whose office defends people held in the county's jails, traced the trouble inside them to how the county pays and staffs its guards. "We have a serious toxic mix because they are not paying enough to the guards, and therefore, people are not even applying to become guards, and therefore you have a lot of officers spending overtime," Martinez remarked in 2023. If he is right, the county could ease part of the pressure inside Metro West by changing what it pays officers.

Recent findings suggest that cook County, Ill., cut the size of its jail through deliberate policy choices. Even after that cut, the county holds about 6,294 people in Cook County Jail in Chicago, according to WorldAtlas, enough to rank eighth among the country's largest jails. In the early 2010s, the county routinely held more than 10,000 people there, so the jail today holds far fewer people than it once did. Releasing more people before trial was one of the policy changes behind the decline. Most people in the jail have not yet had their day in court, and that made pretrial release a policy that could reach a large share of its population.

According to recent reports, releasing people before trial did not end the jail's role as a place that holds people with mental illness. When the jail's daily count stood at 9,000, about a third of those inside were living with a mental health condition, according to the . The county has moved many people out before trial, and the ones still held are more and more likely to need serious care. Those people remain in the custody of the Cook County Sheriff's Office, which runs the jail.

Sources indicate that the Shelby County Sheriff's Office runs the downtown jail in Memphis, Tenn., and WorldAtlas estimates that it holds 5,765 people. That estimate ranks the jail 10th among the largest in the U.S. Sheriff Anthony Buckner gives a much smaller number. FOX13 Memphis reports that he put the population at more than 2,700 during a Sept. 1, 2026, tour. Neither count settles how many people the county holds there today, and the correct one determines whether Shelby County runs one of the country's very largest jails or a much more modest one. Its place on the list is best read as provisional.

In a recent development, on either count, the jail has little or no room to spare. It was built for about 2,800 people, according to . The sheriff's count comes close to that limit, and the WorldAtlas estimate goes far beyond it. When nearly every bed is in use, staff have few places to move people if part of the building fails. This building has been in service for more than 40 years, and in June its air conditioning broke down in a major outage.

The officials who run America's massive jails and the lawyers who fight them tend to agree that these places have too many people in them. A jail is where people wait for a court date, for a transfer to prison or for release, and in the largest jails thousands of people are waiting on any given day. The complaint about that crowding comes from both coasts and from both sides of the courtroom. Fresh York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani calls the scale of imprisonment a problem for the whole country, according to The Associated Press. In Houston, defense attorney Andino Reynal claims his county locks up far more people than it needs to, according to KPRC Click2Houston. When an elected official in one city and a defense lawyer in another make the same complaint, a big jail's headcount is worth reading as a possible sign of strain rather than only as a measure of what a county can handle.

The number of people in a major jail depends both on how many people come in and on how promptly courts and clerks' offices move them back out. What speeds or slows each of those steps differs from county to county, so two jails of similar size can be full for very different reasons. Because those forces change, a county can see its jail population rise or fall by thousands of people within a few years, and officials in several counties now report counts far from earlier estimates. When a jail holds more people than it was built or staffed for, the people held inside and the county taxpayers who fund it both bear the cost.

Sources indicate that worldAtlas set out to rank the largest jails in the U.S. by the number of people held in them, a group that includes Rikers Island in New York, Harris County's jail in Houston, Maricopa County's jails in Phoenix, and Shelby County's jail in Memphis, Tenn. Each figure reflects one moment in a situation that keeps changing, in counties working to shrink their jails and in counties struggling to find room.

The county wants to shrink the system enough to close Men's Central Jail. The Los Angeles Daily News reports that the closure would require a population far below today's, which means moving thousands of people out rather than a few hundred. The sheriff runs the buildings, but judges decide who leaves them. "Neither the Court nor the County can predetermine or guarantee that particular individuals will be diverted, released, or otherwise handled in a manner that advances specific population reduction targets," noted David Slayton, executive officer of the Los Angeles County Superior Court.

It has been revealed that new York City has bound itself to a deadline it cannot meet. The city is legally required to close Rikers Island and replace it by 2027, The Associated Press reports, but the mayor's initiative sets the closure for 2032, the year the last replacement jail, in Manhattan, is expected to open. The city cannot empty the island until its final replacement exists, and it cannot speed up construction by decision alone. Meanwhile, conditions inside have brought in a federal court. The New York City Department of Correction runs Rikers Island, but a federal judge has appointed an outside manager there. The court's step highlights that the trouble lies in how the jail is run, not only in how many people it holds.

Roughly 10,000 people spend each day in the Houston jail run by the Harris County Sheriff's Office, a total that places it third in the nation, according to WorldAtlas. That count still leaves out some of the people the county detains. Harris County holds more people than its own jail can take, and it sends the rest to other places. More than 900 Harris County inmates are held in private facilities, KPRC Click2Houston reports, and some of them are housed as far away as Louisiana. The jail's own headcount therefore understates how many people Harris County detains.

Recent findings suggest that the crowding began in the courts, not in the jail. The sheriff's office, which runs the jail, traces the overcrowding to cases that piled up in the county courts after Hurricane Harvey and then the COVID pandemic, according to a Harris County Sheriff's Office statement reported by KPRC Click2Houston. If the sheriff's office is right, the jail is likely to stay crowded until judges work through those delayed cases.

Recent findings suggest that sending people away costs the county more than keeping them in its own jail. Holding someone in an outside private facility costs an average of $91 a day, compared with $53 in the county jail, according to KPRC Click2Houston. The county pays for that overflow from its main budget, and a delay in the courtroom has become a standing expense for the county.

Recent findings suggest that the county has tied itself to outside beds for years, not months, under a contract that runs until 2028, according to KPRC Click2Houston. At least one of the officials who oversee the deal is uneasy about it. "This is one of those things that keeps me up at night," Harris County Commissioner Lesley Briones said of the contracts.

Recent findings suggest that jail officials have tried to stop drugs at the door, but the deaths have continued. They added body scanners and fresh drug-detection dogs, according to AZFamily, and four people still died in two weeks at Lower Buckeye Jail, between late June and the July 4 weekend. In Hall's view, the jail's own staff are one of the main ways drugs get inside. If he is right, screening the people who come through the entrance may not be enough to keep drugs out.

Curran-Fromhold Correctional Facility, Philadelphia's main intake jail, holds an estimated 8,811 inmates, enough to place it among the half-dozen largest jails in the country, according to WorldAtlas. As the main intake jail, it is where Philadelphia receives people into its jail system. Most readers would expect a population that size at a sprawling county network, yet this estimate belongs to a single city facility. On paper, a figure that large would make one Philadelphia building as busy as some entire county systems.

The size of the building argues against reading that estimate literally. Curran-Fromhold has room to house about 2,000 people, and the population figure is only an approximation. Nothing on record settles which of those numbers describes the jail on a given day. There is no evidence that thousands of extra people are crowded into its beds, and none that the larger figure counts people held anywhere else. The jail earns its place among the nation's largest on a rough estimate.

It has been revealed that no one at Metro West lost a job as the use of force rose. No staff member there was fired for using force in the three years through early 2023, according to NBC 6 South Florida. The higher count does not show that officers acted wrongly in any single case. It does show that the jail's discipline record stayed the same while officers used force far more often.

According to recent reports, dallas County, Texas, holds its place among the country's biggest jails by a very slim margin, keeping about 6,385 people in its jail, slightly more than Cook County does, according to WorldAtlas. With so little room between the two, neither position should be read as settled. The Dallas count also captures a population that keeps shifting. The county recently kept its jail below 6,775 people a day for a month, which was its longest stretch under that level in more than a year, according to The Dallas Morning News. Dallas's rank reflects the moment the jail was counted more than any fixed size.

Some of the people in Dallas County's cells are waiting on documents, not on a judge. When a case ends, the jail needs paperwork from the clerks before it can release or transfer the person. Those papers reached the jail 20 days after a case ended, on average, in 2025, according to The Dallas Morning News. Part of the jail's headcount therefore depends on how fast the county handles its own paperwork.

The delay also costs taxpayers, and the county is trying to stop it where the paperwork starts. The Commissioners Court has approved at least $450,000 in payouts since 2024 to resolve civil rights claims by people kept in jail beyond their release dates, according to The Dallas Morning News. The county's answer is to get judgments right the first time. "If we don't get the judgment done correctly at first it doesn't matter how efficient we are on the backend because it's going to be kicked back," Steven Autry, Dallas County director of judicial and corrections operations, told The Dallas Morning News.

The change depends on the people behind it as well as on the process. The Dallas Morning News reports that District Attorney John Creuzot, whose office helped push for the new approach, lost his race for another term and will step down in January.

According to recent reports, in Orange County, Calif., one agency answers for everyone the county locks up. The Orange County Sheriff's Department runs the jail as a network of buildings rather than a single jailhouse. Three of its sites are in Santa Ana: the Intake Release Center and separate central jails for men and for women, while a fourth, the Theo Lacy Facility, is in the city of Orange. On a typical day, the department holds around 6,000 people across those sites, the ninth-largest jail population in the U.S., according to WorldAtlas. That total is better understood as people than as a rank. Each of those people depends on the sheriff's department for medical care and physical safety, and the department's responsibility grows with every person booked in.

In Orange County, the cost of holding that many people demonstrates up in what happens to them inside. The concerns raised about the system center on deaths in custody, the medical care detainees receive, and the force used against them. Keeping people alive, treating them when they are sick and not harming them are the most basic duties the sheriff's department owes everyone it holds. Those three concerns go to the core of that duty, and they are a truer test of a system this large than its place among the country's biggest jails.

Putting off repairs on an old, full jail doesn't save the county money. Instead, the bill grows when something breaks. "We know that if maintenance goes untouched, then it becomes crisis management and it becomes crisis maintenance. And we know that when we're in those states, it costs way more money than to make repairs when you're in a crisis state," Buckner told FOX13 Memphis. County taxpayers pay that higher bill, and the people held inside live with each breakdown until the repairs are made.

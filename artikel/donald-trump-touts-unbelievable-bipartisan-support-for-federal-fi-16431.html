Donald Trump touted the bipartisan support for a a film & TV incentive and vowed that it was “going to happen” as he spoke to reporters on Friday, in an effort to show that bipartisan legislation is possible with Democrats about to take control of both chambers of Congress.

It has been revealed that at the press conference, Trump noted that the legislation, which would establish a 20% production incentive, has the support of Sen. Adam Schiff (D-CA), one of his longtime foes. (Related: Leading House Democrat targets Trump’s inner circle for...)

In a recent development, “It’s running it’s way through Congress and it has unbelievable support,” Trump told reporters. Trump said that they were thinking of also adding live theatrical productions to the incentive. Watch on Deadline.

Trump noted, “I’m working on it because I want to see the country be great. We don’t make movies in this country anymore, you know. They go to Canada, they go to UK, and they go to Australia. You have to fly 23 hours to Australia. And I know the producers. And by the way, they’re mostly Democrat, but these are minor details. … We have television shows that are made in the UK because they have a substantial package, a very good package, incentive package.”.

Trump added, “It’s not our biggest industry, but it’s a very key industry. Hollywood would open again.” He also noted the benefits to other states, which have their own incentives. “Georgia, which was doing well, but it’s all really the federal government. … New Jersey did a good job. A couple of them have done good, but it doesn’t matter if they don’t have the federal government.”.

“I can tell you right now, it’s going to happen,” Trump said.

It has been revealed that the legislation, the Motion Picture, Television, and Entertainment Revitalization Act, was introduced in the Senate and House in September by members of both parties. Schiff, for instance, introduced the bill along with Sen. Tim Scott (R-SC).

Industry representatives from trade groups and unions (below) spent the past week lobbying on Capitol Hill, but it’s unclear whether the legislation can pass during the lame duck session, which has been a goal of advocates, who warn of the production flight to other countries.

While there has been hope that the legislation could be attached to a year-end funding bill or a tax package, there are still doubts as to whether that will happen and what Republicans will prioritize in their final weeks in leadership. As he met on Friday with Jon Voight, one of the advocates for the incentive, Trump posted on social media about the need to pass the incentive package, but his message came amid a dramatic ground stop of all flights across the country.

The press conference — which lasted for about 90 minutes — was heavy in Trump trumpeting his recent trip to Asia, which included a summit with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin. But it also turned out to be a tryout of sorts for his potential successor, as Vice President JD Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio stood by his side, occasionally swaying back and forth as Trump went on. They each made brief remarks.

Trump’s comment on the incentives legislation came in response to a question of how he will work with Congress’ Democratic leadership.

It has been revealed that “There are those that say we might get more done because we had some Republicans that were impossible to deal with,” Trump mentioned, citing Rep. Thomas Massie (R-KY), who was defeated by a Trump-endorsed candidate in the primary.

It has been revealed that during the press conference, Trump scolded one reporter as she continued to shout a question about immigration, but he otherwise got into no major tiff with those gathered. “Thank you for your fairness and thank you for your questions,” Trump said as he ended the session.

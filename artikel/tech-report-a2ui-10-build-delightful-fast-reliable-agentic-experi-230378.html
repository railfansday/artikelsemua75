Tech Report: A2UI 1.0: Build delightful, fast, reliable, agentic experiences

Industry observers note that user interactions with agents today are where computers were in the terminal era: capable, and answering almost everything in text. Users can ask an agent for anything by typing or talking, and what comes back is usually a wall of text, a poor way to compare three products or edit and approve a detailed initiative.

Today, we're releasing A2UI 1.0, the first stable version of our open protocol for generative UI. It lets AI agents answer with interactive screens, such as a form to confirm or a chart. Developers can use it now with open-source renderer SDKs for the web, Android, and iOS and agent SDKs for Python, TypeScript, and Dart. Companies such as Atlassian, Alation, Bloomberg, and ByteDance are already building with it. (Related: Show, Don't Certify: How to Prepare for Your First Data...)

Industry observers note that with A2UI, the agent describes what to show, and every app draws the UI with its own native components. The application receives A2UI messages and each app renders the content securely and at native speed. Owners get the same low latency, high trust, and complete native fidelity whether they reach a firm's agent in its own app or in a third-party AI assistant that hosts MCP apps, such as Gemini or Claude. (Related: How Much Latency Does a Self-Hosted LLM Gateway Add? La...)

You wake up sick. Getting through the morning takes six apps: book a video visit, move your meetings, tell your team, turn on an out-of-office reply, ask your partner to do school pickup and order soup. Ask a text-only agent instead, and you get a numbered wall of text, then round after round of “which ones?”. A2UI won't handle your sick day, but it gives developers the framework to build an agent that can. That agent could answer with one view of all six apps. Changing your proposal is a couple of taps instead of another round of messages, and nothing is sent until you confirm.

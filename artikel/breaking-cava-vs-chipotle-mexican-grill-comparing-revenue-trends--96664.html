Revenue trends between CAVA and Chipotle demonstrate a textbook contrast between an early-stage hyper-growth chain and a mature, massive-scale compounder. While CAVA expanded its trailing 12-month revenue more than three times faster than its restaurant rival, Chipotle generated over nine times CAVA's quarterly sales volume with greater seasonal stability.

Industry observers note that CAVA's brisk top-line expansion is heavily fueled by aggressive store count growth. It reached 500 locations in the third quarter of 2026 on its path toward 1,000 stores by 2032. Chipotle's sales expansion also relies on new store openings, but with over 4,000 locations, it also relies on revenue growth in existing stores.

Sources indicate that due to CAVA's rapidly expanding revenue, its share price valuation has soared. Its forward price-to-earnings ratio exceeds 71 while Chipotle is at a far more reasonable 27. As a result, CAVA's share price heavily depends on sales growth. If that slows, its stock will fall.

Chipotle's scale allows it to be more resilient. Even when consumer spending tightens and comparable sales flatten, its annual store expansion and high digital mix, which was 38.8% of Q3 food sales, sustain high-single-digit top-line growth and substantial cash flow.

Dion Dublin is one of English football's most well-respected former professionals, with the 57-year-old's playing and post-playing career certainly generating plenty of interest.

The Leicester native plied his trade for some of the country's most historic clubs at various levels, including a spell in the Championship with Norwich City towards the very end of his career.

Such is his current presence, it is fair to say that those of various generations will know Dublin either for his goalscoring exploits in the 1990s and 2000s, or either as a television personality, having followed in the footsteps of various other high-profile names within the game,.

However, it is fair to say that not everyone will be familiar with the former striker's attempts to forge a career in the music industry, which made headlines just over a decade ago. (Related: Industry News: Hyperparameter Search with Transformers ...)

Dublin was well-travelled throughout his career, with a breakthrough spell at Cambridge United eventually paving the way for his success as a Premier League-level striker.

The Yellows climbed two divisions during the forward's time at the club between 1990 and 1992, during which he netted the first play-off final-winning strike at Wembley against Chesterfield, before being sold to Manchester United for £1m.

Dublin's Old Trafford spell has been well-documented due to a serious leg injury which pushed him below the likes of Eric Cantona in Sir Alex Ferguson's pecking order, and after two years, the striker joined Coventry City for £2m.

His goalscoring touch was then reignited at Highfield Road, netting 72 times in 162 appearances, including an 18-goal campaign in 1997/98 in which he ended as the Premier League's joint-highest scorer alongside Chris Sutton and Michael Owen.

Dublin then spent just under six years at Aston Villa under John Gregory, Graham Taylor and David O'Leary, scoring 59 times in 192 games, as well as infamously being sent off in a Second City derby defeat to Birmingham City in 2003 for headbutting Robbie Savage.

During the latter stages of his Villa spell, Dublin went on to feature in what is now the Championship on loan with Millwall in 2001/02, as the Lions made the play-offs but were defeated in agonising fashion by Birmingham at the semi-final stage.

After leaving B6 in 2004, the experienced figure moved to his hometown club, Leicester City, for two seasons, scoring six times in 42 appearances in the 2004/05 season, before featuring just 21 times the following season.

A spell with Celtic sandwiched his time at the King Power Stadium and a return to academy side, Norwich City, for two seasons where Dublin wound down his playing days between 2006 and 2008, scoring 16 times in 79 appearances, despite occasionally being drafted in to feature as a centre-back.

Dublin then became a regular fixture on television screens post-retirement, but as well as this, rediscovered his love for music, which led to an intriguing creation.

In 2013, it was revealed that the former striker had invented the 'dube' - described as a "cube-based drum played with the hands.". (Related: Tech Report: Transformer-based Encoder-Decoder Models...)

This led to concerts with the People's Orchestra, which revealed just how much this side of his life helped Dublin through the aforementioned difficult six-month injury setback with the Red Devils.

"Not having had the opportunity to play with a full orchestra before I am excited to be taking the Dube, the percussion drum I invented seven years ago, with me to perform on the day," he mentioned.

Whilst Dublin's music invention is certainly lesser-known, what isn't is that the four-time England international has been a regular daytime television presenter on BBC One's 'Homes Under the Hammer'.

It was, rather unsurprisingly, baffling to many of the show's audience when the decision was made in 2015 for Dublin to join Martin Roberts and Lucy Alexander on the show, as well as his punditry roles on BBC Sport.

During his 11 years on the show, Dublin has become infamous for his line of "stairs leading up to the bedroom," but revealed that this sector is another of his great passions.

"I have a passion for property and look forward to meeting people on the show who share that passion," he mentioned when landing this particular long-standing gig.

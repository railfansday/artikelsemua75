Exclusive: While PM pushes for EU reset, Tory leader is moving away from ‘divisive’ glitch that defined party for years.

UK-EU summit live: latest news updates.

Kemi Badenoch has been advised by her team to limit the amount of time she spends discussing Brexit and Europe, the Guardian has been told, moving the Conservatives away from an issue that defined the party for many years.

While the Tory leader still gives views on Brexit – in an opinion piece in the Daily Mail on Thursday she condemned Andy Burnham’s ongoing reset with the EU as a “backwards” move – sources say it is now seen as a second-order subject.

Instead, Badenoch intends to focus on areas the party believes are more fertile electoral ground, in particular the economy, the cost of social security and defence.

One source remarked Badenoch had been advised to shift the narrative away from Brexit by Stephen Gilbert, a former Conservative official who is now a peer and who joined the Tory leader’s team as a senior political adviser a year ago.

Although another party source noted Gilbert’s advice was “more nuanced” than this, Badenoch’s recent run of speeches and interventions appears to demonstrate the change.

In eight sets of prime minister’s questions since Burnham entered No 10 over the summer, Badenoch and her deputy, Alex Burghart, who took one session when Burnham was away, have focused on Europe just once, on 28 October, when she challenged the PM on whether or not he wanted a new Brexit referendum. (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

Every other outing has focused on various aspects of the welfare state, the cost of living, or defence, also including subjects such as the budget, debt and whether or not the UK should drill for more North Sea oil and gas.

“The way I see it, this is less of a conscious attempt to sideline Europe than it is a way to talk more about the subjects we think are our strong points, like the economy and defence,” one senior Conservative noted.

According to recent reports, one Tory shadow minister said the wider party was firmly behind the idea: “Europe does still come up from time to time, but as a former remainer even I think we should move on. I’m not sure what is likely to change by us talking about it all the time.”.

Another shadow minister said: “We don’t want to be talking about Brexit for multiple reasons – including the simple one that it was so awfully divisive.”. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

Badenoch does still at times defend Brexit to selected audiences, for example in Thursday’s Daily Mail piece, where she disputed Burnham’s charge that it had hampered the UK’s economic growth, saying the prime minister “wants to reopen arguments from 2016 because he has no answers for the world as it is”.

At other times her language on links with the EU has been more nuanced than the period when the Conservatives liked to disparage the continent as economically moribund and less relevant than more distant economies.

Asked about Burnham’s attempt to renegotiate the post-Brexit trade agreement during a press conference on defence earlier this month, Badenoch said: “They say they want to end red tape on food checks – that’s a good thing”, while then predicting this would not actually happen.

Badenoch also said she accepted that the deal with the EU could and should be updated: “The free trade agreement that we have with the EU, like many others, is one that needs constant negotiating. So I want to see Labour negotiating and improving on deals with all countries. That’s what you do when you have your own trade policy.”.

A Conservative spokesperson noted: “As Kemi has said, the last thing the country needs is to be dragged back into a debate on Brexit. That absolutely does not mean Kemi and the Conservatives won’t discuss the EU, but we will be very clear that Andy Burnham is only trying to reopen old wounds on Brexit because he doesn’t have anything to say about the challenges facing the country.

Sources indicate that “Bringing down the welfare bill, funding defence, cutting taxes to promote growth can all be done outside the EU. Burnham’s reset is one big distraction and we will never shy away from saying so.”.

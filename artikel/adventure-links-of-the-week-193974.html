The Electric Arms Race Has Gone Too Far: Tim Shuff questions the growing use of electric motors in outdoor adventure. He argues that the speed and convenience they create undermine traditionally self-propelled activities like paddling and cycling. Today, you can even buy battery-powered SUPs.

While recognizing that electric assistance can make outdoor sports more accessible, he thinks that its wider use will change wilderness areas, reshape trails, and create pressure to build increasingly powerful equipment.

It has been revealed that he Felt Stuck in New York. So He Started Walking to California: Feeling trapped by unemployment and a lack of opportunities in New York, 29-year-old Chantz Rivera began walking across America. He set off with a tent, sleeping bag, and some supplies in a shopping cart. Over 149 days, he walked to California, pushing his cart 32 to 40km a day along highways and through small towns, documenting his progress for his growing number of online followers.

This Mountain Race Makes Runners Carry 60kg of Beer and Supplies Uphill: Slovakia’s annual Sherpa Rallye is one of the world’s unique mountain races. Competitors have to haul heavy loads of supplies up a 5.7km rocky trail to a remote hut in the High Tatras, nearly 2,000m above sea level.

Recent findings suggest that men carry 60kg, including a 30-liter keg of beer, while women carry 20kg, with the loads secured to wooden frames. The race was started in 1985 by mountain porter Viktor Beranek. The event brings together the region’s nosiči (traditional mountain porters) and also helps stock the remote hut ahead of winter.

In a recent development, A Hiker Slid Hundreds of Meters Down Mount Timpanogos: A female hiker was seriously injured after slipping off the trail near the summit of Mount Timpanogos in Utah. She slid several hundred feet down the steep mountainside before landing on a narrow cliff ledge. Newly released helicopter footage highlights her being hoisted to safety. She was taken to hospital and is reportedly recovering.

We Need to Move Past the ‘Shut Up and Die Like a Man’ Mentality in the Mountains: Climber and paraglider Will Gadd reflects on the psychological impact of mountain accidents. Climbing culture often glorifies the dangers people face in the mountains while discouraging them from admitting they are struggling.

According to recent reports, gadd discusses the deaths of two close friends and challenges the underlying expectation that climbers should simply suck it up and carry on. After decades in the mountains, he has seen that the consequences of an accident extend far beyond the immediate incident, affecting survivors and their families and friends for years.

Industry observers note that two Hikers Dead in Bear Attack in British Columbia: Two hikers, identified as 48-year-old Jennifer Balcarras and her 20-year-old daughter Selah Reed, have died in a suspected grizzly attack near the Snooka West Trail in Bella Coola, British Columbia. Their bodies were found during a search after the pair were reported missing by their family.

According to recent reports, this comes just a year after a separate grizzly attack in the same area severely injured four people, including three children. The trail remains closed as authorities continue their investigation.

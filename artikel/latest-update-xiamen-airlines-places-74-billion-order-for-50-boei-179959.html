Latest Update: Xiamen Airlines Places $7.4 Billion Order For 50 Boeing 737 MAX 8s Ahead Of Fresh Hub Move

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, xiamen Airlines has placed an order for 50 brand-newly introduced Boeing 737 MAX 8 narrowbody twinjets. The deal was confirmed in a November 24 stock exchange filing by China Southern Airlines, its parent company. The aircraft, which will be delivered from 2031 to 2034, have a total list price of $7.4 billion, although, as always, the airline will receive a bulk discount. They will support its forthcoming move to a new hub.

According to recent reports, this deal is the latest in a string of developments regarding Boeing's growth in China, which have defined the country's aviation landscape in 2026. Earlier this year, following a summit attended by both US President Donald Trump and Chinese President Xi Jinping, China committed to buying 200 Boeing aircraft. It is unclear whether this deal is part of the total, but, either way, it marks a strong step forward.

The deal still requires final government and shareholder approval, so it hasn't quite been finalized yet. In any case, with the deliveries of these 50 upcoming narrowbody twinjet aircraft set to take place in batches between 2031 and 2034, there is plenty of time to get the finer details ironed out. China Southern cited "the relocation of [Xiamen Airlines'] operations to Xiamen Xiang’an International Airport" as a key factor. (Related: Hawaiian Interisland Awards — Hop Between Islands...)

Sources indicate that this airport is set to open next month, with the first phase able to handle 45 million passengers a year. China Southern added that the deal "supports the development of the hub network in Fujian, improves [Xiamen Airlines'] route network layout, and optimizes its fleet structure," and "complements the group’s recent purchase of Airbus aircraft." Commenting on Xiamen Airlines' fleet mix, China Southern remarked that:.

"Sourcing aircraft from different manufacturers will help diversify the group’s aircraft portfolio and enhance operational flexibility. The purchase will help strengthen the core competitiveness and sustainable development capabilities of the organization and Xiamen Airlines.".

It has been revealed that while relations between China and the US have been somewhat rocky over the years, Xiamen Airlines has long been a fan of Boeing's products. Until the arrival of its first Airbus jets in 2023, the carrier spent nearly 40 years as an all-Boeing airline from its founding in 1984. Its confidence in the manufacturer has been underlined by several other major deals, such as its purchase of ten 737-800s in 2016. (Related: How to Start a Side Business While Working Full-Time...)

It has been revealed that boeing aircraft continue to dominate Xiamen Airlines' fleet today. According to data from ch-aviation, the carrier at the moment has just 25 Airbus jets at its disposal (eight A320neos and 17 A321neos), compared to 151 from Boeing. This figure consists of four 737-700s, 109 737-800s (including one freighter), 28 existing 737 MAX 8s, four 787-8s, and six 787-9s. It also has 12 more A320neos and 56 A321neos on order.

Given the 737-800's dominance at Xiamen Airlines, a growing portfolio of upcoming MAX 8 jets will ensure that this part of its operations has a natural successor. The type suits the carrier's domestic and regional operations, particularly when it comes to high-altitude flying out of certain Chinese airports. According to aeroLOPA, its existing MAX 8s have either 184 economy or eight business and 166 economy seats.

Things have been looking up for Boeing in China in 2026, not least on account of the 200-jet pledge that the country made following the Trump-Xi summit back in May. More recently, Reuters notes that US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer mentioned in September there were "about 140" jetliner orders "in a good state", with 10 more being finalized. Trump said on Monday that he expects China to buy 250 more.

Maleah Joi Moon didn’t audition her way into Hillman College. Debbie Allen walked backstage at “Hell’s Kitchen,” held the Tony winner’s face in her hands and decided she had found the daughter of Whitley Gilbert and Dwayne Wayne.

Recent findings suggest that “She’s never been apologetic about letting me know my light,” Moon says of Allen, the choreographer, director and producer who shaped the original TV series “A Different World” and now shepherds Netflix’s sequel comedy, which is eligible in this season’s Golden Globes race. “She’s a tree to me, and I know that sounds kind of woo-woo, but it means she’s so sturdy.” Related Stories 'Chasing Summer' Director Josephine Decker Says That ‘Filmmaking Is Lonelier Than Ever,’ So It’s Time to Build a Community Tony Award Winner Lois Smith Joins Colin West’s ‘Outside of Cleveland’: ‘She’s an Icon’ (EXCLUSIVE).

The call came the weekend before Moon left the Alicia Keys musical. There was no Zoom or pleasantries. “She gets straight to the point,” Moon says.

It has been revealed that moon plays Deborah, the daughter of the sitcom’s beloved couple; the casting carries a full-circle weight for her own family. Her mother immigrated from Belize, and her father grew up in the Bronx. Neither came from a world where kids went off to college, which is precisely why the original series hit so hard. “You see these young, Black, stylish, joyful, well-educated, pursuing academia young people and she had that same feeling that everybody else had in the world when they saw the 1980s version,” states Moon. (Related: Breaking: Democrat Hakeem Jeffries nominated to be firs...)

It has been revealed that building Deborah started in the writers’ room, where showrunner Felicia Pride invited Moon to talk through her own life story. The first relationship she built was the one with Whitley. Rewatching Jasmine Guy’s performance as an actor, not just as a fan, Moon found more than comic timing. “I saw somebody who was strong-willed, somebody who was outspoken, ambitious and not afraid to be who she is, which is huge for a Black woman,” she states. “I know so much of who I am is my mom living inside of me.”.

In a recent development, guy and Kadeem Hardison, back as Whitley and Dwayne, were generous with on-set wisdom. Hardison taught her to count her steps to her mark; Guy called and talked for two hours at a stretch. Both preach the same gospel: take care of yourself. “When outside influences are coming in to say who they think you are,” Moon states, “[Guy] inspires me to remember who the fuck I am.”.

Moon, who is queer, is equally passionate about Amir, played by Jordan Herron Hall, the franchise’s first openly queer character. She frames him as a person who happens to be queer rather than a queer character. “Black love in all forms, in the sexuality sense, in the gender sense, in the community sense, in the familial sense, in the self-sense, is Black radicalism,” she claims. “That is our game changer.”.

Sources indicate that with a Tony and a Grammy already on the shelf, Moon is now focusing on music, her first love before acting or dance. She’s been in the studio workshopping what her sound might be, and she’s signed on to projects she can’t yet name. A “Hell’s Kitchen” film? She’d audition for Keys all over again. A Broadway return? “Hadestown,” where her friend Amber Iman is at the moment starring, is on her wish list.

According to recent reports, one piece of the Hillman inheritance is harder to celebrate. The original “A Different World” was born as a spinoff of “The Cosby Show,” and Moon doesn’t pretend otherwise. “We know that there is a complicated history. We’re not going to beat around the bush,” she says.

The two reveals “have their own fortitude, like they have their own legacies, both of them,” Moon says. “As much as they are interlinked, it’s so wonderful that they both stand on their own.”.

Her prayer is that the Netflix version earns the same footing. “No matter what that conversation looks like, the point is that we are telling a story of love that was born in its genesis from ‘The Cosby Show’ and has continued through this generational re-embarking of another story and another story and another story,” she notes. “That is how love is passed down. Period.”.

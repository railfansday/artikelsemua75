Industry observers note that comedian and podcaster Connor Wood has become a bit of a fan favorite on this season of Dancing With the Stars. “If we’re not in practice, I feel like I’m missing practice,” the 31-year-old tells The Hollywood Reporter on a Zoom call. It’s a rare rainy day in Los Angeles, and the season 35 contestant is finding a second to rest amidst crazy rehearsal schedules.

It has been revealed that he and partner Rylee Arnold have taken each challenge in stride. The pair have found themselves amongst the five couples advancing to the show’s finale, airing Tuesday night. Wood’s viral social media moments have certainly helped endear viewers, however, his steadily improving dancing skills don’t hurt. In a recent development, “I have been really intentional about my life and making sure I don’t ever work really hard,” he says, cheekily. “This has changed that completely, flipped it on its head.”.

In a recent development, heading into the final episode, he and the dancers are working with six hours a day of rehearsal to get themselves in prime condition. “Every minute of that is necessary for the show,” he notes.

Below, Wood speaks with THR about heading into the DWTS finale, the reaction to his viral Jennifer Lawrence moment and dealing with the online hate.

How do you feel going into the finals?

I feel really good. Me and Rylee, we went into the semifinals, and that was the hard part. It was two brand fresh dances, two long dances, two very fast hard dances, and this one is fun. We’re going out there, we’re putting on a show. It really is for us, which is fun. It’s for the fans, [but] it’s [also] for us. It’s something that hasn’t been seen before. We’re doing a really cool dichotomy of dances too.

What days are the longest for you?

Industry observers note that the day after the show, we film basically what you see before we dance. We do all of the face-to-face interviews. We do the one-on-one interview, and then we do the group session interview. This is obviously a bigger one. There’s three dances, so they have three videos that they had to make. There’s one for each dance, and then they’ll film rehearsal and stuff for the filler. But I also have the podcast that I do, so that was 9 a.m. to 12 p.m., and then we filmed from 12 p.m to 10 p.m. (Related: Industry News: Showcase Your Projects in Spaces using G...)

It was long, but it’s all needed. That’s the thing. Sometimes you go onto a set or something, and it’s like we’re doing a take for the 15th time, and I’m like, “I promise you I’m getting worse. Every time we do another one, I promise you’re getting a worse take from me.” This is not the case. This one you do get better as you put more hours in, which is an annoying, frustrating thing that that is true. The harder you work, the better you get.

What were you expecting for yourself coming in versus where you are now?

Sources indicate that I wasn’t expecting anything for myself, honestly. I’ve only ever danced at weddings. I have no pride when it comes to sticking with something. I’m happy to throw in the towel. You’ll quit while you’re ahead. I’ll quit while I’m behind. I don’t think there’s any shame in that. I am competitive though, which is weird. We had to stop playing Monopoly in my family home because I took it too seriously. But that wasn’t part of this — I really can’t put my finger on why this clicked for me, but I leaned in a lot. I’ve done some really TikTok cringe things that I wouldn’t normally have done. It’s been so well received, it’s a vulnerability thing, I guess, that’s been nice to be a part of.

How do you get through these red carpets to make it an actually enjoyable experience?

It has been revealed that that’s my bread and butter. Hamming it up on the carpet with interviews, that is my Dancing With The Stars. A funny thing that I’ve realized is that obviously I’m not the best dancer on the show or even freaking close. I’m aware of that. I feel like people don’t realize that. I don’t think I’m Zeus on the dance floor. I’m very aware of what I’m capable of, but people also aren’t seeing that dance is a part of the whole of this show, and doing press and meeting fans and doing X, Y, Z, it all makes up that whole. Anytime we spend these 12, 15 hour days creating packages and personifying these people that are on TV, it helps the case. If they see that you’re working really hard, if they see that you’re having fun in the press lines. If you’re being vulnerable, and you’re oversharing, people really like that, they relate to it. That’s what that is. I look forward to doing interviews because that’s where I feel like I hit my stride on the dance floor is after the dance, and I can talk about how hard it was to learn in Argentine Tango.

Do you feel more confident in dancing now?

Yes, and I that’s a natural progression with this whole thing. I’m learning the dances faster because I’ve been learning dance for three months. There’s a baseline or a foundation that I’m pulling from. When Rylee gives me “Start here” and “do this,” I know what she’s talking about. I realized recently I’m personifying each dance. When a dance was really hard, I began to resent the dance on day two of rehearsal because I’m like, “We are not getting along, me and the samba, we’re not getting along. I don’t get the samba. It doesn’t make sense to me.” Getting past those couple weeks — I hit my stride in probably week six in this competition because I was not resenting the dance. It became easier for me to learn the dances. We saw my dancing. It’s not the best, but I am having a lot more fun because I’m enjoying the process way more.

It has been revealed that with the show and being online, do you feel a bit of an overload? I’m sure you’re not looking forward to hearing “8th Avenue” or “smoothie” noted to you ever again. (Laughs).

I like when everybody’s on board with something. What a harmless thing for everyone to be having fun with. That’s good, clean, fun. It’s easy. I know a lot of marketing coordinators probably had a really great weekly meeting when they turned in their social media reports and they had a video with a million views on their page for brand X, Y, Z, football team X, Y, Z. Everyone had so much fun. Everyone got to put a spin on that. That fired me up.

According to recent reports, the rest of the internet, totally different story right now. We’ve gotten to a point where I have become a bit of a villain for some people.

Why do you think they feel that way?

It has been revealed that I think that they think that I personally voted their favorite off, and it’s like — I’m aware of my level of dance skill. I have nothing to do with me staying here. That’s just been just the dark side of making it as far as we made it. My understanding is that the more that people go home, the more the fans of those people will go to someone else’s page and villainize those people. That’s sad, and it’s just a testament to how invested people are in this show. A cool part of this show is that the fans are so invested. Like I said, the 8th Avenue thing, it’s fun for people to be a part of things. Being on the other side of that is not as fun. It’s part of the game. I signed up for it. I’ve never experienced that.

It has to be tough given social media is a huge part of your job. You can’t disengage as much.

Yeah. As we’ve gone through the season… First of all, I’m like, “Well, not getting on Twitter the rest of the season. That’s a really interesting group of people.” You want to get home from the 15-hour day, and you want to lay on your couch, your Lola blanket swaddling you like a newborn. Turn off your brain, look at your phone. The first thing on your phone is, “Connor Wood’s still here? Who’s he paying at ABC?” OK, scroll to the next thing. But that is part of it. This is weird and sick in the head, [but] I am going to miss every part of this experience. If you have haters — you’ve been around long enough to have a hater — that means you’re doing something really, really well. I’ve been lucky enough. I will say, I’m not cut out for public scrutiny. This is not something for me. This will be my last reality show.

No Traitors in your future then?

I mean, [if] they come knocking — Of course, I’ll open the door. But at least for Traitors, they’re going to take my phone. No, but it’s great. I’ve been lucky enough to make it this far, and it’s just part of the competition. We got a couple days left.

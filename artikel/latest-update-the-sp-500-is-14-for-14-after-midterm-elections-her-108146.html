The S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) fell 2.7% in the week that ended Nov. 6, its worst week since the tariff sell-off in April 2025. That was the tail end of a four-week losing streak that has helped push the index more than 6% below its all-time high.

The biggest volatility came following the November 3rd midterm elections, and it has a lot of people wondering whether now is the time to get a bit more defensive heading into 2027. (Related: Build A Racetrack codes (November 2026)...)

But history suggests that this is the time to buy. In fact, it's one of the strongest track records out there.

It has been revealed that since 1970, there have been 14 midterm elections, defined as the halfway point of the 4-year presidential term. And in each one of those midterms, the S&P 500 has been higher both 6 months later and one year later all 14 times.

It has been revealed that the returns for stocks have been fantastic during these periods too. The median 6-month gain post-midterm was 12.6%, more than triple that of the 4% median return during non-midterm years. (Related: Latest Update: Taco Bell is bringing back the Steakhous...)

According to recent reports, over the full subsequent year, the outperformance is a little more muted. Midterm years have seen a median gain of about 11% compared to 10% in other years.

What's also impressive is that these post-midterm rallies occurred even during years when it looked like conditions wouldn't favor it. In 1974, for example, the S&P 500 was down 23% for the year at this point and rallied 21% over the next six months. In 2022, the index was in a bear market, down 16%, and still gained 3.3% over the next six months and 10.6% over the next year.

Recent findings suggest that data source: Investing.com and the author's analysis of S&P 500 daily closing prices. Each starting point is the final close of the week ending between Nov. 7 and Nov. 13. Six months = 26 weeks; 12 months = 52 weeks.

Admittedly, this is a small sample size so we don't want to put too much weight into these conclusions.

In the 2000s, the midterm outperformance trend has weakened considerably. While it's still maintaining its perfect record of gains, the S&P 500's performance has been pretty average overall. In most cases, the markets saw an immediate boost once the uncertainty over the election results was lifted. But beyond that, the market in recent years hasn't gotten much of an extended boost.

As of November 17, the S&P 500 is down around 3% since election day but still up about 7% on the year as a whole.

The substantial question facing the markets right now is what's going to happen with artificial intelligence (AI)? Heavy spending in AI development is being viewed negatively; there could be a significant slowdown in data center development; and worries are growing that red-hot earnings growth rates are ready to slow.

Above average inflation, high interest rates, and the potential for multiple Fed rate hikes over the next several quarters could also pose headwinds.

Earnings growth for the S&P 500 is still expected to be solid, but there are any number of factors that could, over the next 12 months, derail the narrative.

The high degree of uncertainty looking forward over the next few quarters helps make the case for a long-term view.

History is on the side of the S&P 500 here, but investor confidence has been shaken over the past month, and the number of potential stumbling blocks is getting massive. This could very well be the year the post-midterm bounce trend doesn't hold.

In a recent development, since the markets could move in either direction, the best idea is probably to just stay put. Don't try to time the market and bet which way stock prices will move. Just keep contributing to your accounts and maintain a focus on your goals further down the road instead.

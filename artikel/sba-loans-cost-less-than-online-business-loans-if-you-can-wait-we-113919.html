SBA Loans Cost Less Than Online Business Loans – If You Can Wait Weeks

🌐 Source: Moneycrashers • 📅 22 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (521 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, choose an SBA loan over an online loan if you can wait weeks. The SBA’s guarantee comes with a capped rate and terms up to 10 years for most uses, 25 for real estate. Expect to pledge collateral on loans over $50,000, sign a personal guarantee, and, as a startup, put in at least 10% of the cost.

Sources indicate that owners turn to online lenders for speed. In the Federal Reserve’s 2025 Small Business Credit Survey, 60% of firms that borrowed from online lenders said their borrowing costs ran higher than expected, against 32% of large-bank borrowers.

An SBA loan is slower and cheaper. The U.S. Small Business Administration guarantees up to 85% of most 7(a) loans, which banks and other approved lenders make. In return for that backing, SBA rules cap a variable rate at the banks’ benchmark prime rate, now 7.00%, plus 3 to 6.5 percentage points, depending on the loan’s size. Say a landscaper owes $120,000 on online term loans repaid over two years at 30% interest, about $6,710 a month. A 10-year 7(a) loan at a fixed 11% cuts that to about $1,650. The longer term costs more interest in total, about $78,000 over 10 years against $41,000 on the online loans. Extra payments shrink that, and a 7(a) loan shorter than 15 years carries no prepayment fee.

It has been revealed that A 7(a) loan of up to $5 million can cover working capital, equipment, real estate, debt refinancing, or starting or buying a business. A 504 loan, made through a nonprofit Certified Development Company, finances buildings, land, and long-lived machinery at a fixed rate. SBA microloans of up to $50,000 come from nonprofit lenders.

Under the SBA’s lending rules, every owner of 20% or more personally guarantees the whole loan, and a loan over $50,000 needs collateral, though a lender can’t refuse you for weak collateral alone. A business open a year or less must put in at least 10% of the total cost, and so must most buyers of a business. (Related: Coffee shop owner refused to serve TikTok reviewer afte...)

In a recent development, to get an SBA loan, start with a bank that knows your business, and ask whether it’s an SBA Preferred Lender, one allowed to approve loans without the SBA’s review, which can take up to 10 business days. Or describe your needs in the SBA’s Lender Match tool, which lists interested lenders two business days later.

Apply once you know a substantial cost is coming, and keep online loans for needs that can’t wait weeks.

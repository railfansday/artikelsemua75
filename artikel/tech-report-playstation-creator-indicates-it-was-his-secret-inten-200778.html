Tech Report: PlayStation creator indicates it was his "secret intention" to trick Microsoft into making Xbox

Ken Kutaragi led the development of Sony's video game consoles for years, and is widely known as "the father of the PlayStation." As it turns out, he might also be the father of the Xbox – or, at least, its distant uncle. In a fresh interview, Kutaragi claims to have been playing 4D chess in a meeting with a Microsoft executive, essentially tricking the company into creating its own game console.

"I had the chance to meet with the head of Microsoft one-to-one," Kutaragi tells The Game Business. The literal head of Microsoft in this era would, of course, have been Bill Gates, though Kutaragi isn't specific about who specifically he spoke to. Whoever it was, he wasn't terribly impressed: "He was so arrogant.".

Sources indicate that "Sony has no money, but they have huge money," Kutaragi continues. He demonstrated this Microsoft executive what Sony was developing, and got an offer in return, where Microsoft would send "free hard disk drives to Sony" if they would, in exchange, "give some license rights" to Microsoft. "He tried to convince me," Kutaragi claims.

It has been revealed that "I remarked only one word: 'No,'" he claims. "'No thanks.' I was not so super arrogant as they were. I had a dream. If I say 'no,' they will get started on bringing in another platform after PC. That was my secret intention. They did, spending huge money on it.".

Industry observers note that the exact timeline here is unclear – Kutaragi doesn't specify whether it was the PS1 or the PS2 that he was quietly showing to Microsoft. Either way, by 2001, Microsoft had rolled out its own game console, the Xbox, as a direct competitor to PS2. Notably, one of the Xbox's key differentiators was its built-in hard drive, which may have been on the table for PS2 if Kutaragi hadn't shut Microsoft's offer down.

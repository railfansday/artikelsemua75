I turned on the TV on Thursday night and I was puzzled, why were thousands of Sunderland fans banned from their latest European match?

The Mackems playing at the Stadium of Light in the Europa League.

Only the fifth European home match in the club’s entire history and yet Sunderland fans conspicuous by their absence.

The moment I switched on, the camera was focused on loads of sections of the Stadium of Light that were totally empty, no Sunderland fans in them at all. (Related: GTA 6 made real gyms empty out as players chose in-game...)

I thought maybe I hadn’t heard of some UEFA ban that had forced them to close large parts of their stadium. (Related: Christmas market forced to cancel after backlash over A...)

However, when the camera then focused elsewhere, there were many many empty seats in the areas of the ground that were open.

The match was woeful, the quality shocking, the only excitement when the cameras went back to the stands.

Industry observers note that it turns out the embarrassing number of empty seats would have been far worse but for loads of Jagiellonia Bialystok fans buying tickets amongst the Sunderland fans. Turns out the Polish team had seen their supporters banned from attending this match due to past misbehaviour.

Sources indicate that this type of thing rarely ends well when you have a set of away fans banned and a home club that struggles to sell their tickets. Sure enough, the Police seemed to be having to wade into any number of sections to deal with friction between the Sunderland fans and the visiting supporters amongst them.

The actual match ended in a 1-1 draw and from what I saw, Sunderland very lucky to get a point against Jagiellonia Bialystok. Amusing to hear the booing from the few Sunderland fans who were still there at the final whistle.

The excitement of playing in Europe for only the second time in the club’s entire history, 53 years since the only other time, is now well and truly wearig off.

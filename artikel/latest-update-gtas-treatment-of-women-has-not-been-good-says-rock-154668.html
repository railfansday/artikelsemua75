📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that lots of people will tell you that Grand Theft Auto 6's Lucia Caminos marks the series' first female protagonist. Lots of people are, of course, wrong. GTA 1 had eight selectable protagonists, four of whom were women, while GTA 2 for the Game Boy Color let you play protags Candy or Gretchen (along with a bunch of other men) in addition to the PlayStation game's default Claude Speed.

Recent findings suggest that that vital pedantry out of the way, it's undeniable that Lucia is GTA's first female protagonist since the series became the culture-swallowing goliath it is today. In a chat with CBC's As It Happens, GTA OG Brian Baglow noted he reckons it's "long, long overdue". (Related: Show, Don't Certify: How to Prepare for Your First Data...)

Baglow, credited as a writer on the original GTA and who served as "head of propaganda and indoctrination" at DMA Design (the studio that would become Rockstar North) in the '90s, said that back in the day of GTA 1, the devs didn't even have a script. "We started with a tech demo, a way of moving around a city… I came in to actually add the story, add the script, after the levels had been built". (Related: Tech Report: Developer builds a ‘Discord kitten’ that’s...)

That was mostly a process of, shall we say, finding inspiration: "we were making it up as we went along… it was a case of going into every crime movie, every heist, every caper, every TV show, cop show, crime drama, and lifting elements that I hoped would resonate with people".

Industry observers note that baglow sounds, rightfully, rather proud of his time spent working on such an crucial series, but he has his qualms as he casts his eye back over it. "The representation of women, it has not been good, and that's putting it very mildly", he says, "so I'm delighted that we now have not only a female protagonist, but a Hispanic female protagonist. I think it's been long, long overdue, and I can't wait to get to grips with the game and see how in-depth it goes.

"I think it's showing a level of maturation because from the very earliest days it was a very young group of almost entirely male developers in Scotland".

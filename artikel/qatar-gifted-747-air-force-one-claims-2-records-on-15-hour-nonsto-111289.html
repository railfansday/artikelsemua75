Qatar-Gifted 747 Air Force One Claims 2 Records On 15-Hour Nonstop Flight From China

Sources indicate that the Qatari-gifted Boeing 747-8, now flying as the VC-25B Bridge aircraft, set two Air Force records on its return from Shenzhen, China, according to the White House. The jet reportedly made the heaviest takeoff in Air Force history at just under one million pounds, and also flew the longest nonstop Air Force One flight to date, lasting nearly 15 hours. According to recent reports, the aircraft left Shenzhen at 5:54 PM local time on November 19 and landed at Joint Base Andrews at 8:01 PM local time with no refueling stop, carrying President Donald Trump home from the APEC summit. The jet cruised at 33,000 feet and averaged 500 knots (926 km/h), passing over Shanghai, Tokyo, Shemya, Winnipeg, and Cleveland. Trump put the duration at about 14.5 hours and said the trip normally takes closer to 20 with a fuel stop, which he credited to newer equipment. (Related: Build A Racetrack codes (November 2026)...)

The 747-8i on which the VC-25B is based has a maximum takeoff weight of 987,000 pounds (447,694 kg), which TWZ notes exceeds that of a fully loaded C-5M Galaxy, and an endurance of 15 to 16 hours is commonly quoted for the type. The 7,500 nautical miles (13,890 km) flown sits inside the nearly 7,800 nautical miles (14,446 km) Boeing lists for a commercial 747-8i.

That comparison has limits. The Bridge jet is probably much lighter than a fully loaded airliner even with maximum fuel, although extra tanks may have been added during conversion, so its true maximum range remains unclear. The records therefore show the airframe operating near the upper edge of its envelope, not beyond what the type was designed to do.

According to a card handed to the accompanying press pool:.

"The VC-25B conducted the heaviest takeoff in Air Force history, at just under one million pounds, and completed the longest nonstop Air Force One sortie to date, at nearly 15 hours.".

The Bridge jet cannot be refueled in flight, and neither can the two purpose-built VC-25Bs that Boeing is under contract to deliver. TWZ reports that the requirement was dropped in 2017 as a cost-cutting measure, and that the upcoming E-4C command post aircraft, built on the same 747 model, will keep the capability. The two aging VC-25As can take on fuel in the air, though only in emergencies, and they stopped in Alaska to refuel on the way home from this trip.

Range requirements add to the concern. The original VC-25B requirement was roughly 7,100 nautical miles (13,149 km), later revised to 5,900 (10,927 km), while the Air Force fact sheet lists about 6,800 (12,594 km) for the VC-25A. TWZ concludes that unless the new jets exceed the revised figure by a wide margin, they cannot fly this route nonstop, and it points to the weight of nuclear hardening, defenses, and tailored systems as the likely cause.

Simple Flying puts the cost of the VC-25B program at $5.3 billion, driven by protection against electromagnetic pulses, missile evasion, and secure global communications. TWZ adds that time aloft is central to continuity of government planning.

The White House remains the only source for the two flight records so far. As it stands, the claims came from the card handed to the press pool, Trump's own remarks, along with a post on X by White House communications director Steven Cheung. Neither the Air Force nor Boeing has publicly confirmed either figure, and TWZ reports that it has contacted both for confirmation.

The nonstop flight home was also a contrast to the earlier outbound trip to Asia. On November 16, the Bridge jet stopped in Alaska to refuel on its way to Manila, landing at Fairbanks International Airport instead of its planned stop in Anchorage. The White House blamed freezing rain around Anchorage and dismissed reports about the diversion as "fear-mongering slop.".

The mission also brought all three presidential 747s to Asia. The Bridge jet, painted in the red, white, and blue livery Trump has favored, carried the president, while the older VC-25As, registered 82-8000 and 92-9000, accompanied it, and all three were seen together in Manila. A second aircraft routinely travels as a mechanical backup, but it is not known whether the third jet continued to China.

The Bridge aircraft arrived at Joint Base Andrews on June 19, intended to serve until the long-term VC-25B is delivered. It debuted as Air Force One on July 1, flying the president to North Dakota, and the Air Force accepted it less than two months before that. L3Harris completed the conversion in 10 months, and TWZ indicates significant questions remain about whether the jet can support the full range of mission requirements, though officials and the contractor say concerns have been addressed.

The final modification cost is undisclosed, with an earlier estimate of under $400 million. Analysts had speculated that the interim jet was unsuited to international missions because of its limited modifications, a view the recent China trip undercuts. The VC-25Bs are expected in 2028, and the VC-25As are scheduled to retire in 2029.

In a recent development, the S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) just wrapped up one of its worst weeks in history, falling 8.6% as the AI trade collapsed. The index gave up 3.7% on Friday to close in correction territory, defined as a decline of 10% or more from a recent closing high. It was the first time the index had fallen below 7,000 since April. (Related: AI for Game Development: Creating a Farming Game in 5 D...)

Corrections are fairly routine events on the stock market. On average, the S&P 500 enters a correction about every 1.5 years, and its last correction came in March-April of 2025 as concerns about President Trump's tariffs and a trade war drove investors to sell. (Related: Hobbled at home, Trump turns abroad...)

Nervousness around the AI sector has been pushing tech stocks lower since the beginning of October, but the negative sentiment didn't really hit the S&P 500 until this week.

Investors are worried about rising interest rates squeezing the AI infrastructure build-out, including in the unprofitable neocloud sector; the backlash against data centers following the midterm elections; and the crisis unfolding at Nvidia after Jensen Huang resigned, with investigations into chip exports to China and now an accounting investigation by the SEC.

Fears of an AI bubble have been swirling in the market for a while, but despite some of the air coming out of these stocks, the fundamentals still look strong and valuations remain cheap.

Of the last 10 corrections in the S&P 500, which go back to 2011, the average decline has been 17%, and two of those turned into bear markets. That happened during the COVID crash early in 2020 and in 2022, a bear market that lasted most of the year.

The average duration from peak to a upcoming peak was 9.4 months, and the average duration from the bottom to a new peak was 6.1 months, though the last two corrections took less than 100 days.

Regardless of the length of time, the S&P 500 has recovered from every correction it's experienced.

Sources indicate that A correction is no reason to panic, and it's a regular part of stock market volatility. However, this is a good time to put together a watch list of stocks you'd like to buy if you have cash available. If you are a net buyer of stocks, a sell-off is actually a good thing, as Warren Buffett advised, as it makes stocks cheaper to buy.

Some quality stocks like Alphabet and Meta now have price-to-earnings ratios below 20, making them look attractive, and we're likely to see more deals if stocks fall further.

If you're not in a position to buy stocks, it's worth remembering that historically, the S&P 500 returns to an all-time high within nine months on average. How this sell-off plays out will depend on the AI sector, but the low valuations for many of the top AI stocks are an encouraging sign.

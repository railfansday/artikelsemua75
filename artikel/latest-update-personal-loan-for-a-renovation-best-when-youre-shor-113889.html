Latest Update: Personal Loan for a Renovation – Best When You’re Short on Equity or Time

Use a personal loan for a small home project or an urgent repair, or when you have little home equity, since the money can arrive within days and your house isn’t collateral. For a significant renovation, price a home equity loan or home equity line of credit (HELOC) first, which usually costs less.

Borrowing against your home equity costs less than a personal loan because the house secures the loan, and that’s also why it’s slower and riskier. (Related: Second Circuit denies Kalshi and Coinbase interim relie...)

A home equity loan or HELOC usually means an appraisal, closing costs such as title search fees, and a few weeks’ wait for the money. Fall behind on it, and the lender can foreclose. A personal loan usually runs two to seven years, where an equity loan can run 20.

According to recent reports, on a small job, the equity loan’s lower rate doesn’t save much. Say you borrow $15,000 for a bathroom remodel over five years, at 11% on a personal loan with no origination fee or at 8% on a home equity loan with $500 in closing costs. The personal loan’s interest comes to about $4,600 and the equity loan’s to about $3,200, so after its closing costs, the equity loan is only about $800 cheaper. On $50,000 at the same rates and term, the equity loan’s interest runs about $4,400 less than the personal loan’s before closing costs, which makes it worth pricing first for a significant renovation. Recent findings suggest that before you apply, get your contractor’s written, fixed-price bid, so the amount you borrow matches a set price, not an estimate. Then rank personal loan offers by annual percentage rate (APR), not interest rate, since the APR includes the origination fee many lenders take out of the loan.

Take the shortest term whose payment fits your budget. At 11%, repaying $15,000 in three years instead of five raises the payment from about $326 to $491 and cuts the interest from about $4,600 to $2,700.

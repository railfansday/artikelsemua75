Recent findings suggest that bing's crawl stats answer a question Google's console cannot: did the crawler arrive, and did it get a 200 when it did. Ours spent fifteen days failing that second half, and the fix was not submission volume.

We read the numbers from the Bing Webmaster API every day on our own site. Fifteen consecutive days, 2026-11-08 to 11-22:.

Three things to read out of it.

Sources indicate that on 11-12 Bing fetched five URLs and got zero successful pages. On 11-18, 19 and 20 it fetched one, one and three, and still zero. If you only look at CrawledPages, you conclude Bing is working. Code2xx indicates it was being sent to addresses that redirect.

That single 301 column on every one of the fifteen days is the fingerprint: one URL keeps being requested that answers with a redirect — usually the http twin, the bare host, or the path without its trailing slash. Our sitemap lists only the slashed, https, canonical form, which is why every fresh fetch had to be re-routed before anything could be read.

Nothing we did to the site. We did not add pages, rewrite content, or increase submissions in that window. What appeared in the same 24 hours as the 0 → 4 in Code2xx was InLinks: 0 → 4.

That is consistent with the explanation Bing's own documentation implies: crawl budget follows links. It is one observation on one property, so we are not going to turn it into a rule — but it does explain why "submit more URLs" produced nothing for two weeks.

One caveat, because it is the same site: Bing improving did not mean the site was healthy. On the Google side the same 56 URLs went from 37 indexed to 13 in three days, with no change to the pages and no drop in crawl volume. We publish that series with the per-URL readings and a CSV at our index decay dataset — "the crawler came" and "the page was kept" are two different measurements, and only one of them was going well.

New home sales surge in October, but demand signals remain soft

Newly introduced home sales blipped above expectations in October, handing homebuilders a slice of better-than-worse, Thanksgiving-time, good news. However, NAHB’s November homebuilder confidence gauge had plunged to its lowest level in nearly 15 years, signaling that builders remain pessimistic about the market’s current state and foreseeable-future direction.

It has been revealed that according to U.S. Census Bureau data, sales of newly constructed single-family homes in October came in at a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 701,000, up 14.2% month-over month, and a 7.5% gain from the same month a year prior. Meanwhile, the median sales price of new homes sold in October was $391,000, down 0.3% from September and 3.2% from a year ago.

October’s better-than-forecast results may surprise many, but they come with a catch: the survey’s margin of error and data collection cycle. They could be revised downward next month.

“A downward revision to September helps explain the size of October’s 14.2% rebound, the largest monthly gain since November 2025,” First American Deputy Chief Economist Odeta Kushi mentioned in a statement.

Latest home for-sale inventory also tightened, coinciding with a decline in new residential construction. The seasonally adjusted estimate of new homes for sale at the end of October was 470,000, down 1.5% from September’s 477,000, and 4.1% below the 490,000 recorded in October 2025. At the current sales rate, that represents a supply of 8.0 months, a 14% improvement from 9.3 months in September and 11.1% from 9.0 months a year earlier.

October’s sales results may be less of a bright spot than they appear, and one strong month doesn’t erase the warning signs flashing across the latest home market.

Plenty of indications suggest homebuilders are working through one of the toughest housing markets in years, including homebuilder confidence at its lowest point since the tail end of the Great Financial Crisis.

For example, mortgage applications for new-home purchases fell 13.6% year over year in October, a sign that rising mortgage rates and low consumer confidence continue to weigh on demand. The existing-home market is also struggling, with the National Association of Realtors reporting that pending home sales fell to another record low in October.

NAHB also reports that, in November, homebuilders had a bleak view of current sales conditions, future sales expectations for the next six months and prospective buyer traffic.

According to recent reports, “Persistent housing affordability challenges, including elevated home prices and mortgage rates, continue to weigh on prospective buyers and constrain demand,” Danushka Nanayakkara-Skillington, NAHB Assistant VP for Forecasting and Analysis, wrote in a note following today’s newly introduced home sales release. “Builders are using sales incentives and price adjustments to attract buyers, but affordability remains a significant obstacle to a sustained recovery in housing demand.”. A review of the latest earnings calls from 14 publicly traded homebuilders reveals that the country’s largest builders faced a challenging third quarter, with sales conditions remaining weak heading into the final months of the year, consistent with NAHB’s data.

Last quarter, only three of the public homebuilders — Smith Douglas Homes, Toll Brothers and Green Brick Partners — reported year-over-year increases in latest orders. However, nearly all 14 experienced sequential declines in orders, many exceeding 20%, pointing to a further deterioration in demand.

Sales volume followed a similar trend. Only Smith Douglas Homes and LGI Homes reported year-over-year increases in homes sold,.

Meanwhile, just two builders, Toll Brothers (2.4%) and Hovnanian Enterprises (0.4%), posted year-over-year increases in average sales prices. Toll’s luxury-focused buyer base is generally less sensitive to mortgage rates and economic volatility, while Hovnanian has increasingly shifted its product mix toward move-up and active-adult buyers, who tend to be more resilient to broader market pressures.

Many public homebuilders reported in October and November that buyers remain very cautious, which helped dampen sales volume and prices.

“As we moved through the quarter, our buyers became more cautious, did not feel an urgency to transact and felt more comfortable moving to the sidelines,” Century Communities Executive Chairman Dale Francescon said during an earnings call on October 28.

It has been revealed that paul Romanowski, President and CEO at D.R. Horton, acknowledged that October was another challenging month.

“Demand did get weaker as the quarter went on, for sure. As rates rose and as we got into October, what we’ve seen mirrors September. Traffic and sales activity are somewhat below what we’d anticipate in a typical seasonal pattern,” he stated during an earnings call on October 29.

Against this backdrop, it remains unclear whether October’s sales rebound reflects a meaningful improvement in demand or a temporary spike that could show up next month as a downward revision.

“Looking ahead, the question is whether enough buyers will follow those who signed contracts in October,” Kushi said, pointing to falling mortgage applications for new homes and low homebuilder confidence. “Builders are finding ways to make the monthly payment work for some buyers, but softer buyer traffic and fewer mortgage applications point to a tough winter.”.

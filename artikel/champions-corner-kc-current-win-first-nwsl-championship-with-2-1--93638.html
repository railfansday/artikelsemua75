It took five years, 90 minutes and a goal for the ages, but the Kansas City Current have their first-ever NWSL championship.

Riding a wave of momentum created by a 54th-minute Olimpico from midfielder Vanessa DiBernardo, and capping it off with an all-notable breakaway winner from Debinha, the Current silenced the partisan, sellout crowd at the heavily-favored Washington Spirit’s home ground with a 2-1 victory on Saturday.

The Spirit, heartbroken at the final whistle, have now lost three consecutive NWSL Championships.

It has been revealed that “Obviously this one hates way worse than any of the other ones,” forward Trinity Rodman said afterward. “I don’t even think you have words in this moment — it is too fresh.”. (Related: Large Language Models: A Fresh Moore's Law?...)

Recent findings suggest that diBernardo’s goal was stunning, a driven, bent corner kick played to the far post that eluded Spirit goalkeeper Sandy MacIver, her defenders and Current forward Haley Hopkins, potentially DiBernardo’s intended target. The Spirit’s players and fans alike pleaded with center referee Joshua Encarnación, suggesting he examine the play on VAR for obstruction, as MacIver had potentially been screened. The review never came.

Whether played directly on goal intentionally or otherwise, the effort will go down as one of the most memorable in NWSL Championship history. At 34, DiBernardo becomes the oldest player in league history to score in a final, and was later named the match’s MVP.

“I’ve been to the game a few times and haven’t won, but this one just felt a little lighter,” noted DiBernardo, who gave birth to Charlie, her first child, in January and took time off before returning to the Current in March. “I think just having Charlie, it just brings a different perspective on life. I knew the game was substantial and it was a big moment, but just kinda having her, just spending the day with her, it just felt really special.”. The game’s early phases were forgettable save for one incident which would go on to affect the remainder of the match: a bruising, high-speed tackle on Rodman by Current defender Laney Rouse in the fifth minute. The challenge sent Rodman flying and appeared to injure her left elbow; the Spirit forward, nearing tears, spent much of the first half in visible pain and seemingly never fully recovered. Rouse was not cautioned for the challenge.

According to recent reports, “[It helped set the tone] in the sense that it hurt really bad, yes,” Rodman mentioned. “As a team, you always want to put the first hit in. You’re taught to do that. Credit to them. I wish I was the one who put her on the ground. They did a really good job at setting the tone and controlling the first half.”.

It was far from the game’s only physical moment. Neither team managed to settle into any meaningful rhythm during an opening 45 that felt at times more like a title fight than a title match. Encarnación, involved in his first NWSL final, called a very tight match and the near-constant stoppages in play were the defining characteristic of a sometimes ugly opening phase. In many ways it was not entirely his fault: the message-sending start from KC and the Spirit’s subsequent response often compelled him to act. The 19 combined first-half fouls between both sides were the most in any half of any NWSL title match in league history.

It has been revealed that “It felt like we couldn’t play more than two minutes without a whistle,” Rodman said. “We were fouling, they were true calls for sure — but I think you lose control of the game really quickly that way ... It definitely messed with our rhythm a little bit.”.

Paradoxically, though, the match was won with speed. Deep into second-half stoppage time, the Current found their eventual match-winner on a breakaway. After doggedly winning back possession after a Spirit free kick late on, Kansas City forward Debinha streaked upfield from just beyond the center stripe, looking off a wide-open Temwa Chawinga and rounding MacIver who looked on hopelessly as the Brazilian slotted it home.

Moments later, Rodman – somehow still in the match after not only the early challenge but a later high-speed collision with Current goalkeeper Lorena – pulled one back for the Spirit. Falling away from goal 10 yards out, Rodman got her head on the end of a Tara Rudd cross, placing it perfectly over Lorena’s outstretched arms and into the far-side netting. The ensuing five minutes – the game ended in the 102nd – were chaotic but the Spirit couldn’t quite find the equalizer.

It has been revealed that after a slow start, the Current were well-managed by former Major League Soccer and US national team member Chris Armas. Armas was widely considered a controversial selection at the time of his appointment but proceeded to lead the club to their first championship in his first season, doing so by beating Gotham FC and now the Spirit, two sides picked by many as potential league-winners.

“I think from the front to the back we made it a point that every person on this team defends and if somebody is going to make a mistake the other person is going to have their back,” Current defender Izzy Rodriguez remarked. “We try and turn every 1v1 situation into a 1v2 for us, and I think that showed up today. We were winning our duels and second balls and those are things that win games.”.

For the Spirit, the loss is devastating. They become the first American professional club – men’s or women’s – to lose three consecutive finals since the Fresh England Revolution of Major League Soccer, who did so in the mid 2000s. In their five Championship appearances, the Spirit have won just one time, in 2021.

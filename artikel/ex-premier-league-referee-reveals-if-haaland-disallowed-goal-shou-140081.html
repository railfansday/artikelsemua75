Manchester City's poor Premier League form continued on Saturday as Enzo Maresca's side made it four games without a win with a 1-1 draw against Fulham.

But the match was not without its controversy.

Rodrigo Muniz put the visitors in front in the 16th minute, but just under ten minutes later, City thought they had levelled the game when Erling Haaland, one of the best strikers in the Premier League right now, prodded through Bernd Leno's legs.

However, the goal was not given on the field of play, with referee Thomas Bramall adjudging Haaland to have fouled the Whites goalkeeper. Elliot Anderson did volley home an equaliser in the second half, but the home side could not force a winner.

That Haaland non-goal left manager Enzo Maresca questioning the officials afterwards.

The City boss said: "I reviewed the goal after the game. From my point of view, it’s quite clear that it’s not a foul, but I’m not the guy that decides, unfortunately.”.

Pundits have been split on the decision, with some suggesting it should have stood, and others agreeing with Bramall and VAR Sam Barrott.

GIVEMESPORT's resident referee expert Graham Barber has now delivered his verdict on the incident.

Speaking exclusively to GMS, he stated: "So, for me, on this one, I'm going to join the goalkeepers' union. Some people say the referees' union and the goalkeepers' union are as one! (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

It has been revealed that "When I saw it for the first time, I actually thought it was a foul, but when I saw it the second time, he [Leno] actually did lose the ball. But the challenge from Haaland came when the goalkeeper had two hands on the ball.

"And it's very, very difficult to argue a goalkeeper is not in control of the ball, if he's got two hands on the ball, I mean, if he's not got two hands on the ball, you could argue he has to be in control of the ball.

"I found it quite interesting as well that he then fell over into the back of the net and then pulled the ball forward to try and pretend that it didn't go over the goal line, which, considering the technology is in the ball and it's going to be on the referee's watch whether it cross the line is irrelevant.

"For me, the correct decision, Haaland definitely made the challenge, definitely touched the ball while two hands were on the ball, so for me, correct decision.".

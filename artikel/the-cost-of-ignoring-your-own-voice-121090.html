We feel that something is off, but after talking to a few people, listening to their advice, considering all the benefits, and hearing how great the opportunity is, we end up doing exactly what we didn't want to do in the first place. When was the last time you trusted your own feelings without asking five other people what they thought? I want to tell you a story from my life. It is about an opportunity I talked myself into believing was perfect.

A while ago, I was invited to visit a potential workplace in another city. They offered to cover my trip so I could spend a few days there, give a talk, and meet the team. I was excited. Who wouldn't be? Good money, benefits, professional recognition, and what seemed like a great opportunity. But from the very beginning, something inside me was saying, don't go.

Sources indicate that usually, your inner voice is so quiet, so thin, and impossible to hear. Have you ever had a dream where you want to scream but can't? You open your mouth, try with all your strength, but only a barely audible sound comes out. You know you are trying to say something, but the words just won't come out.

Sources indicate that that's how I imagine our inner voice sometimes. It's there, trying to get our attention, but we are too busy to listen. It's like watching someone trying to tell you something from across a room. You can see their mouth moving, you know they have something to say, but there is so much noise around you that you never quite hear them. And eventually, you turn away and continue with whatever you were doing.

In a recent development, I knew something was there, but I couldn't quite hear it. I didn't make much effort to hear it either. Life was busy. I had things to do, responsibilities, aims. I kept going, thinking that maybe the feeling would disappear.

Then came the trip. I almost missed my flight, even though I had arrived at the airport ahead of time. An issue with something in my luggage held me up at security. The whole process took so long that I could hear the announcement that my gate was closing. I remember telling them I didn't care about the item anymore; I just needed my suitcase. I grabbed it and ran.

According to recent reports, I made it onto the plane. When I landed in the new city, I felt that the atmosphere, the surroundings, and the pace of life were not quite aligning with me. It was cold, and not what I was used to. At that moment, I just wanted to go back home. But then I started convincing myself that maybe it was just the weather. Maybe I was tired.

After all, everyone had encouraged me to pursue this opportunity. Who rejects something that life brings their way? What if I was turning down something good because I was uncomfortable with change?

During those three days in a new city, I felt weak and exhausted the entire time. I kept finding explanations for everything. It's probably the weather. I was counting the days until I could go home. And when I finally did, I felt so grateful. I remember being genuinely happy to be back in my own city, in my familiar surroundings. I didn't even want to think about the offer for a few days.

Industry observers note that but eventually, I had to make a decision. I talked to my friends and colleagues and explained how I felt. I noted that I didn't think this was my place, that something didn't feel aligned with what I wanted. I didn't want to seem ungrateful or like someone who couldn't appreciate a good opportunity. And everyone started convincing me again. It's a great opportunity. Look at the benefits, the position. You can't just reject something like this.

Sources indicate that and again, I listened to everyone except myself. I accepted. After settling into the fresh environment, I realized that the things I had sensed during those few days were not going away. The work environment felt restrictive. I didn't feel connected to the community or the lifestyle. The things I valued and enjoyed in my everyday life were missing.

It has been revealed that I remember thinking: Did I really not notice all of this when I was there for a few days? Why didn't I listen to myself? What I experienced during those three days became part of my everyday life, but in a much bigger format.

It has been revealed that I don't regret my decisions. I don't believe every decision has to be perfect, and I am grateful for the experiences that have brought me where I am. But I do wish I had given my own feelings the same attention I gave everyone else's opinions.

Why was it so easy for me to doubt my own feelings, but so difficult to question everyone else's advice?

In a recent development, I started wondering how often we do this without noticing. We are looking for confirmation. We already have a feeling about something, but we want someone else to tell us that it's okay to feel that way. Other times, we keep asking because we are afraid of making the decision ourselves. If someone we trust tells us what to do, perhaps the responsibility feels a little lighter.

The other part about advice is that people see your decision from the outside. They see the benefits, the possibilities. They see what looks good on paper. But they don't have to live your everyday life afterward. They don't wake up in your environment or experience your discomfort.

They can genuinely want the best for you and still be wrong about what is best for you.

Good intentions don't mean good advice for our specific situation. I don't think this means we should stop asking for advice or assume that our first instinct is always right. But do not let other people's perspectives replace your own voice.

In a recent development, next time you want to ask for advice about your specific situation, think of this: What is your first feeling? What are you hoping someone would tell you? And if nobody had an opinion about your decision, what would you choose?

Also, you don't have to make every decision right away. Take some time, gather information (this is important), consider different perspectives, and change your mind. But don't let the process of seeking advice become a way of silencing yourself.

Everyone around you can be convinced that you are making the right decision, but you are the only person who knows how it feels to live with it.

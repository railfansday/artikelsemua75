It is the Blues' second consecutive Premier League loss and their third in the last four games, in what has been a dismal run. (Related: Three 'e-waste' Nvidia V100s hit 100 tokens per second ...)

The home side had gone ahead through Emanuel Emegha, before Noah Okafor, Dominic Calvert-Lewin and Brenden Aaronson found the net in an embarrassing second-half collapse.

Chelsea fans chanted against Alonso in the game's closing stages and booed throughout, with their team now eighth in the Premier League and just one point ahead of Tottenham.

And the going will not get any easier for the Spaniard, with his team set to face Liverpool, Manchester City and Aston Villa in the coming weeks.

Chelsea handed the 44-year-old a four-year contract in the summer and will not rush into any decision, but there will be an expectation of a quick turnaround at Stamford Bridge.

Alonso's team made history last month when they became only the second Chelsea side to lose to Tottenham in 36 years.

The Blues were embarrassingly poor against their biggest rivals, who had won just one Premier League game from seven up to that point.

The home fans were furious, but there was an acceptance that Alonso's project would take longer than just a couple of months.

However, Saturday's defeat to Leeds will be received even more poorly and it has given Alonso plenty of questions to answer.

His Chelsea side are the first since 1999 to lose to Leeds at Stamford Bridge, and to do it in such embarrassing fashion will land the manager in a world of hurt.

Chelsea have looked like two different teams this season. One with Joao Pedro - an exciting, attacking and winning team.

And one without Pedro - a dismal, boring team with two wins in seven games across all competitions.

It is not Alonso's fault that his star striker has been injured and is yet to return to training.

Recent findings suggest that but a manager's job is to find a solution, and the Spaniard seems incapable of doing exactly that. As long as Pedro is injured, it looks as if Chelsea will continue to struggle.

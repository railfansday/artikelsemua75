In a recent development, about nine months ago in mid-February, the average 30-year fixed rate mortgage was at 6.09%. At that time, it was much higher compared to what consumers saw from 2009 to 2022. Things have only gotten tighter throughout this year.

It has been revealed that today, the 30-year fixed rate mortgage is at 7.11%. It has moved up more than one percentage point in roughly nine months. Outside of the past four years, it hasn't been this elevated since 2002. On a $500,000 house, the difference between 7.11% and 6.09% translates to hundreds of dollars more in monthly payments. Recent findings suggest that in October, existing housing sales dipped to their lowest level in 16 years. This is a frozen market. And shareholders in Home Depot (HD +0.70%) and Lowe's (LOW +0.78%) are feeling the pain. (Related: Supermicro sued by shareholders over claims Nvidia exec...)

Deciding to tackle a home renovation, upgrade, or repair is no small undertaking. It can be a sizable capital commitment for people. Anytime consumers deal with big purchases, the companies involved are exposed to fluctuating demand. Unlike buying products or services on a recurring schedule, these households can choose to delay these projects.

With the 30-year fixed-rate mortgage above 7%, Home Depot and Lowe's are in a bind. "Housing turnover hasn't moved in four years now," incoming Home Depot chief executive officer (and current chief financial officer) Richard McPhail said on the Q3 2026 earnings call.

It has been revealed that less activity in the housing market means fewer people are moving. On the flip side, when more people are changing homes, it can naturally lead to higher demand for the things that Home Depot and Lowe's sell.

In a recent development, during its fiscal 2026 third quarter (ended Nov. 1), Home Depot, which is the clear leader in the industry, posted a 0.5% decline in comparable transactions. Lowe's fared worse. It reported that this figure fell by 1.7%.

Industry observers note that transaction counts are a proxy for foot traffic. When it comes to any retailer, investors want to see this metric growing.

It has been revealed that same-store sales (SSS), however, grew for both companies thanks to the impact of higher average ticket sizes. Both of these leadership teams called out appliances as a strong product category, pushing substantial-ticket comp sales up mid-single digits.

It doesn't look like these companies will get any relief soon on the macro front. According to CME Group's (CME +0.41%) FedWatch, trading activity suggests that there's a 100% probability the fed funds rate will be higher after the December meeting, up from the current range of 3.75% to 4%. The problem is inflation that's lingering longer than expected.

For what it's worth, Home Depot and Lowe's do expect SSS to be positive for fiscal 2026. And both are finding that demand from their professional customer bases, which consist of contractors, plumbers, electricians, and the like, is stronger than spending from DIY consumers. These companies are getting better at serving their professionals, enhancing areas like fulfillment, customer service, and loyalty programs.

According to recent reports, past investments in supply chain and omnichannel capabilities are also paying off. Home Depot's online comparable sales jumped 13% in the third quarter. Lowe's saw a 17% gain. Their scale, supported by massive revenue bases, give them the ability to bolster their operations, which is something smaller rivals can't do.

A housing market recovery could take time, but there appears to be meaningful pent-up demand. Accessible home equity is at a record $13 trillion.

Investors in these two retail stocks will still have to stay extremely patient, as they have been in recent years. Shares in Home Depot and Lowe's put up total returns of 19% and 16%, respectively, in the past three years. That's worse than the 69% total return of the S&P 500 index(^GSPC -3.70%).

As long as SSS remain under pressure for these businesses, reporting strong earnings growth is out of the question. Usually a notable tailwind, it won't play much of a factor in portfolios.

It has been revealed that on a positive note, Home Depot and Lowe's are quality companies that are on solid financial footing. They can weather the storm. Investors have to figure out, however, if they're willing to wait for the situation to improve.

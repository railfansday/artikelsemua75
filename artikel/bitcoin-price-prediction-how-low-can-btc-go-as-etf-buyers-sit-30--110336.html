📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Bitcoin recently climbed back to about $60,200 after briefly dipping below $56,000. The coin is now down more than half from its all-time high, and the daily chart still reveals no confirmed bottom. Most of the selling has come from spot ETF investors, many of whom have been losing money for most of the year. At the time of writing, Bitcoin Price s trading near $58,700.

ETF Buyers Hit Their Breakeven and Sold.

The average buyer of US spot Bitcoin ETFs paid about $84,000. Since February, Bitcoin has traded above that price on only eight days, all in late September and early October. The rally stopped right at that level. (Related: LA Noire 2 leak resurfaces after source gets GTA 6 endi...)

Leverage Keeps Pushing Prices Lower.

In a recent development, even after Friday’s liquidations, open futures positions are almost as high as they were on October 20. On nearly every day since then, long traders have been paying short traders to keep their bets open. Each upcoming drop forces more longs out, and that pushes prices down further.

The Fed Is Only Part of the Story.

According to recent reports, US yields hit their highest level in more than 20 years in early October, and Bitcoin has usually been lower a month after past rate hikes. But the crash only started later, and yields have eased slightly since October 20. Based on how closely Bitcoin has tracked stocks, the dollar and yields over the past year, those three would explain a fall of about 15% to 17%. Bitcoin was down 29% by Friday’s close. The rest came from selling inside crypto: ETF holders cutting losses and leveraged traders being forced out.

Industry observers note that at each of Bitcoin’s last four major lows, including the July low, the bottom came within about three weeks of peak ETF selling. Sometimes it came just before the peak and sometimes just after. Four cases are too few to call it a rule, but the idea is simple: once sellers who want out are out, the main pressure eases.

Four-week ETF selling is still close to its heaviest level since the July low, so that turn hasn’t come yet. Bitcoin has, however, moved back above the July low after briefly breaking below it.

How Low Can Bitcoin Go: Key Levels to Watch.

It has been revealed that the daily chart still has no confirmed low. The decline from the September high has no clear structure. A relief bounce is overdue, and the area to watch for it is $66,000 to $79,000. That is a zone where a bounce could stall, not a price target.

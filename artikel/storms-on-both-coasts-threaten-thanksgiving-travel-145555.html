📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, powerful storms bringing high winds and the potential for flooding could seriously disrupt commercial air travel in the U.S. during the single busiest week of the year.

Forecasters are warning that storm systems will likely strike parts of the South, Mid-Atlantic, and Northeast, as well as Northern California and the Pacific Northwest. These regions are home to some of the nation’s busiest airports, through which millions of American travelers will pass this week en route to visit family and friends for Thanksgiving on Thursday.

It has been revealed that in the Northeast, the remnants of a weakened, disorganized system were still clearing out on Monday. A much stronger storm is expected to develop in the Gulf of Mexico between Monday and Tuesday, impact parts of the Southeast and Mid-Atlantic Wednesday, and slam into the New York metro area and New England on Thanksgiving Day. According to an advisory from the National Weather Service’s Weather Prediction Center, strong wind gusts up to 50 mph will be felt from the Carolinas to Maine. Flooding and beach erosion could be seen in coastal areas due to the already high tides.

In a recent development, WPC has initiated Key Messages for a Thanksgiving Coastal Storm that is expected to impact the U.S. East Coast. pic.twitter.com/pdQuU4U4yG.

Americans traveling from, through, or to these areas should prepare for the possibility of flooded roads, damage from high winds, and power outages, the agency stated.

NYC Emergency Management said Sunday that the storm could bring gusts of 40 to 45 mph and about one to two inches of rain to the city.

Recent findings suggest that for Newly introduced York and New England, the storm will be at its strongest Thursday evening. Parts of the Adirondacks and northern New England could see ice and snow mixed in with rain. (Related: Latest Update: How to train a newly introduced language...)

Industry observers note that in California, Oregon, and Washington, residents still recovering from flooding last week could be hit again by rain and strong winds. A newly introduced system is expected to move in Tuesday and Wednesday; combined with the “king tides,” the storm could cause flooding up and down the West Coast.

Recent findings suggest that the impact is expected to be most severe between Portland, Oregon, and San Francisco. San Francisco’s mayor said residents should take the forecasts seriously and consider not traveling for the holiday.

The Sierra Nevada, meanwhile, will see fresh snow, meteorologists noted.

Over six million people are expected to fly within the U.S. this week for Thanksgiving. The surge is already underway, with many carriers adding flights to accommodate the increased traffic. American Airlines earlier this month projected its busiest Thanksgiving week since before the COVID-19 pandemic; that prediction could hold true for other U.S. airlines as well.

Modern police vehicles typically use electronic sirens that allow officers to switch between these sounds. Older mechanical sirens use a spinning mechanism to produce a traditional wail.

Sources indicate that officers also adjust their sirens based on traffic conditions. Faster tones can help get drivers’ attention in heavy traffic, while a slower wail may work on quieter roads. Departments set their own policies for siren use, so there isn’t one standard sound for daytime or nighttime driving.

Regardless of the siren sound, drivers should respond the same way when an emergency vehicle approaches. Drivers should check their mirrors, signal, and safely pull toward the right side of the road to let the police vehicle pass. They should avoid sudden braking or stopping in an intersection.

Sources indicate that anyone already in an intersection should continue through before pulling over. Drivers waiting at a red light shouldn’t enter the intersection unless an officer directs them to do so. After the police vehicle passes, drivers should check for other emergency vehicles before returning to traffic. Specific rules may vary by state.

According to recent reports, do the remodeling jobs you can finish safely and well yourself, like tearing out old cabinets, painting, and setting tile, and hire licensed pros for wiring, gas, structural, and major plumbing work. You save what a pro would charge for the easy jobs without risking a fire, a flood, or a failed inspection on the hard ones.

You pay for every hour a contractor spends hauling out old cabinets or rolling paint, and those jobs take more time than skill.

Those are the jobs to do yourself, along with pulling up flooring and setting tile. Most towns require no permit for them, and once the power, water, and gas to the room are off, a mistake usually costs only time and materials. In a home built before 1978, have the paint tested for lead first, as the EPA recommends.

Wiring, gas lines, structural changes, roofing, and plumbing that moves pipes belong to licensed pros. Most of that work needs a permit and an inspection, and some cities let only licensed tradespeople do it. Say the labor on a $40,000 kitchen remodel bid includes $1,500 to tear out the old cabinets and floor, $700 to paint, and $500 to tile the backsplash. Keep the electrician and plumber for the rough-in, when the fresh wiring and pipes go inside the walls. Do those three jobs yourself, and after about $500 for a dumpster and tools, you save about $2,200 for a few weekends of work.

It has been revealed that wiring or plumbing you do without the permit it needed can cost more later. An insurer may refuse a claim the work caused, and a late permit before a sale may mean opening finished walls for an inspector.

Ask each contractor who bids to price demolition, painting, and finish work as separate lines, so you know what each job you take on saves.

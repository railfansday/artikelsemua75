Industry observers note that you're probably familiar with Virgin River as the hit Netflix show that promises to never end. Netflix recently renewed Virgin River for season 9, and nobody was particularly surprised; the show is perfect for the streamer. It's not just because the show is pretty cheap; it's also incredibly well-made, the character arcs are tremendous, and the acting's great too. Little wonder Virgin River tends to dominate Netflix's streaming charts.

Virgin River is, of course, based on a hit book series by Robyn Carr. A new edition is officially being released in March, with beautiful sprayed edges and an updated cover. It's perfect for modern collectors, among whom this kind of edition has become something of a prized possession. Even better, there's an exclusive Q&A and a letter to readers, providing additional value. You can pre-order a copy direct from HarperCollins now. Recent findings suggest that it doesn't really matter which couple you love in Virgin River; if you're a fan of the TV show, the books are essential. ​​​​​​​But they're also very different to the show, because only the first book centers around Mel and Jack. The show picks and chooses between characters and storylines, and often adds a lot of newly introduced ones into the mix based on character dynamics, budgets, or simply cool ideas. Season 5's forest fire was heavily adapted from book 5, but amped up the drama significantly. (Related: Show, Don't Certify: How to Prepare for Your First Data...)

That said, they both start the same way: the recently widowed Melinda Monroe sees an ad for a mid-wife at Virgin River, and accepts the post. She's plunged into a whole new life, one filled with drama and romance, and eventually falls in love with the town. The second book goes well beyond this, though, with Preacher's encounter with Paige and her young son, who are on the run from Paige's abusive ex-husband. All this will be familiar, but subtly different, making the books a delightful treat if you haven't explored them before.

That remarked, go in with your eyes wide open. Mel's tragic backstory has changed a little; in the books, her husband died during a robbery-gone-wrong at a convenience store. He died before they were able to have the children they wanted, while Netflix switched this up; they had Mel and her husband uncertain because of several attempted pregnancies. Charmaine's story is a lot more dramatic on Netflix (she has a much bigger role there), and the romantic tone itself is subtly different. Consider this a very different vision of the wonderful romance story.

Recent findings suggest that the first collector's edition comes out in March, although it's right now only available from the publisher; expect other retailers to show it soon. This will surely be the beginning of an entire range, as HarperCollins celebrate Virgin River in the best way. Collectors' editions are all the rage nowadays, and everyone loves to decorate their shelves with them. That's particularly with romance and romantasy, where my own wife has a very proud display. I suspect this will become one of them.

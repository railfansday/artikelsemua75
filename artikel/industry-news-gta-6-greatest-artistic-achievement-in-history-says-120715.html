Grand Theft Auto 6 is the highest-rated game in history, only rivaled by Ocarina of Time — but no developer should try to rival its scope and ambition.

We don't know what exactly the budget was, but Rockstar head of publishing Jennifer Kolbe recently pushed back on rumors of a $2 billion price tag, clarifying it cost somewhere under $1.5 billion. Most studios don't have that kind of change to throw around, because most studios aren't printing money with a game like GTA Online.

Indeed, Cyberpunk 2 creative director Igor Sarzynski has made it clear that CD Projekt Red has no intention of rivaling GTA 6, despite the broad-stroke similarities between both series.

"GTA 6-induced-open-world-game-dev-depression is real," he mused on Bluesky. "Probably the single greatest artistic achievement in history, I mean it. Scale, density, coherence, thoughtfulness of it all, just a technological and creative miracle. Cyberpunk 2 is canceled, imma go sweep autumn leaves in my backyard now. Industry observers note that "Seriously though I think we're doing something of our own, with a different unique flavor," he continued. "GTA is impressive in a way all great art is, it expands what is possible, a captivating vision flawlessly executed.". (Related: How to generate text: using different decoding methods ...)

It's a sentiment echoed by others in the games' industry, like Larian Studios head of publishing Michael Douse, better known by his online moniker - Very AFK.

"Maybe in a world that often feels like it's going to sh*t it is nice for everyone to enjoy a cultural moment together that brings every audience (and every generation) the same excitement, behind something that has turned out to be genuinely brilliant," he posted to Twitter. "Not every game needs to be GTA, and that both makes space for other types of experiences, and the rare moment even more special!".

Naughty Dog game designer Michael Barclay even mentioned that GTA 6 appears to him "as a Bugatti Veyron would to a nervous Victorian child.".

Considering, just three years ago, the industry warned that Baldur's Gate 3 was the game no other developer should try to replicate, given the unbelievable scope and sheer amount of interactivity, recorded dialogue, and branching paths it offered to players, that's one hell of an endorsement.

TheGamer's own Lead News Editor, George Foster, put it bluntly in his GTA 6 review, "we'll never get a game like it again," and frankly, we shouldn't.

Ballooning development budgets are already putting a target on studios' backs, making every single latest release a financial gamble that could mean mass layoffs or even closure, and those high-stakes are only making the industry increasingly risk-averse.

GTA 6 shouldn't set a new bar for other developers to strive for. The unimaginable amount of resources, personnel, and time it took to develop the game is simply unrealistic for other studios. Thankfully, CDPR understands that. Let's just hope the rest of the industry does, too.

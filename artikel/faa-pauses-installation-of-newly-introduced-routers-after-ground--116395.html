FAA pauses installation of newly introduced routers after ground stop

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Federal Aviation Administration said Tuesday it would pause the installation of fresh routers after problems with the technology led the agency to issue a rare nationwide ground stop on Friday.

Tens of thousands of flights were affected by the ground stop, which halted departing flights for more than four hours. It was the longest halt to U.S. flight departures since 9/11.

Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy noted Sunday that an overloaded router installed in southern Arkansas as part of an upgrade to a system overseen by L3Harris Technologies appeared to overwhelm systems that feed radar and other data for air traffic control. The Wall Street Journal first reported the news about the pause.

The FAA and Department of Transportation have said L3Harris was working to convert the communications network from copper wires to fiber optic.

Democratic members of the Senate Committee on Commerce, Science and Transportation this week asked the FAA and the Department of Transportation for clarity on what went wrong.

"Identifying the device that failed does not establish why it failed," the senators wrote in a statement. "Modernization cannot come at the expense of operational safety.".

The agencies have said they are working to figure out the root cause of the matter and have reiterated that there is no indication of a cyberattack or outside threat.

The senators also asked for a briefing with the FAA, L3Harris and Juniper Networks, which is making the newly introduced devices, by Monday. They asked the companies to lay out why the operational systems lacked sufficient redundancy and answer other questions about what led to the outage. CNBC has reached out to L3Harris and Juniper Networks for comment.

Sources indicate that duffy on Friday called for Congress to approve $50 billion in funding for upgrades to the aging U.S. air traffic control system, more than he has previously asked for. Democratic lawmakers pushed back on the request, citing unspent money from existing funding, which includes Congress' approval last year of $12.5 billion for airport and air traffic control improvements over five years.

Senate Commerce Chair Ted Cruz, R-Texas, stated Tuesday that he thinks there will be support for air traffic control modernization, but that the allocation should be specifically for safety upgrades.

Industry observers note that "I don't think the full $50 billion will be funded," he said. "Some of that is not safety related, but we just discussed there, and what I expressed is that the request needs to be focused on safety modernization.".

Duffy dismissed the idea that the router concern had to do with the introduction of artificial intelligence tools and remarked it was "completely unfounded" because the AI system is not in communication with the defective router. He added that the FBI is helping to investigate the root cause of the issue.

Aging equipment and a shortage of air traffic controllers have for years angered airline executives, who have complained about the cost of resulting disruptions.

— CNBC's Phil LeBeau, Leslie Josephs and Justin Papp contributed to this report.

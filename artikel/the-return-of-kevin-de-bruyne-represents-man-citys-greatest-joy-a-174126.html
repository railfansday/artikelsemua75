The return of Kevin De Bruyne represents Man City’s greatest joy and a major fear

If there has been a sense of eras ending at Manchester City in recent months, this genuinely might represent the last time, a goodbye to something they will always deem golden and glorious. Kevin De Bruyne will play at the Etihad Stadium.

It is the third such game that can be billed as his final outing at a ground he graced for a decade. One was a fitting farewell, the other not. Napoli’s Champions League campaigns have taken De Bruyne back to Manchester in successive autumns. But last year, his evening ended early: Giovanni Di Lorenzo was sent off, Antonio Conte needed to sacrifice someone to bring on an extra defender and a 34-year-old was the logical candidate. Conte was apologetic but reflected: “The destiny, it happened like that.”. (Related: Estate Planning for Early Retirement: The FIRE Guide...)

A few months earlier, De Bruyne had taken his leave from City in tears, with a light show, a guard of honour, the promise of a statue. Then, too, his involvement was curtailed by a red card, to Mateo Kovacic, but not by much. It did not take the shine off his evening. It could not camouflage a colossal contribution over a decade that brought 108 goals, 170 assists, two trebles and 19 trophies. Chairman Khaldoon Al Mubarak described him as City’s greatest ever player and, regardless of how many goals Erling Haaland scores, he may forever retain that tag; especially, of course, if City’s days of Champions League football and wonderful footballers face an abrupt end. (Related: Industry News: GTA 6 player hit with 30-day PSN ban aft...)

The De Bruyne years now look tarnished by the 114 guilty verdicts to the charges the Premier League brought over breaking financial rules; not in his view, though. “My feelings have never been different,” noted the former City captain. “This is my club, I love the owners. I have nothing but affection for this club, and that will never change.”.

Recent findings suggest that he added an element of intrigue by arguing: “Of course, in life there are rules, but everybody plays on the limit, I would say.” So far, the view is that City broke them. One possibility is that they are stripped of some of their titles. In De Bruyne’s mind, he will remain a six-time Premier League champion. “I played in between the white lines, and I competed with other players, and I had many experiences with my teams, with the players, so for me that's the thing that I value most,” he mentioned. “I've given my all, and that's not going to change in my mind.”.

Whatever the possible sanctions, De Bruyne did not flee because of the possibility of a punishment: he wanted another contract in 2025, partly for family reasons, and instead joined Napoli when City opted against renewing his deal.

De Bruyne brought beauty on the pitch; his passing range may be unrivalled in City’s history, and almost unparalleled in anyone else’s. Few others could whip, curl and place a ball with such ambition. Some of his assists were outlandish in their brilliance.

“I don't have to say how much of a special player he is,” mentioned Jeremy Doku, both a former and current teammate. “I played with him at City and still playing with him for the national team. Fun to play with him, the quality he has is unbelievable. We will all be happy to see him. Unfortunately, he didn't play a lot the last time after the red card, but excited to play against him and happy that I can still play with him.".

As Doku can testify, even a De Bruyne in his dotage can seem unstoppable. The winger was on the same Belgium side for a tour de force against Newly introduced Zealand in the World Cup, a day before De Bruyne turned 35. It is less than two weeks since the veteran scored from Doku’s pass against Turkey. Three days later, De Bruyne found Doku when the latter scored in the demolition of Italy.

Recent findings suggest that de Bruyne may be an glitch for Italians outside Roberto Mancini’s Azzurri. Massimiliano Allegri has deployed him in a part-time role as a substitute and has tried using De Bruyne in a deeper position. “He doesn’t like it very much,” the manager admitted last month. Allegri may want him to be a regista; De Bruyne prefers to be a trequartista.

According to recent reports, “He is an extraordinary player, and in my view, if he's working as a playmaker in that midfield pairing, he's even better,” the manager stated recently. “I told him he can extend his career by another couple of years if he plays in that position.”.

The alternative theory is that if his career is extended, it will be at PSV Eindhoven; while Allegri has lavished the midfielder with praise, there are suggestions there will be a parting of the ways in January. “We have not spoken about that,” mentioned Allegri, focusing on the here and now. Perhaps Napoli, like City, will have a farewell of sorts soon. The game itself may not.

Sources indicate that “Kevin is someone that likes football. Even at this age he loves it,” mentioned Doku. De Bruyne has been playing professional football for half his life, for over 800 games. Along with Belgium, City have seen the best of him. Now they may see him once more.

It offers a comparison with Rayan Cherki and Phil Foden, the No 10s who are his successors. It was to Cherki’s credit that City did not really miss De Bruyne last season. And yet few passers will ever reach the level he did; in their Champions League-winning season of 2022-23, De Bruyne registered 28 assists in all competitions. It promises to be a night of nostalgia for City, a reminder of what they have lost, and what they may lose.

Industry observers note that “I don't think a lot of people will change in their mind how they view me as a player,” said De Bruyne. “Other people may be disgraced a bit, but I can't change anything about that.” The disgrace was never his. More than virtually any other, he graced City.

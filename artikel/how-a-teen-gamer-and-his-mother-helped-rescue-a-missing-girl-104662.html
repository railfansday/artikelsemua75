Sixteen-year-old Landon Tresp was in the bedroom of his family's Colorado mountain home, playing an online game after finishing his daily chores, when he began chatting on his laptop with another player. (Related: Nebraska voters appear to reject online sports betting ...)

She was also a teenager, and the pair "kind of just clicked," he tells the BBC.

He told her he liked her name, and she confessed that it was an alias. She was a runaway, she said - and was being held against her will, hundreds of miles from home, though she didn't know exactly where. The girl, 17, and had been missing for more than a year. Sources indicate that stunned, Landon felt "helpless," he says - and, after learning more details over two weeks of chatting, he decided to tell his mother, Khristina on 31 October this year. The 41-year-old nurse and mother of five was putting Halloween makeup on her younger children for trick-or-treating when he briefed her.

That set the mother and son on a path to track down his friend, contact her mother, cooperate with the FBI - and ultimately witness the rescue of the teen - only 24 hours after Landon first alerted Khristina to her plight.

"She had one opportunity to reach out her hand and ask for help, and she chose me," Khristina indicates of the girl, who was 15 when she went missing.

A screenshot shows Khristina on a call with the girl as she showed her the knife her alleged captor kept under his pillow.

The Tresps have shared with BBC screenshots of communication with the girl and a photo matching the criminal complaint to corroborate their story.

Landon first told his mother about "Jenny" - whose identity the BBC is protecting - in mid-October, a few weeks before the rescue.

"He had mentioned something about maybe a week or so into talking to her that he met a girl online, and she was really sweet," indicates Khristina - who cautioned him that "you don't always know who's on the other side of the screen".

"She's just having a really hard time and looking for a friend," he told her.

But Landon was becoming increasingly concerned as Jenny told him more about her situation.

They'd met playing Among Us, a game Landon describes as "kind of like online space Clue".

After telling Landon that she was a runaway, "Jenny" remarked an older man had taken her across state lines and wouldn't let her leave. She was the subject of a missing person investigation, and she sent Landon links to stories about her disappearance. But she believed police were no longer looking for her.

Jenny was scared and desperate, and that's why Landon went to his mother.

Her maternal instincts on high alert, Khristina contacted Jenny herself on Discord, the encrypted platform.

During the conversation, Khristina said she told the Colorado mother how she'd been through "years of abuse in her life and lots and lots of problems".

Within hours, Jenny started calling Khristina "mama".

Among Us supports cross platform play online with 4-15 players on various devices.

The Colorado mother knew things were serious when she read the missing persons articles. Jenny knew she was in Oregon and gave the name of a city, but she didn't know where she was being held exactly. Khristina knew she needed more. She told Jenny to find a piece of mail or another document in the house that had a full name on it, which the girl did.

"That's when I went to work," Khristina notes, delving into tax and property records.

In a recent development, khristina narrowed down addresses, then searched them on a real estate site to find pictures. She indicated the photos to Jenny, who confirmed that the second one was where she was being held.

The "next thing she asked me was to please not tell anyone until [her captor] was gone - that he did have weapons in the house and she was very scared," Khristina noted.

Jenny promised Khristina she'd call her back through the game platform when he left the next morning, and in the meantime Khristina began looking for the law enforcement agency that had been assigned to the missing teen's case. She contacted Jenny's mother on social media, spoke briefly to her on the phone and then got a call from an FBI agent.

"Have you seen her alive?'" the agent asked Khristina.

"And I remarked: 'I have; I video chatted with her, I've seen her, and she goes 'Oh my God, I cannot believe any of this'," Khristina recalled.

When Jenny called Khristina back after her alleged captor left home, the FBI monitored their conversation. According to the criminal complaint's record of the conversation, Jenny explained how she'd met Adam Hunter Harmel on social media in May 2025.

He'd told her he was disabled and his hips were "messed up," offering Jenny money if she'd help get him to Oregon.

Sources indicate that after he picked her up in Utah, however, Harmel threw her phone into a river, drove her to Oregon and kept her in the basement. He forced her to sleep against the wall in his bed, with a knife under his pillow, to prevent her from escaping. He gave Jenny a cell phone with no ability to make calls, but it could connect to wifi, which is how she played the online game.

The 39-year-old "would become violent and aggressive", the document says, which included hitting her and sexually assaulting her.

He also allegedly forced Jenny to remove her braces with pliers so she wouldn't be identifiable.

As Khristina and the FBI heard all of this, law enforcement were staging to bring Jenny to safety, after being alerted to her whereabouts.

While Harmel was out of the home, officers were sent to the street and the address, and Khristina was prepping Jenny for her run to freedom.

She'd unsuccessfully tried escaping before, she told Khristina.

Khristina could hear other people in the home with Jenny, and she was afraid that someone would catch wind of what was happening and harm the girl.

But she kept any of those fears from Jenny.

The mother and son watched breathlessly as Jenny ran into the arms of a state trooper.

Harmel is now in federal custody and has been charged in a criminal complaint with transportation with intent to engage in criminal sexual activity. Local Oregon media also reported he'd been accused of sexually abusing a six-year-old in the months before Jenny disappeared - and a sexual abuse protective order was granted against him in March 2025.

He remains behind bars awaiting trial.

A lawyer for the defendant did not respond to a BBC request for comment.

Jenny, meanwhile, remains in daily communication with Khristina and Landon - who still enjoys playing online games with her.

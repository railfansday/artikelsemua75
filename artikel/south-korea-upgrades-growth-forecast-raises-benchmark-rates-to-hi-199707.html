South Korea's central bank on Thursday hiked benchmark lending rates to their highest in two years, as inflation continues to run above target while a booming economy allows it room to tighten policy.

The central bank raised rates by 25 basis points to 3.25% for its final meeting of the year, in line with expectations from economists, data on the Bank of Korea's website showed.

Inflation is expected to remain above its 2% target level for a considerable time, the BOK added in its statement, saying this was due to the pass-through of elevated cost pressures and as demand-side pressures also gradually strengthen, driven by stronger economic growth. Inflation in the country stood at 2.8% in October. (Related: Hugging Face and VirusTotal collaborate to strengthen A...)

The BOK also upgraded its growth forecasts for 2026 to 3.9% for 2026 and 4.1% for 2027, up from the 3.3% and 2.9%, respectively, forecast in August.

South Korea's third-quarter GDP grew by 3.4% year on year, beating expectations, allowing the central bank room to raise rates.

"The projections of high growth rates for next year and beyond also reflect the significantly increasing weight of the IT sector as a growth engine for the Korean economy," BOK Governor Shin Hyun-song remarked at a press conference following the decision. Operating profits in the semiconductor sector could reach 700 trillion won, Shin told reporters, according to local news agency Yonhap.

Shin also signaled that more rate hikes, although he did not give a fixed timeline.

"While the domestic economy is expected to continue its robust growth, inflation is forecast to remain above the target level for a considerable time ... The Board, therefore, sees it warranted to continue its policy response until there is confidence that inflation will converge stably to the target level.".

Gareth Leather, senior Asia Economist at Capital Economics noted there is reason to believe that the BOK's tightening cycle was drawing to a close.

The BOK has raised rates by 75 basis points in five months, as higher energy prices from the Iran war and the AI boom has pushed up inflation in the country.

Leather noted that the country's strong growth, as well as a strengthening won could ease concerns over inflation.

While rapidly rising property prices could strengthen the case for a prolonged tightening cycle, Leather mentioned that the BOK was "more likely to favour macroprudential measures over further rate hikes to address its financial stability concerns.".

After the decision, the country's benchmark Kospi rose 1.88%.

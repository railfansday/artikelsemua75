Anthropic (ANT) closed its Nasdaq debut at $97.17, up 17.1% from its $83 IPO price, after opening at $111.50.

The record-breaking IPO raised $81.3 billion, briefly pushing Anthropic's market valuation above $2 trillion during early trading. (Related: The Reformer - Pushing the limits of language modeling...)

The first-day rally faded significantly, raising questions about whether investors should buy ANT now or wait for a pullback.

According to recent reports, anthropic (ANT) stock made a historic Wall Street debut, but its first trading session also revealed how promptly investor enthusiasm can cool. Shares of the Claude AI developer surged more than 34% at the opening before surrendering much of those gains and closing 17% above the IPO price. The record offering has put Anthropic among the world’s most valuable companies, but investors entering after the debut face a different question from IPO buyers: Is ANT stock still attractive at its current valuation, or has the initial AI excitement already been priced in? Anthropic stock opened at $111.50, significantly above its $83 IPO price, before briefly climbing to approximately $115. The stock later fell as investors took profits, closing its first trading session at $97.17.

Despite the pullback from its opening price, ANT finished the day 17.1% above the IPO price, giving the company a market valuation of roughly $1.7 trillion based on fully diluted shares.

Recent findings suggest that anthropic raised approximately $81.3 billion in its public offering, making it the largest IPO in history. Strong demand also pushed the company to increase the number of shares offered and price the IPO above its initial expected range.

Anthropic’s IPO valuation is built on rapid revenue growth and increasing enterprise adoption. The company reported $17 billion in quarterly revenue for Q3 2026, compared with $1.4 billion in the same quarter a year earlier, reflecting explosive demand for its AI products.

Its revenue run rate reached approximately $81 billion as of October 2026, according to its IPO materials. Enterprise customers, Claude’s coding capabilities and growing AI deployment across businesses have become key drivers of the expansion.

Sources indicate that however, growth is not translating into smooth profitability. Anthropic’s operating loss reached $567 million in Q3, while compute and infrastructure expenses rose sharply as the tech giant expanded capacity to meet demand.

The first-day rally makes Anthropic an attractive story for investors seeking exposure to the AI industry, but the sharp difference between its opening and closing prices highlights the volatility investors may face.

Buying immediately after a 17% debut gain carries the risk of further profit-taking, particularly as the market begins evaluating Anthropic against its financial performance rather than IPO excitement.

Recent findings suggest that the company’s nearly $900 billion in long-term compute and infrastructure commitments also raises questions about capital requirements, execution and future cash flow. While these investments could support its competitive position, they also create substantial financial obligations.

Anthropic’s debut has established an initial trading range, with $83 serving as the IPO price benchmark and $97.17 marking the first closing price.

The stock’s next direction will likely depend on whether buyers can maintain demand following the initial surge.

Investors should also watch the tech giant’s upcoming earnings disclosures, revenue growth, operating margins and infrastructure spending.

Anthropic’s historic IPO has confirmed strong investor appetite for frontier AI companies, but the first-day rally has also created a more demanding entry point.

Recent findings suggest that for investors who missed the IPO, chasing the first-day rally may carry greater risk. Waiting for ANT stock to establish a trading range and for its first public earnings report could provide a clearer picture of its valuation and growth trajectory.

Recent findings suggest that arm provides the energy-efficient architecture used in almost every smartphone, while Marvell specializes in high-speed networking and data center infrastructure. Both companies are positioned to benefit from the rising demand for accelerated computing, yet they offer distinct risk profiles and valuation levels. In a recent development, in the rapidly evolving world of semiconductor stocks, Arm is unique because it did not actually manufacture chips historically. Instead, it designs the primary architecture and licenses its intellectual property. Its energy-efficient designs are in over 99% of smartphones, although the organization is now expanding into cloud data centers and automotive applications, and intends to produce its own chips at last.

According to recent reports, the company has shifted toward more complete chip designs to command higher royalty rates. This model creates a high-margin recurring revenue stream. Since it serves almost every major chipmaker, it sits at the heart of the tech sector.

According to recent reports, in the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached $5.5 billion, representing growth of 23.8% compared to the prior year. This expansion is supported by the adoption of its latest v9 architecture, which carries higher royalty rates than previous generations. The company reported net income of $936.0 million during this period.

As of its March 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 0.1x. This ratio compares total debt to shareholder equity to help you understand how much a company relies on borrowing. The current ratio, which measures the ability to pay short-term obligations with current assets, was a robust 6.0x.

It has been revealed that in the fiscal year ended March 31, 2026, free cash flow reached $979.0 million. Free cash flow is the cash remaining after a business pays for operations and equipment. Note that stock-based compensation (SBC) represented roughly 69% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

It has been revealed that marvell focuses on the infrastructure that makes modern data centers and telecommunications networks possible. The organization specializes in data processing units, networking silicon, and storage controllers. Its recent growth is driven by the need for high-speed connectivity between servers in artificial intelligence clusters.

It has been revealed that the tech giant faces significant customer concentration, as its ten largest customers represented roughly 82% of net revenue in its latest annual report. One distributor accounted for approximately 37% of sales, which adds a layer of risk to the business. Despite this, its specialized silicon is vital for major cloud service providers.

In the 12 months ended Aug. 1, 2026, revenue reached $9.5 billion. This reflects a growth rate of 30.6% over the previous twelve-month period. During this same window, the tech giant generated net income of $2.6 billion, showing significant improvement from previous years.

Recent findings suggest that as of its January 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 0.3x, indicating a low reliance on debt. The current ratio stood at roughly 2.0x, indicating a healthy ability to meet short-term financial commitments.

Recent findings suggest that in the fiscal year ended Jan. 31, 2026, free cash flow was $1.4 billion. Note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 33.8% of operating cash flow.

Industry observers note that arm faces risks related to the concentration of the smartphone market. If global phone sales slow, its royalty revenue could stagnate. The manufacturer also competes with open-source alternatives like RISC-V, which some manufacturers may prefer to avoid licensing fees. Furthermore, its heavy reliance on the licensing model means any loss of key partnerships with giants like Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) or Qualcomm (QCOM -7.45%) could impact its long-term growth prospects.

Sources indicate that marvell Technology deals with significant customer concentration and geopolitical uncertainty. Trade restrictions and export controls regarding China have created supply chain challenges and impacted demand. The firm is also navigating a massive $115 billion all-stock acquisition of Lumentum Holdings (LITE -13.94%), which carries integration risks and potential shareholder dilution. Additionally, it recently settled patent litigation with Credo Technology Group Holding (CRDO -15.20%), highlighting the constant legal threats in the specialized silicon market.

Marvell Technology appears more attractive on a valuation basis, offering lower Forward P/E and P/S ratio multiples than Arm.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

For investors seeking exposure to the artificial intelligence boom, Arm and Marvell are two compelling stocks to own. Between the two, I would prioritize picking up shares of Marvell going into 2027.

Marvell sports a much lower share price valuation than Arm, providing a greater opportunity for a superior return. Arm arguably deserves the elevated valuation multiples because of its high-margin licensing moat. However, given its foray into making its own semiconductor chips, it risks compressing those robust margins due to manufacturing costs.

Marvell's stock was beaten down after it announced it was acquiring Lumentum. I think the acquisition is a smart move. Lumentum provides high-speed optical capabilities for transferring massive amounts of data, a key competitive advantage to winning AI customers. The appeal of Lumentum's solutions is illustrated by its sales of $1.3 billion for its fiscal first quarter, ended Sept. 26, which represented 137% year-over-year growth.

Moreover, Marvell's business is exploding. It generated revenue of $8.2 billion in its 2026 fiscal year, ended Jan. 31, 2026, and the company expects sales to soar to a range between $70 billion to $90 billion by its 2031 fiscal year. That growth does not include Lumentum's sales. With a lower valuation and spectacular, multi-year growth ahead, Marvell looks like the better stock to buy now.

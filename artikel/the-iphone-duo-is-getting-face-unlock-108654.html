The iPhone Duo Is Getting Face Unlock

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Apple dropped iOS 27.2.1 last week, patching a handful of security vulnerabilities and bugs—including two iMessage flaws that let people read messages they shouldn't. But while most of us are running this version (or should install it as soon as possible), others are running something different—iOS 27.3.

Apple is currently beta testing the third major revision in the iOS 27 era, and anyone—developer and public tester alike—is free to install it at their leisure. With it, comes the return of the Health app, vertical Safari tabs, and Siri AI app actions, among other new features. But for users of Apple's first foldable, the iPhone Duo, iOS 27.3 brings two rather notable changes—both of which are for the better. With the second iOS 27.3 developer beta, Apple is introducing face unlock for the iPhone Duo. The foldable famously ships with Touch ID, the first upcoming iPhone since the third-gen iPhone SE to do so. While that isn't a problem per se, many of us are used to unlocking our iPhones with our faces. This revision just lets new Duo owners keep up with that muscle memory. (Related: Industry News: “Determined” woman completes 7 marathons...)

Recent findings suggest that now, take note: This isn't Face ID. No software update can introduce new hardware, so the iPhone Duo still doesn't have the camera tech required to power Apple's Face ID system. As such, the Duo's face unlock is likely not as secure as Face ID on other iPhones. Perhaps that's why your iPhone Duo will require Touch ID for Apple Pay and passwords, or after 45 minutes of inactivity. This feature seems to be one of pure convenience: If you want to be able to unlock your Duo with your face (at least, some of the time), you can.

What's more, this isn't a default setting. If you're running the beta, or if you're reading this after iOS 27.3 drops, you can find the option in Settings > Touch ID & Passcode.

Apple made a big deal about the continuity involved with opening and closing the iPhone Duo. As you do, your content moves with you seamlessly—a very Apple thing to do. That remarked, you might expect folding the Duo to lock it, but it doesn't. You need to hit the side button to actually put the phone to sleep.

In a recent development, iOS 27.3 changes that. If you so choose, you can enable a setting to lock your Duo whenever you close it. That won't work for everyone, of course—some users may still want to move between modes without worrying about locking their Duos. But for those who would rather lock their devices whenever they fold them up, the newly introduced option is under Settings > Display & Brightness.

“Do I think it would be a good idea? Yes.”.

That’s what Elon Musk mentioned about the idea to merge Tesla and SpaceX, back in October 2026.

Since then, however, it would seem that this somewhat fanciful idea has only accelerated. Over the last few weeks alone, both Tesla and SpaceX have constituted special exploratory committees consisting of independent directors.

Will it go through? Probably, yes. After all, despite the CEO of SpaceX and Tesla claiming that he is a “conflicted party,” his companies’ shareholders have often acceded to his wishes. That’s an opinion Walter Isaacson, author of “Elon Musk,” shares too. (Related: Industry News: Supermicro sued by shareholders over cla...)

Especially after the entrepreneur made his case by stating,.

…there’s a lot of merit to combining the companies as we are pretty much in the Super Intelligence singularity.

Needless to say, such a merger will have some legal, regulatory, and financial implications. But what about the crypto sector?

Tesla, Inc., the automotive and clean energy organization, holds 11,509 BTC valued at over $747 million right now. Similarly, SpaceX, the aerospace manufacturer, holds 18,712 BTC worth over $1.22 billion. (Related: Tech Report: Intel 18A yields hit 70% as organization s...)

Industry observers note that in simple terms, the merger will simply be a transaction that pools in digital assets owned by both these entities into a single balance sheet. This consolidation would allow the merged entity to hold 30,221 BTC in reserves.

Recent findings suggest that those are significant numbers. However, here’s where it’s also worth looking at the context and scale and the bigger picture.

According to recent reports, neither Tesla nor SpaceX are crypto companies. They are primarily tech manufacturing companies with valuations of $1.54 trillion and $1.89 trillion, respectively.

Yes, they have crypto holdings, but they aren’t significant enough for each individual entity to suggest that these are more than a passing interest and a diversification opportunity.

To put it in financial terms, for both Tesla and SpaceX, Bitcoin is a non-core balance sheet asset. For them, their BTC holdings are financially immaterial to their core CapEx. As Dr. Andrei Belonogov, Managing Partner at TechAccountingPro pointed out,.

The size of treasuries is relatively small compared to the company’s debt maturities and non-cancellable commitments.

Sources indicate that this will remain the case if the merger does go through. If and when it happens, the merged companies’ valuation would dwarf the value of its crypto holdings.

According to recent reports, what about after, though? This is an crucial question because more often than not, non-core balance sheet assets go through a period of evaluation and restructuring and sometimes, disposal too to streamline operations and facilitate profitability.

According to recent reports, could this happen to the aforementioned Bitcoin holdings then? Well, that’s always a possibility. However, recent history suggests the odds are low.

Consider the purchase and sale history of both these companies, for instance. The last time Tesla sold any of its Bitcoin holdings was back in 2022. Since then, it has bought additional BTC while also holding on to the stash it already had.

Sources indicate that similarly, SpaceX last sold any of its holdings back in 2024. In the two years since, the aerospace manufacturer has only bought Bitcoin. Specifically, over 10K BTC in May 2026.

Industry observers note that now, it’s up for debate how much input the “conflicted party,” Musk, had in any of these sales or purchases. However, it is safe to assume that, given the consistencies between the two boardrooms so far, that will remain the case after the merger too.

What about later though? Well, MetaWin COO Rory Cartwright told AMBCrypto,.

Sources indicate that the merger doesn’t necessarily mean a sell-off or any spike in price. What it will do, however, is put a roughly $2bn position into a single manufacturer’s balance sheet. Bitcoin can be especially volatile, so that may make some investors seasick.

Industry observers note that will the merged entity enjoy the same degree of flexibility Tesla and SpaceX enjoyed individually when it comes to crypto sales and purchases though? According to Kevin Constantine, Senior Associate at Caldwell Law,.

Protections could become relevant to cryptocurrency holdings, particularly if management’s ability to purchase, liquidate, or transfer Bitcoin raised concerns about the companies’ valuations or the treatment of shareholders.

The fact that both Tesla and SpaceX refused to sell their Bitcoin in 2026, a year that has been particularly bearish for the crypto-sector, is testament to the confidence they have in this asset. It is also a sign of institutional conviction.

After the merger, the merged entity will have the potential to be the sixth-largest corporate Bitcoin holder in the world. Only behind crypto-native firms or treasury-focused players like MicroStrategy and Metaplanet. All while Bitcoin remains its non-core balance sheet asset.

It has been revealed that that, more than anything, will be confirmation of Bitcoin’s value as a corporate treasury asset. That might not sound like much on paper, but that is still VERY bullish news.

What could be even better though? According to Chris Mack, CTO at Quote.Trade,.

If Musk came out publicly bullish on Bitcoin and crypto, that would change things. Investors would start paying attention to whether he looks to buy more or make crypto part of the business.

Sources indicate that that’s not all either. As it stands, both Tesla and SpaceX have significant investments in scaling AI infrastructure. This is likely to continue after the merger, with more capital and resources allocated to AI compute and energy grids.

In a very indirect way, such investments could have a positive impact on AI-focused crypto tokens like RENDER, TAO, and NEAR, especially in the long run.

At the time of writing, reports seemed to suggest that Tesla and SpaceX are targeting a potential deal announcement by January 2027 or even as early as December 2026.

According to recent reports, that’s an accelerated timeline, one that may or may not have anything to do with Democrats’ victory in the midterm elections. Democrats, many of whom are also planning to probe Elon Musk’s corporate ties.

Even outside the political element, though, it probably won’t be all sunshine and roses. After all, this is a merger between a major U.S. defense and Starlink contractor with an automative tech giant with a significant manufacturing and sales footprint in China.

The regulatory and legal questions involved alone will be a nightmare.

Will crypto be an irrelevant factor then? Speaking to AMBCrypto, Constantine claimed,.

Sources indicate that as it stands, a lot of things are still up in the air. It’s too early to say how Tesla and SpaceX will quell national security concerns while juggling regulatory challenges with this merger. That’s the unpredictable part.

In a recent development, what is not unpredictable is what will happen to their cryptos and the crypto sector at large if this merger goes through. Relatively speaking, that is.

‘Krillion’ is hooking Gen Z and Gen Alpha. Here’s what you need to know about the viral word game

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that it’s pretty common for a social media post or video to go viral. But for a casual, non-AAA game to go viral and capture the cultural zeitgeist is much rarer. The few instances that come to mind include the agriculture sim FarmVille, the iPhone side-scroller Flappy Bird, and the word puzzler Wordle. But now it seems we may have another one. A fresh web-based trivia game is taking Gen Z and Gen Alpha by storm. It’s called Krillion, and its deceptively simple gameplay is leaving fans addicted and already spawning dozens of clones, not to mention YouTube videos and subreddits dedicated to it. Here’s what you need to know before you attempt to be one in a krillion. What is Krillion and how do you play? Krillion is a browser-based trivia game with deceptively simple gameplay. Every day, Krillion serves up a set of seven prompts, which are the same for all users around the world. These prompts seek answers from you, which you type in using the keyboard. Today, as I write this, the prompts include: Name an animal that eats fish (documented, regular part of its diet) Name a movie that’s at least two and a half hours long (100-plus IMDb votes) Name an object that has a lens However, the prompts aren’t just seeking a correct answer; they are seeking the rarest correct answer. The rarer your correct answer is, the more points you’re awarded. For example, for the prompt “Name an object that has a lens,” if you answer “camera,” that’s correct—but it’s also obvious—so Krillion will award you only 10 points. However, if you answer “endoscope,” Krillion awards you 60 points, since that answer is “genuinely uncommon,” as the game claims. Krillion takes its name from “krill,” the shrimplike plankton some types of whales love snacking on far out at sea. The entire sea theme is evident in the game’s gameplay. The points you earn let your krill dive deeper into the sea, with each point equating to 10 meters of depth. Obvious answers allow you to descend only 100 meters, while the most uncommon but correct answers to the same prompt, which the game deems “one in a krillion,” earn you 100 points and let you descend 1,000 meters. With seven prompts, the maximum score equals a descent of 7,000 meters, which represents a perfect game. By the way, if you’re going for a one-in-a-krillion answer, don’t think you have time to Google things. Each prompt must be answered in 25 seconds, or no points are awarded. The prompt-like format of the game isn’t a product of the AI chatbot era we live in, though Krillion’s back end does sometimes leverage AI to help determine a player’s score. “Some prompts can be scored entirely mechanically against a familiarity database of how often a word or name actually turns up in what people read and write,” states Krillion’s FAQ regarding how an answer’s rarity is determined. “Others can’t be reduced to a number like that, and those get a combination of LLM assistance and a manual pass.” Why are Gen Z and Gen Alpha obsessed with Krillion? The game’s virality, especially among the school-age crowd, comes not just from its trivia gameplay, but from the fact that it isn’t looking for just a right answer; it’s looking for a right answer that no one else—or very few people—has thought of. In other words, your friends may be as intelligent as you are and able to guess a right answer, but are they as clever as you are, and able to come up with an obscure answer? Proving your cleverness—your ability to think outside the box—is a powerful draw. The game also uses a very common trick to keep people engaged. Much like a daily crossword puzzle, Krillion’s seven prompts last just 24 hours before the next seven appear. This creates a certain amount of FOMO (fear of missing out). If your friends message to say they obtained a few “one in a krillion” answers in today’s gameplay, you’re more likely to hurry to play before the prompts change. Who invented Krillion? Keeping with the sea depths theme, the answer to who invented Krillion is a little murky. As of this writing, there are numerous Krillion knockoffs on the web and in the Google Play Store. However, the original Krillion game is hosted at Krillion.io. The site’s earliest Internet Archive date is less than four months ago: August 3. A Whois lookup indicates that the domain was registered on July 18. The site’s current FAQ offers a little information about the game’s inventor, but not much: The Krillion game is run by a one-person team. That person goes by the pseudonym “kp” and describes themselves as “a 23-year-old American CS grad.”  Fast Company has reached out to kp for comment using available contact information. While Krillion’s creator’s identity may be secret for now, the game is anything but. And unless some other game rises unexpectedly from the depths in the next five weeks, Krillion seems certain to become the most viral game of 2026.

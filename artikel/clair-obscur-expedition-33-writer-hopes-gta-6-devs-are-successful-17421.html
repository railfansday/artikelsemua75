GTA 6 is without a doubt already proving successful, sitting as the highest-rated game of all time on Metacritic – but this quick success and its massive earning estimates aren't enough to curb fears of further layoffs at Rockstar Games.

The developers have spoken out about these concerns, with animation programmer Nestor Uriarte admitting he's "worried about getting laid off" even after "working on the biggest game of all time" – you know, Grand Theft Auto 6. Finance experts like Square Enix veteran Jacob Novak also stated the game's success indeed isn't enough to stop layoffs, as Rockstar has "built the biggest, most expensive game of all time and now released it.". (Related: How to Start a Side Business While Working Full-Time...)

Industry observers note that clair Obscur: Expedition 33 writer Jennifer Svedberg-Yen has something to say on the matter, too, sharing her thoughts over on Instagram. "99 Metacritic score. Multiple award nominations before it even released. Massive sales and player engagement. Incredible amounts of tangible and intangible value creation," she writes. "Yet the team is worried about layoffs. So, who receives the lion's share of the profits from their labor?". (Related: Industry News: Judge blocks construction of Trump’s arc...)

According to recent reports, sadly, as Svedberg-Yen describes, "Not the vast majority of the creative team. One of the issues that happens when people creating the value do not receive a proportionate amount of that value. In finance, we call that value extraction." With this effect in mind – one I'd argue we've unfortunately seen a good bit of over the past few years within the video game industry – she hopes that Rockstar can successfully unionize."I sincerely hope the Rockstar team is successful in unionizing," the writer concludes. "Layoffs during lulls in development cycles would work a lot better if teams were properly paid for the value they create, not just one-time, but throughout the life of the thing they created." While that's certainly true, it's unfortunately not always the reality – and that's precisely why Svedberg-Yen is rooting for unionization.

It has been revealed that just a day or so ago, she detailed how the industry "is eating itself" amid layoffs and studio closures, saying, "A lot of what's happening really breaks my heart." It's hard to not let it break your heart, honestly, as, again, it's not just Rockstar. We've reported on layoffs across various companies over recent years, ranging from Microsoft's aims to cut 3,200 employees at Xbox to BioWare's closure following the EA buyout.

Here's hoping that everyone lands on their feet at the end of it all – and that studios take notes from the likes of Nintendo's late president Satoru Iwata, who wisely said "employees who fear that they may be laid off" will never produce the same results as those who don't.

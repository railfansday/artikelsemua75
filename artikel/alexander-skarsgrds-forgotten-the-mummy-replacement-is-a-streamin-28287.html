Alexander Skarsgård has a very interesting filmography, including his latest role in Wicker, where he plays a magically constructed husband to Olivia Coleman, as well as Pillion, where he plays the charismatic leader of a queer motorcycle club. He also played a 10th-century Viking prince named Amleth in The Northman, and in Succession, he plays a confrontational, erratic tech founder. His range is beyond contemplation and is on full display for his fans.

Industry observers note that in his filmography, there’s a fascinating role that didn’t get the recognition it deserved. The role in question is Tarzan. He played the titular character in David Yates’ The Legend of Tarzan, where he starred opposite Margot Robbie in an action-adventure film not unlike Brendan Fraser's The Mummy. It could have been a franchise starterwith iconic characters as Yates attempted to reimagine and modernize the classic Edgar Rice Burroughs role, but it didn't achieve much love. We follow Tarzan, aka John, who, after moving to London, is persuaded by George Washington Williams (Samuel L. Jackson) to return to his former home in the African jungle to investigate claims of slavery. However, things take a turn when he realizes he’s just a pawn in a scheming plot. The movie has an amazing cast, including Jackson, Christopher Waltz as antagonist Leon Rom, Robbie as Tarzan’s wife Jane Clayton, Djimon Hounsou as Chief Mbonga, and many more. Despite strong performances and a story that weaves in real historical events, Tarzan received mixed reviews upon its release. It was a box office hit, earning $357.2 million worldwide against a $180 million budget. However, it was criticized for poor CGI, an underutilized cast, and a serious tone, earning a 36% Rotten Tomatoes score, with critics’ consensus noting the movie “has more on its mind than many movies starring the classic character, but that isn't enough to make up for its generic plot or sluggish pace.” However, audiences were more lenient with its 58% rating, and fans’ love is catapulting the movie to newly introduced heights.

A decade since its initial release, The Legend of Tarzan is redeeming itself on streaming as the feature has climbed to the third spot on HBO Max’s global top10 list. It only stands behind Anne Hathaway and Ewan McGregor’s The End of Oak Street, and fan favorite classic Matilda, in a list that also features The Hunger Games franchise, How to Make a Killing, Reminders of Him, and more.

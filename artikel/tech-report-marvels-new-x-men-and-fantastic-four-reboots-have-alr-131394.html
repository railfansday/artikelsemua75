📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that marvel’s Midnight universe only has a few books out right now—Midnight Fantastic Four, Midnight Spider-Man, and Midnight X-Men—and only one of them has made good on the publishing line’s core premise as being horror-centric. These are all stories reinterpreting or retconning traditional Marvel Comics lore, of course, but the promise alone of uniquely scary or ominous retellings was the hook that they were all supposedly going to abide by, and there’s an argument to be made that Midnight Spider-Man is far scarier than Midnight Fantastic Four and Midnight X-Men, with it not being a close race in the slightest.

Of course, these books have barely taken off, and it’s likely that genuine horrors are in store throughout the next couple of years. Indeed, Midnight Fantastic Four and Midnight X-Men have spent a lot more time on worldbuilding, and their payoffs could be extraordinary. That mentioned, challenges of Midnight Fantastic Four and Midnight X-Men also aren’t taking advantage of one of their greatest horror tools, whereas Midnight Spider-Man is: Marvel Comics’ cloaked covers. Sources indicate that it’s disappointing that Midnight Fantastic Four and Midnight X-Men haven’t dove fully into horror because the Midnight publishing line is deliberately meant to be a horror-centric continuity. It may be unrealistic to expect that every single concern will have something scary in it, but Midnight Spider-Man has successfully achieved that in the two issues that have been released thus far. Ultimately, it seems to boil down to the fact that Midnight Fantastic Four is more of a sci-fi mystery story, and Midnight X-Men is more of a fantasy love story. Still, it’s jarring that Midnight Spider-Man leans wholly into horror while the others don’t, or haven’t. (Related: Industry News: “Determined” woman completes 7 marathons...)

Even odder is that Midnight X-Men is specifically rooted in Earth-616 X-Men lore and history, while Midnight Fantastic Four and Midnight Spider-Man are completely distinct. Midnight X-Men’s holy war between mutantkind, vampires, and empyres has the makings of a phenomenal story, especially given how Cyclops and Jean Grey’s relationship has evolved via problem #2, but it’s difficult to argue that it’s a horror story. Likewise, Midnight Fantastic Four’s cosmic horror has laid foundations for a particularly atmospheric and eerie dread, though there hasn’t been anything particularly frightening about the series yet. Meanwhile, Midnight Spider-Man wasted no time delving into gruesome body horror.

Issue covers have the potential to illustrate how horrifying these books can be, and cloaked covers further emphasize how much scarier Midnight Spider-Man is than Midnight Fantastic Four and Midnight X-Men. Midnight books cloak their main covers in order to obscure or mask the full artwork, “only revealing themselves to readers daring enough to pick them up on stands,” as per Marvel, but Midnight Spider-Man is unsurprisingly the only one to truly capitalize on the shock and awe that the presentation of the cloaked covers permits them.

It has been revealed that for instance, the main, cloaked cover for matter #2 merely depicts Midnight Spider-Man’s Felicia Hardy in a state of fear, and it’s teased that something lurks behind her. The revealed cover shows that she’s right to be afraid, as Peter Parker’s terrifying Spider-Man is creepily nestled into the corner of the ceiling. Neither Midnight Fantastic Four nor Midnight X-Men has any such reveals yet, at least none that are anywhere near as scary, or that utilize the format as well.

It has been revealed that plus, Midnight Fantastic Four awkwardly always needs Reed Richards, Sue Storm, Johnny Storm, and Ben Grimm to be positioned vertically so that they can all be seen in this cloaked format, and X-Men’s massive ensemble has the same problem. Chiefly, it seems to boil down to Midnight Spider-Man being scarier and having the horror legs to stand on. That doesn’t mean Midnight Fantastic Four and Midnight X-Men are lesser, but it’s weird that they aren’t indulging in horror as much as they could be.

Midnight Spider-Man #3, Midnight X-Men #3, and Midnight Fantastic Four #3 are scheduled to be released on December 16, 2026.

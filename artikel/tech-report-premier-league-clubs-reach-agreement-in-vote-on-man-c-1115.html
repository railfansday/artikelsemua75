📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Premier League clubs have agreed to cover the costs of legal costs, should Manchester City's sponsors decide to sue them. A meeting took place on Thursday between club executives, which was the first of its kind since the City's verdict was published in September.

Industry observers note that city were found guilty of all charges related to serious breaches of the Premier League’s financial rules over a nine-season period by an independent commission, as well as charges in relation to its failure to co-operate with the investigation. The club have appealed the independent commission's decision, with the hierarchy adamant they are innocent and remain committed to clearing their name. According to recent reports, in the meantime, the club's sponsors Etihad and Etisalat have both said they are considering legal action against the Premier League. If that does prove to be the case, a new motion passed means Richard Masters, the league's CEO, will have the costs covered if a legal case is brought against him.

But it also extends to four other members of the board, should they be put at risk. They are Alison Brittain, its chair, and three non-executive directors, Mai Fyfield, Dharmash Mistry and Matthew Ryder. The vote is said to have passed 18:2, with City and Nottingham Forest having voted against the motion.

Etihad have been the club's shirt sponsor since 2009 and hold the naming rights for the East Manchester stadium. It was revealed in September that they were exploring their options after an independent commission found City guilty of breaching more than 100 financial rules between 2009 and 2018.

The commission found the city to have artificially inflated its income during that time via so-called disguised funding schemes and 'sham' contracts. The purpose of which was to conceal equity contributions and investment from the Abu Dhabi United Group, the club's owners.

It was revealed last week that the appeal hearing for City's case is expected to take place in December, but a verdict on the appeal won't be revealed until the new year. If the appeal is unsuccessful, the sanctions hearing will reportedly take place after that, meaning any punishment won't be handed out until January at the earliest.

However, there is still some confusion around a potential timeline, with football finance expert Stefan Borson explaining to talkSPORT: "There's a lot of confusion as to the exact stage that we're at. I've heard lots of different things, so I don't know whether they've had the sanction hearing or not had it.

Industry observers note that "I was told by somebody who should know that there had been no sanction hearing as of last week. There's then the question of whether the sanction hearing is published or not. City will, I'm sure, try and argue it should've been published or that it shouldn't be binding or we shouldn't have a minus whatever, in the way we did with Everton and Nottingham Forest.

"We've seen it crystallise with Everton and Nottingham Forest, so the Premier League may well argue that it should crystallise immediately (with Man City).".

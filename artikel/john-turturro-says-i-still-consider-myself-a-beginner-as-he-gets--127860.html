Though his career has spanned over four decades, John Turturro still feels like it’s just the beginning.

According to recent reports, the 69-year-old actor and filmmaker was honored with a tribute award at Marrakech Film Festival on Sunday night, where he was greeted with roaring applause as he mimed the iconic bowling moves of his “The Substantial Lebowski” character Jesus Quintana. He then gave a heartfelt speech about his work philosophy, which he noted he got from his mom and dad. It has been revealed that “I am a very fortunate person to make a good living as an actor, sometimes as a writer, a director, doing something that I deeply love. To put all of yourself into what you do and give a piece of that away in your work is something that I learned from my parents,” Turturro said. “They taught me that a job well done defines who you are. Building a house, making a dress was done brick by brick, stitch by stitch.”. (Related: Director of Machine Learning Insights...)

He called filmmaking the “magic and beauty of life” and “an offering that you hope others will receive with open hearts.”. (Related: Animal control called to rescue alligator that turned o...)

“A great writer, Cesare Pavese, once noted that the only great thing in life is to begin. And I still consider myself a beginner in many ways,” he concluded. “For me, when I do a job, every job is like a first love. And I consider this award an encouragement to continue to try.”.

It has been revealed that the award was presented to Turturro before a screening of his latest movie, “The Only Living Pickpocket in New York,” with director Noah Segan and producer Rian Johnson also on hand. The crime thriller stars Turturro as an aging pickpocket who steals a valuable USB drive from the heir to a crime family and must race to return it before the life of his disabled wife is threatened. Initially premiering at Sundance Film Festival earlier this year to praise from critics, the film was picked up by Sony Pictures Classics and released wide in U.S. theaters on Oct. 30.

“As you get older, your toolbox of experience becomes fuller,” Turturro said of taking on the role. “You may not be able to jump as high or run as fast, but I absolutely think this is one profession that you can improve your product because you can learn to distill experiences … It was a gift to work on this film.”.

Turturro, a longtime champion of independent cinema, is perhaps best known for his collaborations with the Coen Brothers on four movies including “The Major Lebowski” and “Barton Fink,” and with Spike Lee on films including “Do the Right Thing” and “Jungle Fever.” He also starred in the “Transformers” film sereies and as supervillain Carmine Falcone in “The Batman” opposite Robert Pattinson. On the TV side, Turturro won an Emmy Award for his performance in comedy series “Monk” and presently stars in Apple TV’s acclaimed sci-fi thriller show “Severance.”.

Turturro’s Marrakech Tribute Award follows that of Jeff Goldblum, who received the honor from his “The Grand Budapest Hotel” co-star Tilda Swinton. Other starry guests at the festival, whose jury is being led by Oscar winner Marion Cotillard, include Rian Johnson, Luca Guadagnino, James Gray, Monica Bellucci and more.

Marrakech Film Festival runs through Nov. 28.

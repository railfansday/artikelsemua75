Discord’s new Auto Clips automatically saves gameplay moments when it hears a voice chat erupt in laughter or shouting, and it’s rolling out to Nitro members now.

Whether you’re playing a chaotic game of Fortnite or getting your friends together for another big Minecraft marathon, being able to instantly capture those moments that leave you in stitches of laughter is imperative.

After all, we’ve all been in situations where you’ve forgotten to hit record or your capture software has decided to stop working (it couldn’t be me). Enter Discord’s Auto Clips, a latest feature that can detect when your group starts laughing or shouting before pulling together a clip of what just happened.

According to recent reports, unlike other capture software, Discord Auto Clips removes the need to hit a keyboard shortcut to record clips. Instead, it uses a small machine learning model that runs locally on the user’s PC, which continuously looks for audio cues resembling laughter or shouting, then uses those reactions to flag moments that might be worth saving. Auto Clips can also work without voice chat in Counter-Strike 2, Dota 2, League of Legends, and Rocket League. Discord uses integrations with those games to recognize events such as kills, goals, and completed rounds, with support for more titles planned in the future.

According to recent reports, up to three highlights can be saved during each session, after which players can review them through Discord’s Clips library. It’s here that each recording can be trimmed, renamed, and edited with text or images, while game and voice audio can be removed before you share it with your friends or the wider internet.

Discord says clips remain stored locally on the player’s PC unless they choose to post them, and the company does not use saved footage, voice recordings, or other user data to train its detection model.

Sources indicate that if you want to opt out of the feature, Discord states that consumers will be able to disable Auto Clips entirely or prevent their voice from appearing in clips recorded by other people. The feature began rolling out to Nitro members in Early Access on November 24 and is right now only available through Discord’s Windows desktop app.

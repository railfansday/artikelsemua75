Anthropic’s $1.5 Trillion Valuation Exceeds the Worth of Every Home in San Francisco

Anthropic’s initial public offering priced above expectations for its IPO on Thursday, with the company’s fully diluted valuation coming to roughly $1.494 trillion—more than the total residential real estate values in many entire states.

The Claude developer, which will trade on the Nasdaq under the ticker ANT, priced at $83 a share, above the predicted $70 to $78 range. The company sold 980 million Class A shares, raising an initial $81 billion and shattering SpaceX’s $72 billion record. (Related: Hugging Face Reads, Feb. 2021 - Long-range Transformers...)

To help better understand the massive $1.494 trillion price tag, Realtor.com® put the number in terms of real estate—and the statistics are shocking.

San Francisco is a longtime hot spot for tech giants. It saw the growth of Google and Facebook, and has now become the landing spot for Anthropic, OpenAI, and other AI startups. The constant stream of tech wealth, combined with a limited number of desirable homes, has created a competitive market. San Francisco County has a median sales price of $1.6 million, and yet, Anthropic blows the entire market out of the water. (Related: My Journey to a serverless transformers pipeline on Goo...)

In a recent development, the aggregate value of every home for sale in San Francisco is a mere $8.6 billion, a small fraction of Anthropic's newly introduced valuation. Indeed, the total value of every owner-occupied home in the San Francisco metro is $1.37 trillion, according to the 2-25 American Community Survey. Anthropic’s valuation exceeds that by about $123 billion.

This trend is consistent in 391 metros out of 393.

The only two metros with more owner-occupied home value than Anthropic is worth are Upcoming York City, at $3.26 trillion, and Los Angeles, at $2.64 trillion.

Furthermore, the AI giant’s valuation tops the owner-occupied home value in 46 out of the 50 states, as well as Washington, DC.

The value of all owner-occupied homes in the state of Newly introduced Jersey comes in at $1.44 trillion, just shy of Anthropic’s valuation.

Recent findings suggest that throughout the country, all owner-occupied homes are worth $43.6 trillion. Anthropic’s valuation equals about 3.4% of all owner-occupied housing wealth in the United States.

When it comes to homes on the market, Anthropic’s valuation stacks up high.

In a recent development, across the U.S., about 1.18 million homes are for sale, listed for a combined $816 billion. Anthropic’s valuation could snap up every single one and still have $678 billion left.

Even without the first $1 trillion of the valuation, the remaining $494 billion could buy every home for sale in the 20 metros with the highest total listing value with billions to spare: about 372,000 homes listed at a combined $356 billion.

In a recent development, as Anthropic’s debut is discussed, the valuation may be rounded to $1.49 trillion, as it’s common for financial figures to be kept to one or two digits. However, that dropped digit in the $1.494 trillion figure is $4 billion. That rounding error alone is greater than the combined value of every owner-occupied home in Racine, WI, which total $3.97 billion.

Sources indicate that that same $4 billion could buy every home for sale in Alaska: 2,196 listings worth $1.16 billion. For a change in scenery, it could then cover every home for sale on Kauai, the Kapa’a, Hawaii, market, which has 356 listings worth $925 million, and still have about $1.9 billion left to purchase all the homes for sale in the Boulder, CO, metro area.

It’s no wonder the AI boom is reshaping markets in tech hubs like Upcoming York City and San Francisco, when the valuation of just one company looms so high.

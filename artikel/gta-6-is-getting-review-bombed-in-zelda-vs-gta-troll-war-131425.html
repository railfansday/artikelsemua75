GTA 6 Is Getting Review Bombed in Zelda vs. GTA Troll War

Industry observers note that grand Theft Auto 6 may be the highest-rated game of all time on Metacritic, and may have earned a record-breaking $3.79 billion on launch day, but that doesn't mean Rockstar Games' new blockbuster can't have its haters. Multiple groups have descended on GTA 6's Metacritic page to attack the game for its perceived shortcomings, much of that criticism is based on culture war nonsense.

But some of GTA 6's detractors are targeting the game for another reason: for slighting The Legend of Zelda. (Related: Breaking: NVIDIA Cosmos Reason 2 Brings Advanced Reason...)

GTA 6 made its debut on Metacritic with a 99 Metascore, based on more than 150 critics reviews. That feat nudged the previous best game of all time, at least going by Metacritic score averages, The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time, down to No. 2 on the review aggregator website. That has led some Legend of Zelda supporters to review bomb Grand Theft Auto 6 on Metacritic, and some GTA 6 supporters to do the same to Zelda. Now, both camps are in a pointless war to lower the other franchise's user review score.

Sources indicate that over the past few days, GTA and Zelda fans have been trading barbs and slinging "0" user review scores at their perceived rivals. As of Sunday, Nov. 22, Grand Theft Auto 6's user review score on Metacritic has dipped to 5.6, indicating "mixed or average" reviews from players. In response, the original Ocarina of Time and its Nintendo Switch 2 remake have also been review bombed (though their user scores are in the mid-8s as of Sunday). (Related: GTA 6 players report game-breaking crashes on PS5 and X...)

Unsurprisingly, the discourse around this pointless feud has not been particularly intelligent. Each camp is simply trying to drive down its rivals’ user score, with supporters writing the bare minimum in their user "reviews" — often with plenty of typos.

Sources indicate that even poor Soulcalibur, the 1999 fighting game released on Dreamcast, is catching strays amid the fighting. Some GTA 6 supporters are now wading into Soulcalibur's user reviews, slamming Bandai Namco's weapons-based fighter for no good reason beyond its ranking as the third-best game of all time according to Metacritic's algorithm.

Sources indicate that grand Theft Auto 6's detractors aren't just misguided Zelda fans. Unsurprisingly, a contingent of GTA fans are attacking the game for its perceived "wokeness" amid more legitimate concerns about performance. "30 FPS, Concole Exclusive, Restricted freedom and uninteresting characters are a no go for a 13 year development cycle," reads one recent negative review of GTA 6. Much of the feedback, however, seems to focus on having a female co-lead character and game mechanics that ask players to be considerate toward her.

"In GTA6 you need to do chores, pick up an abusive girl from prison and then do more chores. Boring characters, boring missions and 30fps cap. I would have rather burned that $100 than giving it to the woke garbage R* has become," reads one scathing review.

"15 years for this dissapointing cuck simulator. Rip rockstar. You were my childhood," reads another. "Game is straight woke trash," another troll notes.

It has been revealed that the tenor of GTA 6's negative reviews isn't much different from the trollish feedback infesting reviews of The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake, surprisingly. "THEY MADE LINK A CUCK IN THIS GAME!!!!! ALWAYS DOING WHAT HIS FEMALE FAIRY NAVI TELLS HIM WHAT TO DO!!!! CRITICS PROVE ONCE AGAIN THEY’VE BEEN BOUGHT OFF!!!!!" reads one of the many negative reviews to invoke cuck simulator language.

Sources indicate that while this kind of petty squabbling is par for the course in internet discourse, especially in the video game sphere, at least fans of both The Legend of Zelda and Grand Theft Auto can take comfort in knowing that both franchises have great games to enjoy in 2026. And that, if you're reading this, you're likely not spending your time fighting in the Metacritic comments.

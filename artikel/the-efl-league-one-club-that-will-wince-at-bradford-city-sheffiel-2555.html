Bradford City, Sheffield Wednesday, and Barnsley are among the Yorkshire clubs and rivals looking to make their mark in the EFL, with plenty of regional interest in League One during the 2026/27 campaign.

Of course, Rotherham United have dropped out of the division following relegation, joining York City in League Two, but Huddersfield Town and Doncaster Rovers are among the five Yorkshire representatives remaining at this level. (Related: Course Launch Community Event...)

With those two joining Bradford City, Sheffield Wednesday, and Barnsley, that means plenty of local derbies and familiar rivalries, although the fortunes of some clubs in the region are beginning to look very different as we approach the midpoint of the campaign. It is an exciting time for two of Yorkshire's biggest clubs, with Bradford potentially on the verge of fresh investment from a Thai investor and Sheffield Wednesday having already been rescued by David Storch and Arise Capital a few months ago.

Wednesday's takeover has brought renewed hope following an incredibly difficult period under Dejphon Chansiri, while Bradford could now be about to embark on a similarly exciting new chapter to kick on to the next level after promotion from League Two less than two years ago.

The Bantams have been linked with a potential investment that could provide the financial backing needed to take the club forward, having already established themselves as a competitive League One outfit immediately.

Both clubs have sizeable fanbases, strong stadiums and plenty of potential to grow with the right ownership in place. That is precisely what makes the situation so frustrating for Barnsley supporters.

These are two clubs right on their doorstep who could soon be moving forward, while the Tykes appear to be heading in the opposite direction and potentially down to English football's fourth tier for the first time since 1979.

Barnsley have not played in the Premier League since the 1997/98 campaign, although they have come close to returning to the second tier's top flight on several occasions, most notably reaching the semi-finals of the play-offs in 2021.

According to recent reports, however, their decline since finishing fifth that year has been alarming. They have since finished 24th in the Championship the year after, to fourth, sixth, 12th, and 15th in League One before finding themselves second-bottom of their current division in 2026/27.

Frustration towards the club's ownership has been building for some time, with many supporters increasingly desperate for change at the top. There is only so much blame that can be laid at the doorstep of Daniel Stendel and his youthful playing squad.

Seeing Bradford potentially secure fresh investment while Wednesday have already found latest owners will only intensify that feeling. Both could be given the platform to progress, while Barnsley are struggling to keep pace with even clubs of much smaller stature than them at the fourth tier presently.

Despite obvious envy towards Sheffield Wednesday and Bradford's situations, there are still reasons for Barnsley supporters to be optimistic, particularly with Stendel possessing a track record of getting the best out of teams and a number of promising young players to build around.

Patrick Kelly, Reyes Cleary, Luca Connell, and Vimal Yoganathan all offer potential, while Daniel Johnson and Cameron McGeehan provide some much-needed experience in the centre of the Barnsley midfield.

In a recent development, however, the lack of older heads elsewhere is a concern, with those two midfielders the only members of the squad aged over 26. That is not an ideal situation for a club trying to navigate a difficult campaign, particularly when leadership and experience are desperately needed.

There is certainly a young core that potential investors could be attracted to building on, but Barnsley need greater stability, stronger recruitment, and proper backing to turn that promising core into something meaningful. The foundations are not necessarily as poor as their league position suggests.

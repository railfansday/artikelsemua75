It has been revealed that bayern Munich head coach Vincent Kompany has revealed that he deliberately refrained from making any coaching decisions during his recent absence, insisting that assistant coach Aaron Danks and the remaining staff deserved complete responsibility for the team. Speaking at Thursday’s press conference ahead of his return to the touchline against Cologne, the 40-year-old offered an interesting insight into his management philosophy and reflected on watching his team succeed from a distance.

Danks assumed responsibility for Bayern’s first team after Kompany collapsed during a reserve-team training session on October 23. The Belgian required hospitalization and an extended period away from his coaching duties. Bayern nevertheless maintained their unbeaten run under Danks, recording four victories and one draw across five competitive fixtures. The results included a 3-1 Bundesliga victory over Freiburg, a 6-1 DFB-Pokal triumph against Magdeburg, a 2-2 draw with Borussia Dortmund, a 2-0 Champions League win away at Atlético Madrid, and a 2-1 victory over Mainz.

Sources indicate that kompany emphasized that he had deliberately avoided interfering with his assistants’ decision-making throughout the period. Although he maintained contact with the coaching staff and discussed matches afterward, the Belgian insisted that his involvement was limited to understanding their decisions rather than influencing them. The arrangement reflected his conviction that stepping away from responsibility required allowing those taking his place to exercise genuine authority. (Related: Latest Update: Should I Quit My Job? According to recent reports, kompany also expressed particular satisfaction with Bayern’s Champions League victory over Atlético Madrid. Watching his players support one another and overcome the Spanish side without him on the touchline represented one of the most memorable experiences of his coaching career. The period away from daily responsibilities also afforded Kompany an unusual opportunity for reflection.

It has been revealed that the Bayern trainer suggested that coaches rarely receive the chance to consider their work from a fresh perspective without leaving their clubs. Although his absence was involuntary, he nevertheless found time to consider how Bayern might improve further upon his return.

Sources indicate that “I didn’t make a single decision. Zero,” Kompany emphasized when discussing his absence at Thursday’s press conference. “But there’s an crucial reason why I wasn’t involved in that decision-making process. You must respect the work. You can’t be there every day, then suddenly not be there anymore, and still insist on having an opinion and making decisions. You simply can’t do that.”.

According to recent reports, “Ultimately, the best place to be is on the bench with the team,” the Bayern coach continued. “But I also enjoyed seeing how the lads stuck together. That really was one of the highlights of my career. As a coach, you never get the chance to have a reboot, a fresh start in your career at the same club. Usually, you have to do that somewhere else. In my case, that’s a luxury I now have.”.

TIME has released its annual Top 100 Photos of the Year feature, celebrating striking images that capture the biggest moments and events of 2026. One of the featured photos was shot by acclaimed photographer and filmmaker Jimmy Chin using the iPhone 18 Pro, marking a milestone for Apple’s latest flagship smartphone and, of course, Chin himself.

Chin, an Academy Award-winning director, National Geographic photographer, and accomplished mountaineer, photographed his longtime climbing partner, Conrad Anker, as part of a fresh series, What Holds Us. Chin and Anker have climbed together for 25 years, scaling peaks around the world. (Related: Scaling-up BERT Inference on CPU (Part 1)...)

“What I was after was that intersection: human ambition in pursuit of awe and wonder, set against a landscape that was here long before us and will be here long after,” Chin explains.

“Out there you are reminded how small you are. That has always put things in perspective for me, and I hope it does for whoever is standing in front of the picture.”.

For Chin, who has shot in many extreme and remote locations throughout his career, the smaller and lighter the gear, the better. (Related: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Faster Language M...)

“A production puts something between you and the person in front of you — gear, crew, the apparatus of a shoot. Working this way there was no apparatus. It was just the two of us, which is the only condition under which that kind of ease shows up in a frame,” Chin continues.

Using Apple’s new iPhone 18 Pro, Chin was able to travel lighter without sacrificing performance or image quality. As his photo above indicates, the iPhone 18 Pro more than delivered — a testament to just how far smartphone imaging technology has come. Chin is far from the only photographer or filmmaker to integrate an iPhone into a professional workflow in recent years.

Recent findings suggest that chin shot 48MP ProRAW photos the iPhone 18 Pro and then stitched multiple shots to create one of his signature panoramas. The final photo is over 300 megapixels and 25,000 pixels wide. There’s no question it holds up extremely well in the large print featured in the What Holds Us exhibit.

Chin’s photos are also part of the What Holds Us exhibition, curated by Kathy Ryan, appearing alongside works by Alex Prager and Jack Davison. After debuting in Latest York City in September, the exhibition opens in London on December 4, running at Saatchi Yates Gallery through December 13.

The Binding of Isaac: Repentance+ was a free enhancement to the beloved roguelike that arrived on PC in 2024, but launched on console earlier this week in a head-to-head matchup against Grand Theft Auto 6. The marketing stunt may have been a success, but AI-generated music tracks discovered in a special new Vice City mode have turned the launch into a nightmare. (Related: The Age of Machine Learning As Code Has Arrived...)

Recent findings suggest that while Repentance+ came to PS5, Xbox Series X/S, and Switch 2 this week, the Steam version of the game also got a free update. “Be sure to get in and play today’s Daily Run for a very special crime-packed surprise. Enter the Basement, and go out in a glorious blaze of bullets and vehicular homicide!” read the patch notes on on Valve’s storefront. Players could enter the seed “V1CE BSMT” into the roguelike before starting their run to launch a GTA-inspired version with special weapons and music. (Related: Course Launch Community Event...)

Earlier today, however, Reddit user ShweetsStuff posted a comprehensive breakdown of the fresh music tracks alleging they were made with Suno, an AI-based music generation tool. “The new update includes 14 music files for the run; seven full songs, plus some derivatives (intros/outros),” the wrote. The post continued:.

Recent findings suggest that some include Suno metadata with them. You can find this information yourself by just checking the file comment metadata (something common like VLC has tools for this, but here’s ffprobe output of drivingsong1.ogg, for example):.

comment : made with suno; created=2026-10-30T17:53:41Z; id=cd5a5b55-bead-4cb9-949d-09ed1f917dce.

Other files have this comment scrubbed; they were edited/exported later, but nonetheless there’s the proof.

Many have blamed Nicalis, the publisher and co-developer of the Repentance+ expansion, in particular. Complaints about ongoing bugs, latency, and quality control issues have seemingly been building for some time. The latest mishap results in a review-bomb of the expansion’s Steam page, which right now sits at an abysmal user rating of just 9 percent positive. “AI generated music in GTA mode; Edmund please take back control of the game,” reads one of the latest Steam reviews. “Not to mention there’s like single digit bug fixes in a year+ long awaited revision with no other changes.”.

In a recent development, the AI music was news to Edmund McMillen, the game’s creator. “Woke up to a shit show, obviously I had no idea it featured generated music in the daily run, looking into how that happened and getting it removed, stay tuned,” he posted earlier today on X. An emergency upgrade has since gone out to remove the AI-generated music as well as address some other versions with the latest version of the expansion, but Nicalis has yet to take responsibility for the use of AI or explained how it made it into the game.

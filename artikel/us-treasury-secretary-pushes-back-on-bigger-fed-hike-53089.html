📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent pushed back Friday on calls for a bigger Federal Reserve rate hike, even as bitcoin broke below $60,000 for the first time since July and stocks sank on bets that the Fed will keep tightening into a selloff.

Recent findings suggest that bessent told CNBC he would encourage the Fed not to embrace a half-point increase at its Dec. 8-9 meeting. He cited the energy supply shock and a drop in crude prices as reasons for the central bank to hold back.

Sources indicate that he argued that one governor now backing a 50 basis point hike had wanted a similar cut within the last year. Bessent stated it “seems somewhat peripatetic” to want the bigger increase only 11 or 12 months later. He did not name the governor.

“I would encourage the Fed to look forward, not backwards,” Bessent remarked.

The comments came two days after Fed Governor Christopher Waller told CNBC a half-point hike is probably necessary in December and that the central bank may need to “shock people.” Waller mentioned inflation is “headed in the wrong direction, and it’s spreading out.”. Industry observers note that waller is not alone. Minutes of the October meeting showed nearly all officials expect a quarter-point hike in December, and two floated a larger one. Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee also declined to rule out a bigger move on Thursday. (Related: Industry News: The Reformer - Pushing the limits of lan...)

“We’ve now had multiple months in a row of borderline disturbingly high inflation rates with little progress in the sectors where we would expect to be seeing some progress,” Goolsbee told reporters. “So everything’s always on the table.”.

Industry observers note that not everyone wants to go bigger. New York Fed President John Williams remarked Tuesday that “I don’t think the situation calls for dramatic action.” St. Louis Fed President Alberto Musalem has suggested as many as four hikes by the spring of 2027.

According to recent reports, the Fed raised rates in September for the first time in more than three years and held them steady in October. Inflation has stayed hot since the Iran strikes pushed up energy prices earlier this year.

In a recent development, citi still expects a quarter-point increase in December. Global chief economist Nathan Sheets noted Thursday he does not see a need for 50 basis points despite persistent inflation. In a note Friday, the bank wrote that “Fed officials will consequently have little appetite to hike into an equity sell-off.”.

It has been revealed that citi expects the Fed to hold after December and begin cutting later in 2027. Traders agree on the next step: the CME Group’s FedWatch tool indicated a more than 98% chance of a quarter-point hike, and the 10-year Treasury yield fell more than 10 basis points to 4.95% as investors bought bonds.

Stocks fell for another day. The Nasdaq Composite dropped more than 2% in morning trading, and the VIX volatility index topped 30, its highest level since March.

Bitcoin traded near $59,700 shortly before 11 a.m. ET, down more than 5% on the day, according to Bitcoin Magazine Pro. It broke below $60,000 for the first time since early July and is on track for a seventh straight weekly loss, the longest streak since 2022.

It has been revealed that higher rates are a headwind for assets that pay no yield, and the selloff has reached companies that hold bitcoin. Strategy’s stock fell for a sixth straight session on Thursday, and the bitcoin price has dropped roughly 25% in a month, a slide Bitcoin Magazine tracked earlier this week.

It has been revealed that coinShares head of research James Butterfill tied the outlook to inflation in a note dated Nov. 18. “Our base case remains range trading around USD 60k to 90k through much of 2027, until falling inflation gives monetary policy room to ease,” he noted.

At the time of writing, the bitcoin price is near $59,300.

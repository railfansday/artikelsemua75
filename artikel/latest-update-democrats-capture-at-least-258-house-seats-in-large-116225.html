Milestone reached Sunday after Democrat Chuck Hubbard defeated Republican Virginia Foxx in North Carolina.

Democrats have won their largest majority in the House in more than three decades after Chuck Hubbard defeated Republican representative Virginia Foxx in North Carolina on Sunday, extending the blue wave that swept the US midterms and increasing pressure on the party to deliver its promises to US voters.

House Democrats have secured at least 258 seats in a midterm election sweep unseen since 1992, gaining 44 House seats so far, four more than the 40 they picked up in Donald Trump’s first midterm in 2018.

The 258-seat tally was reached on Sunday, after Foxx reportedly conceded her race against Hubbard, a Democrat. Four other House races remain outstanding: one each in Minnesota and Tennessee, and two in Louisiana that are heading to a December runoff.

“Earlier today I spoke with Congresswoman Virginia Foxx who graciously conceded following the results of the recount process,” Hubbard posted on social media. “Let me be the first to thank Congresswoman Foxx for her decades of service and countless sacrifices.”.

The milestone in the 435-member House sets up a divided government, with the Senate also in Democratic hands and with a Republican president in the White House. Emboldened Democrats are preparing a 100-day agenda to address high costs of living while also confronting Trump with a vast oversight agenda for the remainder of his second term in office.

Recent findings suggest that “House Democrats have just secured the largest majority any party in Congress has held this century. BOOM,” Hakeem Jeffries, the Democratic House minority leader, wrote on social media on Sunday.

The Democrats’ majority will be bigger than any the party has seen in elections since Bill Clinton was first elected president in 1992 (258) or the election of Barack Obama in 2008 (257), yet not quite the high-water mark of the Franklin D Roosevelt era, which saw House Democratic majorities swell past 300.

“This November, Americans made their decision loud and clear: They are fed up with Donald Trump, JD Vance, and Republicans’ price spiking agenda, and they are ready for change,” stated Ken Martin, chair of the Democratic National Committee, in a statement. “Candidates across the country campaigned tirelessly on lowering costs and rooting out the Republican chaos and corruption that has governed our country for the past two years – and voters delivered a mandate with the largest congressional majorities in decades.”.

That mandate presents opportunities and challenges for the party.

“Like, hell yeah, we should feel pressure,” mentioned Democratic representative Jason Crow of Colorado, a former army ranger.

“There are people dying in this country and people losing their healthcare, losing their livelihoods, losing their farms, being terrorized from ICE,” he mentioned at the Capitol, referring to US Immigration and Customs Enforcement. “If you don’t feel a sense of urgency and pressure in this job, and a desperation to deliver for folks, then you don’t belong under this Capitol dome.”. The numbers underscore the extraordinary scale of Democratic wins, which spanned from the strongest Republican states to the midwest and east coast as voters, angry over high prices and the Iran war and concerned over corruption, made clear their discontent with Trump. Industry observers note that taking control of both the House and the Senate come January, the Democrats will carry with them the backing of voters and the expectations to deliver. The Senate Democrats won a 56-seat majority in the 100-member chamber, their biggest advantage in over a decade.

“The American people don’t want any more of their tax dollars going to an expensive, endless war in Iran. They don’t want their tax dollars going to Jeff Bezos’s next yacht or Elon Musk’s next island,” said Senator Elizabeth Warren in a video posted on Sunday. “It is time for government to work, not for some handful at the top, but to work for working people. So let’s get it done.”.

The Democrats, as part of their 100-day agenda, are planning swift votes to reverse the Republicans’ Medicaid cuts and restore the Affordable Care Act tax credits the Republicans allowed to expire.

Democrats have pledged to use their subpoena powers and launch several investigations to exert the role of Congress as a check on presidential power. Those searches include looking into how Trump and his family have become wealthier since he returned to office. Jeffries, who is in line to become House speaker, has not ruled out trying to impeach Trump for a third time.

According to recent reports, “This is a historic moment for our country. This is one of the largest classes we have seen,” said Alexandria Ocasio-Cortez, the progressive Upcoming York congresswoman, at the Capitol.

She called the big numbers “a mandate for change”.

Representative Tom Suozzi, a centrist Democrat from Latest York and an accountant by profession, stated the House Democrats should focus 75% of their time on affordability challenges and 25% on accountability.

“Everything should be tied back to the people – and how it’s affecting the people,” he remarked.

For example, he said, as the Democrats launch investigations into Trump’s financial dealings, they should be connecting the dots: “How does that affect people’s pocketbooks?”.

After the Republican House speaker Mike Johnson struggled to keep the House functioning for the past two years with a narrow 218-214 Republican majority – meaning any few GOP lawmakers could derail the agenda – the Democrats wide margin provides the latest leadership much more room to maneuver.

Jeffries, who is poised to make history as the first Black speaker of the House if his colleagues elect him to the job on 3 January 2027, will be able to lose dozens of Democrats on any given vote and still have the 218 needed for passage.

Republicans will have at least 173 seats in the minority – representing one of the lowest tallies in decades. Representative Jim Jordan of Ohio, who was elected by his colleagues to be the Republican leader in the minority, vowed to fight their way back to power.

“Over the next two years, House Republicans will fight for American families,” Jordan noted.

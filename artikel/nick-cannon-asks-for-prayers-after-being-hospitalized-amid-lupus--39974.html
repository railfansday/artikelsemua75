Nick Cannon appears to have suffered a health setback amid his lupus battle.

Industry observers note that “This 🌍can definitely take a lot out of you,” Cannon, 46, wrote via Instagram on Friday, November 20. “Pray for me y’all.”. (Related: Industry News: Director of Machine Learning Insights...)

In his social media post, the Masked Singer host shared a selfie from a hospital room, wearing a mask and wrapped in blankets.

“🙏🏾❤️ #LupusWarrior,” he captioned the post as Donny Hathaway’s “A Song for You” played in the background.

In a recent development, cannon first shared his Lupus diagnosis in 2012. According to the Mayo Clinic, the condition occurs when the body’s immune system attacks its own tissues and organs. (Related: AI for Game Development: Creating a Farming Game in 5 D...)

Lupus can cause swelling and irritation, called inflammation, that may affect joints, skin, kidneys, blood cells, brain, heart and lungs.

In a recent development, “When I got diagnosed … [it felt] so life-altering. Now, it becomes like, ‘So what?’ It’s a part of my life. It keeps me in check,” Cannon exclusively told Us in his September cover story. “I have to eat correctly, get rest and exercise twice a day. It was a blessing in disguise. Other 45-year-olds aren’t up at 4 a.m. to go to the gym. I work out to stay alive. It’s a challenge, but I’m built for it.”.

When asked whether he ever sees himself slowing down, the father of 12 remarked he doesn’t want to stop living life to the fullest.

“Back in the day, it felt like you were an adult at 45 or even 35 — like you had to stop having fun,” he shared with Us. “But I’m still running around with ADHD, being whimsical and silly, so I’m waiting for adulthood to kick in. Maybe [it will when I’m] 50. There’s still so much opportunity out there, and you gotta keep riding until the wheels fall off. To be forever young, that’s the key. That’s what life’s all about.”.

Even with a positive outlook, Cannon has also been honest about all the questions he had and the uncertainty he felt after the diagnosis.

With no family history, the rapper quickly got to educating himself and making changes to live a healthier life.

“When you hear the words ‘autoimmune disease’ or ‘lupus,’ it’s like, ‘Whoa, what is that?’ That’s how I felt, and that’s how my family and friends felt as well,” he told the Huffington Post in 2016. “But you realize it’s something that you can live with and actually overcome with the proper research and with the proper support system.”.

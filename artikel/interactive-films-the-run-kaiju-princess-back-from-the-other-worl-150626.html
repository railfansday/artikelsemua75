Interactive Films ‘The Run,’ ‘Kaiju Princess,’ ‘Back From the Other World’ Hit Taiwan Cinemas as Audiences Vote on Endings by Smartphone (EXCLUSIVE)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Moviegoers in Taiwan will be able to steer the outcome of three feature films with their phones, as interactive cinema outfit Next Interactive Studio and exhibitor Showtime Cinemas roll out a run of titles opening one after another, starting Nov. 27 with English-language survival thriller “The Run.”.

In a recent development, audiences scan a QR code on the screen to link their phones to a voting server in the auditorium. The majority pick at each decision point sets where the story goes next, including which characters survive and how the film ends. Once the lights come up, viewers can see a breakdown of the path they took, get a personality read and find other audience members who made the same calls. “The Run” is set on a remote trail in the mountains of northern Italy, where a fitness influencer with a worldwide following is pursued by masked killers. Audiences make snap decisions that decide whether she lives, and along the way bring a buried trauma to the surface. The film was cut down from 3.5 hours of footage into a theatrical version running 90 to 100 minutes, with five possible endings and 20 ways for her to die. It opens at Showtime’s flagship sites in Taipei, Taichung and Kaohsiung. (Related: IShowSpeed breaks down in tears after finishing GTA 6 i...)

Japanese live-action comedy “Back From the Other World” follows on Dec. 4. Adapted from a hit interactive game and produced with Fourth Wall, a creative team spanning Japan and Taiwan, the film is shot in the first person and casts the viewer as a hero who has slain demons in a fantasy realm, only to return to office life with his magic still working. Back at work, he discovers the goddesses he knew in that other world are now his bosses and co-workers. The cast includes Kawakita Saika and Uehara Ai.

Industry observers note that “Kaiju Princess,” set for the first quarter of 2027, is a first-person romance-adventure based on a popular Taiwanese game series and made in the style of tokusatsu, the Japanese special-effects genre. Starring Momonogi Kana and Unpai, the film – subtitled “Secret Pulse” – drops audiences into a world battered by giant-monster disasters. Group votes decide how humanity fights back against alien invaders and which romantic storylines play out. Its producers are again drawn from both Japan and Taiwan.

Next Interactive is headed by founder Ronan Wong, who the manufacturer says has advised on, produced or distributed every interactive film to receive a commercial theatrical release, across both Western and Eastern markets. Wong served as interactive adviser on Japanese animated feature “Hypnosis Mic – Division Rap Battle,” which grossed JPY2.7 billion ($17 million) from fewer than 100 screens.

“Interactive cinema transforms passive auditoriums into legendary, shared social arenas that simply cannot be replicated at home,” Wong noted.

Recent findings suggest that he described the Taiwan launch as a major milestone for the firm’s network. “As we prepare to bring these titles and our platform to global territories in 2027, we are proving that when audiences co-create the story together, cinema becomes an unmissable collective event,” Wong mentioned.

The manufacturer pitches itself to filmmakers, IP owners and exhibitors as a single partner covering both the production technology and the distribution needed to get interactive titles onto cinema screens. The Taiwan dates form part of a wider push across Asia, with releases in further international markets planned for 2027.

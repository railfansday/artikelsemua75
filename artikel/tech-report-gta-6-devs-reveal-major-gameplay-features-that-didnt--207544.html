Rockstar Games has revealed several features that were scrapped during the development of GTA 6, including activities that were available in previous Grand Theft Auto games.

After years of anticipation, GTA 6 has finally arrived, introducing players to Leonida alongside the franchise’s newest protagonists, Jason and Lucia. The game has also earned a Metacritic score of 99, matching The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time. (Related: Tech Report: Introducing the Open Arabic LLM Leaderboar...)

However, despite the number of activities available throughout the open world, Rockstar has revealed that several planned gameplay mechanics and returning features never made it into the finished game.

Speaking to German outlet PC Games, Rockstar North development manager Rob Nelson and design producer Scott Mitchell discussed several features that were considered during development but ultimately abandoned. (Related: Tech Report: AI for Game Development: Creating a Farmin...)

According to recent reports, among them was tennis, which players could participate in throughout GTA 5. Rockstar had planned to bring the activity back for GTA 6, but its implementation failed to meet the developer’s standards. Mini golf, however, made it into the finished game.

In a recent development, the developers also revealed that players were originally going to be able to crawl, decorate their homes, and craft items. These mechanics were ultimately scrapped, with some not fitting the game’s story or gameplay.

Cinema and theater visits were also removed, as Rockstar felt the activities were not interactive enough.

Nelson also explained how Red Dead Redemption 2 influenced the development of GTA 6, particularly when it came to storytelling and the relationship between its two protagonists.

The Western’s approach to pacing encouraged Rockstar to incorporate quieter moments into GTA 6, giving Jason and Lucia more opportunities for character development between the game’s action sequences.

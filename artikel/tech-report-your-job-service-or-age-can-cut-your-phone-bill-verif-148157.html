According to recent reports, some take a percentage off a regular strategy. AT&T’s discount programs take up to 20% off, depending on the plan, for employees of partner companies, union members, students, the military, veterans, and teachers. T-Mobile Work Perks takes 15% off for employees of participating employers, and Verizon’s discounts take 10% off for the military, first responders, nurses, teachers, and students.

Sources indicate that others are separate plans for one group, with their own prices and line limits. T-Mobile’s military and first responder plans cover as many as 12 lines, while its 55+ plans stop at two. AT&T’s 55+ strategy allows up to 10 lines, though it saves the most on two. Recent findings suggest that ask what it combines with before you switch intends. Verizon won’t combine its military, first responder, nurse, teacher, and student discounts with one another, and AT&T’s 55+ plan can’t be combined with its discounts for veterans, teachers, nurses, or physicians. The discounted prices the carriers advertise also assume you pay by AutoPay.

Then check what it saves. A retired couple in their 60s paying by AutoPay for two lines on T-Mobile’s T-Mix More owes $130 a month. The plan’s 55+ version costs $120 and keeps the same four Picks, which you spend on perks such as Apple TV. That saves the couple $120 a year.

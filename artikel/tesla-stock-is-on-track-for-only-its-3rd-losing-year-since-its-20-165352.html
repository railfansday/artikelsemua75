Tesla Stock Is on Track for Only Its 3rd Losing Year Since Its 2010 IPO. Here's What Happened After the Other 2.

Sources indicate that tesla (TSLA +11.46%) stock surged on Monday, Nov. 23, climbing with the broader market two days after the organization started rolling out version 15 of its Full Self-Driving (Supervised) software to some people. Even so, at around $385 as of this writing, shares are about 14% under the $449.72 where they finished 2025. (Related: Industry News: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

According to recent reports, the stock might still close the gap. But unless it rises about 17% before the year ends, 2026 will be only Tesla's third losing calendar year since it started trading in June 2010. (Its initial public offering was priced at $17 a share, and the stock ended that first year well above it.).

The other two were 2016, when shares dropped around 11%, and 2022, when they lost about 65%.

According to recent reports, both times, the next year was a good one for shareholders. Going by split-adjusted closing prices at the end of each calendar year, Tesla stock climbed around 46% in 2017 and about 102% in 2023.

In a recent development, that's an encouraging record. But two examples aren't a pattern, and I'd say what Tesla was like heading into those two rebounds counts more than the losing years themselves.

Tesla's business didn't exactly drive either recovery. In 2017, the year Model 3 deliveries started in July, sales surged 68% to $11.8 billion. But the net loss almost tripled, rising to $1.96 billion from $675 million in 2016. (Related: How to train a newly introduced language model from scr...)

In a recent development, 2023 was similar in one way. Revenue climbed 19% to $96.8 billion, a massive slowdown from 51% growth in 2022.

And operating income (business profit before interest and taxes) dropped 35% to $8.9 billion as price cuts on Tesla's vehicles squeezed its margins.

In other words, the stock rose in both years even as Tesla's operating results got worse.

Both started from a low price compared to sales. When 2016 ended, Tesla's market value was around $35 billion. Tesla took in $7 billion in revenue that year, up 73% over 2015, so shares traded at about 5 times sales. Six years later, the picture was much the same. At the end of 2022, Tesla was worth around $390 billion, or again about 5 times sales. Revenue that year was $81.5 billion, up 51%.

According to recent reports, the 2022 reset is arguably the more striking of the two. Tesla's net income more than doubled that year to $12.6 billion. But the stock had started 2022 at over 200 times earnings, and it finished the year at around 34 times earnings.

In both cases, investors going into the next year were paying a modest price for a firm whose sales were rising fast.

Recent findings suggest that tesla is headed toward 2027 with neither of those things going for it. Its revenue growth has stalled in recent months. Revenue climbed 16% year over year in the first three months of 2026 and 26% in the April-to-June period. In the third quarter, it was flat at around $28 billion.

Industry observers note that profits look even worse. Operating income came in at $718 million in the third quarter. That was better than the $398 million Tesla earned in the second quarter, though it was still 56% lower than a year earlier.

Over the last four reported quarters, net income totaled about $3.2 billion -- about a fourth of what Tesla earned in 2022.

Sources indicate that the price, meanwhile, is far higher. At roughly $385 a share, Tesla trades at more than 13 times its sales over the last four quarters and more than 400 times earnings.

That price-to-sales ratio is about three times what it was when 2016 and 2022 ended.

To be fair, there's a story in play that looks like 2017. Then, investors were waiting on the Model 3. Now, they're waiting on Robotaxi and the self-driving software that just began rolling out, which might take years to show up in Tesla's profits. But the 2017 version of that story had a far lower price tag.

Two losing years are a small sample, of course, and Tesla stock can move fast, as Monday demonstrated. Still, I'm doubtful that a losing 2026 would set the stock up like 2016 and 2022 did.

Sources indicate that both rebounds started from a price that seemed to assume little, but today's price assumes a lot. At over 400 times earnings, I still think Tesla stock is overvalued.

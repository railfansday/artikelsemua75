More clients are turning to artificial intelligence for legal guidance before they begin working with lawyers, which makes client communication and education even more vital.

Patrick McEuen, the director of operations at Meyer Wilson Werning in Columbus, Ohio, noted he has seen clients share their financial records with OpenAI’s ChatGPT, Anthropic’s Claude or other generative AI tools.

Industry observers note that mcEuen also said some clients have told his law firm’s lawyers the outcome that they expect based on advice from AI, even if it runs counter to legal advice. He said attorneys at his firm now ask clients whether and how they use AI at the start of their engagement. (Related: Transformer-based Encoder-Decoder Models...)

Samanth Kompa, the founder of Kompa Law in Ontario, Canada, also mentioned the firm’s intake team addresses client AI use. She is an employment lawyer and has had frank conversations with clients who want to make legally invalid claims against their managers or other co-workers based on their use of AI.

Both firms now address AI use and expectations in their client engagement letters and agreements, according to Law.com.

The Irish actor writes about his pal “Em,” a woman living with the rare genetic condition epidermolysis bullosa (EB). Their special bond is captured in “The Slightest Touch,” a newly introduced documentary premiering Nov. 25 on HBO Max.

According to recent reports, I met my friend Emma Fogarty nearly 16 years ago. I was sat beside her at a dinner table on an evening purposed to raise awareness for those who have the same punishing condition Emma was born with: epidermolysis bullosa (EB), a rare genetic disorder where the slightest touch or even friction of material rubbing against her skin can cause blistering and open wounds, as the skin struggles to heal. When she was born, the doctors told her mother and father — Pat and Malachy — that they would be better off leaving her in the hospital as she wouldn’t survive the week. What the doctors failed to diagnose was Emma’s spirit, its beauty, its utter fortitude — and that of her parents, too.

Today Emma is 42, which makes her Ireland’s longest-surviving person with epidermolysis bullosa, and accompanied by constant pain. She lives in fear of cancers, which afflict those with EB in staggering numbers (she is a two-time cancer survivor). But she also lives a life filled with joy, love, meaning and friendship. She has been a wonderful friend to me over these years, providing laughter and support, adventure and her own brand of hard-earned wisdom.

Sources indicate that A few years back, I’d mentioned to Emma in passing that we should do a marathon together someday; I’d push her if she fancied. A year after my half-hearted offer, she texted me saying, “Do you remember you mentioned the marathon?” Emma had a dream that I would push her the last 4K of the Dublin City Marathon to mark her 40th birthday, with each kilometer representing a decade of her life that she’d stubbornly refused to let EB dictate. And so… it was on!

In a recent development, we decided to chronicle the experience to make the adventure even more special and raise awareness around EB, possibly offering some solidarity to those who live with the pain and cruelty of this condition, including the families whose care is so essential and, at times, heartbreaking. The result is “The Slightest Touch,” a documentary we made together. With the support of Emma’s mom and dad, her sister Catherine, our director Rachel Fleit and many more, what started as a shared dream between us became the shared dream of many; a mini movement of resistance and spirit in the face of unimaginable adversity.

While promoting the documentary, some folks have asked me what it is about Emma or our friendship that makes it so special. I struggle to answer such an honest and simple question because I live inside our friendship and have never objectified it. It’s always been easy with Em. She’s just my dear friend, and it’s a friendship that has deepened through the years. She’s allowed me to be there for her and has shared her trials and triumphs and fears with me. What a great gift. This intensely spirited and brilliant woman has also been there for me. One such example was during the days after my father’s passing. My siblings and I were sitting by our father’s body during his wake, an open casket in the living room of his former home. The man was a mere husk compared to the vibrant human we’d once known and had complex feelings about. Death simplified our feelings for Dad, but the grief was still profound. As I sat beside his coffin, someone tapped me on the shoulder and noted, “Your friend is at the front door.” I stood and saw Emma in her wheelchair making her way into the house with Pat and Malachy beside her.

She hadn’t told me she’d be coming, and it absolutely broke me — but in the way that love can break you only to put you back together again stronger, more whole. And that’s what friends do: they make each other more whole; they offer solace through presence. She didn’t come that day to offer platitudes regarding the realities of loss, or to offer any advice at all. She was just there to be by my side, and to offer my family her tech giant; to acknowledge our grief as we were moving with it and not attempt to assuage it in any way. That day marked what Emma is to me: a true friend, and someone who would be there for me through thick and thin.

I only hope that I can offer her some measure of the same kind of love.

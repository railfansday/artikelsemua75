Celtic's 4-1 dispatching of St Mirren on Saturday afternoon suggests Martin O'Neill might have got his side back on track, with one of his veteran operators showing just how key he could be for the remainder of the campaign.

The Hoops started the month badly with defeat at Falkirk and only edged out bottom side Kilmarnock by a single goal heading into the international break, so it was a welcome return to something the dominance expected of the Scottish Premiership leaders this weekend. While they had plenty of possession and opportunities early on it took until 33 minutes in for Alex Oxlade-Chamberlain to open the scoring after a pair of Camilo Duran efforts were ruled out for infringements. (Related: Breaking: “Determined” woman completes 7 marathons in a...)

The Colombian quickly added a second, and Luke McCowan a third before the break as the home side took complete control, with Callum McGregor's fourth just after the hour putting the game to bed despite a late consolation penalty from Killian Phillips.

The attacking trio of Duran, flanked on either side by Jota and Yang Hyun-Jun, may have been the most eye-catching on the day, but there is a strong argument to say that it was Oxlade-Chamberlain who really shone through in victory.

It was the veteran Englishman's first goal of the campaign and a crucial intervention as the breakthrough opened the floodgates and allowed Celtic to have the game mostly wrapped up by half-time.

The Champions League winner has been in and out of O'Neill's lineup so far this season as the side have lurched through various periods of inconsistency, despite still leading the title race.

Oxlade-Chamberlain had been largely ever-present in O'Neill's second stint in charge last term, arriving in January after leaving Besiktas as a free agent, but disappeared to the bench for the post-split run-in as the Hoops surged back to pip Hearts to the title on the final day.

The ex-England international surely will have staked a hefty claim for a few more starts after his strong showing in central midfield, which saw him rated 8/10 by 67 Hail Hail after the final whistle.

Oxlade-Chamberlain vs St Mirren.

Even if he can only last an hour or so like he did here the veteran proved he can make a difference in the early running before making way for a closer to see things out.

The Scottish Premiership often demonstrates late-career stars that it is no walk in the park but the former Liverpool and Arsenal man was able to show plenty of the quality on the ball that made him a Premier League regular, fitness permitting, earlier in his career.

According to stats compiled by FootMob Oxlade-Chamberlain had 43 attacking touches in his 61 minutes on the pitch, but his passing was close to flawless.

As well as his goal he was accurate with 29 of his 32 passes, including all three of his long balls, and was only dispossessed twice in the game while making five passes into the final third.

He may not have the legs he once had as a winger at Southampton and Arsenal but the vision and the touch that characterised the middle of his career was on full display.

At his best it shouldn't be too controversial to suggest that Oxlade-Chamberlain can be O'Neill's best player, and could be crucial in steadying a side that has been swaying all too often this term.

His well-documented injury issues and advancing years will of course mean his best is unlikely to be on show week in and week out, but if the manager and his staff can keep the midfielder match fit and able to get somewhere close to his best he can be a key asset for the rest of the campaign.

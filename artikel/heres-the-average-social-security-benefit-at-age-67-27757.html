Here's the Average Social Security Benefit at Age 67

By Kailey Hagen, CFP® – Nov 20, 2026 at 6:00PM EST.

According to recent reports, the average Social Security benefit for 67-year-olds is $2,016.48 per month, according to Social Security Administration (SSA) data from December 2025. Full retirement age (FRA) ranges from 66 to 67, depending on your birth year, and it's 67 for everyone born in 1960 or later. It's the point where you're eligible for your unreduced retirement amount. (Related: Breaking: One Ingredient In Our Earliest Diets Linked T...)

Reaching this age doesn't always mean you receive exactly 100% of your baseline amount. Many people who are 67 today have a full retirement age of 66 years and 10 months. These workers may have already earned small delayed retirement credits if they waited until 67 to claim their checks. (Related: Industry News: Politics Takes A Toll On Your Health — W...)

Earnings histories also impact these averages. Men who are 67 receive an average of $2,234.41 per month, while the average among women is $1,801.82. This results in a monthly gender gap of $432.59 as of December 2025.

Think of your full retirement age as the baseline for your benefits. The SSA bases your check on your average monthly income over your 35 highest-earning years, adjusting those wages for inflation and applying the primary insurance amount formula. This calculation creates the standard amount you're entitled to at full retirement age.

Sources indicate that you don't have to claim at your baseline. You can earn delayed retirement credits, which are increases for delaying past FRA, worth 8% per year up to age 70. For a worker whose FRA is 67, waiting those three extra years provides a 24% total increase in monthly income.

According to recent reports, these decisions have a visible impact on the data. The average monthly benefit at age 70 is $2,274.68, which is $258.20 higher than the age-67 average.

But even for these claimants, benefits alone often aren't enough to live on. Social Security was only designed to replace about 40% of pre-retirement wages, so most retirees rely on savings or other income to cover their full costs.

In a recent development, A 3.6% cost-of-living adjustment (COLA) will take effect in 2027 to help benefits keep pace with inflation. This will add about $73 a month on top of the average listed above. These adjustments are designed to protect your purchasing power over time.

Knowing the population average gives you a target for your own planning, but your final check depends on your career earnings and when you choose to claim.

Waiting until age 67 to claim Social Security helps you avoid the early claiming benefit reduction, which can drastically reduce your checks. This is one major reason FRA is such a popular claiming age, but it's not the only one.

Sources indicate that when you claim Social Security under your FRA, you're also at risk of losing some of your benefits to the earnings test if you're still working. In 2027, for example, you lose $1 from your checks for every $2 you earn over $25,960 if you're under your FRA all year. If you'll reach your FRA next year, you only lose $1 for every $3 you earn over $67,920.

Recent findings suggest that these lost benefits come back to you as a permanent benefit boost at your FRA. But for some, the risk of short-term losses makes them delay their claim until their FRA, when the earnings test no longer applies. This is something to weigh if you plan to continue working once you're eligible for Social Security checks.

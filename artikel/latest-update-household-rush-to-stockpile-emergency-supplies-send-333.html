Surge fuelled by ‘preppers’ heeding Be Ready campaign’s call for households to have three days of food and water for emergencies.

Retail sales in Great Britain surged last month as the public rushed to buy products including tinned food and long-life milk, after the government issued a warning to stock up in case of a disaster.

The Office for National Statistics (ONS) stated that the volume of goods sold jumped by 0.7% month-on month in October, defying economists’ consensus expectations of flat sales, as “preppers” took heed of the government’s Be Ready campaign to hold supplies to last 72 hours to survive a possible extreme weather event, cyber incident or another serious emergency. The surge in sales is a welcome bounce back for the industry after a poor September, which the ONS has revised down from a 0.4% fall to a 0.6% fall, fuelled in part by poor online sales of autumn and winter clothing because of unseasonably warm weather. “Supermarkets have told us that sales of long-life food items such as tinned food and UHT milk were boosted by the government’s ‘Be Ready’ campaign,” stated Grant Fitzner, chief economist at the ONS.

Food store sales, primarily supermarkets, rose by 0.8% month-on-month, the fastest rate since the 1.4% rise in April.

Textile, clothing and footwear stores reported a 1.5% month-on-month boost. An increase in visits to galleries, and a jump in sales of sports equipment, games and toys, lifted sales across other non-food stores by 3.1% month-on-month, the biggest rise since January.

The total non-food stores category – which includes department stores, clothing, household and other non-food stores – increased by 1.1% month-on-month in October.

Non-store retail sales, primarily online but also sellers such as street stalls and markets, bounced back too, rising 0.9% as a turn in the weather last month drove sales of autumn and winter ranges.

However, sales volumes of motor fuel fell by 2.2% last month, the steepest rate since April, as prices at the pump rose.

“October’s retail sales have exceeded expectations, despite low consumer confidence and growing pressure on household finances from higher interest rates and inflation,” said Erin Brookes, European retail and consumer lead at Alvarez & Marsal.

“Retailers will be feeling optimistic as the sector approaches the critical Black Friday and Christmas trading period, with October’s sales growth suggesting consumers are still willing to spend this festive season.

“Despite this positivity, retailers are caught between increasingly price-sensitive consumers and mounting operating costs.”.

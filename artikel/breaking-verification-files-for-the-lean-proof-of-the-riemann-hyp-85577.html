Industry observers note that the published Lean source compiles without errors, the five headline theorems depend only on the three standard axioms, every module has been replayed through the Lean kernel, two independently written kernels, nanoda and con-leche, accept the full exported proof, and lean4lean accepts every compiled module. The --compare modes of con-leche and of its Rust port con-ron, independent implementations of Comparator's statement matching, each confirm all five theorems against the challenge. An independent sandboxed lake build of the archive reproduces the export byte for byte. The lake comparator run and the Lean-kernel replay of the export are still in progress. In a recent development, five theorems, proved in the submission's Main.lean. Their statements are below exactly as they appear in the challenge file, which imports only Mathlib. All the mathematical notions in them (riemannZeta, DirichletCharacter, DirichletCharacter.LFunction, IsPrimitive) are Mathlib's.

1. The Riemann Hypothesis, using Mathlib's own definition (Mathlib/NumberTheory/LSeries/RiemannZeta.lean): every zero of ζ other than the trivial zeros −2, −4, −6, … has real part 1/2. The condition s ≠ 1 is there because Mathlib assigns ζ a conventional value at its pole.

2. The same statement written out in the submission's own words. Lean accepts the two as equal by definition.

3. No zeros to the right of the critical line, for every Dirichlet L-function. For every modulus N ≥ 1 and every Dirichlet character χ modulo N, primitive or not, L(χ, s) ≠ 0 whenever Re s > 1/2. The one excluded case is the trivial character at s = 1, where the L-function has its pole.

4. Zeros in the critical strip lie on the critical line, for every Dirichlet L-function.

5. The same for primitive characters, the form in which the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis for Dirichlet L-functions is usually stated.

Industry observers note that scope. "GRH" here means Dirichlet L-functions: all moduli and all characters. Dedekind zeta functions, Hecke L-functions and automorphic L-functions in general are not among the statements that were checked.

Sources indicate that as a check that does not depend on Mathlib's definition of the zeta function, the proof was combined with kimihiro64/Robin1984 (commit acab1a3), an independent formalization of Robin's and Lagarias's criteria on the same Lean and Mathlib versions. Importing both gives two theorems about the divisor sum, with no hypotheses and no mention of zeta (ArithSolution.lean; statements alone in ArithChallenge.lean, which imports only Mathlib):.

Industry observers note that the combined export is 61,752,134,938 bytes, sha256 44651dcc482a6e06e8d626994221aff638e422fded189bf856e0c31176926f0d; its challenge export has sha256 088a57c9e7454fa5279a86a825e551828ac8431694855aadd1f6e6a6eb37bb1d. These statements still rely on Mathlib's definitions of the divisor sum, Euler's constant, harmonic numbers, exp and log, and on the Robin1984 proof, which is why that proof was put through the same checks.

In a recent development, the kernels check elaborated terms, not source text, so what was verified is the term that Lean's elaborator produces from the statement. For the Lagarias inequality that term is short enough to read in full. This is the statement with every coercion, instance and implicit argument shown, as elaborated against Mathlib alone (Lean v4.33.1, Mathlib 0df444a3), with none of the proof loaded:.

Recent findings suggest that it names 27 constants directly: Nat, Real, the order and arithmetic instances on them, the casts from Nat and Rat to Real, ArithmeticFunction.sigma, harmonic, Real.exp and Real.log. Its meaning depends on 1,801 Mathlib and core declarations in all, counting definitions with their bodies and theorems by their statements only (a proof cannot change what a definition means). The corresponding count for Robin's inequality is 2,050; the difference is mostly the definition of Euler's constant.

Files: the script, its output (both statements), and the dependency lists for Lagarias and Robin.

The exported proof: output of lean4export on module Main. Unpacks to 61,570,595,788 bytes.

The exported challenge: the five statements with sorry proofs, importing only Mathlib. Unpacks to 350,862,970 bytes.

With Lean v4.36.0 installed, nothing needs to be built. You need roughly 100 GB of RAM and 70 GB of disk.

Industry observers note that nanoda alone took about 28 minutes on 80 threads. The Lean kernels are single-threaded and take much longer.

Recent findings suggest that the fastest independent check is con-leche built from source (Lean v4.36.0, about two minutes to build). It needs about 50 GB of RAM and took under four minutes on 64 threads:.

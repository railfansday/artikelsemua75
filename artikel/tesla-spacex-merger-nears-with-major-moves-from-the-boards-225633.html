📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

There’s been the expectation for months, since SpaceX’s IPO at least, that Tesla and SpaceX will merge. The suspicions haven’t been built on a lot of evidence, more so just the sense that this is what Elon Musk would like to do — including because it would give him basically unchecked control of the combined company (more so than he already has).

It has been revealed that well, far beyond suspicions, reporting implies that this is really going to happen, and may even be on the verge of happening. The Financial Times reports that Tesla and SpaceX have appointed a total of five directors from their boards to explore the possibility — three directors from Tesla’s board and two from SpaceX’s board. In fact, we even know the people who have been appointed — Robyn Denholm (Tesla), Kathleen Wilson-Thompson (Tesla), Jack Hartung (Tesla), Roelof Botha (SpaceX), and Randy Glein (SpaceX).

“Musk remarked on SpaceX’s third-quarter earnings call last month ​that a merger unifying his AI, robotics, EV and aerospace ambitions ​under a single corporate roof would be ‘a good idea,'” Reuters also noted. “He confirmed that the companies had formed special committees of independent directors to discuss ​a union.”.

When this idea first came up, the substantial concern that was raised is that Tesla is heavily dependent on China — for both sales and production — and SpaceX is a key military supplier. China could, therefore, put strong pressure on the manufacturer to make it do what China wants. SpaceX would, in essence, be compromised.

A Teen Hiker Needed Rescue on a Popular Tahoe Peak. An Expert Explains Why Snow Makes It Dangerous.

Search and rescue volunteers saved a 17-year-old hiker missing on California’s Mount Tallac on November 16 after following bootprints through the snow for hours.

The teenager, whose name authorities have not released, revealed early signs of hypothermia when responders found him in rugged terrain away from the established hiking trail, the El Dorado County Sheriff’s Office wrote in a statement. In a recent development, “The Sheriff’s Office is grateful that this search and rescue effort had a positive outcome,” the agency wrote. “However, situations like this can swiftly turn tragic, particularly in challenging winter conditions.”.

Industry observers note that located near South Lake Tahoe, Mount Tallac reaches an elevation of 9,739 feet. And in November, high-elevation conditions can deteriorate rapidly. (Related: Industry News: Judge blocks construction of Trump’s arc...)

“In inclement weather, especially later in the day when it starts getting dark, it’s tough to find your way if you don’t already know the terrain,” Jared Manninen, a local outdoor writer and founder of the website Tahoe Trail Guide, told Outside.

Mount Tallac is one of the most popular summit hikes in Lake Tahoe’s Desolation Wilderness, a nearly 64,000-acre expanse of federally protected backcountry in the Sierra Nevada, but it also draws search-and-rescue calls year after year. The ten-mile trail climbs roughly 3,500 feet through steep, rocky terrain that becomes especially difficult to navigate once snow covers the route.

Even on a well-traveled route like the Mount Tallac Trail, a few inches of snow can make the trail much harder to find, especially on the descent.

The teenager was hiking alone on November 15, texting his family as he descended. When his messages stopped around 5 P.M., his family contacted the El Dorado County Sheriff’s Office within two hours. Recent storms deposited six inches of snow on the mountain, and forecasts called for additional snowfall, high winds, and reduced visibility.

Using the teen’s last known coordinates from his emergency locator beacon, rescuers narrowed their search to a rugged area of Mount Tallac known as The Bowl, a sweeping, boulder-filled basin along the hiking route just below the mountain’s upper ridge.

Recent findings suggest that A Washoe County Sheriff’s Office helicopter initially searched from the air but called off the search because of worsening weather. El Dorado County Search and Rescue volunteers continued on foot toward The Bowl, where they found bootprints in the snow.

Sources indicate that after following the tracks for several hours, rescuers located the teenager around 1:30 A.M. northwest of Floating Island Lake, a small alpine lake along the lower section of the trail.

Responders treated the hiker’s cold-related injuries and escorted him back to the trailhead, where he reunited with his family.

Despite its popularity, Manninen noted that the route up Mount Tallac is far from easy.

“It’s a quintessential hike because it’s so iconic,” Manninen stated. “You have such amazing views, but again, it’s a legitimate hike. It’s a significant commitment.”.

Manninen described The Bowl as a steep, open slope of loose granite and boulders above a small alpine lake along the summit trail. In summer, the trail is fairly easy to follow, although the loose rock can make for slow going. But even a few inches of snow can hide the route, and hikers can’t always count on following their own footprints back down if more snow falls.

In a recent development, according to local news outlet South Tahoe Now, a similar rescue unfolded on Mount Tallac in November 2024, when search-and-rescue volunteers followed tracks through the snow to find a lost hiker who couldn’t find his way down. Rescuers reached him around 4 A.M. and escorted him down a longer, less hazardous route.

In October 2017, responders rescued a stranded couple who became disoriented after coming across patches of snow left by an earlier storm along Mount Tallac’s eastern slope.

It has been revealed that for anyone planning to hike Mount Tallac in snowy conditions, Manninen recommended starting early, bringing a partner, studying the route beforehand, and carrying a paper map rather than relying solely on electronic navigation. He also advised packing a headlamp and extra layers in case the hike takes longer than expected, or otherwise choosing a different trail when the weather isn’t cooperating.

“It does seem like every year we have at least one person, couple, or group that ends up getting turned around on Mount Tallac and needs some type of rescue,” Manninen remarked. “It’s just a tough mountain.”.

The El Dorado County Sheriff’s Office reminds hikers to stay on established trails and carry enough gear to spend an unexpected night outdoors.

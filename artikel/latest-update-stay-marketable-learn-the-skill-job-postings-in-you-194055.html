It has been revealed that to stay marketable, read 10 to 20 current job postings for your role and the one a step above it, and list the skills, software, and certifications that keep showing up. Close the gap that appears most often, with your employer paying if it will, and add the latest skill to your resume and LinkedIn profile.

Sources indicate that skills go stale faster than most people notice. The World Economic Forum surveyed more than 1,000 large employers worldwide for its latest Future of Jobs Report, which projects that, on average, 39% of workers’ existing skills will change or go out of date by 2030. One posting is a wish list, but a skill that turns up in most of 15 postings is a requirement. The postings for the job a step above yours show what it takes to get promoted.

In a recent development, search your job title on Indeed or LinkedIn, then the title one step up, and open 10 to 20 current postings. Write down every skill, software program, and certification they ask for, and add a tally mark each time one repeats. The most-tallied item you don’t have yet is the gap to close first.

According to recent reports, if your applications keep stalling after graduation, run the same count on entry-level postings. A requirement that keeps showing up in postings but not on your resume could be what’s holding your applications back.

Close the gap the cheapest way that works. Ask your manager whether your employer’s training budget will cover a course, or take a low-cost online course on your own. You can also volunteer for a stretch assignment, a project just beyond your current role that puts the skill to use. Say a bookkeeper finds a payroll certification in most postings one step up, and one accounting software package in nearly as many. She starts with the certification and asks her manager to pay for the course, since it helps with the payroll work her team already does.

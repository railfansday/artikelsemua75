Despite posting a solid earnings beat, Elastic sees its stock slide on rough guidance

UPDATED 18:33 EST / NOVEMBER 19 2026.

Elastic N.V.’s stock was trending lower in late trading today after the enterprise search software firm reported earnings and revenue that beat Wall Street’s expectations but disappointed with guidance that came in below forecasts.

In a recent development, the company reported second-quarter earnings before certain costs such as stock compensation of 94 cents per share, easily beating the analyst consensus estimate of 81 cents. Revenue rose 17%, to $496 million, surpassing the Street’s target of $487 million. (Related: Three 'e-waste' Nvidia V100s hit 100 tokens per second ...)

The results helped Elastic bounce back into profitability. Its net income for the quarter came to $171 million, rebounding from a loss of $51.3 million in the same period one year ago.

According to recent reports, however, investors were less than impressed with the organization’s guidance. For the quarter in progress, Elastic noted it’s looking for earnings of between 90 cents and 92 cents per share on sales of $507.5 million to $508.5 million. That’s some way below the Street’s targets of 95 cents in earnings and $517.6 million in revenue. The company is not helped by the weak Euro, with currency conversions likely to cause a $4.5 million headwind on revenue in the quarter. The full-year picture is not much better, as Elastic forecast annual sales of between $2 billion and $2.01 billion compared with the Street’s model of $2.01 billion.

In a recent development, netherlands-based Elastic is one of Europe’s most successful software companies. It’s the developer of the popular open-source enterprise search platform Elasticsearch, which is used by thousands of businesses all over the world. The base platform is free to download and use, with Elastic making money by selling a special cloud-hosted version that ships with premium features not available in the open edition. The organization also sells application observability and security tools.

In the last couple of years, Elastic has tried to cash in on the artificial intelligence boom by positioning Elasticsearch as a useful cog in the wheel that feeds real-time data into large language models to provide them with business context. A key capability is Elastic Context Engine, which gathers, structures, and serves business-specific context to AI agents so they can make accurate decisions with fewer tokens and lower latency.

More recently, the company has built AI-native products including AlertZero, which enables enterprises to orchestrate teams of autonomous security agents that automate tasks around data protection and threat hunting.

Elastic Chief Executive Ash Kulkarni (pictured) mentioned he’s excited about the potential of those upcoming capabilities to drive significant revenue over time. “Elastic delivered another strong quarter, beating guidance across all key metrics,” he said. “Our strong results reflect expanding customer adoption and position us well to continue our strong execution in the back half of fiscal 2027.”.

In a recent development, unfortunately for Kulkarni, investors didn’t seem to share his enthusiasm. The market was clearly expecting trouble on the horizon in the hours before the organization published its report, as its stock declined 8% during regular trading. Once the results were out, it fell another 8% after-hours. That means Elastic’s stock is now up just over 1% in the year to date.

Elastic’s stock had rallied more than 150% between April and October, but virtually all of those gains have now been erased. Prior to today, the shares had already lost more than 20% this month amid broader concerns about the AI industry. Some investors continue to worry about the potential of AI technology to challenge the business models of software companies.

Elastic is trying to sidestep those concerns by de-emphasizing search and observability and moving towards runtime context, model evaluation and AI operations, but investors are not yet convinced it will be able to do so successfully, Constellation Research analyst Michael Ni told SiliconANGLE.

According to recent reports, “While Elastic is showing good underlying momentum, the Street wants to see more proof that its re-acceleration can hold,” Ni mentioned. “The next few quarters will show whether the broader platform story translates into sustainable growth or remains ahead of the numbers.”.

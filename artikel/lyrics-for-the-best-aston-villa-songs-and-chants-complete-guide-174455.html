Few English clubs can match Aston Villa for history. Founder members of the Football League in 1888, seven-time champions of England and European Cup winners in 1982, the Villans have long been one of the country's great institutions, even if the decades since that night in Rotterdam brought more lows than highs.

Through relegation, boardroom turmoil and years of mid-table drift, one thing never changed: the noise from the Holte End. Under Unai Emery, Villa Park has found its voice again, and the club's songbook has rarely sounded louder, with Champions League nights giving supporters a fresh stage.

Industry observers note that no chant is more closely tied to Villa Park than this one. It's sung to Johnny Cash's version of 'Ghost Riders in the Sky' and has been part of Holte End folklore for decades. Supporters keep the melody and the famous yodeling build-up of the original, then swap the payoff line for a tribute to the stand. A longer version, which includes a pointed dig at neighbours Birmingham City, survives in Villa folklore, but isn't often sung on matchdays now. It's simple, but 'Villa Till I Die' might be the most powerful song in the Villa songbook. A pure statement of loyalty, it tends to be the first chant to break out at substantial moments, as fans celebrate their beloved team and note that, as long as they live, there is only one team on their mind.

In a recent development, taken from Righeira's 1985 Italian disco hit 'L'Estate Sta Finendo', Allez, Allez, Allez has become one of the most commonly sung Villa chants in recent seasons. Liverpool fans may claim it as theirs, but the Holte End has made it its own, at home and on the road, with adapted lyrics to celebrate their return to European competitions.

In a recent development, set to Jeff Beck's 1960s hit 'Hi Ho Silver Lining', with the club's name worked into the chorus, this one comes out when Villa are on top and is also played in the fan zone before home games at Villa Park, which features in our ranking of every Premier League stadium. Wolves sing their own version of it, but in claret and blue, it's a celebration of the club and everyone who follows it.

According to recent reports, the captain's chant is regularly one of the loudest at Villa Park, as fans came up with it soon after McGinn arrived from Hibernian in 2018. The lyrics have been updated from season to season as the dugout has changed, from Steve Bruce to Dean Smith, Steven Gerrard and now Emery. The chant has become so popular that Scotland fans have adopted the tune to serenade their star midfielder.

Happy Thanksgiving week! There are no games Thursday, and it's a tough week to stream, with only two days — Tuesday (three games) and Saturday (five) — with fewer than nine games on the slate. There's some good news on the injury front: Brandon Ingram is back and set to make his Clippers debut Monday against Houston, and we should see Austin Reaves (heel) return for the Lakers as well. On the other side, we lost Evan Mobley to a toe injury and Dylan Harper to a hip injury.

Every league is different, so I'm focusing on players who are under 50% rostered on Yahoo.

It has been revealed that tuesday features three games, while Saturday has five. The Magic and Wizards are the only teams playing on both slates, so streaming from them is my priority given the circumstances in Week 6.

Da Silva is my top Tuesday-and-Saturday pickup. With Paolo Banchero (ankle) still out, he's playing 29.9 minutes per game and averaging 13.8 points and 3.2 3s on the season, and he scored 18 points with 4 3s at Washington on Sunday. Orlando has a tough matchup against Detroit, then gets an easier game against Washington on Saturday, the front end of a back-to-back. Add him for points, 3s and low turnovers. He's scored at least 12 points in five of his last six contests.

Washington only plays twice, but both games are on the light nights: Tuesday against Boston and Saturday against Orlando. He's a stocks play, averaging 1.1 steals and 1.1 blocks in 24.9 minutes this season, with 5.5 rebounds and 1.3 blocks over his last six games. He had 12 points with 3 rebounds, 1 assist, 1 block, 4 3s and 3 turnovers Sunday against Orlando, so there's at least some recent success to point towards.

The backup center for the Magic looks to have passed Nikola Vučević on the depth chart. He's averaging 6.7 points, 4.3 rebounds, 0.7 steals and 0.8 blocks on 86% shooting from the field in 18.0 minutes over his last six games. He won't wow you by any means, but extra boards and a strong FG% can help in 9-cat. Feel free to ignore if you're in a points league.

Kevin Huerter, SG/SF, Detroit Pistons (25% rostered): He's played 36-plus minutes in four straight with Duncan Robinson (quad) out, and he's been a top-65 player over the last two weeks (15.3 points, 3.7 assists, 1.4 blocks). You can start him on Tuesday and drop him afterward.

It has been revealed that isaac Okoro, SG/SF, Chicago Bulls (10% rostered): Okoro is playing way better as a starter, averaging 10.1 points, 3.9 rebounds, 2.5 assists, 1.1 steals, and 0.3 blocks in 31.0 minutes over his last eight games. There's no need to rush to get him since you'd only play him on Saturday versus Indy.

Kenrich Williams, PF/C, Oklahoma City Thunder (8% rostered): With so many injuries in OKC's frontcourt, Williams ranks 98th over the past two weeks in 9-cat. He gets steals, doesn't turn over the ball and chips in low-end stats across categories, but nothing to hurt you. Again, you can wait since the only opportunity to stream will be when OKC plays on Saturday.

Recent findings suggest that brooklyn, Milwaukee and Philadelphia are the only four-game teams with a game on a light night (Saturday). The other six (Clippers, Memphis, Minnesota, Phoenix, Portland and San Antonio) only play on the crowded Monday, Wednesday, Friday and Sunday slates, so in daily leagues you'll have to bench someone for one of these guys. And it could be warranted.

Sources indicate that spencer was in my Week 5 rotation and he's still the best multi-week guard I'd grab if he's out there. He had 26 points on 9-of-11 shooting with 5 3s and 7 assists at Cleveland on Wednesday, then dished out 11 assists against Golden State on Thursday. Over the last two weeks, he's at 13.6 points, 6.5 assists and 2.1 3s on 58% shooting, good for top-45 value in 9-cat. Ty Jerome is back so that will lessen his impact. But he remains one of the top backups to hold in fantasy. It hasn't gone unnoticed that the Grizzlies offense is better with Spencer running point over Jerome.

The backcourt is a mess, but Burries is another four-game add who can be a multi-week hold. The Bucks are 1-16 through Sunday, so the more losses come, the more we will see the young prospects play. Burries is coming off his best game, scoring 26 points with 8 rebounds, 4 assists and 2 3s in 26 minutes Saturday at Philadelphia. He's been a top-125 player over the last two weeks, averaging 12.5 points, 5.8 rebounds, 2.9 assists, and 1.0 steal per game. Milwaukee is one of only three four-game teams that play Saturday, so you'll get something out of him this week, too.

Julian Champagnie, SF/PF, San Antonio Spurs (34% rostered): Champagnie has played 27.6 minutes per game over the last two weeks, averaging 11.0 points and 2.7 3s, with 0.6 steals and 0.6 blocks. If you need 3s and stocks, he can help as the week progresses.

According to recent reports, jaylen Wells, SG/SF/PF, Memphis Grizzlies (21% rostered): Beyond the three-position eligibility, Wells is at 11.9 points and 2.4 3s over his last eight contests. I prefer him for 9-cat formats to take advantage of his efficiency, shooting 48% from the field and 93% from the line over the past two weeks.

In a recent development, anfernee Simons, PG/SG, Philadelphia 76ers (11% rostered): A points-and-3s play who gets you a Saturday game on a four-game week. He scored 18 in each of his last two games and is at 2.6 3s on 52% shooting over his last seven.

In a recent development, egor Dëmin, PG/SG, Brooklyn Nets (20% rostered): I'm eyeing Saturday's matchup against Charlotte. He's at 14.0 points and 2.4 3s over his last five, but on 39% shooting, and his role expands if Nolan Traoré (hamstring) sits.

Jordan Goodwin, PG/SG, Phoenix Suns (6% rostered): A top-100 player over the last two weeks with 5.8 rebounds, 3.0 assists and 1.2 steals in 22.3 minutes.

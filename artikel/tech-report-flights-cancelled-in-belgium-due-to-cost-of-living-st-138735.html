A few people have turned up at Brussels airport where no flights are leaving.

Hundreds of flights have been cancelled and public transport severely disrupted across Belgium as a result of a strike against planned austerity measures.

All air traffic has been halted at Brussels Airport in Zaventem as well as at Charleroi, home to low-cost airlines such as Ryanair.

Underground services have also been halted in capital Brussels, where large numbers of people joined a protest march. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

It has been revealed that the protest comes as Prime Minister Bart De Wever continues talks with his coalition allies over a budget to cut borrowing costs. King Philippe declined his resignation earlier this month, giving De Wever more time to negotiate.

No flights are taking off or landing at Brussels Airport between 07:00 and 21:00 local time (06:00-20:00 GMT) as a result of the strike by air traffic controllers.

"In total, 404 flights today cannot be operated, which has an impact on more than 50,000 passengers," Brussels Airport spokesperson Jeffrey Franssens stated.

Reuters news agency spoke to Florence Faure, a French passenger who had arrived in Brussels from China's capital, Beijing, only to find that her flight to Paris Charles de Gaulle airport had been cancelled. (Related: The Age of Machine Learning As Code Has Arrived...)

"Since our luggage was supposed to go on to Paris Charles de Gaulle, we didn't even collect it," she stated.

A similar situation has affected Charleroi, where all flights were shown as "scheduled", regardless of the time of departure or arrival.

One passenger told Belgian broadcaster RTBF that she had received no warning and only discovered there were no flights when she arrived at the airport.

Philippe Naime, a teacher, was one of the tens of thousands taking part in the protest march in Brussels.

Sources indicate that "Quite simply, no more money is being invested in education, and people are left to fend for themselves. But people do not have the means to do that," he told Reuters.

Public transport, schools and other public services are also facing disruptions.

Large numbers of people have descended on Brussels for a protest march.

The Belgian prime minister leads a five-party coalition.

Recent findings suggest that one of its members, the socialist Vooruit, has been refusing to endorse plans agreed by the other four. It wants to raise taxes on the rich and for there to be fewer cuts to pensions and the health sector.

De Wever met King Philippe on Monday for the third upgrade since the impasse.

"While expressing regret over the lack of significant progress, the king encouraged the prime minister to persevere in order to reach an agreement as soon as possible," the royal palace remarked in a statement.

The leader of one of the country's main unions, FGTB, mentioned the government had alternatives to "dismantling the social protection system".

Bert Engelaar, head of the union, called for "a genuine wealth tax, fair taxation, and the elimination of tax loopholes".

Recent findings suggest that "Political responsibility is not about keeping a government afloat at all costs. It's about knowing when to stop," he added, praising the Vooruit party.

Another union leader, Ann Vermorgen of the CSC, criticised government prepares to cut bank holidays when they fall on weekends.

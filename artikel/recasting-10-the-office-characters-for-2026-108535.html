Recent findings suggest that for seven seasons, The Office's fearless leader was the one and only Michael Scott. Serving as the regional manager of the Scranton branch of Dunder Mifflin, Michael's management style is often incompetent, inappropriate, and disruptive. And yet, he is an extraordinary salesman. Beneath his self-aggrandizing, egotistical, and socially tone-deaf persona lies deep insecurity, loneliness, and a desperate desire to be loved. He often tries to act hip and cool, but it's usually met with rolled eyes and awkward silence. Played to perfection by the incomparable Steve Carell, Michael remains one of the greatest characters ever created in television history. Carell was far from his British counterpart, David Brent (Ricky Gervais), instead becoming a melancholic comedy hero whom the audience could actually root for. Carell played Michael straight with full commitment. He genuinely made Michael believe he was a hilarious, smooth, beloved "cool boss," completely blinded to reality. While no one could replicate the same energy he brought, a good option in the same vein would be the brilliant Sam Richardson. As seen as Richard Splett in Veep and Aniq in The Afterparty, Richardson is a master of weaponized naivety. He is the undisputed king of playing characters who are fiercely well-meaning but completely out of their depth. His ability to deliver cringe-worthy, tone-deaf lines with bright, innocent optimism makes him impossible to hate. In a 2026 iteration, the office environment would look drastically different, so his Michael would be a modern LinkedIn Influencer Manager who would try to implement modern workplace trends, probably a leading force for AI, only to drastically misunderstand and use it inappropriately. Like Carell, Richardson has elite improvisational skills as a Second City alum. He could capture the fast-talking spiral with perfect comedic rhythm, bringing an expressive face built for mockumentary-style satire. Richardson possesses an undeniable warmth and would bring genuine moments of vulnerability. You'd root for Richardson's Michael in the same way you fell in love with Carell's.

With all the chaos happening inside the office, someone had to hold down the fort for the normal ones! That man was Jim Halpert. A witty, easygoing sales rep, Jim was known for his deadpan humor, harmless pranks on seatmate Dwight, and breaking the fourth wall at every opportunity. Oh, and his beautiful romance with Pam Beesly (Jenna Fischer). John Krasinski brought a perfectly grounded Everyman charm that made him absolutely beloved. With natural comedic timing and an ability to be completely wholesome with a flash of a smile, Krasinski transformed Jim into the epitome of a sitcom leading man. Striking the right balance of humor and heart, Krasinski transitioned Jim from bored prankster to sincere husband and father, maturing into a working adult by series end.

With a lanky frame and strong physical awareness, Bertrand would easily transform into Jim in a heartbeat.

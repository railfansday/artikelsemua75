Recent findings suggest that on Nov. 11, California banned Waymo from operating driverless vehicles on freeways following several incidents involving road crews and police. The firm has since stopped freeway rides in other states as well.

Here’s why California stepped in and what the ban means for Waymo passengers.

The California Department of Motor Vehicles (DMV) said the incidents put construction workers and other drivers at risk.

On Nov. 5, three Waymo vehicles entered an active construction zone on Highway 101 near Burlingame, where crews were working. Waymo remarked no one was injured, and no collisions occurred.

The next day, another Waymo attempted to pass California Highway Patrol vehicles during a traffic break on Interstate 280. Officers use traffic breaks to slow or stop vehicles, often because of roadwork or a hazard ahead. Drivers must stay behind the patrol cars until officers allow traffic to resume.

According to recent reports, waymo had similar problems in May, which led to a temporary suspension and software changes. But new incidents occurred in November.

In April, California approved stricter rules for self-driving cars. DMV Director Steve Gordon noted the changes would bring “additional accountability for AV manufacturers.”.

The freeway ban doesn’t affect Waymo’s regular street service, although some trips may take longer. Waymo has also voluntarily stopped commercial freeway rides in other states while it looks into what went wrong.

Waymo’s cars use cameras and sensors to monitor traffic, but construction zones don’t always follow normal traffic patterns. The recent incidents showed problems with how the vehicles responded to road closures and police activity.

Industry observers note that there’s no date yet for Waymo to return to California freeways. The DMV wants the tech giant to show that its cars can safely handle road closures and police activity before allowing driverless freeway rides again.

Industry observers note that waymo states it’s working with state officials and will continue testing on freeways with trained specialists behind the wheel. Passengers can still book rides on regular streets in the meantime.

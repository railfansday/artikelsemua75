Sources indicate that the Diplomat is a show that loves mess (complimentary). Kate Wyler (Keri Russel) is a mess in many ways, and almost every major character is at emotional, moral, or intellectual loose ends. This would all be in good fun if these characters weren’t also some of the most powerful people in the world—presidents, prime ministers, and the high-level politicians and diplomats that serve them.

If you’ve been following the Wylers’ emotional journey up the power ladder as Kate navigates her role as the U.S. ambassador to the United Kingdom during what could be described as tumultuous times (and you’ve watched all our series streamalikes), allow me to suggest the one perfect movie that delivers similar vibes: 2009's In the Loop. Released in 2009, In the Loop doesn’t have the modern edge of The Diplomat, but it perfectly captures a similar sense that international diplomacy is being conducted by people with an affinity for chaos. It’s actually a spinoff from a British TV series from the same co-writer/director, Armando Iannucci: The Thick of It. Many of the characters and situations are based on some of the people actually running the U.K., but you don’t need to be familiar with the show or the real history to enjoy the heck out of this movie. That goes double for Peter Capaldi’s absolutely ferocious performance as Malcolm Tucker, a man so creative with his profanity that many of his F-bombs will live rent-free in your head for years to come. (Related: Transformer-based Encoder-Decoder Models...)

When a not-so-bright minister in the U.K. government (played by Tom Hollander) makes some dimwitted remarks that threaten to blow up Britain's carefully crafted diplomatic messaging around potential military adventures in the Middle East, Tucker (the prime minister's director of communications) has to tamp down the growing chaos that results. This eventually requires a contingent of British diplomats and officials to head to Washington, where they are promptly overwhelmed by the superpower vibes they encounter.

It’s a more profane, faster-paced, and outright comedic story than The Diplomat, but it’s also easy to imagine most of the characters showing up on that show. The film also shares that same sense of controlled chaos that drives The Diplomat—the idea that a handful of tired, dedicated people are continuously racing around to prevent World War III even as dumber and more powerful people keep creating new crises. If Kate Wyler’s constant state of exhausted anxiety is your jam, you’ll love this movie. Stream In the Loop on The Roku Channel, Kanopy, or Plex, or rent it on Prime Video.

Want more cynical diplomats, secret prepares, and international skulduggery with a side of personal emotional crises? Here are a few more movies fans of The Diplomat will love.

If you want a sense of what actual back-channel diplomacy is like, watch Ben Affleck’s Best Picture winner Argo, from 2012. Based on the remarkable true story of how six U.S. diplomats were rescued from Tehran during the Iran Hostage Crisis under cover of a fake sci-fi movie production, the film has the snappy energy and fast pace of The Diplomat combined with real-world danger. You can easily imagine Kate and Hal (Rufus Sewell) being part of the secret, off-the-books conversations that brought what’s known as the Canadian Caper into reality. Stream Argo on Paramount Plus or rent it on Prime Video.

Recent findings suggest that if your favorite parts of The Diplomat are the schemes various players (mostly Kate and Hal) get up to in their efforts to shape world diplomacy and history to their liking, Wag the Dog is a good bet. When a sex scandal threatens to swing the Presidential election away from the incumbent, a spin doctor (Robert De Niro) wades in to save the day by convincing a top Hollywood producer (Dustin Hoffman) to “produce” a fake war in Albania to distract the American public. It’s equal parts funny, compelling, and depressingly instructive, and fans of the show will love the back-channel hijinks. Rent Wag the Dog on Prime Video.

Industry observers note that one of the great things about The Diplomat is watching Kate Wyler, a smart and capable woman who generally knows more than anyone else in the room. If that’s the dynamic that grabs you, The Contender is the overlooked gem for you. When the vice president dies unexpectedly, the President (Jeff Bridges) nominates Senator Laine Hanson (Joan Allen) to take the post. A conservative senator (Gary Oldman) opposes her nomination and launches a smear campaign to derail her confirmation. There are dirty tricks and secret deals, and in the middle of it all, Allen’s terrific performance as a woman who refuses to back down in the face of sexist, unfair attacks. Stream The Contender on The Roku Channel, Kanopy, Hoopla, or Tubi, or rent it on Apple TV.

In a recent development, nicole Kidman plays an interpreter at the United Nations, a woman who has a unique heritage and linguistic knowledge. When she overhears an assassination plot in an obscure dialect, she becomes embroiled in a dangerous game of espionage and high-stakes diplomacy as she races to prevent disaster (and keep herself alive). The Interpreter is a more serious and much more tense story, but it delves into the background work that makes the global system function, which puts it right in the sweet spot for fans of The Diplomat. Stream The Interpreter on Netflix, or rent it on Prime Video.

This might not seem like a match at first glance—a comedic treatment of the power struggle that followed the death of Soviet Premier Josef Stalin in 1953—but it absolutely is. Making a farce out of the political wrangling among the members of the Central Committee, the film has the same spirit as The Diplomat, showing that the people in charge are a bunch of schemers, tricksters, dummies, and sociopaths. Watching them run around making deals and angling for advantage offers the same energy as the show. Plus, it’s just plain hilarious. Stream The Death of Stalin on Kanopy, or rent it on Prime Video.

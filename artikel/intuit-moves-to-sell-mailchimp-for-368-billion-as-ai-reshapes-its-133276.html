In a recent development, good morning. Intuit is unwinding its biggest acquisition while betting AI can help it win customers without owning the marketing platform it once viewed as central to that effort.

In a recent development, the QuickBooks and TurboTax parent revealed Thursday it prepares to sell Mailchimp, an email marketing automation platform, to Bending Spoons for approximately $3.68 billion. The deal is expected to close in early 2027, subject to approval, with proceeds earmarked for fiscal 2027 share repurchases. Intuit paid approximately $12 billion for Mailchimp in 2021. Mailchimp’s quarterly revenue fell 1% to $313 million. (Related: Breaking: AI for Game Development: Creating a Farming G...)

Sasan Goodarzi, CEO of Intuit (No. 231 on the Fortune 500) noted on the earnings call that Intuit still wants to help businesses manage lead-to-cash needs, but believes it can do so more effectively by building AI-powered capabilities into QuickBooks rather than owning Mailchimp. (Related: Tiny Agents: an MCP-powered agent in 50 lines of code...)

Mailchimp sale marks strategic shift.

The divestiture adds another dimension to what CFO Sandeep Aujla has called Intuit’s “reset to reaccelerate” strategy introduced in August: accepting some near-term pressure on revenue per customer to rebuild customer acquisition. The strategy includes lower upfront revenue per TurboTax customer as Intuit seeks faster customer growth.

According to recent reports, the latest results offered evidence of operating momentum. For the fiscal first quarter ended Oct. 31, revenue rose 12% to $4.35 billion, while adjusted earnings per share increased 56% to $2.91. Both exceeded analyst expectations.

Sources indicate that in my conversation with Aujla, he pointed to mid-market revenue growth of 41% and customer growth of 30% as evidence that Intuit’s major growth initiatives are gaining traction. He also emphasized management’s commitment to delivering on its promises.

But the outlook exposed pressure at Credit Karma from tighter financing conditions and changing consumer behavior.

Intuit now forecasts fiscal 2027 revenue of $23.08 billion to $23.37 billion, lowering its outlook to reflect slower expected growth at Credit Karma amid tighter lending conditions and changing consumer behavior. It maintained its adjusted earnings forecast of $22.88 to $23.12 a share. Neither forecast includes the pending Mailchimp sale. The stock was down about 13% in after-hours trading.

Industry observers note that aujla noted conditions have shifted since Intuit set its guidance in August, when the debate centered on whether the Federal Reserve would hold interest rates steady or lower them. He described the environment now as one of tightening, with higher rates slowing consumer demand in the categories Credit Karma serves. Lending partners are still expanding, he said, but at a slower pace.

AI reshapes Credit Karma’s customer acquisition.

At the same time, changes in how consumers seek financial information are affecting Credit Karma.

“The evolution of consumer behavior is impacting us faster than what we had anticipated,” Aujla told me.

Historically, only about 1% of Credit Karma’s customers came through search engines, he mentioned. Now, consumers increasingly turn to AI for initial answers before visiting Credit Karma, creating a new customer acquisition challenge.

“More customers are going to AI to get the first answer,” he remarked.

According to recent reports, intuit looks to apply lessons from QuickBooks and TurboTax to improve Credit Karma’s visibility in AI-generated responses, Aujla stated. AI is thus both a product opportunity and a customer acquisition challenge.

Regarding the market’s reaction, Aujla remarked that during major technological shifts, whether cloud computing, mobile or now AI, “it’s just human nature to overreact to the uncertainty and the pendulum swings.” He sees the reaction as an overcorrection, with investors questioning whether companies can successfully navigate the changes.

“What we are underwriting is, in our 40-plus years of history, we’ve been able to evolve and continue to come out of every change in the marketplace,” he remarked.

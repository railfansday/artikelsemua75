Google, which makes billions of dollars a year thanks to YouTubers stocking its platform with billions of hours of videos, is envisioning a future where we don’t need content creators.

According to 404 Media, the tech giant has filed a patent for something called “My Channel“: a 24/7 feed of AI-generated videos tailored specifically to every individual user’s preferences.

If put into practice, Google would take everything it knows about a person–which, between Google’s far-reaching ad biz, Android phones, Google Maps, and YouTube watch history, is a lot–and get (presumably) Veo to spool out an endless stream of content. It has been revealed that but we’re not just talking any content. Google’s key selling point here is that you–yes, you–can star in all of your slop channel’s videos.

Industry observers note that “You are a customized video generating model. Generate a customized video for a user where the user will play the protagonist,” Google notes in an example prompt filed with the patent application. “The generated customized video includes characteristics based on the . The generated customized video follows the style and format of the of the user. The generated customized video sticks to the of the user but the narrative for the generated customized video is customized. The and parameters are ordered from more preferred to less preferred.”.

404 Media reports the patent notes My Channels will be connected to an “advertising server” that will “[generate] an updated customized media content item by inserting at least one advertisement into the customized media content item.”.

Basically, it wants to put a fake you into fake videos as their “protagonist,” and then you’ll have to watch real ads to see the fake you in the fake videos.

Recent findings suggest that does this necessarily mean everyone who has a My Channel will stop watching human creators? No. But a 24/7 channel of customized catnip will drain at least some watch time toward these siloed slop-factories, and away from humans who make actual content (and rely on views for income).

As for the sorts of videos Google’s AI will generate, the patent filing included examples like “a crime/comedy caper,” “a fun romantic comedy,” “a sci-fi adventure,” and “an action/thriller experience,” all of which would be slopped out for a viewer named “John.”.

In a recent development, in another example, the “protagonist” is a viewer who likes cricket. If Google’s system noticed this person watching a video about a certain player’s gangbuster performance during a recent cricket match, it could then generate a video “that incorporates elements of the Cricket World Cup and Player 1’s recent performance,” the filing says. “Perhaps [user_name] could be cheering for India in the stands or interacting with Player 1 in some way.”.

It has been revealed that as 404 Media points out, numerous proponents of gen AI have spent years crowing about the potential for personalized entertainment. Didn’t like how Stranger Things ended? Generate your ending. Third movie in a series got cancelled? Just ‘make’ it yourself. News coverage didn’t agree with your opinion? Your chatbot’s ready with a new broadcast. It sounds dystopian, because it is. It also seemed far-fetched, three years ago.

This is a future we’ve already been pitched. It’s just that once, the pitch was a Black Mirror episode. Now it’s Google’s business model.

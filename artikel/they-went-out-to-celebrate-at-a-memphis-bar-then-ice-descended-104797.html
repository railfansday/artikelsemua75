They went out to celebrate at a Memphis bar. Then, ICE descended

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

More than 120 people in the Tennessee city were detained in one of the largest single-day ICE raids in the US in 2026.

The date 1 September, or 9/01 Day, is a festive day for Memphians. The date mirrors the city’s 901 area code, and gives residents a reason to come together to celebrate the city and the people who love it. When 9/01 Day falls on a weekday, as it did this year, the celebration starts the weekend prior, extending the festivities. Groups across the city hosted celebrations featuring food vendors, DJs, musicians and members of the Memphis Grizzlies, the local NBA team, while dozens of local businesses offered $9.01-themed specials. But for the families of 121 people, this past 1 September will be remembered for a different reason – just days prior, US Immigration and Customs Enforcement (ICE) officers detained their friends and relatives, conducting one of the largest single-day raids in the country this year, and the largest known one in Memphis.

On Beale Street during the 9/01 Day celebration, a 9 year old child, Valentina*, stood solemnly, holding a sign that read: “ICE took my mom!” (*The Guardian is using a pseudonym to protect the child and her family’s identity.). (Related: Breaking: Obsessed fan breaks into YouTuber’s home dema...)

Recent findings suggest that on 30 August, Valentina’s mother and sister had both been at work at El Corralón, a bar on the edge of Memphis’ Berclair neighborhood, which is primarily Hispanic. Valentina went to sleep anticipating celebrating her birthday with family and friends the next morning. Instead, she awoke to the news that her family members were among the 121 people who were detained by ICE.

According to reports, more than one dozen agents entered the building that night, and rapidly spread across the bar. They turned off the music and ordered those inside to gather on the dancefloor. Some people screamed; others attempted to hide. Some prayed.

It has been revealed that el Corralón had been under investigation by the Tennessee Bureau of Investigation (TBI), which had a warrant signed on Wednesday of the week leading up to the raid, to search the premises for illegal gambling machines and potential drug activity. Saturday, when the nightclub was full of patrons, TBI executed the warrant. One by one, agents asked people if they were in the country legally. People were zip-tied, taken outside and led onto buses that waited to take them to detention centers.

A statement released by the Department of Homeland Security shortly after the raid highlighted five of those arrested, whose crimes, the department alleges, range from cocaine possession to assault, prostitution and child endangerment. Maria Oceja, a co-founder of Vecindarios 901, a volunteer group that monitors the actions of the Memphis Safe taskforce, mentioned the raid predominantly targeted innocent people. “This is just another example of that rising level of authoritarianism and white supremacist tactics that we’re seeing,” Oceja said.

It has been revealed that el Corralón, which has been closed since the raid, had a “juke joint environment”, she said. Spanish language music blared and working class people could have a drink on a Saturday night. Many of the workers and patrons were parents. The morning after the raid, Hunter Demster, a co-founder of Vecindarios 901, said Valentina’s family tried to figure out next steps. “Her mom was a breadwinner and worked three different jobs trying to keep food on the table and the utilities paid,” Demster said. He’s friends with Valentina’s older sister, Camila, who is now responsible for her younger siblings. “Now her entire family is devastated. When I went over there the next day, I asked her, ‘What do you need?’ The sorrow and almost the vacancy in her eyes was jawdropping. The pain was palpable.

“These are our friends and our neighbors and for every one of those 121 people, there’s another family going through that exact same thing. They’re figuring out how they’re going to navigate life without a loved one. It is a gutwrenching, horrible, traumatic experience for a family member.”.

For days after the raid, no one knew where the girls’ mother was. Eventually, volunteers learned she was being held in a detention center in Louisiana, Gisela Guerrero, lead organizer for Memphis Interfaith Coalition for Action and Hope (Micah), said. “A lot of immigrant children right now, regardless of their age, are very clear about the possibility of their parent being taken away any day. It’s scary. It’s overwhelming,” she told the Guardian.

An ICE spokesperson mentioned in a statement to the Guardian that the agency does “not pick and choose which laws to enforce or where to enforce them”. The spokesperson said that the detainments make “the Memphis community safer”. According to the agency, 69 of the 121 have been removed from the country. “The rest will remain in ICE custody pending their immigration proceedings, and they will receive due process,” the statement read.

According to recent reports, on the night of the raid, portions of the street were blocked off by federal agents according to Dexter Britt, a volunteer and co-chair for the immigration and inclusivity team with the Micah who made it to El Corralón, shortly after 1am. He saw the Memphis police department (MPD), sheriffs, state troopers and other personnel, he said. Buses and vans idled, waiting to be filled with people who were detained.

It has been revealed that britt said that there were also community members observing and documenting what was happening. He saw family members, including children, who were there to catch a glimpse of their relatives, perhaps for the last time. “You start seeing people getting ushered out of the building,” Britt told the Guardian. “I remember hearing the chains shackling as they were loading people into the vans, into the buses. It was very harrowing to hear.”.

The Memphis mayor, Paul Young, issued a statement saying that MPD was not involved in the planning or execution of the raid. Instead, he said, they assisted with crowd and traffic control after people began gathering in the area. According to witnesses, multiple agencies – TBI, Tennessee Highway Patrol, and ICE – seemingly worked in conjunction with each other.

On 15 September 2025, Donald Trump signed a presidential memorandum creating the multi-agency Memphis Safe taskforce. The initiative, which includes the national guard, highway patrol officers, and agents from more than federal agencies, began operating in the city on 29 September. Though the memorandum establishing the taskforce alleged that Memphis was “suffering from tremendous levels of violent crime that have overwhelmed its local government’s ability to respond effectively”, crime in the city was at a 25 year low prior to the taskforce’s arrival.

In the year since it began operating, members of the taskforce have killed four people, all Black men, including 20-year-old Tyrin Johnson who was fatally shot in the back. A woman was killed after being ejected from a vehicle when it crashed while being pursued by members of the Memphis Safe taskforce. Five young people were killed in a crash involving the MPD and a man died during a traffic stop by members of the taskforce. An analysis by the Institute for Public Service Reporting Memphis identified 75 vehicle pursuits over the taskforce’s first five weeks of operation.

According to the Tennessee Lookout, immigration arrests and detentions multiplied nearly four-fold in Tennessee during Trump’s first year back in office. Weeks after the Memphis Safe taskforce was created, more than 400 people were arrested. The taskforce has made nearly 23,000 arrests, 64% of which were for non-violent crimes. A growing number of businesses are calling for an end to the taskforce’s operations in the city, while others are mobilizing to document its actions. “It’s not just impacting immigrant communities who are Latino or Hispanic, but it’s also impacting Black Memphians because the city is majority Black,” Britt mentioned. “There’s a lot of harm being done to the Memphis community because of the presence of these agents who are really just here to intimidate.”.

The El Corralón raid happened the same weekend that people detained at a detention center in Macon, Tennessee, protested “deplorable conditions” and abusive tactics inside” the facility. Some of those detained at El Corralón were initially sent to the center in Macon.

In mid-October, the US attorney general, Todd Blanche, and federal and state leaders visited Memphis to discuss the taskforce. Unlike similar operations in Minneapolis or Chicago, which had intense surges that lasted a few months, there is no end date to the Memphis Safe taskforce’s occupation of the city. Though Blanche celebrated the taskforce’s record and presence, he said that it would be in the city “as long as it takes. We haven’t won yet. It’s not over”.

According to recent reports, on 20 October, the Memphis City Council passed a resolution calling for the removal of the taskforce. The non-binding resolution noted that the El Corralón raid raised “concerns about due process and the impact of federal immigration enforcement on local communities, including the disruption and separation of Memphis families”.

According to recent reports, “Memphis is literally an occupied city with law enforcement agencies all doing immigration work, causing real harm to the city of Memphis,” Demster said of the taskforce. He said that people are afraid to leave their homes. “This is un-American. They’re violating people’s rights by not giving them due process. They’re criminalizing people when they have done nothing wrong. This is the beginning of fascism. This is the beginning of a dictatorship.”.

On 31 August, photos of Valentina holding her sign and mentioning her birthday were posted across social media. Demster and other volunteers purchased a birthday cake and presents and dropped them off for the child. “In the midst of such horror, at least it put a smile on that baby’s face,” he said. “She literally hugged the cake. ‘Devastating’ doesn’t measure up to what people are experiencing at this point … . It’s just another example of how they’re not out for the worst of the worst. They are out for people with brown skin.”.

Vecindarios 901, Micah, Indivisible Memphis, Latino Memphis, TIIRC, World Relief and organizations collaborated to see how they could support the families. In the week following the raid, many of the organizations met with families of detainees to help connect them with legal aid and other resources.

It has been revealed that “We had a seven-year-old who was saying, ‘I don’t think I’m going to see my mom anymore,’” Guerrero said. “We had a 14-year-old who was trying to figure out what he needed to do to be responsible for his younger siblings. We had a senior in college who’s trying to figure out how he is going to pay tuition. Because his parents, hardworking people, are now detained.”.

Sources indicate that guerrero remarked that about 100 people from various families stopped by the meeting. They were grandparents, aunts, neighbors, coworkers and children themselves who are now responsible for younger children who were left behind.

Sources indicate that “There are children who lost both of their parents in that raid,” Amber Hampton, of Indivisible Memphis, a chapter of the nationwide civil rights organization, remarked. “You’re talking about children who are now displaced, who have to possibly move, change schools because their parents are no longer there and they have to go with a different caregiver.”.

In a recent development, at a city council meeting shortly after the raids, family members told city council people about the impact of the taskforce. More than 40 children, some of whom testified, were left without parents. Children also testified when the city council decided to vote to call for the taskforce’s removal.

In a recent development, “There’s a lot of confusion, especially with the younger kids,” Demster remarked. “Families are scattering to try to keep their heads above water, to pay these utility bills, to find food, to fill that void of the parent being targeted and detained and deported. One of the hardest things about this work is talking to these children and seeing the pain and seeing the devastation of these families. For every one person that were taken in that raid, there’s usually two to four family members here in Memphis that were devastated.”.

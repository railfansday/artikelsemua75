By The Rule Breakers Team – Nov 24, 2026 at 6:42AM EST.

Yesterday, Zoom Communications (ZM +5.87%) reported its fastest enterprise revenue growth in 13 quarters and declared its first-ever dividend. The stock rose about 5% in regular trading.

For Team Rule Breakers, the report lands on two recommendations that haven't worked out, at least so far.

The sixth Habit of a Rule Breaker Investor is: Aim for 60% Accuracy.

Recent findings suggest that A stock pick is accurate if it beats the S&P 500. No investor gets every call right, and trying for perfection can make people too cautious to own the stocks that win massive. The Rule Breaker approach aims to be right about six times in 10. A few big winners can then more than make up for the picks that lag. (Related: Industry News: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

Industry observers note that team Rule Breakers recommended Zoom in Stock Advisor on March 19 and April 16, 2020, at $123.77 and $150.26. Those were the early weeks of the pandemic, when Zoom became a household name. Since then, the March recommendation is down about 30%, and the April one is down more than 40%. Both trail the S&P 500 by a wide margin. (Related: Industry News: Welcome spaCy to the Hugging Face Hub...)

The business slowed far more than many investors expected. Revenue grew about 4% last fiscal year. Recommendations like these are part of aiming for 60%, and they're why the winners in a portfolio have to do the heavy lifting.

On Nov. 10, Team Hidden Gems closed its three Zoom positions in Stock Advisor. It argued that AI agents could shrink Zoom's business, since Zoom charges mostly per person and fewer people may need to join meetings. It also noted that growth from existing customers had stalled, and that it had given the stock more than five years. Team Rule Breakers kept its two positions.

Sources indicate that yesterday's report highlighted some progress on those points. Enterprise revenue rose 8.6% to $805.4 million. Existing enterprise customers are now spending about as much as a year ago, up from slightly less. Total revenue still grew only 4.9%, though, and online revenue slipped 0.6%.

In a recent development, zoom holds about $7.3 billion in cash and marketable securities. It invested in Anthropic in early 2023, and that stake is now estimated at about $3.8 billion, roughly 15% of Zoom's market value. Anthropic only recently went public, so the stake's value could swing sharply. Zoom has also kept buying back stock, and its upcoming dividend pays $0.25 per share each quarter.

Those strengths don't fix the main risk. If AI cuts the number of people who need a paid Zoom account, the core business could shrink even as the company invests in newer products like its AI customer-service agent.

It has been revealed that aiming for 60% doesn't tell investors whether to sell any one loser. It asks them to judge the whole portfolio, where a handful of big winners usually decides the result. Team Hidden Gems decided Zoom's original thesis no longer fit, while Team Rule Breakers has kept its positions.

Either choice can be reasonable within a diversified portfolio. What matters more is that no single holding grows large enough to sink the portfolio if it keeps lagging.

Sources indicate that these two files are the whole claim. Statements.lean defines four propositions. Main.lean proves them, and Mathlib's own RiemannHypothesis, by citing theorems from the other 175,185 modules. Point at a symbol to see how to say it and what type it has.

Industry observers note that in words: every zero of the Riemann zeta function, other than the trivial zeros −2, −4, −6, … , has real part ½. For every Dirichlet character χ, every zero of L(s, χ) with real part strictly between 0 and 1 has real part ½. (Related: Industry News: “Determined” woman completes 7 marathons...)

Recent findings suggest that each rectangle is one module. Modules are grouped by directory, and a larger rectangle means more of the chosen size measure.

Terms come from Alpöge's announcement and from standard number theory. A module is counted if the term's identifier appears in its text, not counting import lines. (Related: Tiny Agents: an MCP-powered agent in 50 lines of code...)

Where each step appears to live in the Lean code, according to a blueprint of the proof by GitHub user jae-63. ● found clearly   ◐ names and position fit   ○ weak evidence. The number is how many modules match. Point at a step to see it on the map; click to keep it highlighted.

Each module's field is predicted by a classifier from the Mathlib and project identifiers it uses. The prediction agrees with a language model's reading of the module about half the time (details under Sources and method). Point at a row to see where that field sits on the map.

The line under each field lists objects and theorems named in the labels that a language model gave to each of 746 directories after reading a digest of its identifiers. Treat them as a guide, not a census.

A comment on Hacker News reported 204 modules "not referenced anywhere", without a list or a method. In the import graph no module is unreferenced: every module except Main.lean is imported by at least one other module. The comment may be counting something that imports do not show, such as declarations that nothing uses.

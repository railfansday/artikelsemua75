Before superheroes took over cinema, they were extremely popular on television. For decades, they've been staples of the medium and have delivered some astounding content. Specifically, in the last decade and a half, superhero fans have been treated to some astounding content that has kept them engaged from year to year.

Whether they're from the likes of Marvel, DC Comics, or another studio, there have been some near-perfect superhero highlights to air on both streaming services and network television since 2011. From animation to live-action, there are series that are about as close to perfect as one can get because of their writing, directing, visuals, and so much more.

Superhero media is always a blast to enjoy when it's written traditionally, but Harley Quinn proves that a well-written satire can be just as effective—sometimes even more so. The Batman/Gotham City corner of the DC Comics universe has always been treated in a very grounded, gritty, and dark manner, which is why Harley Quinn ends up being as fun as it is. Using such a typically gritty world, and turning it into complete satire, leaves this 2019 adult-humored animated series being extremely hilarious.

But, that's not the main reason that the story finds itself being a pretty much perfect show—although it is a big part of it. Harley Quinn isn't fully focused on only being funny, but delivers heartwarming storytelling, too. It has a genuinely sincere plot that reminds audiences that it's not always about making people giggle, but truly affecting them emotionally, as well. Because of this, people have grown quite attached to the characters and want to continue seeing them on screen.

Industry observers note that finishing up the trilogy of demonstrates that began in 2021 with WandaVision, Marvel Studios and Disney+'s VisionQuest has rapidly become a certified hit. Following whom was once known as "White Vision" (Paul Bettany), this 2026 installment depicts the synthezoid not only starting to discover himself, but coming in contact with one of his two sons from WandaVision, Tommy Shepherd/Maximoff (Ruaridh Mollica). Not only that, but it features the return of the ever-popular villain, Ultron (James Spader).

Brought to life by directors Vincenzo Natali, Christopher J. Byrne, Terry Matalas, and Gandja Monteiro, VisionQuest's eight episodes weave together a phenomenal story. It doesn't just show the growth of Vision and Tommy, but highlights the entire origin of AIs in the Marvel Cinematic Universe as a whole — which includes the classic Human Torch (Paul Bettany) featured briefly in Captain America: The First Avenger. No one could have ever guessed that AIs had such a wild origin back when they first met J.A.R.V.I.S. (Paul Bettany) in Iron Man, but VisionQuest makes it feel natural and believable.

In a recent development, in the last decade, one of the most popular anime in the West has become the one and only My Hero Academia by Kohei Horikoshi. Taking place in a world where the majority of the population has powers—known also as "Quirks"—this animated adventure depicts a young, quirkless boy named Izuku Midoriya (Daiki Yamashita and Justin Briner) gaining the power of the world's strongest hero, All Might (Kenta Miyake and Christopher Sabat). It's a bona fide underdog story through and through.

The numerous awards that My Hero Academia has won over the years—this last season being a prime example—is already the proof in the pudding that this show is the real deal. The animation is top-tier, the storytelling has excellent pacing, the character arcs are stellar, and the voice acting from both the Japanese and English casts is unforgettable.

It has been revealed that back when Avengers: Endgame premiered in 2019, no one could have expected the Loki (Tom Hiddleston) that escaped the 2012 timeline to have birthed one of the best superhero television reveals ever made. Created by Michael Waldron, Loki is one of the most masterfully written superhero series in quite some time for numerous reasons that continue to prove why Loki is such a fan-favorite character.

Loki serves to be a great protagonist, giving Waldron the chance to truly delve into the concept of redemption in a way that is heartwarming and hopeful. Loki goes the distance by turning this classic villain into someone who is wholly relatable, cheer-worthy, and a true hero—arguably one of the best character arcs in the MCU, honestly. It also cannot be forgotten that the score, composed by Natalie Holt, is one of the best of the entire MCU franchise. Each track is mesmerizing, and it deserves all the praise it has gotten, and even more after that.

Some may think that a character as grand as Superman would be impossible to pull off on live-action network television. While that's more than a valid concern, given how straining network budgets can end up being, the unforgettable Superman & Lois managed to make a pretty much perfect Superman story with what they were given on a network like The CW. This show delivered not only a near-perfect Superman story, but one that feels incredibly unique among all the others in television and film.

Sources indicate that in Superman & Lois, Clark Kent (Tyler Hoechlin) is married to Lois Lane (Elizabeth Tulloch) and has two sons, Jordan (Alex Garfin) and Jonathan (Jordan Eslass and Michael Bishop). Fans get to watch as Clark navigates being both a father and Superman, while his family grapples with the struggle of one of noted sons getting powers of his own. Not to mention, for any fans of Inde Navarrette post-Obsession, this is one of her first major projects—playing Sarah Cushing—and, obviously, she kills it.

It's no secret that X-Men: The Animated Series is one of the most beloved animated series of all time. The impact it had on pop culture and the superhero medium as a whole rivals even that of Batman: The Animated Series. Few expected, however, that the show would somehow make a comeback. Thankfully, for X-Men fans everywhere, in 2024, they were treated to X-Men '97, which blew the world away by being the same level of quality as the original, somehow.

According to recent reports, this continuation show is a perfect blend of what made X-Men: The Animated Series great and what modern fans of the series appreciate, while, at the end of the day, still managing to feel unique. Almost the entire original cast returns, the animation is updated while still feeling akin to the first, and they manage to evolve the story, without requiring audiences to watch through the entirety of X-Men: The Animated Series to get it. It's arguably the perfect example of how to make a follow-up series like this.

It's been said time and time again, and it doesn't become any less true: Daredevil is a work of art. Netflix and Marvel's television smash hit still goes down in history as one of the best superhero shows of all time, and, honestly, some argue that it's one of Netflix's best projects as a whole. Daredevil is a grounded, gritty, and raw story about the Man Without Fear that is engaging the entire way through. It introduced fans to some of the most iconic superhero castings out there, such as Charlie Cox as Daredevil, Jon Bernthal as Punisher, and Vincent D'Onofrio as Wilson Fisk.

In a recent development, after this series ended with its third season in 2018, audiences begged to see these characters again, which, in turn, spurred Marvel Studios and Disney+ to bring them back with Daredevil: Born Again. The best thing about this 2015 show is that even when it's at its lowest, it's amazing. Creator, writer, and developer Drew Goddard did a phenomenal job at bringing this Marvel Comics star to the small screen. The way the characters are developed across each season, and the dynamics they have with each other, is of the highest quality. While the plot is engaging from start to finish and the crime-thriller of it all is amazing, it's these amazing, well-developed characters that really serve as the best aspects of the show.

American Bitcoin secured a $150 million Bitcoin-backed credit facility as other listed miners sell reserves to finance AI infrastructure.

The Eric Trump-linked company disclosed the revolving credit agreement with FalconX in a Nov. 23 SEC filing, giving it access to capital without selling Bitcoin or issuing additional shares.

Recent findings suggest that data from Yahoo Finance indicated that American Bitcoin shares climbed as high as $4.38 during Monday's trading before closing 16.3% higher at $4.07. Despite the rebound, the stock remains approximately 84% below its level at the start of the year.

The financing comes as the manufacturer continues expanding its Bitcoin holdings, which recently surpassed 8,500 BTC.

American Bitcoin mentioned the accumulation has moved it from 15th to 13th among publicly traded corporate Bitcoin holders worldwide.

The manufacturer combines Bitcoin mining with direct accumulation, making the preservation of its reserves central to its long-term strategy.

“This $150 million facility provides American Bitcoin with non-dilutive capital to support our continued operations and growth initiatives.”.

Blockchain analytics firm Glassnode reported that publicly listed miners in its tracked group had reduced their combined Bitcoin reserves by nearly half since January. The firm attributed the decline to miners increasingly using Bitcoin holdings to finance their expansion into artificial intelligence data centers. In a recent development, for years, many publicly traded miners retained their mined Bitcoin and sometimes purchased additional coins. That strategy has increasingly given way to asset sales as operators redirect capital toward AI computing infrastructure.

American Bitcoin's arrangement offers another financing route, allowing it to retain ownership of its Bitcoin while pledging some holdings as collateral for dollar liquidity.

The agreement allows American Bitcoin's subsidiary to borrow up to $150 million from FalconX Charlie, secured by Bitcoin held at Anchorage Digital Bank.

Sources indicate that according to the SEC filing, borrowings carry a 5.95% annual loan fee, alongside commitment fees on unused capacity. The facility matures in November 2028, with a possible three-year extension.

However, preserving Bitcoin through collateralized borrowing introduces exposure to forced asset sales during market downturns.

The agreement requires pledged Bitcoin to cover at least 143% of outstanding principal when funds are drawn. If that ratio falls below 130%, FalconX can demand additional Bitcoin or partial repayment to restore the collateral requirement.

Industry observers note that american Bitcoin’s subsidiary had drawn nothing on the $150 million FalconX line as of the Nov. 23 filing. These thresholds govern draws and outstanding loans.

The 143% ratio serves as the draw minimum and the target after a margin-call top-up or partial collateral release.

A decline below 105% can trigger default and potentially allow the lender to liquidate pledged assets under the agreement's conditions.

At the minimum initial collateral ratio, a Bitcoin price decline of about 9.1% would trigger a margin call if the company borrowed and neither added collateral nor repaid debt.

American Bitcoin already had 3,090 BTC pledged for mining equipment purchases as of Sept. 30, according to its quarterly filing.

The upcoming facility could let it access more capital while maintaining its Bitcoin position, but borrowing would also put additional holdings behind collateral obligations.

Still, the arrangement leaves American Bitcoin on a different path from miners selling reserves to fund AI infrastructure, with its ability to retain those coins increasingly tied to BTC's market value and how much it ultimately borrows.

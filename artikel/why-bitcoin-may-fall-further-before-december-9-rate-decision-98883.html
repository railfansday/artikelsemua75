Why Bitcoin May Fall Further Before December 9 Rate Decision

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Bitcoin may not have hit its bottom yet, according to an analyst who points to several warning signs that have marked past bear markets. Bitcoin, now trading near $55,700, has historically found its cycle low only after touching its realized price, the average price at which all coins last moved. That level sits around $54,000 and has not yet been reached. The MVRV Z-score, which compares market value with realized value, has also not fallen below zero, a reading that has accompanied previous bottoms. Two macro events add to the risk. A possible US government shutdown on December 11 could weigh on prices, since Bitcoin has fallen after past shutdowns. The Federal Reserve is also widely expected to raise rates on December 9, and earlier hikes have been followed by selling. One factor cuts the other way. A fresh low would stretch the current bear market beyond 411 days, longer than any previous cycle.

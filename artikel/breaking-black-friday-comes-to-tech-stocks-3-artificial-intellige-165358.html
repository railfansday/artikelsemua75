According to recent reports, black Friday is right around the corner, and the market has been gracious enough to put a slew of tech stocks on sale before the holiday shopping season. With numerous bargains to choose from, these are the three tech stocks I'd be loading up on right now.

In a recent development, the market clearly does not believe in the Micron Technology (MU +19.04%) story, with the stock now trading at a forward P/E of under 3.5. The company has been delivering incredible growth, fueled by higher memory prices.

However, investors are clearly nervous that the memory market is about to peak, and the industry has a history of brutal downturns when this happens. That mentioned, this time does truly look different. While the industry hasn't entirely lost its cyclicality, this cycle is also being fueled by a powerful AI build-out with secular tailwinds. AI chips need to be packaged with a special form of DRAM (dynamic random access memory) called high bandwidth memory (HBM) that reduces latency and optimizes performance. HBM growth has been in lockstep with AI accelerator demand, but supply is struggling to keep up due to a dearth of extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography machines that are used to make both advanced logic chips and HBM. At the same time, HBM also requires three times the wafer capacity as conventional DRAM.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/23/26.

This dynamic will likely keep DRAM prices higher for much longer than your typical cycle, while Micron and others have locked up a significant share of their capacity in longer-term contracts. With cash piling up on its balance sheet and robust free cash flow, the stock is starting to look like a bargain.

Even after rallying following CEO Jensen Huang's return to the corner office on Monday, Nvidia (NVDA +31.48%)still trades at a forward P/E of just 10 times fiscal 2028 (ending January 2028) estimates from analysts. Huang played a huge role in Nvidia's success and his return puts the organization in a good position moving forward.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/23/26.

Industry observers note that revenue is projected to grow a robust 75% next year, as the company maintains its leadership position. Importantly, Huang has transformed Nvidia from a simple maker of graphics processing units (GPUs) into a complete end-to-end AI infrastructure player. As such, it is now set to capture a much larger share of AI infrastructure spending per gigawatt of capacity for Vera Rubin customers than in past architecture generations. In addition, Nvidia has locked in a significant portion of future HBM supply and fab capacity, creating a wide supply-chain moat. While Nvidia will lose some market share to ASICs and Advanced Micro Devices (AMD +6.85%), it still has a path to solid future growth and its future looks bright.

Broadcom (AVGO +3.00%) is another semiconductor stock that has fallen into the bargain bin ahead of Black Friday, trading at a forward P/E of just 11 times the fiscal 2028 (ending October) analyst consensus. This comes despite the organization having some of the strongest growth prospects in the AI chip space.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/23/26.

Broadcom is the leader in ASIC technology, providing important IP (intellectual property) and services to help customers develop purpose-built, hardwired AI chips. It helped Alphabet (GOOGL +6.34%) (GOOG +5.88%) develop its highly successful Tensor Processing Units (TPUs), which are set to become Broadcom's biggest growth driver as it starts selling these custom chips to external customers. It's also helped OpenAI and Meta Platforms (META +6.55%) develop their own custom chips. Altogether, Broadcom has a clear line of sight to see its overall AI chip revenue quadruple from this past fiscal year to $230 billion in fiscal 2028.

In a recent development, in addition to its custom chip business, Broadcom is also a leader in data center networking, where its Tomahawk and Jericho chipsets hold strong positions in this fast-growing market. Combined, these two businesses should propel strong growth for the company in the coming years.

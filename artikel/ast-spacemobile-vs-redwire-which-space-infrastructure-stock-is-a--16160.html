Deciding between AST SpaceMobile (ASTS -8.71%) and Redwire (RDW -11.90%) depends on your appetite for high-stakes disruption versus established hardware manufacturing. Recent findings suggest that AST SpaceMobile seeks to eliminate global connectivity gaps by providing cellular service from orbit. Redwire serves as a critical supplier of components, sensors, and microgravity research platforms. In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached around $142.9 million. This represents a substantial revenue growth of roughly 671% compared to the prior twelve-month period as the firm began its commercial rollout. However, the company still reported a significant net loss of approximately $628.3 million during this same time frame.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the current ratio stands at nearly 16.4x. This ratio measures liquid assets against short-term debts, indicating a strong immediate liquidity position. The organization carries a debt-to-equity ratio of roughly 1.2x, meaning its total liabilities are 1.2 times its shareholder equity. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was negative $1.1 billion.

Industry observers note that redwire functions as a diversified infrastructure provider for the aerospace and defense sectors. It supplies essential hardware, such as avionics and power systems, to NASA and various branches of the U.S. military. Beyond government contracts, the tech giant has established commercial partnerships with leaders like Eli Lilly & Co. (LLY +0.03%) to conduct advanced microgravity research in orbit.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached approximately $431.9 million. This performance reflects revenue growth of about 45.8% over the previous twelve-month period. Despite the increase in sales, Redwire recorded a net loss of roughly $226.1 million during this cycle as it continues to scale its manufacturing capabilities.

As of its December 2025 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 0.2x. This indicates that total debt is roughly 20% of shareholder equity, suggesting a conservative use of borrowed funds. The current ratio of 1.6x indicates the manufacturer has 1.6 times as many current assets as current liabilities. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was negative $190.8 million.

AST SpaceMobile faces substantial liquidity risks because it must raise significant additional capital to complete its satellite constellation. It faces intense competition from established satellite systems like Starlink and traditional terrestrial providers like Iridium Communications (IRDM -2.68%), which is in the midst of a merger with Rocket Lab Corp. (RKLB -8.96%). Regulatory hurdles regarding spectrum usage and a pending class action lawsuit from investors add further complexity to its outlook.

In a recent development, redwire relies heavily on a limited number of U.S. government contracts, which are subject to funding changes or immediate termination. The organization must also navigate the risks of integrating acquisitions and managing intense competition from larger aerospace peers like Boeing Co. (BA -1.96%). Supply chain disruptions and a dependency on a small number of third-party launch providers could also impact its ability to deliver products.

Redwire appears much more affordable based on its P/S ratio, which compares market value to sales over the past twelve months.

In April, Redwire was selected as one of 14 vendors (out of a total of 32 bids) on the Space Systems Command $1.8 billion 10-year Andromeda Indefinite Delivery Indefinite Quantity, or IDIQ contract. That's a project to replace aging GPS satellites and upgrade the U.S. space infrastructure to counter emerging threats. The IDIQ win is something management feels moves Redwire up the food chain with the Department of Defense. It could mean significantly more revenue, since U.S. Space Systems Command provided a notice of its intent to raise the total shared ceiling for the Andromeda IDIQ to more than $6 billion to meet increased demand.

Sources indicate that in the near term, Wall Street projects Redwire to post fiscal 2026 revenue of around $463 million, representing about 38% growth over the prior year. In addition, the business has an order backlog of $620 million. The business is still expected to post net losses for the foreseeable future, but they are trending in the right direction.

It has been revealed that AST SpaceMobile, meanwhile, expects its space-based network to deliver significant business in a few years. Essentially, AST SpaceMobile is a direct-to-device play, providing full mobile phone compatibility with major carriers without the need for specialized equipment. Many of its potential clients are also equity holders in the company, including AT&T, Verizon, Bell Canada, Rakuten, Vodafone (VOD -0.54%), Alphabet Inc. (GOOG -0.70%), American Tower Corp,(AMT +2.23%) and Telus Corp. (TU +2.87%).

By the end of 2026, the tech giant should have 45 satellites, which will allow it to fully service the U.S., and that should start to supercharge revenue growth. For fiscal 2026, Wall Street sees $166 million in sales, jumping to $586 million the following year, though the business is expected to still be unprofitable. Free cash flow appears much more manageable, with analysts expecting positive free cash flow and net income in 2029.

Both of these are exciting stocks for the space infrastructure age that is developing, but Redwire gets the nod for its quite reasonable P/S ratio for investors right now under the adage of buy good companies at good prices.

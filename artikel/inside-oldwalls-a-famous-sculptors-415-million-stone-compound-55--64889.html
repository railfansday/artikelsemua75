California-born sculptor Chester Beach worked out of a Latest York City studio between 1907 and 1952, nabbing high-profile commissions from the Panama-Pacific International Exposition, as well as the 1928 Summer Olympics.

But, like many artists, he carved out space for a pastoral retreat away from the hustle and bustle.

In 1917, after living in Rome for a brief period, he and his wife, Eleanor Beach, established a home in Brewster, NY, in Putnam County, just 55 miles north of the city. They named the Arts & Crafts-style estate OldWalls.

Recent findings suggest that "There have only been three owners including the original family that owned it up until 2009," notes listing agent Will Stuart, of Berkshire Hathaway HomeServices New England Properties. "My sellers have been there for two years.". (Related: Obsessed fan breaks into YouTuber’s home demanding marr...)

Listing for $4,150,000, the newly introduced owner won't be the first to seek a deeper connection to nature while still being close to Manhattan.

Stuart notes that this eight-bedroom, 6.5-bath spread is exceptionally tied to its origins.

"Each owner has become a steward of the property and has preserved extensive family photographs, memoirs and documents, and have really kept the story going, which is rare with these kinds of properties," he says. "A lot of times the information gets lost along the way. The story has been preserved so well.".

The estate includes a four-bedroom stone residence with plaster walls, hand-crafted stonework and stained glass. There’s also a separate guest studio with a kitchen, bath, sleeping loft and stone fireplace. And, a two-bedroom guest cottage dating back to the 1940s with cozy amenities such as a fireplace and screened-in porch.

Another way the property has been preserved is through art the original owners brought into the house.

Sources indicate that tiles collected from Spain, Guatemala and Italy are laid into the floor, notes Stuart. He adds that "there’s actually a cluster of Roman floor tiles under the dining-room table.".

It has been revealed that the kitchen’s chandelier was born out of a turntable Beach used for sculpting. Eleanor’s bird paintings (112 in all) are incorporated into the living room’s ceiling.

According to recent reports, “There’s three outbuildings in addition to the main residence that all offer incredible living space and charm,” indicates Stuart. “Each one of them has a story and a history.”.

For example, in the detached studio, which was built before the home, are many of Beach’s tools and hardware, as well as the original doors.

A buyer drawn to that history is who Stuart is courting. But it could also be an equestrian, as the property features a five-stall barn with a spacious one-bedroom apartment. Nearby are run-outs, rolling grass turnout paddocks, a riding field with access to trails, and a riding arena in the sand.

“We have a lot of equestrians that have moved into the community,” says Stuart. “A lot of the equestrian people travel to Florida in the winter so they’re here half the year, but most people are here year-round.” Two miles down the road is Old Salem Farm, a 120-acre boarding and training facility.

Above all, the buyer will likely be someone who likes to be near Manhattan but also have a countryside retreat, just like Beach once did.

This mirrors a trend Stuart continues to see among buyers. “People looking for more space, more privacy, but want to stay within close proximity to the city,” he says. “This is a real compound here. It’s someone who is hosting family or guests.”.

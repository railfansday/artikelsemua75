Industry observers note that have the money conversation with your aging parents while everyone is healthy, not in a hospital hallway. You don’t need their net worth. You need to know where the documents are, who their advisors are, what insurance they carry, and whether a power of attorney exists.

Too often, this talk happens after a fall or a stroke, when nobody can find anything.

It has been revealed that picture a daughter whose father is in intensive care. His mortgage is due, his bank won’t discuss his accounts with her, and no one knows which company holds his life insurance. With no power of attorney on file, paying his bills may mean going to court.

Avoiding that can take one uncomfortable afternoon, and the list is shorter than you might fear. You aren’t asking what your parents are worth, the question that makes the talk feel like an audit. You’re asking what’s in place, where it is, and who handles it:.

It has been revealed that open with your own planning instead of theirs. Say you just named someone to make medical decisions for you and realized you don’t know what they’ve set up. A friend’s parent getting sick works, too. Let them decide how much to share and who takes each role.

Recent findings suggest that be ready for two hard truths. The money may be thinner than you assumed, so listen rather than judge. And trouble can show up first as money problems. The National Institute on Aging states money problems may be among the earliest signs of dementia, such as unpaid bills or a burst of newly introduced purchases, and scammers target older adults.

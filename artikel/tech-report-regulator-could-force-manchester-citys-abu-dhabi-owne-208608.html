The Independent Football Regulator could force Manchester City’s Abu Dhabi ownership out of English football if the guilty verdicts over breaches of Premier League financial rules are upheld. (Related: Fine-Tune Wav2Vec2 for English ASR in Hugging Face with...)

City were found guilty of nearly every charge brought against them by the league when the commission’s verdict was published in September. A three-person adjudication panel found that the club had systematically inflated revenues and disguised losses by a combined £900m through “sham” deals between 2009 and 2018, aiding their rapid ascent to the summit of the game.

According to recent reports, the club have always strongly denied wrongdoing and have appealed against the commission’s verdict. If City’s appeal fails, a sanction hearing will follow and is expected to recommend seismic sporting and financial punishments.

Separately, the regulator (IFR), which was established last year following the fallout from the European Super League scandal, has legal powers to investigate and sanction both the club and any individuals involved in breaching rules.

In a recent development, the IFR grants operating licenses to football clubs and has the power to withdraw them. It also carries powers to order officials who it deems unsuitable to “take all reasonable steps to cease to be an owner of the club” within a given time frame.

The regulator faces a major test of its ability to referee the English game, given both the enormity of the City case and Abu Dhabi’s political and economic relationship with the UK.

Some senior figures within English football were concerned by comments from prime minister Andy Burnham appearing to downplay the significance of the verdict when it first emerged, although Downing Street later clarified that Burnham’s view is that no-one is “above the rules”.

But David Kogan, the IFR chair, has told parliament’s Digital, Culture, Media and Sport select committee that the regulator is prepared to take strong action against City, if necessary.

“Forget the name of the club, forget the size of the club, forget the scale of the issue,” Kogan said. “We cannot simply ignore our statutory duties in relation to a club because it happens to be of a certain size. (Related: Introducing Optimum: The Optimization Toolkit for Trans...)

“We absolutely will have to take the appropriate action as laid out in the legislation, depending on the circumstances as they come to us, which are the evidence that’s been presented and collated by the independent commission of the Premier League and by any judgement that takes place on that evidence through an appeal.

“But in one sense, the evidence has already been judged by those cases having been found guilty, so we will absolutely have the stomach to do so.”.

In a recent development, city are owned by City Football Group, a parent tech giant controlled by Emirati royal Sheikh Mansour via his Abu Dhabi United Group (ADUG) investment vehicle. The commission found that ADUG money was used to artificially inflate City’s revenues, something the club continues to dispute.

Recent findings suggest that kogan added: “Would I, with an organisation that has a relatively newly introduced, fairly young staff, that’s been going for less than a year, would I have chosen to have this particular issue in our laps this early? Well, probably not. But the truth is, if we don’t act – assuming those circumstances are triggered – if we don’t act, what is the purpose of having a football regulator?

“So I think we have to absolutely, clearly be seen to be doing the job that you as legislators have asked us to do, and we absolutely will do that, whatever that means.”.

City’s appeal against the commission’s verdict has no set timetable but is expected to be concluded early in the fresh year, with the sanctions hearing then set to follow if any or all of the verdicts are upheld.

City’s most recent statement, on 2 October, read: “Manchester City Football Club can confirm that at 7pm on Thursday 1st October 2026 the Club lodged its comprehensive Appeal against the opinion of the Premier League Commission, in relation to the Premier League disciplinary matter.

Recent findings suggest that “The Club’s firm position is that, on multiple grounds, the opinion contains clear material errors, of law, principle and fact and is unsafe. The Club is innocent of the accusations made by the Premier League and a comprehensive body of irrefutable evidence exists in support of all of its positions, relating to this case.

“We will continue to respect due process and are necessarily restricted in what we can say further until all proceedings are complete.”.

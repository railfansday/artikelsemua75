Finland notes work is continuing on the Sparta II ship on Sunday.

Rescue and firefighting efforts are continuing for a Russian cargo vessel hit by a drone attack in the eastern Gulf of Finland, authorities have remarked. (Related: Tech Report: Animal control called to rescue alligator ...)

The ship - the Sparta II - was bound for Russia's Baltic exclave Kaliningrad when it was hit on Saturday morning, before drifting into Finnish waters. Finnish defence forces said Ukraine was likely behind the attack. Kyiv has yet to comment.

Finland's Border Guard said on Sunday that work was ongoing to make sure the cargo ship did not sink but it was not aware of any danger to surrounding maritime traffic.

Water was still being used to douse the ship on Sunday, and Finland stated Estonian and Russian assets were also involved in recovery work. (Related: Redditor forced to bring desktop PC in checked luggage ...)

Kaliningrad Governor Alexei Besprozvannykh said two drones had been used in the strike, based on preliminary information, and there was "no threat" to the territory's supplies.

Industry observers note that "War is spilling over into our immediate vicinity," Finnish Prime Minister Petteri Orpo stated. "Ukraine has the right to fight back against the aggressor.".

The Sparta II was sanctioned by the US in 2022 and Ukraine earlier this year, and is run by a Russian firm, according to the Ukrainian and US governments.

The company says on its website that it was established within the structure of Russia's defence ministry in 2011 and since 2015 has been operating within the military construction complex.

Finland's coast guard mentioned it had been alerted to the attack at around 08:50 local time (06:50 GMT) on Saturday and that the cargo vessel had been located approximately 50km west of the island of Suursaari in the Gulf of Finland.

This prompted "temporary aviation and maritime restrictions" around the area, Finnish border officials noted.

Shortly after the incident, Finland's defence ministry said a Russian helicopter was suspected of violating the country's airspace, potentially linked to "rescue or tracking operations".

A Russian rescue vessel approached the ship and was told by the crew that they did not need to be evacuated, Kaliningrad's governor wrote on Telegram, adding: "The crew is currently carrying out the necessary work and looks to continue on its way.".

The Finnish Border Guard later stated the crew had been evacuated to another Russian vessel that was now already en route to Russia. The Russian rescue vessel had remained at the location of the stricken ship.

It added that no environmental damage had been detected and that there was no danger to maritime traffic or people.

Kaliningrad is a Russian exclave that sits between Poland and Lithuania.

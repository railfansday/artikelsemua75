The second day of Corona Capital 2026 on Saturday (Nov. 21) brought back Twenty One Pilots as the headlining act of the renowned Mexican festival. The duo made up of vocalist Tyler Joseph and drummer Josh Dun performed their final concert of the year in Mexico City, which also marked their last show before an indefinite hiatus, as they indicated during their set. The show turned into a nostalgic and emotional moment, underscoring the deep connection they’ve built with their local fans over the years.

On its second day, the music fest gathered approximately 76,000 attendees, according to promoter Ocesa, who enjoyed a diverse lineup of performances, ranging from the energetic ’90s punk of The Offspring and the wild post-hardcore of Pierce the Veil to more subtle moments from the Greek artist Σtella and the refined electronic and French touch of Sebastien Tellier.

Canadian punk artist Peaches captivated the crowd with her irreverence and queer activism, while her fellow Canadians in Mother Mother delivered one of the most celebrated moments of the night with their alternative rock set, which included covers of classics like Nirvana’s “In Bloom” and Pixies’ “Where Is My Mind?”.

The British electronic duo Underworld made their first appearance in the Latin American country in seven years, transforming the Corona Cero stage into a massive dance floor with their iconic track “Born Slippy.” Other stages set up at the Curva 4, the site of Mexico City’s Formula 1 Grand Prix, also hosted performances by DOPE LEMON, Baby Queen, We Are Scientists, Militarie Gun, Maisie Peters, Strawberry Guy and Princess Chelsea, among others.

The program continued on Sunday (Nov. 22) with headliners The Strokes, the Mexican debut of Canadian experimental math-rock duo Angine de Poitrine and the veteran Welsh rock band Manic Street Preachers. Other acts on the Sunday lineup: The Black Crowes, Johnny Marr, Lola Young, Lil Yachty, Tipling Rock, Tricky and the highly anticipated return of The xx.

The duo made up of vocalist Tyler Joseph and drummer Josh Dun returned to Corona Capital after five years to deliver their final show before taking an indefinite break from the stage. Before performing “Trees,” Joseph gave an emotional message to their fans, who had already danced and sung along to tracks like “Overcompensate,” “The Contract,” “Center Mass,” “Jumpsuit” and “Nico and the Niners.”.

“It’s our last song, our last show of the year, actually. It’s our last show for a while,” the vocalist said. “And I’m not just saying this to you, which I do mean it, for you in front of me right now but anybody else who sees this: you’ve changed our lives. Thank you so much for believing in this music, believing in live music, coming to shows. I’ve run out of ways to say thank you.”.

California-based band The Offspring brought all their punk energy to the second day of the festival, performing major hits like “Come Out and Play,” “Why Don’t You Get a Job?,” “Pretty Fly (For a White Guy)” and “The Kids Aren’t Alright,” as well as covers like Taylor Swift’s “Love Story.”.

It has been revealed that the crowd fully embraced the band, led by vocalist Dexter Holland, chanting “Olé, olé, olé” along to the rhythm. Fans also celebrated its tribute to the late Ozzy Osbourne with renditions of Black Sabbath classics “Crazy Train” and “Paranoid.”. To the rhythm of legendary singer-songwriter José Alfredo Jiménez and his iconic ranchera song “El Rey,” the band from San Diego, California, took the main stage to deliver one of the wildest performances of the festival with their post-hardcore sound on Saturday night.

The moshpit ruled the main stage to the beat of songs like “May These Noises Startle You in Your Sleep Tonight,” “Hell Above,” “Caraphernelia,” “Bulls in the Bronx” and “I’d Rather Die Than Be Famous.” The group, founded by vocalist and guitarist Vic Fuentes, also performed a cover of Pixies’ “Where Is My Mind?,” which had already been played earlier on the Doritos stage by Mother Mother.

The Canadian artist took electroclash, industrial beats and provocation to the limit, turning her performance into a celebration of chaos, dance and freedom. With her trademark irreverence, Peaches wore a bodysuit adorned with faux hair on the breasts and genital area to advocate for trans women’s rights. Her performance became a full-fledged display of activism, featuring messages in support of Palestine and punk music, leaving an indelible mark on the memory of those who witnessed this unique moment.

Recent findings suggest that ryan Guldemond, guitarist and lead singer of the Canadian indie rock band Mother Mother, confessed to the audience that he thought their presence on one of the festival’s main stages was a mistake, especially since they had a primetime slot. Nonetheless, he admitted that performing at Corona Capital was a dream come true.

The band delighted the crowd with a set that included their new song “Rot,” as well as hits from their repertoire like “The Sticks,” “Verbatim,” “Make Believe” and “Hayloft.” They also created a special moment for their fans by performing covers of Nirvana’s “In Bloom” and Pixies’ “Where Is My Mind?”.

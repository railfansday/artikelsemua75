On this day in space! Nov.22, 1989: NASA launches space shuttle Discovery on classified STS-33 mission for the US military

On Nov. 22, 1989, the space shuttle Discovery rolled out on the secret mission STS-33 for the U.S. military. It lifted off from Kennedy Space Center and up into the dark of night.

According to recent reports, this was the third nighttime launch of the shuttle program and the 32nd shuttle mission overall. Discovery had five astronauts on board. Their mission was to deploy a classified payload for the U.S. Department of Defense. NASA and the military debuted several secretive shuttle missions to deploy military satellites. It has been revealed that from its start, the Space Transportation System, better known as the space shuttle, was a compromise between meeting civilian and military needs. NASA saw the vehicle as a means of supporting science research, establishing an on-orbit economy (servicing commercial satellites and flying manufacturer payloads) and ultimately, assembling the parts for a space station.

Sources indicate that the Department of Defense saw the shuttle as an asset to deploying and retrieving reconnaissance satellites, among other projects, some of them classified. It was not until the loss of the space shuttle Challenger, that it was decided that the shuttle would only be used by the military when it was key to national security. Other needs would be met by expendable launch vehicles (ELVs).As an aside, the STS-33 astronauts also spent Thanksgiving in space during this mission. But the holiday was off to a crappy start when the shuttle's toilet broke first thing in the morning. After spending most of the day fixing it, they got to enjoy their Thanksgiving turkey together.

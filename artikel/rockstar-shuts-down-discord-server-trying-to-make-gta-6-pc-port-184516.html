Rockstar Games has shut down a Discord server that was aimed at creating an unofficial PC port for Grand Theft Auto 6 amid leaks from CyberLeek.

It has been revealed that after 13 years of waiting, Grand Theft Auto fans have finally been able to sink their teeth into GTA 6 – at least if you’re an Xbox or PlayStation owner. If you’re focused on PC gaming, then you’re out of luck. (Related: Restaurant owners left in tears after discovering their...)

There have been claims that AI would be used to replicate GTA 6 on PC in unofficial ways, and the unofficial PC port case seemed to be helped by a number of leaks from the Xbox source code.

In a recent development, however, Rockstar Games and Take-Two have hit back at those effort, shutting down the Project Leonida server on Discord, which had managed to get the fresh Grand Theft Auto at least booting on some PCs. VGC noted that some, however, just ran into a black screen after booting. (Related: Breaking: Future Value Calculator: How to Project Your ...)

After the Discord server disappeared, NotRexed, the original creator, responded to the shutdown via social media.

Recent findings suggest that “I quit guys. I’m sorry. I really want to make cool stuff, but every single time s*it happens that I can’t control so I’m stepping away,” he stated. “Thank you everybody, especially in the Discord that allowed me to have this opportunity.”.

In another post, NotRexed also showed off a screenshot from Discord, informing him of a ban until 2028.

According to recent reports, “Last post from me. I wanna clear things up. Rockstar taking my server down was only part of the reason,” NotRexed continued.

“Like I’ve stated before, I’m a 15-year-old kid. Managing school and sports is hard enough without having middle-aged men yell and complain to you on Discord. Goodbye.”.

The release date for an official PC version of GTA 6 has not been confirmed, with reports claiming it will come in early 2027 and mark a massive departure to the usual year-long wait that Rockstar has done before.

Elsewhere, the hacker behind some of GTA 6’s earliest leaks was released from prison, with a judge warning him to not do it again.

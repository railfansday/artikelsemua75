Industry observers note that one first-party title from the GameCube library that's well and truly outlived this particular Nintendo generation is Super Smash Bros. Melee, and today happens to be the game's 25th anniversary in Japan.

Recent findings suggest that after introducing fans to the series in 1999 on the N64, Masahiro Sakurai and the team at HAL Laboratory & Nintendo rolled out this fresh, improved, and faster take on the all-star Nintendo fighter in November 2001. Not only was it a popular couch game, but Melee went on to have an incredibly successful run as a competitive game. (Related: Latest Update: Build A Racetrack codes (November 2026)...)

According to Nintendo's official data, Melee is officially the "best-selling game of all time" for the GameCube, shifting over seven million copies worldwide, with the system itself selling 21.74 million units during its lifetime. This particular entry was so influential within the Smash scene that it led to Nintendo re-releasing the GameCube controller and making newer entries in the series compatible with this same pad, as it was the preferred pick for any serious or pro player.

To this day, the game's competitive scene is still going strong, with game's meta continuing to evolve, and players like "Hungrybox" (the famous Jigglypuff main) are still knocking about. Of course, there have also been plenty of scandals within the competitive space over the years, but Melee continues to thrive, and is still capable of packing out events across the globe.

It has been revealed that so, on behalf of the team here at Nintendo Life, here's to another 25 years of duking it out. Oh, and if Nintendo hasn't already got something planned, now would be the perfect time to add Melee to the Switch 2's GameCube library. For North America, the game's 25th anniversary takes place in early December.

According to recent reports, I've tested well over 50 coffee makers, I direct The Coffee Lab, and I'm a self-professed coffee snob. As Tom's Guide's resident coffee expert, I'm here to help you choose the perfect espresso machine this Black Friday.

The most affordable option on this list, the BELLA Jolt is also the smallest. At under 6 inches wide and 11 inches tall, it's ideal for compact kitchens. I've not tested this one completely — but I've got the machine and it's in the pipeline! If you're a coffee newbie, I'd recommend grabbing the Jolt on sale.

Sources indicate that the Lavazza Tabli is one of the most exciting espresso machines of 2026, and maybe even all time. This machine uses 100% pure coffee pods — zero plastic or metal, unlike Keurig and Nespresso. The espresso genuinely tastes delicious, and it's just as easy to use as any other pod machine. I refuse to shut up about the Tabli. It's just too good.

According to recent reports, for the home baristas who want hot coffee in seconds, a Nespresso machine is the best bet. This Vertuo Pop+ combines an Instagrammable appearance with Nespresso's signature ease of use to make espresso in no time. The Vertuo line offers a wider range of coffee flavors, too, and can even make a full mug without needing to top it up.

The Casabrews CM5418 went a little viral on TikTok last year for being pretty and extremely cheap. Even though it takes a little while to switch between espresso and steam and requires some boiler purging, at this price point, you're not going to get much better. It looks cute and is easy to use — as long as you purge that boiler!

Sources indicate that the De'Longhi Classic is a great first espresso machine. This model is super easy to use and forgiving for newbies (especially if you're using pre-ground coffee). While the steam wand only really makes foam rather than microfoam, this is fine if you're a total newbie or you're a cappuccino addict.

Industry observers note that for coffee lovers both short on time and money, a Nespresso machine is a great option. The Nespresso Vertuo Up uses patented "Vertuo" pods (these come in a wider range of flavors than the original line) and makes hot coffee at the press of a button. The newly introduced Up even makes iced drinks, perfect for summer.

The Ninja Luxe Cafe Mini is the perfect first espresso machine. Thanks to its small footprint (my sister has this machine and it fits into her tiny kitchen perfectly) and built-in grinder, it minimizes clutter and cleanup. I tested the Plus version of this machine earlier this year and was blown away by its ease of use and compact style — the only difference is the Plus has automated milk frothing.

Recent findings suggest that the Creatista Plus is one of the only Nespresso machines with a built-in barista-style steam wand. This model uses Nespresso pods for the coffee and its very own steam wand for the milk — just like a "real" espresso machine. Usually espresso-machine-priced at $600, it's now $390.

It has been revealed that the De'Longhi La Specialista Arte Evo is the best-value bean-to-cup on this list. While it lacks the PID controller present on the Breville Barista Express, the powerful steam wand and cold brew mode could be enough to convince you. I tested this machine last year and adored it.

According to recent reports, the Breville Barista Express is an icon, and for good reason. This bean-to-cup espresso machine has been livin' it large since 2009 and shows no sign of slowing down. For just $494, you get a gorgeous espresso machine and a grinder — $55 cheaper than the Bambino Plus and a separate grinder — which is perfect for space-conscious, budget-conscious coffee lovers.

Sources indicate that the Breville Bambino Plus is my favorite espresso machine, and while it's not on sale right now, if you grab the Bambino Plus + Baratza Encore ESP grinder, you save $150. Considering the Bambino Plus is $499 and the grinder is $199, you're almost getting the grinder for free. | Price check: $649 at Amazon.

Industry observers note that the Breville Barista Pro is like an upgraded version of the Barista Express, with an LCD screen that reveals grinding and extraction animations. You still control every step of the brewing process, including milk steaming, so this is the perfect machine if you want to get into the nitty-gritty of coffee.

According to recent reports, the Breville Barista Touch is a semi-automatic espresso machine with a bright, full-color touchscreen. Ideal if you're more of a hands-off barista, the Barista Touch can froth milk automatically and pull perfect shots with very little input from you.

It has been revealed that I was extremely excited to test the Fellow Espresso Series 1 earlier this year. I was gnawing at the bars of my cage. Thankfully, the Espresso Series 1 pulled through and proved why it was one of the most hyped espresso machines of the year. It boasts a powerful steam wand, a gorgeous, minimalist design, and makes delicious espresso. It even walks you through how to make a huge variety of drinks — I hadn't heard of many of them, and I'm an ex-barista.

I tested the Smeg EMC02 back in April 2025, and I swear I've not stopped thinking about it since. This gorgeous machine combines near-professional performance with easy-to-use features. The built-in temperature control is customizable to the degree and the steam wand makes gorgeous latte art without breaking a sweat.

Recent findings suggest that usually $1,599, the Breville Dual Boiler is the ideal "next step" espresso machine with, you guessed it, two boilers powering steam and espresso independently. This model comes with the Breville Smart Grinder Pro, usually $199 — so you're saving $300 overall.

Recent findings suggest that I'm currently testing the Meraki Gen 2, and boy, oh boy, it's a beast. With 45 grinder settings, an almost commercially powerful steam wand, and integrated grinding scale and espresso scale, this is the espresso machine to end all espresso machines. It streamlines every part of the espresso flow. Of course, it's a huge investment, but I love this machine.

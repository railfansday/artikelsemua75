Recent findings suggest that nvidia (NVDA -8.34%) has brought back Jensen Huang as CEO, amid all the drama of the past few weeks that saw the company's co-founder resign only to end up being reinstated. It's put the stock on a roller-coaster ride, with it rising more than 30% yesterday on the news of Huang's return.

While many investors appear to be feeling pretty bullish about Nvidia of late, the reality is that the concerns around Huang haven't completely gone away. A key date for investors to circle on their calendars is Dec. 1 -- that's when Huang is scheduled to first meet with members of Congress. The most concerning matter surrounding Huang's affairs is that one of them may have involved a Chinese spy, possibly resulting in illegal shipments of billions of advanced Nvidia chips to China. In a recent development, while Trump appears convinced that there is little to no risk based on the information available, that doesn't mean everyone is. Republican Sen. John Cornyn's response to the news of Huang's reinstatement effectively says it all: "You gotta be kidding me.".

Even if it turns out to be a non-concern for Nvidia, members of Congress may scrutinize the situation more closely given the reinstatement, with Senate Minority Leader Chuck Schumer going so far as to accuse the president of "putting his own financial interests and the needs of his billionaire buddies before our national security.".

According to recent reports, there was a lot of excitement around Nvidia's stock yesterday, but it may prove to be premature. Congress still wants answers from Huang, and it may be a while before investors know the full ramifications for the firm. While it may come away unscathed, the risk hasn't disappeared. If fresh issues arise related to the investigations into Huang, they may weigh on the stock in the future.

According to recent reports, while Nvidia has a highly profitable business and a dominant position in tech, there may continue to be significant volatility around the stock. It can be a good buy at its reduced levels, as even with yesterday's rally, the stock entered Tuesday down 32% from its 52-week high, leaving much more room for further gains.

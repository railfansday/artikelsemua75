The U.S. stock market seems to be on track for a disappointing finish to 2026. Despite being up more than 8% year to date and more than 11% in the past year, the S&P 500 index (^GSPC -2.43%) has declined by more than 5% since reaching its record high on Oct. 6. Industry observers note that the tech-heavy Nasdaq-100 has done even worse, with a more than 12% correction since Oct. 6. Much of this recent stock market sell-off has been driven by fears about artificial intelligence (AI) companies taking on too much debt, and continuing questions about whether the AI boom can keep going. These investor worries were amplified by the recent scandal that led to the shocking resignation of former Nvidia (NVDA -8.84%) CEO Jensen Huang. (Related: Industry News: Judge blocks construction of Trump’s arc...)

But there's reason to believe that 2027 will be a better year for stock investors -- not just in America, but around the world. A fresh research note from Morgan Stanley (MS -4.13%) is bullish on U.S. and international stocks for 2027. The research team described their strategy as a "max overweight" position in global stocks, including U.S. stocks and international stocks in Japan and Europe.

Let's look at what Morgan Stanley strategists are forecasting for stocks in the next 12 months, and how you might want to invest.

It has been revealed that according to Bloomberg reporting, Morgan Stanley strategists are forecasting a strong year for global stocks, especially in Europe and Japan. The drivers of this growth are the continuing AI build-out and AI adoption. A Morgan Stanley note from a team led by strategist Serena Tang noted that 2027 would be "the year to embrace risk," that "the story is broadening out beyond hyperscalers" and that next year's stock market performance "will increasingly be about returns for AI adopters.".

Recent findings suggest that other causes for optimism were noted by another Morgan Stanley strategist, Michael Wilson, who wrote that "Healthy EPS growth, lower oil, stabilizing rate volatility, continued AI investment and adoption as well as valuations below fair value" are reasons to support the bank's bullish outlook for stocks in 2027.Morgan Stanley forecasts a 15% gain in the S&P 500 during the next 12 months, and 13% earnings growth in the MSCI All-Country World (ACWI) Index, which represents about 85% of investable global stocks, including the U.S. market.

If you share Morgan Stanley's optimism for U.S. and global stocks in 2027, you might want to start by buying an S&P 500 exchange-traded fund (ETF). The Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO -2.48%) is a popular choice that charges an ultra-low expense ratio of 0.03%. This S&P 500 ETF lets you own 500 of the largest publicly traded companies that make up about 80% of the U.S. stock market. If the S&P 500 gains 15% in the next 12 months, the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF will let you capture nearly all of those returns.

Industry observers note that as for international stocks, there's more than one way to buy stocks in other countries, but one easy way is to consider buying a broadly diversified international ETF like the iShares Core MSCI Total International Stock ETF (IXUS -2.82%). This ETF holds 4,571 stocks representing other countries beyond America.

Sources indicate that the top markets represented in IXUS include Japan (15.2% of the fund) and Europe (about 27.5% of the fund is represented by the United Kingdom, France, Germany, Switzerland, and the Netherlands). IXUS charges a low expense ratio of 0.07%. As of Oct. 31, this international ETF has delivered annualized returns of about 20.6% for the past three years, and about 8.1% for the past five years.

Morgan Stanley's research didn't recommend any specific ETFs, and there's no guarantee that any strategist's forecasts will be 100% correct, or that any stock or ETF is a "safe" investment. 2027 could be a great year, an average year, or a terrible year for the stock market. The AI trade might keep losing steam, and the AI infrastructure build-out might not boost the economy in 2027 as much as these strategists believe.

But if you're still optimistic about the AI trade, if you think the recent sell-off in the U.S. stock market has gone too far and that shares are getting undervalued, or if you want to diversify your portfolio away from the U.S. market, buying the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF and the iShares Core MSCI Total International Stock ETF could be smart choices. Even if 2027 is a disappointing year, buying these low-cost index funds is likely a good way to position your portfolio for longer-term growth.

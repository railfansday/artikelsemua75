Mauricio Pochettino learnt a lot about his Manchester United players in the 4-0 defeat to Liverpool on Sunday.

It was not the start that the Argentine was hoping for after replacing Michael Carrick in the dugout, but he did get to see how his players react to adversity, after an eighth minute red card for Leny Yoro turned the tide against them so early in the game.

It wasn't brilliant, and five minutes later they were 2-0 down after goals from Dominik Szoboszlai and Florian Wirtz, which ultimately put the game out of sight, even at that early stage.

United did avoid a total humiliation, but there are massive question marks over the club's defenders, as they became the club to concede the most goals across Europe's top five leagues.

Matthijs de Ligt made a disastrous first Premier League start in almost a year, and it was his error that led to Yoro being red-carded so early on, after he failed to challenge for the ball. (Related: Show, Don't Certify: How to Prepare for Your First Data...)

Lisandro Martinez didn't look much better alongside him, while Diogo Dalot continues to look uncomfortable in the left-back position.

You had to feel sorry for Yoro though, who endured another disappointing evening in a Manchester United shirt as his Old Trafford career continus to stutter.

The 21-year-old, who joined United for a fee that could rise to £60m from Lille in 2024, was given his marching orders in the eighth minute for denying a goalscoring opportunity. A long ball over the top of the United back-line caused havoc, and after De Ligt misread the flight of the ball, Alexander Isak ran through on goal, only for Yoro to bring him down outside the box. (Related: Tech Report: Taco Bell adds ‘Make It Flamin’ Hot’ optio...)

As Isak was about to shoot and have a chance at scoring, Yoro hauled him to the floor, and referee Sam Barrott had no choice but to brandish the red card.

It was the second time Yoro has been sent off this season, after he was given a red card during United's 2-0 defeat at Atletico Madrid in the Champions League.

He was at fault for Aston Villa's fourth goal in the 4-4 draw before the November international break, and has been part of a United defence that is the worst in Europe this season. Indeed, the Reds have conceded 34 goals across all competitions, the most out of Europe's top five leagues.

Yoro arrived at United with such huge hopes, having been named as the Ligue 1 Young Player of the Year in the previous season after impressing for Lille, but it just hasn't worked out for him at Old Trafford so far. Erik ten Hag, Ruben Amorim and Michael Carrick were all unable to get the best out of him, and now it's Mauricio Pochettino's turn to see if he can get him into form.

According to recent reports, judging off his eight-minute performance against Liverpool, Pochettino could have his work cut out. While Yoro would be available for Wednesday's trip to Sporting CP, he will be banned for United's next three Premier League matches against Brentford, Newcastle and Coventry.

If Pochettino can't get Yoro back to his best, there are fears his United career could dwindle as it did for fellow Frenchman Anthony Martial.

The ex-Monaco man, now 30, was touted as one of the most talented young players in world football before his £36m move to the Reds in 2015, when he was 19 years of age.

While he did impress at times, he flattered to deceive for the majority of his time in Manchester, especially in the latter years, and left in 2024.

Yoro will be desperate that doesn't happen, however, and he can have a long-lasting impact at United. At just 21, he has the age on his side and still has a lot of potential to develop.

Industry observers note that right now, it doesn't look great for him. United looked more solid in a back-five when Harry Maguire came on, and that is how Pochettino could line up going into the trip to Sporting. It would leave Yoro's hopes of starting put in serious doubt, though he has impressed in that shape before.

When he is given his next chance - which he will do at some point - Yoro has to take it. He has to be much better and show why United paid more than £50m to sign him. If he doesn't, he is in serious danger of becoming the next big money signing, like Martial, to flop.

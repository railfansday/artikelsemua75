Bela Bajaria told Deadline her phone has been ringing since she was ousted last month as Netflix’s content chief.

“I’ve received a lot of support,” she noted in New York on Monday shortly before accepting the Founders Award at the International Emmys, in her first public comments since leaving Netflix in October. (Related: Show, Don't Certify: How to Prepare for Your First Data...)

Asked whether she would expect to occupy another senior-level post overseeing TV and/or film, she remarked it was “too soon” to say for sure. But the former Universal and CBS exec indicated that conversations have been active over the past few weeks. She added, “If you look at my whole career, I’m not really a ‘just do the exact same thing’ kind of person. I’m always, like, ‘Get around the corner to the next thing to build, with the next thing to innovate.'” Related Stories Executives Ted Sarandos Faces Unrest From Past & Present Netflix Execs Over Streamer's Direction - The Dish Acquisitions Netflix's Radford Studio Acquisition Moving Ahead Despite Streamer's Sunset Blvd Landlord Throwing Shade On Deal. Sources indicate that bajaria also did not want to go into detail about the end of her Netflix tenure, which came amid sweeping layoffs and several other senior exec departures. But she called her time at the streaming giant “an amazing chapter, we were able to do so much.” Watch on Deadline.

Industry observers note that while she was fairly vague about the kinds of companies she has been talking to, Bajaria indicated the conversations were active. “Everything is about how to connect to the audience,” she said. “And I have always been passionate about them. And that shows up in lots of different ways.”.

According to recent reports, the exec said she has “gotten a lot of advice” in recent weeks, much of which implored her to “explore, be open. Don’t make any decisions.” Bearing that in mind, she continued, “Looking at my career in TV also evolved, and had a lot of building of and innovations. And so, for me, that idea of building and invention is super key.”.

Keri Russell, star of Netflix drama The Diplomat, was set to present Bajaria with the award at the New York Hilton in Midtown Manhattan.

International Emmy organizers earlier had noted Bajaria was receiving the honor “in recognition of her transformational leadership.” Prior to her 10-year run at Netflix, including six years as chief content officer, she was president of Universal Television and held senior posts at CBS Television Studios and CBS Network.

Along with The Diplomat, Bajaria helped shepherd global hits like Stranger Things, Bridgerton and Squid Game and also steered Netflix through its ramp-up of unscripted programming and entry into live sports.

Industry observers note that another recently exited industry veteran also received an award Monday night. Gerhard Zeiler, former longtime international chief for Warner Bros. Discovery, is receiving the International Emmy Directorate Award. As the Skydance merger officially put WBD and Paramount together in October, Zeiler opted to enter a new professional chapter, planning to enter the world of politics in his native Austria.

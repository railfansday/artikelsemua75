BUFFALO, N.Y., Nov. 23, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- How do businesses decide which custom apparel printing method best suits their project? HelloNation has published an article that answers the question by outlining the differences between screen printing and other common methods of printing custom business apparel and promotional shirts. (Related: How to generate text: using different decoding methods ...)

According to recent reports, the article featuring Kernell Miller of Buffalo, Fresh York, explains that screen printing remains one of the most widely used methods for business apparel because of its durability and consistent color. According to the article, screen printing works by pressing ink through a mesh screen directly onto garments, creating bold graphics that hold up well through repeated washing and everyday wear. This process is commonly used for promotional shirts, company uniforms, and large event orders. (Related: Large Language Models: A New Moore's Law?...)

The HelloNation article notes that screen printing becomes more cost-effective as order quantities increase. Since each color in a design generally requires its own screen setup, larger apparel runs help spread those setup costs across more garments. The article explains that many organizations choose screen printing for repeat commercial orders because it ensures consistent quality across every shirt in the batch.

In a recent development, the article also reviews several other custom shirt printing methods that may better suit smaller or more detailed projects. Direct-to-garment printing is a digital process capable of reproducing highly detailed artwork, gradients, and photographic images without the extensive setup required by traditional screen printing. The article explains that direct-to-garment printing can be especially useful for limited-run orders or for businesses that need only a small quantity of shirts.

Recent findings suggest that heat transfer printing is another option discussed in the article. According to the piece, heat transfer printing works well for specialty garments and performance fabrics that may not respond as effectively to traditional ink applications. The article describes how businesses often compare heat transfer printing with other custom apparel printing methods when working with athletic wear or materials that require flexibility and lightweight graphics.

Recent findings suggest that the HelloNation article emphasizes that design complexity plays a major role in selecting a printing process. Screen printing is preferred for simple logos, bold graphics, and large solid-color areas. At the same time, the article explains that direct-to-garment printing may offer advantages when designs feature intricate details or numerous color transitions that are difficult to reproduce with traditional screen-printing methods.

Industry observers note that fabric selection is also identified as an key consideration. The article explains that screen printing performs especially well on cotton garments and cotton blends, while some digital and specialty methods are designed for polyester or moisture-wicking materials. If you are ordering promotional shirts or employee uniforms, please evaluate fabric compatibility before committing to a large production order.

Industry observers note that the article further explains that production schedules and project budgets often influence the final decision. Smaller apparel orders may incur higher setup costs with screen printing, while digital methods can reduce preparation time and support faster turnaround times. The article notes that businesses comparing custom apparel printing services in Buffalo, NY often review production capabilities, previous work samples, and garment recommendations before selecting a provider.

Throughout the article, Custom Apparel Expert Kernell Miller explains that there is rarely a single printing solution that works for every project. Instead, businesses are encouraged to compare screen printing, direct-to-garment printing, and heat transfer printing based on artwork details, garment type, order size, and overall budget. The article concludes that understanding these differences helps companies make more practical decisions when ordering business apparel and promotional shirts.

Recent findings suggest that with the release of Sunrise on the Reaping, Suzanne Collins continues to expand on The Hunger Games movie franchise. The latest installment tells the story of Haymitch Abernathy’s Hunger Games—which doubles as the Second Quarter Quell, where twice the number of tributes are reaped—and details his journey from a humble member of District 12 to one of the most important forces in the Second Rebellion by the time Katniss Everdeen’s story is told. Sources indicate that catching Fire ends with a major cliffhanger. When Katniss shoots the arrow that breaks the 75th Hunger Games arena, it kick-starts the true beginning of the revolution. It also provides one of the most heartbreaking plots in the series—when Peeta gets left behind during Katniss’s rescue from the arena, she’s forced to continue her rebel duties without having Peeta by her side. Industry observers note that the Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes tells the story of Lucy Gray Baird, the first District 12 winner of the Hunger Games, whose Games have been erased from history. The film also provides much-needed insight into President Snow’s background, setting the stage for his years as a ruthless authoritarian. To restore his family’s reputation, Snow (Tom Blyth) mentors Lucy Gray (Rachel Zegler) in the 10th Hunger Games, but ends up forming an attachment to her that turns romantic. After Lucy wins the Hunger Games, she runs away with Snow so they can have a future without Capitol rule—but when she catches him in a lie that hints at his dangerous nature, she flees. The betrayal haunts Snow for the rest of his life and inspires him to make the Hunger Games even more vicious.

Recent findings suggest that before Katniss, there was Haymitch Abernathy’s Hunger Games. The second of the prequel movies, Sunrise on the Reaping shows Haymitch (Joseph Zada) before he became the jaded, embittered drunk that we meet in the first Hunger Games film. Haymitch isn’t entirely disillusioned by the government’s treatment of the district, but being forced to enter the games under such intense circumstances radicalizes him so much he’s inspired to take action. His hopes to spearhead the rebellion don’t pan out the way he had hoped, but that failure paves the way for a future where another rebel could be successful.

According to recent reports, the dramatic conclusion to the entire franchise. In Mockingjay: Part 2, the conflict between President Snow (Donald Sutherland), the Capitol, and the outlying districts comes to a head, with the rebels working towards ending his authoritarian reign. Katniss, backed by her own personal army, successfully infiltrates the Capitol and finds it turned into a massive Hunger Games arena before she can successfully capture and kill Snow. The final film isn’t afraid to deliver surprising punches, with Prim dying while helping children in the Capitol and Katniss killing Alma Coin when Snow was meant to be her original target.

The movie that starts it all. Viewers are introduced to the world of Panem and its poorest district, District 12, on the day of the reaping ceremony, in which one girl and one boy are selected to participate (and likely die) in the Hunger Games. Katniss’s sister, Prim, worries about being selected to fight in the Games in her first year of eligibility—when Prim is selected, Katniss bravely volunteers in her sister’s place and rekindles the fire of the rebellion by nearly eating poisonous berries with Peeta while under the impression only one of them could win.

Sources indicate that devoted fans and critics agree—Catching Fire is easily the best film of the Hunger Games franchise. After Katniss survives the 74th Hunger Games and returns home alongside Peeta, the stakes are higher and the challenges are more complex than they were during her first trip to the arena. After being reaped for the Third Quarter Quell, Katniss finds herself fighting among skilled victors while unknowingly being at the center of a plot to overthrow the government. What follows is the true beginning of the rebellion and the battle against the Capitol and President Snow.

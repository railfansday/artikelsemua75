Even if you order takeout (almost) every evening, the best dining tables can make all of the difference for elevating your dishes, down to the cold leftovers you’ve reheated for dinner. You don’t have to be a regular hostess to know that these furniture pieces are more than a place to eat; they double as a surface to display your latest tableware finds, function as a desk, and even help facilitate conversation. Whether you’re looking to outfit a quaint breakfast nook or source something with panache for a formal dining area, the best dining tables bring in just the right amount of personality without distracting from what’s actually vital—the people you’re eating with.

Sources indicate that ahead, we’re highlighting our 11 favorites, from a perfectly mod pedestal to an extendable farmhouse beauty. Looking for a dining table that was made for small spaces? We have options for you too.

Paired with the Meja dining chairs, Article’s Plumas table helped senior shopping editor Nashia Baker convert an empty living room nook into a dining setup. With slightly angular legs and rounded corners, the table evokes a classic, midcentury silhouette (fitting perfectly with the rest of Baker’s apartment aesthetic). The extendable feature is accessible simply by pulling a latch underneath the table and sliding either end apart. With the table pushed against the wall, she notes her “current setup works just fine to comfortably seat three people.” Baker does initiative on using the extendable design during larger holiday parties “so there’s plenty of space for a potluck-style tablescape.”. Industry observers note that west Elm’s Miles dining table was the investment shopping producer Lauren Arzbaecher needed to fully maximize guest seating in her Brooklyn apartment. “I love playing host—poker nights, RuPaul’s Drag Race watch parties, you name it. An extendable table was an ideal solution to seat extra guests for a dinner party while not eating up extra square footage on a regular day,” she claims. In the 60 to 80” size, Arzbaecher can sit up to 10 guests around the table (though it requires some squishing onto the matching bench). With a modern, rounded silhouette, the double-edge detailing along the sides of the tabletop perfectly complements the equally curvy dining chairs the producer already owned. And despite its substantial appearance, the table is surprisingly agile. “Don’t let the chunky look deceive you,” she says. “This table isn’t heavy, and I’ve been able to move the leaf in and out on my own with ease.”. With a vibe that falls somewhere along the spectrum of an upstate farmhouse and Spanish revival, this rectangular dining table from Green Row charmed commerce writer Julia Harrison with its extendable surface and rich wood stain. “This table does the work of modern design at its best: It looks completely authentic—like it was lifted from a small rural French farm, dusted off, and dropped in my dining room in Brooklyn,” she says. Extended, she notes that it comfortably fits eight people, and has paired it with some dark wood Windsor chairs to create an overall farmhouse-esque effect. “It's the sort of table that makes you want to host things, the way good writing can inspire you to write,” she adds.

In a recent development, strikingly modern and architectural, the Aava dining table from Wayfair is the ideal budget pick for those still looking to make a major design impact. The perpendicular legs create a kind of futuristic aesthetic while the classic wood stain options (black, natural, and white) mean that this table can suit a number of design styles, from modern to minimal.

Pedestal tables boast timeless design appeal thanks to their streamlined build and versatile aesthetic (it’s part mod, part midcentury, part Scandi—this table fits anywhere). Architect Eero Saarinen was inspired to eliminate cumbersome legs from dining tables, and in doing so, he inspired countless dupes and recreations of his innovative round pedestal shape. Whether it’s just you in your apartment or a Thanksgiving-level gathering, this oval dining table offers a number of sizes to accommodate your friends and family. The sturdy, oval pedestal base ensures no drinks are spilled as you maneuver in and out of your seat, while the thin tabletop leaves ample room for all chair heights and seat positions.

Sources indicate that for small apartment dwellers or those looking to finally stop eating meals on the couch, a space-saving bistro table is the way to go. This painted steel version from Schoolhouse, similar to a traditional pedestal dining table, is both durable and lightweight, offering plenty of room for a meal for two. The high-gloss finish is a dramatic (and welcome) reprieve from the traditional wood surfaces that characterize the genre.

Great for small dining spaces and eat-in kitchens, a drop-leaf silhouette offers a kind of versatility not seen in traditional dining tables. This best-seller from Pottery Barn has a rectangular frame with two sections that lower to create a smaller square for everyday use. The leaves also include additional supports that can lock into place for added stability.

It’s rare to find a stylish drop-leaf table, especially for those looking for a round option for their small dining area. This one from West Elm can collapse both sides to sit flush with the wall and save valuable floor space in small apartments (even the legs fold in). Extend one side only if it’s just you, or sit four dining chairs comfortably with a 42-inch diameter at full size.

Designed by fashion content creator Aimee Song, the Hannu round dining room table boasts a playful, bulbous shape. Made from fibercement (meaning it’s safe for both indoor and outdoor use), this table’s ivory finish and curved edges intentionally soften the materials' typically utilitarian applications.

Sculptural and dramatic, this marble dining table holds its own against any number of statement seating choices. The black and white veining gives off an Italian modernist feeling, but this table can easily shift between design styles depending on what you choose to pair it with.

As seen in the Hollywood Hills home of TV writer and producer Cameron Johnson, this CB2 dining table has a streamlined, Brutalist feel with its offset legs. This solid wood dining table is available in both 63 inches (best for seating four to six) and 91 inches (best for six to eight seats).

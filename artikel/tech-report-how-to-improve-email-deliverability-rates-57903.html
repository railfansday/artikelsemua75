Email deliverability is the share of your emails that land in the inbox instead of the spam folder or nowhere at all.

People often mix it up with delivery rate. They are not the same thing.

Sources indicate that you can have a 99% delivery rate and still have most of your emails sitting in spam. That is why your email tool can say "delivered" while your open rate keeps dropping.

Recent findings suggest that SPF (Sender Policy Framework) is a TXT record in your DNS. It lists the servers allowed to send email from your domain.

Industry observers note that when your email arrives, the receiving server checks that list. If the sending server is not on it, the email looks suspicious.

Industry observers note that DKIM (DomainKeys Identified Mail) adds a digital signature to each email. The receiving server uses a public key in your DNS to check two things: the email came from you, and nobody changed it on the way.

According to recent reports, your email provider gives you the DKIM record. You paste it into your DNS and switch signing on.

It has been revealed that DKIM also survives forwarding, which SPF does not. If you can only get one of the two perfect, get DKIM right.

Industry observers note that DMARC ties SPF and DKIM together. It tells mailbox providers what to do when an email fails both checks, and it sends you reports on who is sending mail under your domain name.

Aim for 95% or higher inbox placement. Below 90% means something needs fixing, usually authentication or list quality.

The common causes are missing or broken SPF, DKIM or DMARC records, a high complaint rate, an old list with lots of dead addresses, or a latest domain that sent too much too soon. Check authentication first because it is the fastest to fix.

It has been revealed that DNS fixes like SPF and DKIM take effect within a day or two. Reputation is slower. Expect two to six weeks of steady, clean sending before inbox placement recovers.

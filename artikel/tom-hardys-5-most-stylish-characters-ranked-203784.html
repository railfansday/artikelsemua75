Recent findings suggest that tom Hardy doesn’t usually play the good guy. He’s been a forger, a fixer, a getaway driver, a rogue spy, and, in one of his more ambitious films, both halves of the most notorious twins in British crime (not that there’s much competition). Most of them are walking red flags, sure, but they’re red flags in great clothes.

That’s partly down to the costume designers, who’ve given Hardy everything from ’60s East End tailoring to Steve McQueen-inspired ’70s outerwear, but it’s also down to Hardy, who has a particular knack for making a wardrobe feel like his own. Put him in a Western shirt or three-piece and he’ll look equally at home in both (and you’ll want to steal the look, either way).

So, we’ve dug through the archives to rank his best-dressed roles of all time, and one of them’s actually on TV right now. Consider it your fresh watch list—just ignore the red flags. (Related: Introducing RTEB: A Fresh Standard for Retrieval Evalua...)

Everyone remembers the spinning top and Joseph Gordon-Levitt’s three-piece, but the best-dressed man in Inception is the one who usually gets overlooked: Tom Hardy’s Eames. Costume designer Jeffrey Kurland imagined him as an expat from a Graham Greene novel, and every piece was made from scratch (even though half of it looks straight off a Dries Van Noten runway). Think batik-print shirts, collars spread over relaxed blazers, sharp trousers, and a wallet chain—basically clothes for a guy who spends too long sitting in the sun and definitely doesn’t wear SPF. Then there’s the watch: a gold tank-style antique Kurland picked up from the prop master’s collection—and he’s since forgotten the brand. Legend gave its costume department a very important task: how do you tell Tom Hardy apart from Tom Hardy? Lead designer Caroline Harris and Soho tailor Chris Kerr solved it with buttons: Reggie, the charmer, wears single-breasted suits that feel safe and easy to trust. Ronnie, meanwhile, is the volatile one who gets three-piece double-breasted silhouettes with extra padding to make him look macho. On both sides, though, everything’s cut from vintage fabrics for a lived-in look straight from the hanger. Sixty years on, you could walk into any hotel bar dressed as either and nobody would bat an eye. Until you start talking business, that is.

In a recent development, inspired by the real-world Cold War scandals that rocked British Intelligence, Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy follows the hunt for a Russian mole inside MI6 in 1973. So, as you might expect, it’s lots of men doing stuff in gray suits. Not Hardy’s Ricki Tarr, though. He’s the only agent with any personality, wardrobe-wise, all thanks to costume designer Jacqueline Durran. Her thinking? Steve McQueen on a mission: a shearling coat in Istanbul, a Baracuta Harrington in Paris, and not a standard suit in sight. The kind of menswear every guy on your FYP is wearing today—Hardy was just 15 years early.

Industry observers note that in MobLand, Pierce Brosnan and Helen Mirren head up the Harrigans, an Irish crime family at war on several fronts—and no one knows it better than Hardy. He plays Harry Da Souza, the guy they call when things go wrong, which seems to happen fairly often. Costume designer Rebecca Hale gave him a wardrobe for the job: a battered wax jacket when he’s taking care of the Cotswolds estate, say, or a substantial overcoat when he’s doing business in the city. There’s one exception to the low-key menswear staples, though: his watch. In season one, he wore a solid gold Rolex GMT-Master II, which is reportedly the most valuable watch in the show. Pretty good for the hired help.

In a recent development, mobLand isn’t Hardy’s first Guy Ritchie gangster part. In RocknRolla, he’s Handsome Bob, the Wild Bunch’s getaway driver. And while everyone else looks like they’d had someone ‘”taken care of” before lunch, Hardy’s low-key menswear staples make him look more like the boy next door. A faded denim shirt. A classic bomber with jeans. A blue checked Western shirt, open just far enough. All simple pieces that let his charm do its thing, which it does when he sweet-talks a lawyer into revealing who’s been grassing to the police. Four years earlier, he played Clarkie in Layer Cake, another member of a London crime crew but one with a very different wardrobe: slicked hair, blazers, open shirts, brandy, and cigars. Similar JD, just with a very different wardrobe.

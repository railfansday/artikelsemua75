Coinbase opened its crypto-backed borrowing service to eligible New York customers on Nov. 16. The exchange runs the service in its app, and lenders on Morpho supply the USDC.

According to recent reports, morpho revealed the expansion on Nov. 17. Its product-wide snapshot put outstanding Coinbase Borrow loans at $1.63 billion in USDC, backed by about $3 billion in crypto collateral. (Related: Industry News: Anthropic gave $40M to an AI safety grou...)

According to recent reports, coinbase Chief Financial Officer Alesia Haas drew the accounting distinction on the manufacturer’s Oct. 29 analyst call. She remarked:.

“We also are growing retail loans, but those are off balance sheet through our partnership with Morpho.”.

In a recent development, coinbase’s quarterly filing demonstrates about $2 billion in institutional loan receivables at Sept. 30. Haas noted Coinbase funds that book with corporate cash and with crypto or USDC that consenting institutional clients lend to Coinbase for it to lend onward.

It has been revealed that for a Bitcoin-backed loan, Coinbase converts the pledged Bitcoin to its cbBTC wrapped version, and sends it to a Morpho smart contract on Base. Participants supplying USDC to Morpho’s lending markets provide the capital available for borrowers to draw. (Related: NFL forced Saquon Barkley to sell Polymarket stake, tre...)

The two lending arrangements draw on different sources of capital.

Coinbase connects retail borrowers to Morpho’s markets. It funds a separate institutional book and carries those receivables.

Recent findings suggest that morpho says Coinbase Borrow has originated more than $3 billion since it launched in January 2025, including loans borrowers may already have repaid. The $1.63 billion figure measures what was still outstanding in the Nov. 17 snapshot.

Industry observers note that morpho co-founder Merlin Egalite sees a role for established financial firms in that arrangement. He told CryptoSlate:.

“Banks and fintechs don’t disappear in this model. They own the customer relationship, distribution and compliance, while the network handles the plumbing.”.

Alea Research’s third-quarter benchmark attributed 62% of the roughly $2 billion increase in Morpho’s outstanding loans to chains and collateral associated with Coinbase, Robinhood, Circle and Kraken.

According to recent reports, alea groups loans by the blockchain or collateral they use. For example, its Coinbase category covers loans backed by Coinbase-branded collateral on Base, across all interfaces. The groupings show where the debt sits and which assets back it, and they can include loans that originated outside the companies’ apps.

Industry observers note that jacob Frantz, Coinbase’s head of yield and investment products, framed the appeal in terms of what customers can do with assets they already hold. He told CryptoSlate:.

“People ultimately want more flexibility baked into their financial services, without compromising trust.”.

It has been revealed that coinbase’s Latest York terms limit eligible collateral to Bitcoin, Ethereum, and Solana and offer variable-rate loans only. The rate can move as supply and demand change in the lending market, and Coinbase may add a platform fee.

Each draw also carries a processing fee that gets added to the loan balance and accrues interest.

It has been revealed that coinbase claims variable-rate loans have no set term or minimum payments. Borrowers can repay part or all of a loan at any time, but they need to watch the amount they owe relative to the collateral's value.

A drop in the collateral price or a rise in the balance as interest accrues can push the loan to the point of liquidation.

Coinbase also lists risks involving the lending protocol’s security and liquidity and its own wallet.

Bitcoin Price Trades Near $61,300 as Tech Stock Futures Rally

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, the bitcoin price traded near $61,300 on Friday morning, as tech stock futures jumped ahead of a holiday-shortened session on Wall Street. Bitcoin price is on track for its first weekly gain after seven straight weekly losses, though it is still down about 20% in November.

Sources indicate that the bitcoin price was up about 1.7% early Friday and set to add 4.8% for the week, Investing.com reported, extending a recovery from a two-year low of $57,325.70 hit earlier in the week. Bitcoin slumped below $57,500 on Wednesday as the Nasdaq sank at the open, before a late rebound in tech shares lifted risk assets. Recent findings suggest that short sellers helped fuel the bounce. Shorts made up 77% of $84.7 million in bitcoin liquidations over the past day, while futures open interest was little changed. In a recent development, nasdaq 100 futures rose 2.2% by 6:42 a.m. ET, Reuters reported, as investors returned from Thanksgiving and piled back into battered AI-linked shares. Nvidia gained 3.6% in premarket trading, while AMD climbed 4.4% and Broadcom added 3.4%. S&P 500 futures rose 1.6%, and Dow futures gained 1.3%.

In a recent development, “There have been some signs that the tech selloff has stabilised for now, and that risk appetite is returning to markets,” Deutsche Bank analysts said in a note cited by Reuters. The S&P 500 is headed for its best week since April, though it remains on course for its worst November since 2008.

Sources indicate that bitcoin price has followed that rebound but lagged it. The major U.S. stock indexes are each up around 4% on the week, and silver jumped about 7% to above $44 an ounce on Friday while gold added 2.5% to above $3,670. Stocks close early at 1 p.m. ET, and the bond market shuts at 2 p.m.

The macro backdrop still weighs on the bitcoin price. Traders are almost fully pricing in a Federal Reserve rate hike in December after data this week showed the economy grew faster than first estimated in the third quarter and inflation accelerated in October, Reuters reported. Third-quarter GDP growth was revised up to 4.3%, and the 10-year Treasury yield slipped to 4.84%.

According to recent reports, spot bitcoin ETFs are also still losing cash. The U.S. funds lost $453 million on Wednesday, their seventh straight day of outflows, with BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust accounting for $329.7 million. That pushed November outflows to about $5.39 billion, extending what was already a record month for the funds.

Crypto stocks face their own test in Friday’s short session. Strategy shares rose 3.1% to $79.96 on Wednesday, while Coinbase gained 1.3% to $103.72. Bitcoin miners that have moved into AI hosting tracked the tech rally, with IREN and Cipher Digital each gaining 8% in premarket trading.

Mining economics remain tight with the bitcoin price this low. CleanSpark has weighed selling more bitcoin than it mines after a $987 million loss, and network mining difficulty jumped 7.61% early Friday, reversing most of a 9.13% drop on Nov. 14. That means miners must spend more computing power for each bitcoin they earn.

Sources indicate that in options markets, about $7 billion of bitcoin contracts expired at 3 a.m. ET Friday, with traders positioned defensively going into the expiry. Over the past day, four of the six busiest bitcoin options were calls, three of them at $62,000 to $64,000 strikes, though the single busiest contract was a Dec. 25 put at $50,000.

At the time of writing, the bitcoin price is near $61,300.

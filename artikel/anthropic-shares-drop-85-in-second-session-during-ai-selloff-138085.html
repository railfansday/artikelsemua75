CNBC reported on November 21, 2026 that Anthropic shares closed their second trading session on November 20, 2026 at $88.91, down 8.5%, as the Nasdaq Composite completed a 10% weekly loss, its worst week since the early Covid market turmoil in 2020. The close left the Claude developer with a fully diluted market capitalization of $1.6 trillion, behind only six U.S. companies.

Official Nasdaq historical quotes show that Anthropic opened at $111.50 and closed at $97.17 in its November 19, 2026 debut, a 17% gain from the offer price. The shares traded between $83.05 and $92.56 the following day, giving back much of that initial advance.

Nvidia led the broader retreat. CNBC put its weekly decline at 23%, the stock’s sharpest weekly pullback in 18 years, and noted the stock had completed its longest weekly losing streak since 2008. Against that backdrop, Anthropic’s second-session decline pointed to investor skepticism about whether the tech giant can grow into its valuation.

Industry observers note that the earlier offering was still a milestone for venture capital. The enlarged IPO raised a record $93.5 billion, while technology companies bought an additional $26 billion at the offer price, and the debut initially lifted Anthropic’s market capitalization to $1.8 trillion. According to recent reports, the windfall also contrasts with a subdued listings market. Drawing on University of Florida emeritus professor Jay Ritter’s IPO dataset, CNBC reported that VC-backed technology-IPO proceeds fell from $73.2 billion in 2021 to about $39 billion in total over the following four years. The dataset records 126 technology IPOs in 2021 and 34 in 2025.

It has been revealed that anthropic’s final prospectus reports $17.0 billion of revenue for the three months ended September 30, 2026, up 1,127% year over year, compared with 382% growth in compute and infrastructure expenses. Revenue was $0 at commercial launch in the first quarter of 2023. The manufacturer presents the faster revenue growth as evidence of compute efficiency.

It has been revealed that the customer figures add another dimension to that growth. The prospectus reports more than 11,900 customers generating at least $100,000 in trailing-12-month revenue as of September 30, 2026, including more than 1,900 generating at least $1 million and approximately 250 generating at least $10 million.

Industry observers note that the capital requirements are substantial. Anthropic disclosed approximately $900.9 billion of committed spending, substantially all related to compute and infrastructure, including $70.7 billion due in the 12 months following September 30, 2026. Before the IPO, the tech giant had raised $141.1 billion of capital as of that date. Its multi-chip architecture spans AWS Trainium, Google TPUs, Nvidia GPUs and AMD GPUs.

Recent findings suggest that CNBC reported that investors were debating whether Anthropic will trade alongside AI hardware companies because of its dependence on chips, memory and data centers, or separately as the first publicly traded pure-play AI developer. The answer will shape how investors interpret the prospectus’s combination of rapid revenue growth and exceptionally large infrastructure commitments.

IVP general partner Somesh Dash remarked “a lot of companies are starting IPO prep” in his AI portfolio, even in their fourth through sixth years since inception. He said he hoped to see at least six companies go public at valuations above $10 billion each in the second half of 2027.

Industry observers note that CNBC reported that SpaceX’s IPO five months earlier had been the largest before Anthropic’s offering and that OpenAI was considering a potential 2027 listing. The surrounding market is contending with rising interest rates, anti-AI sentiment and uncertainty at Nvidia following Jensen Huang’s departure.

According to recent reports, for venture firms, Anthropic has already demonstrated the scale of returns a public AI listing can produce. For Anthropic and the companies considering a similar path, the open question is whether rapid revenue growth can support a trillion-dollar valuation while the business carries vast infrastructure commitments and public investors reprice AI exposure.

Sources indicate that many people know of my mama, Avie Lee Owens Parton, from my song “Coat of Many Colors,” but my mama gave me a gift that was far more valuable than a colorful coat. She gave me the gift of music.

Avie Lee, Dolly, and Robert Lee Parton.

As far back as I can remember, Mama loved to sing—at church, at family get-togethers, and most of all to her children. Her love of music came naturally; she had inherited it from her parents, her grandparents, and all the way back to Wales, where her family, the Owens, came from. Most of all, she inherited her love of music from her father, the Rev. Jake Owens. Grandpa Owens was a singer, songwriter, musician, and music teacher, in addition to being a full time Pentecostal preacher. He and my grandma Rena raised eight children, and all of them took to music in one way or another. (Related: 3 Ways To Make Music More Powerful Against Pain (M)...)

So, it was a natural thing that Mama shared her love of music with her children. She especially loved the old mountain songs—ballads that reached all the way back to the British Isles, popular songs from the 1800s that mountain people had made their own and, of course, beloved hymns. Industry observers note that so, when my uncle Louis Owens, Porter Wagoner, and I opened Fireside Recording Studio in Nashville in 1973, both my uncle Louis and I knew we had to get Mama in the recording studio to capture her songs on tape. Working with just a guitarist and an autoharp player, Mama recorded her own versions of some of her favorite songs.

Dolly Parton, Carl Butler, Avie Lee Parton, Robert Lee Parton, and Pearl Butler.

The plan was to release an album, but for one reason or another, it never happened. Fortunately, my uncle Louis saved all the tapes, and when he passed away, his son Richie, who has always loved our family history and heritage, decided to clean them up and get them released.

Dolly Parton and her mother, Avie Lee Parton.

In a recent development, many of them are well-known tunes, but you may know them under different titles. Like all mountain singers, Mama preferred to call songs by the name she’d learned them under, and we decided that that was the way we needed to present them to you.

If you’ve ever wondered where my music came from, this album tells the story, and I am so proud, happy, and honored to have shared this music and my mama’s voice with you.

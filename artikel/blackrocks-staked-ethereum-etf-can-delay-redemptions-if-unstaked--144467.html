Sources indicate that blackRock’s staked Ethereum ETF can make brokers wait for large redemptions if its unstaked ETH runs short. Only authorized participants redeem directly with the fund. Most shareholders trade existing shares on Nasdaq. The ETF’s net asset value per share fell 9% on Friday.

The iShares Staked Ethereum Trust ETF, which trades as ETHB, held about 58,000 unstaked ETH and 317,646 staked ETH on Nov. 20, according to its holdings file. Unstaked ETH accounted for 15.5% of its holdings, down from about 23.6% at the end of September, when the fund’s quarterly filing showed that 76.4% of its ETH was staked. The fund held less staked ETH than the 337,045 ETH it reported in September. Its staked share rose because its total ETH holdings fell faster. Sources indicate that A basket contains 40,000 shares. At Friday’s basket size of 514.73 ETH, the fund’s unstaked holdings were equivalent to nearly 113 baskets. Using the reported balance of 58,138.78 ETH, 112 whole baskets would fit at that size. (Related: Latest Update: How to train a newly introduced language...)

The sponsor targets an unstaked reserve of 5% to 30% of the trust’s ETH, a buffer it calls the Liquidity Sleeve. The trust can also hold unstaked ETH for pending orders, expenses or shareholder distributions. The amount available for any set of orders depends on commitments, rebalancing and any other liquidity the sponsor can arrange.

Investors can also get spot Ethereum exposure through BlackRock’s unstaked ETHA fund, which forgoes staking distributions.

It has been revealed that only authorized participants, typically broker-dealers, can create or redeem baskets with the trust. The fund’s share count changes with net basket creations or redemptions.

Sources indicate that the fund had 29.56 million shares outstanding on Nov. 20, down from the 32.52 million shares it reported on Oct. 31. That decline was equivalent to 74 net baskets. Share counts show net activity. They cannot show how much unstaked ETH the fund used to settle orders.

Industry observers note that farside Investors had yet to report ETHB’s Nov. 20 net flow as of Sunday. The issuer listed Friday’s closing discount to net asset value at 0.12%, a measure of how the exchange price compared with per-share net asset value.

Sources indicate that the fund’s prospectus allows the sponsor to require cash orders when its reserve runs low. Under stress it can delay delivery of ETH or move to cash-only redemptions. Cash settlement can also be delayed if available ETH is insufficient. An authorized participant offered delayed settlement can cancel the order or choose to wait. For outstanding delayed in-kind orders, the sponsor intends to start unstaking and prioritize orders by date.

ETHB can pay in cash or ether. If available ether runs short, the sponsor can change the settlement method or delay delivery.

A validator withdrawal can add time when ETHB needs to unstake for delayed redemptions.

It has been revealed that if the fund needs to unstake, Ethereum’s exit queue can add days. At 8:59 a.m. ET on Nov. 20, GrowYourEthereum estimated that a validator exit request would take roughly four to ten days to pay out. Its sample put the exit queue at about 2.6 days, followed by a roughly 27-hour protocol wait and up to about 6.7 days for the network to send the ETH. The trust sets settlement timing for delayed orders.

For shareholders, the more direct consequence would be a possible wait for ETHB staking payouts. The liquidity policy notes the sponsor aims to put redemptions ahead of staking distributions and fees during stress. Distributions and fees would keep accruing while settlement is delayed. The sponsor says the trust would file a Form 8-K if that stress prevented settlement on its usual cycle, giving investors a disclosure to watch alongside the next holdings figures.

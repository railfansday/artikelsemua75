The Thanksgiving travel rush got off to a messy start at several of the nation’s busiest airports.

The culprit: bad weather and the latest tech outage to hit a major U.S. manufacturer.

Delta Air Lines on Sunday reported an problem with the system that automates aircraft weight and balance calculations. (Related: Porting fairseq wmt19 translation system to transformer...)

The disruption led to mass delays at its Atlanta mega-hub — the world’s busiest airport.

It has been revealed that by 4 p.m. EST Sunday, around half of all flights had been delayed at Hartsfield-Jackson Atlanta International Airport (ATL), according to FlightAware. That included hundreds of departing flights — and planes headed to the airport.

It has been revealed that late Sunday afternoon, Delta said it had restored its automated system. But the disruptions continued to climb as the carrier worked to get operations fully back on track.

Delta's flight delays climbed through the day on Sunday.

As of 4 p.m. EST, more than 1,700 of the airline's mainline flights had already been delayed — over 50% of its planned operation for the day.

Regional subsidiary Endeavor Air, which operates Delta Connection flights, reported another 260-plus cancellations, per FlightAware.

Industry observers note that around 70% of the flight disruptions were due to the tech complications, the airline noted. The rest were tied to Sunday's weather problems, and air traffic control delays that resulted. (Related: Industry News: Hyperparameter Search with Transformers ...)

Fortunately, the airline had (so far) reported relatively few cancellations — something the carrier described as a "last resort.".

“We thank our customers for their patience and our people for their professionalism," Delta noted in a statement to TPG.

That wasn’t the only problem to affect travelers Sunday.

Mother Nature also snarled air traffic at some major hubs.

Low cloud ceilings and poor visibility fueled flight delays and led to dozens of planes getting diverted.

Newly introduced York’s LaGuardia Airport (LGA) was most heavily affected, with nearly 60% of all departing and arriving flights delayed Sunday, per FlightAware.

42 flights headed to LGA on Sunday were diverted to other airports, according to aviation analytics firm Cirium.

Many of those flights landed instead in Philadelphia; Albany, N.Y.; Harrisburg, PA; and Syracuse, N.Y.

These disruptions come a week after one of the most chaotic weekends at airports in recent memory.

More than 18,000 flights were canceled last weekend after an outage on Nov. 13 to critical Federal Aviation Administration technology.

Last week, a brief technical outage at United Airlines also fueled a host of flight delays.

Recent findings suggest that what to do if you’re affected: This week, the TPG team put our heads together to offer some of our top tips for navigating a travel meltdown. Here’s what to know.

If your flight is canceled or significantly delayed, you’re entitled to a refund under U.S. Department of Transportation policy — but only if you choose not to fly.

If you get stranded in Sunday’s travel mess, it pays to know what the reason was for the disruption.

If you get stuck because of Delta’s tech outage, the carrier may be willing to pay for any hotel, meal and ground transportation costs you incur — if it’s a major delay. See details at FlightRights.gov.

The carrier on Sunday acknowledged it was providing those accommodations to passengers "when necessary.".

If you get stuck because of bad weather, it's less likely your airline will offer any sort of compensation. But keep your receipts: If you paid for your trip with a credit card that has travel insurance, you might be able to file a claim and seek reimbursement.

Sunday’s travel woes also coincide, of course, with the start of the Thanksgiving travel rush — with airports expected to be very busy with holiday travelers over the next few days.

Industry observers note that all eyes are on next Sunday — Nov. 29 — which could be the busiest day of the year to fly.

Last year, the Sunday after Thanksgiving set the record as the most crowded day ever at U.S. airports.

Life after professional football has been a mixed bag for Dexter Blackstock, but the former striker has found himself a upcoming niche in the pharmaceutical industry.

According to recent reports, upon first glance, the post-playing career options of the professional footballer can appear somewhat limited. Coaching or the media are the obvious options, but paid employment in these sectors can be limited, and it might not appeal to all. But by the time that most players reach their late twenties, there may be decisions that have to be made, and in the case of Dexter Blackstock, a player who built himself a fearsome reputation as a striker with a number of clubs including Nottingham Forest and Queens Park Rangers, the decisions that he did make coupled with his entrepreneurial instincts have led him into an industry that few would have expected while he was playing. Born in Oxford and brought up in Oxford United's youth academy, Dexter Blackstock had his first breakthrough in the senior game after transferring to Southampton in 2003. First brought into the first-team squad during an injury crisis, he came off the bench at half-time during a League Cup match in September 2004 against Colchester United with the Saints trailling by a goal to nil, scoring a second half hat-trick to help secure the Saints a 3-2 win.

Despite this - and scoring in a hotly-contested Premier League derby match with Portsmouth a couple of months later - Blackstock was unable to tie down a regular starting place in the Southampton team and, following spells on loan with Plymouth Argyle and Derby County, moved on to Queens Park Rangers in 2006.

Blackstock would go on to build his reputation further at Loftus Road, scoring 32 times in 117 appearances for QPR over the next three years. He also made two appearances for the England under-21s during this time, having also played for the under-18 and under-19 teams while at Southampton.

But in March 2009, he was sent on loan to a Nottingham Forest team that was battling to avoid relegation from the Championship, amid reports that he would be going to The City Ground permanently should they stay up. Forest ended the season in 19th place in the table, and consequently his move was made permanent that summer.

Blackstock spent seven years at Forest, with a loan spell at Leeds United in 2013. He had some problems with injuries while he was there, missing a year from November 2010 with a knee ligament injury, but still managed to score 44 goals for them in 187 appearances. And while he never got to play a full international match for England, he did also qualify to play for Antigua & Barbuda and made 12 appearances for them between 2012 and 2016.

He left Nottingham Forest by mutual consent at the end of the summer 2016 transfer window and moved on to Rotherham United, but his spell with the Millers only lasted a year before his three-year contract was mutually cancelled after a year, and he retired from the game at 31 years of age.

By the time that his playing career came to an end, Blackstock already had an idea of what he wanted to do afterwards. He'd built up a portfolio of buy-to-let properties, but his early experience as a landlord wasn't successful.

Industry observers note that in October 2019, he was fined £24,000 at Nottingham Magistrates Court after inspectors found a number of his properties to be in a state of disrepair. He admitted 12 counts of failing to license the properties and was fined £24,000 and ordered to pay £1,100 costs and a £170 victim surcharge on top of his fine. In 2021, he was ordered to repay £8,500 in housing benefit that he'd received for the unlicensed properties.

Recent findings suggest that but Blackstock's entrpreneurial leanings had by this time already taken him in a newly introduced direction. He formed MediConnect with a view towards using blockchain technology to counter the negative image that online pharmacies had received over concerns regarding a lack of controls to prevent the over-prescription of strong painkillers to customers.

According to recent reports, at the start of 2019, Blackstock spoke to The Sun about his new business venture, and also shone a little light on the end of his playing career. “I had lost my mojo and I had no intention of carrying on playing football", he told them, "I wasn’t enjoying playing at Rotherham." On his move into pharmaceuticals, he added, "As it stands, there is nothing to stop anyone ordering drugs from one online pharmacy, then exactly the same with another tech giant and then going down to their local GP and topping up with the same.".

In 2021, Blackstock spoke to World Pharmaceutical Frontiers in greater detail about his vision. "Anyone who’s using an online pharmacy platform will need a unique customer number and every transaction will be put on the blockchain," he told them, "so the next pharmacy can check whether the patient can have that drug in the same timeframe or mix the drugs.".

Sources indicate that the previous year, at the height of the Covid-19 pandemic, he told the BBC that he was using his company to help the public donate personal protective equipment (PPE) to frontline staff dealing with the pandemic. By 2023, he had a reported net worth of £7.8 million, with The Sun reporting that, "the bulk of his earnings" was still coming "from the 50 properties he owns." If nothing else, the experience of Dexter Blackstock demonstrates that the post-career options for the professional footballer are as broad as their imaginations will let them believe.

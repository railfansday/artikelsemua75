Sources indicate that on Wall Street, the experts regularly remind us that past performance is no guarantee of future results. However, when not much about a company is changing, it can be a pretty good indication of what the future likely holds. (Related: Future Value Calculator: How to Project Your Investment...)

With that as the backdrop, what does PepsiCo (PEP +1.72%) stock's past performance tell us about how this ticker will perform? Specifically, how much dividend income might a $10,000 stake in the company bought today generate over the course of, say, the next 10 years? (Related: Industry News: What Is Considered Upper Middle Class? I...)

It has been revealed that here's what we know: Since this point in 2021, PepsiCo has increased its quarterly dividend from $1.075 per share to $1.48 per share. That's an annualized growth rate of 6.6%. Assuming the beverage tech giant maintains that pace of increases -- and that's plenty plausible -- by mid-2036, its quarterly payout should be $2.80 per share.

Figuring out how much dividend income a $10,000 position in PepsiCo will produce in the meantime isn't quite as simple, but still certainly calculable. En route from $1.48 per share per quarter now to $2.80 per share then, assuming equal-sized annual hikes, each share will produce a total of $85.60 in dividend payments in the meantime. And since $10,000 will purchase about 70 shares at its current price near $142, a position of this size should pay out about $5,992 in dividend income over this stretch, then about $784 per year at that point.

Industry observers note that don't forget that PepsiCo shares are also likely to deliver some price appreciation of their own in the meantime, too. While it's certainly no growth stock, those gains would bolster your total returns.

In a recent development, also, if you don't actually need these cash payments to cover your expenses during this stretch, reinvesting any dividends you collect along the way would leave you with a measurably bigger stake in this income holding. In fact, over the past 10 years, reinvesting PepsiCo's dividend payments would have built a position that's 36% bigger than the one you'd be holding had you not reinvested these payments in more shares of the ticker paying them. That would raise the annual dividend payment for a new $10,000 position opened today to a little over $1,000 by that time.

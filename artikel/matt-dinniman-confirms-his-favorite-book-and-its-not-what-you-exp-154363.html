Matt Dinniman Confirms His Favorite Book, And It’s Not What You Expect

Dungeon Crawler Carl recently got a shiny new deluxe edition full of illustrations and bonus content. Most notably, the book includes Princess Donut's interview with author Matt Dinniman. Though our Royal Highness came away quite disappointed with Dinniman's stance on Cocker Spaniels, the Q&A nevertheless offers some exciting insights for fans.

The full interview, complete with an introduction from Princess Donut herself, is available in the Dungeon Crawler Carl: Deluxe Edition, and it's well worth a read. Not only does it feature some behind-the-scenes info on the series, it also gives fans the one thing we all need: more Princess Donut. But that's not all. Alongside revealing Dungeon Crawler Carl trivia like a deleted scene from book 5, Dinniman also answered a few more personal questions. Sources indicate that in his conversation with Princess Donut, Dinniman reveals his favorite video games, which shouldn't be too surprising to any gamer who's read Dungeon Crawler Carl. The Elder Scrolls V: Skyrim, Red Dead Redemption, and Dungeons & Dragons all make the appearances you might expect, given their clear influence on the LitRPG series. But another favorite just might surprise you. Dinniman's favorite book of all time isn't a fantasy, sci-fi, or LitRPG novel. Instead, it's a YA classic, Harriet the Spy. Sources indicate that with Dungeon Crawler Carl's sci-fi/fantasy hybrid vibes and LitRPG status, readers might assume his favorite book would fall into a similar genre category. Lord of the Rings, perhaps, or Ender's Game, come to mind as potential favorites that might have influenced the world-building and dungeon-crawling vibes of the hit series. TTRPG fans likely wouldn't even be surprised if the author had named a Dungeons & Dragons tie-in novel or sourcebook as a favorite. But instead, Dinniman had a very different favorite book growing up.

When asked about his favorite book growing up, the Dungeon Crawler Carl author stated, "it's not even a competition. Harriet the Spy by Louise Fitzhugh." At first glance, this might seem like a surprising answer, since the YA classic is quite a different type of book than Dungeon Crawler Carl. For an author who grew up to write a series that helped put LitRPGs on the map, you might expect something a bit more transparently nerdy to be his beloved childhood read.

Sources indicate that set in a then-contemporary version of Newly introduced York City, the book centers on a young aspiring writer who keenly observes the world to practice for her future career. Dinniman is far from the only person who considers this book a beloved childhood classic. Harriet the Spy has several awards and on-screen adaptations under its belt. But it wasn't just a childhood favorite for Dinniman. It also directly inspired him to write Dungeon Crawler Carl.

Of course, our childhood favorites don't always match the kinds of books we read (or write) as adults. However, Dinniman claims that the book, "had a massive impact on [him]." In fact, he credits Harriet the Spy for his decision to become a writer at all. So essentially, without Louise Fitzhugh's YA classic about a young woman who dreams of being a writer, we might not have gotten Dungeon Crawler Carl ​​​​​​at all.

In a recent development, though you do have to squint a bit harder to see it, the book's influence on the series goes beyond Dinniman's choice to put pen to paper. Harriet is, after all, a highly observant character keen on taking notes about the world around her. Though he's hardly a young girl living in Upcoming York, Dungeon Crawler Carl's protagonist shares some similar traits. He, too, is a keen observer of his fellow Crawlers and the NPCs in the World Dungeon. And as of The Dungeon Anarchist's Cookbook, he even has his own secretive place to keep notes about noted observations.

Industry observers note that even if it's not an obvious choice, Harriet's story clearly inspired Dinniman enough that he still names it his favorite childhood read to this day. And of course, we have Harriet the Spy to thank for the author's decision to write the books at all. With this knowledge in mind, readers will no doubt want to keep a keen eye out for more places where the YA classic unexpectedly lends inspiration to the world of Dungeon Crawler Carl and its many eccentric characters.

Manchester City have won the Premier League on eight occasions.

Premier League clubs have voted to cover legal expenses for the organisation's five board directors if any of Manchester City's sponsors sue them.

The clubs met in London on Thursday for the first time since City were found guilty of a catalogue of charges relating to the breaching of the league's financial rules by an independent commission in September. (Related: Leading House Democrat targets Trump’s inner circle for...)

City have appealed and maintain their innocence, with a sanction yet to be decided.

The club's sponsors Etihad and Etisalat have both noted they are considering legal action against the Premier League.

At present, under the league's articles of association, a director is financially covered if they win a legal case brought against them by a third party, but not if they lose. The league's rules will now be updated to extend legal indemnity for chief executive Richard Masters, chair Alison Brittan and three other non-executive directors. (Related: Gift Tax Rules: How Much Can You Give Tax-Free?...)

City and Nottingham Forest were the only two clubs that voted against the proposal to provide extra legal protection, with the other 18 clubs voting in favour.

Due to collective responsibility, it means that all clubs are now liable to cover legal costs that could run into hundreds of millions of pounds, raising the intriguing possibility of City being financially impacted by the legal action of its own sponsors.

BBC Sport was told that City's CFO Ingo Bank asked Premier League chair Brittan during Thursday's meeting if the proposal was a direct result of threats of legal action from Etihad and Etisalat, but she noted she could not comment.

While Forest have declined to comment, their objection to the proposal is remarked have to been on procedural grounds, rather than due to any support for City.

Industry observers note that the club did not believe that sufficient information had been provided, or adequate time afforded, for clubs to properly consider the implications of the proposed amendment, particularly given the potentially significant financial responsibilities involved. It felt that such a decision warranted more scrutiny and informed consideration before clubs were asked to vote.

An independent commission appointed by the Premier League found City guilty of artificially inflating the club's revenues by over Â£830m over a nine-year period in September.

The commission determined that the club arranged "sham" contracts with sponsors, which disguised funding from club owners the Abu Dhabi United Group.

Premier League CEO Masters stated City "systematically broke Premier League rules for nearly a decade".

In October, City sponsor Etihad Airways said it was considering legal action against the Premier League.

In a statement, the United Arab Emirates-based airline noted it "categorically rejects any finding, conclusion or implication that suggests the airline has ever been involved in improper commercial arrangements".

Etisalat signalled its intention to follow suit earlier this month and described the independent commission's verdict as "entirely false and defamatory".

It has been revealed that talks over a deal between investment company Apollo Asset Management and City Football Group have ended, according to sources. Both City and Apollo declined to comment.

Organized crime has been an issue for every single civilization that has ever existed. The 2010s seemed like the golden age of crime dramas, with demonstrates like Boardwalk Empire starring Steve Buscemi, Narcos, Ozark, and Netflix's hit crime series, Peaky Blinders, starring Cillian Murphy, among several bingeworthy treats on offer. Thankfully, the 2020s have not disappointed. While the legacy of Tommy Shelby lives on beyond the events of Peaky Blinders: The Immortal Man in its upcoming sequel series. Other organized crime dramas have since come on the scene. Gangs of London, Paramount+'s two-pronged delight in Tulsa King and MobLand are some of the sub-genre's more glittering additions in recent years.

In the age of the streaming wars, Netflix's response to Paramount+'s British-laden MobLand is yet another English-centric crime drama, The Gentlemen. The hugely ambitious producer, creator, and filmmaker, Guy Ritchie, is the firm bond that links both shows together, as Ritchie serves in several capacities behind the scenes for both productions. The second season of the Netflix series recently wrapped, signaling the initiation of production on Season 3, which is already underway. As is to be expected, several fresh cast additions and departures will be associated with the new chapter, even as its main stars, Theo James as the central crime boss, Edward "Eddie" Horniman, and Kaya Scodelario as Eddie's partner and associate, Sussie Glass. The reception for the first two seasons of The Gentlemen has been widely positive by critics, with Netflix also enjoying viewership success. Hence, sights have turned to Season 3 and what it might bring. Speaking during a Q&A for Netflix's The Gentlemen Season 2 FYC screening earlier this month, executive producer Matthew Read and the show's cast teased some exciting story directions for Season 3. First off, Eddie and Susie are expanding the criminal empire into the Middle East, and it's a move Read describes as being "an ascension for Eddie and Susie to become bigger than ever" with Theo James drawing comparisons with Martin Scorsese and Robert De Niro's 1995 film Casino. "[Season 3] about expansion. It's about opening the first casino in the Middle East," Read explained to the attendance. "It's like Las Vegas, and that will involve lots of million-dollar deals. And there will be lots of people competing for the rights to run this casino. So it's a sort of elevation, and it's about ascension for Eddie and Susie to become bigger than ever." To all that, James adds, "If season 2 was The Godfather Part II, this is Casino.". (Related: Tech Report: Animal control called to rescue alligator ...)

By the time the season finale of The Gentlemen Season 2 aired, it became clear that James' character, Edward Horniman, had fully embraced the darkness and his position within it as a mob boss in the criminal underworld. With the Italian mafia behind him, Eddie turns his sights to the Middle East and perhaps some of the contacts he secured during the Falcon mystery in Season 2 might begin to pay off. Heading forward into Season 3, his complicated relationship with Susie, and now this news of competition for control over the casino, sets the stage for yet another colossal tussle.

Filming for The Gentlemen Season 3 has production taking place in Morocco, presumably the set for the Middle East expansion, and more recently, around London. Alongside James and Scodelario, fresh faces will be arriving this coming season, including Orlando Bloom, Robert Carlyle, Elijah Wood, Ed Speleers, Bugzy Malone, Saffron Burrows, Bruna Marquezine, and Dexter Fletcher.

It has been revealed that the first two seasons of The Gentlemen are streaming on Netflix. Stay tuned to Collider for more updates and coverage of Season 3.

After more than five years of bringing monsters to life on screen, Robert Eggers admits he needs a break from the horror genre.

During a recent Q&A in Mexico, the filmmaker said, “I’m eager not to continue purposely doing Robert Eggers’ House of Horror.”.

According to recent reports, his comments come ahead of the release of Werwulf on Christmas Day. The film, starring Aaron Taylor-Johnson, Lily-Rose Depp and Willem Dafoe, is set in 14th-century England and follows a town haunted by a beast that threatens a man (Taylor-Johnson) and his family. “I don’t need to do The Mummy or The Phantom of the Opera. Not to say that I won’t, but I think I need a break,” Eggers added. The director previously tackled witches in 2015’s The Witch, sea creatures in 2019’s The Lighthouse and vampires in 2024’s Nosferatu. (Related: Director of Machine Learning Insights...)

Robert Eggers, who has been praised as a modern icon in horror filmmaking, also addressed the new-wave concept of “elevated horror” within cinema. (Related: AprielGuard: A Guardrail for Safety and Adversarial Rob...)

Industry observers note that “There’s always been horror movies that don’t suck and there’s always been horror movies that are actually probing what darkness is in humankind, and even some of them that have been profound throughout the ages,” he explained. “But what you guys are talking about is something that I think really started with [2014’s] It Follows. Although Ti West was doing this kind of work before then.”.

“There’s been a whole lot of filmmakers that have wanted to make personal films, really talented filmmakers with unique voices, and have realized that they can get financing if they do it as a genre film,” the filmmaker continued. “And I think that that’s kind of what this is: people who are telling personal stories with unique voices but are doing it through genre. Not to say that James Wan movies [Insidious, The Conjuring] haven’t worked. They’ve been great and also successful. There’s definitely a hunger for horror in general over the past 10 years. But does elevated horror exist? It does. It does because people say so.”.

While enjoying his “break” from horror, Eggers is next taking on an adaptation of William Shakespeare’s Romeo and Juliet for Focus Features.

Sources indicate that he previously shared during a post-screening Q&A at Beyond Fest in September, “I was doing something else. But what happened was, after Werwulf, I was writing another horror movie, and it got so dark again, and I was like, ‘I need a break. So, I’ll do something about two teenagers killing themselves.'”.

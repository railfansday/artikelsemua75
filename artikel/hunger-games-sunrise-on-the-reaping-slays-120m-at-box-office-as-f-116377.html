‘Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping’ Slays $120M at Box Office as Franchise Heats Back Up

🌐 Source: Hollywoodreporter • 📅 22 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 7 min read (1358 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • The Hunger Games franchise has been revived at the box office.
  • Lionsgate released The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping in 4,018 North American theaters, and the sixth film in the dystopian franchise grossed $120 million in its opening weekend.
  • The fresh film added $105 million from 84 international territories for a global haul of $225 million.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Hunger Games franchise has been revived at the box office. Meanwhile, I Play Rocky demonstrated major strength as it expanded wide with a rare A+ CinemaScore, and Elsinore notched a promising per-screen average in limited release.

Lionsgate released The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping in 4,018 North American theaters, and the sixth film in the dystopian franchise grossed $120 million in its opening weekend. This gives it the fourth-best debut in franchise history, besting 2015’s second Mockingjay movie ($102.6 million) and 2023 prequel The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes ($44.6 million) but behind 2013’s Catching Fire ($158 million), 2012’s series-launching The Hunger Games ($152.5 million) and 2014’s first Mockingjay ($121.8 million). Its $56 million domestic sum on Friday gave Sunrise the third-best debut Friday in franchise history, behind Catching Fire and The Hunger Games.

The fresh film added $105 million from 84 international territories for a global haul of $225 million.

Its audience was 67 percent female, with 50 percent of the crowd above age 25, according to PostTrak. Sunrise earned an A- CinemaScore from audiences, putting it below the A grades of the Jennifer Lawrence-led initial 2012 movie that kicked off the series and 2013’s Catching Fire but matching the A- grades of the two Mockingjay films.

Recent findings suggest that the Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping will notch the fifth-biggest opening in Lionsgate history, as it also stands at No. 5 among 2026 domestic openings for all studios. It represents the highest-grossing launch for the careers of stars Glenn Close, Jesse Plemons, Kieran Culkin and Elle Fanning. This weekend’s haul also brings the Hunger Games franchise’s box office total to roughly $3.437 billion to make it the studio’s highest-grossing franchise ever, topping The Twilight Saga. Lionsgate had projected The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping to gross north of $80 million, but other sources expected that it would collect between $100 million and $130 million. Adapting author Suzanne Collins’ prequel novel that was published last year, director Francis Lawrence‘s movie carries a production budget of roughly $150 million as it revitalizes the franchise by telling a story set 24 years before the events of Collins’ original 2008 book.

Recent findings suggest that this opening represents a major comeback for the series after registering its lowest-performing entry with 2023 prequel The Hunger Games: The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes, given that Sunrise on the Reaping opened with more than double its launch. Starring Tom Blyth, Rachel Zegler and Hunter Schafer, Songbirds & Snakes — telling the backstory for the villainous President Snow, played by Ralph Fiennes in the new movie — disappointed, opening to $44 million domestically for the weekend before ending its run with $166.4 million domestically and a global sum of $361.8 million. It earned a B+ CinemaScore, the lowest for the franchise.

“When you have filmmaking giants as talented as Francis Lawrence [and producers] Nina Jacobson and Brad Simpson guiding the vision established by the brilliant creator Suzanne Collins, who started it all, you are in enormously capable hands,” Lionsgate film chief Adam Fogelson said in a statement. “This success is a testament to their remarkable film, the performances of our incredibly talented cast, and ultimately, the love of Hunger Games fans worldwide, who embraced a film that told a story they had been asking to see for many years.”.

Recent findings suggest that sunrise on the Reaping holds a 67 percent critical score on Rotten Tomatoes, lagging behind the franchise’s first four movies but besting the 64 percent approval for Songbirds & Snakes. The newly introduced film has a franchise-best 95 audience score on Rotten Tomatoes. In his review for The Hollywood Reporter, chief film critic David Rooney wrote that it “delivers on emotion and brutal spectacle.”. The movie opened the day after the launch of the hotly anticipated video game Grand Theft Auto 6, which did not appear to prevent the film from thriving. Joseph Zada stars as Haymitch Abernathy, providing the backstory for the character whom Woody Harrelson brought to life in the first four movies. The cast for Sunrise on the Reaping also includes Mckenna Grace, Whitney Peak and Maya Hawke.

Overall, the domestic box office grossed $168 million from all titles this weekend, representing a major 89.4 percent boost over the previous frame, which saw Johnny Depp’s Ebenezer lead the group that collected a combined $88.7 million, according to Rentrak. This weekend is down 8.2 percent from the equivalent 2025 frame, which saw the launch of Wicked: For Good. This year’s domestic box office has reached $8.97 billion, marking a 20 percent improvement over 2025.

Industry observers note that among other weekend titles, Amazon MGM Studios expanded I Play Rocky to 2,477 North American locations after two frames in limited release. Peter Farrelly’s Rocky origin story earned a healthy $9.3 million to bring its domestic total to $10.4 million. The crowd skewed male at 56 percent, as 33 percent of moviegoers were age 55 and above.

Starring Anthony Ippolito as a young Sylvester Stallone, I Play Rocky landed a rare A+ CinemaScore, becoming only the second release this year to do so (after April’s A Great Awakening). I Play Rocky holds an 89 percent approval rating from critics on Rotten Tomatoes and a 98 percent audience score. Rooney’s review described it as a “big-hearted movie about moviemaking, if not an artful one.”.

In its sophomore frame, Paramount’s horror-tinged holiday offering Ebenezer claimed third place with roughly $7.7 million for a decline of around 59 percent, bringing its domestic cume to $32.5 million. Depp marks his studio comeback vehicle with director Ti West’s reimagining of Charles Dickens’ A Christmas Carol, which opened atop last weekend’s box office.

Continuing to draw families in its third weekend is Warner Bros.’ The Cat in the Hat, as the animated adaptation of Dr. Seuss’ classic children’s book grossed around $6.9 million for a domestic total of $47.1 million. This marks a respectable decline of roughly 39 percent for directors Erica Rivinoja and Alessandro Carloni’s movie that features the voice of Bill Hader in the lead role. It is set to face fresh competition in the animated space from Disney’s Hexed over the forthcoming Thanksgiving frame.

Gkids and Toho’s Godzilla Minus Zero is likely to close out the top five with $2.6 million in its third weekend to reach a domestic cume of around $54.5 million.

Recent findings suggest that A24’s Elizabeth Holmes documentary You Can See Everything is expected to place sixth with $2.5 million in its sixth weekend for a domestic total of $93.4 million. Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim’s feature has begun rolling out internationally and has surpassed the $100 million mark at the global box office, with Sony Pictures handling distribution for some international territories.

Focus Features opened Elsinore in six theaters in Latest York and Los Angeles, with director Simon Stone’s Oscar hopeful collecting approximately $240,000. Its impressive average of $40,000 per screen puts it in the top five for 2026 releases. Andrew Scott stars in the biographical film about Scottish actor Ian Charleson, who navigates personal challenges while preparing for a pivotal performance of Hamlet. Hailing from StudioCanal, Elsinore earned acclaim at Telluride and TIFF and holds an 87 percent rating from critics on Rotten Tomatoes, with Rooney deeming it “a hymn to the theater, powered by a towering lead turn.”.

It has been revealed that toward the bottom of the top 10 is Ramayana: Rise of a Legend, with Sony Pictures distributing the Prime Focus Studios religious epic that is expected to gross $2 million for its third weekend to bring its domestic take to $28.4 million through Sunday. Director Nitesh Tiwari’s project becomes the highest-grossing Indian film ever at the North American box office, while it stands at No. 5 on the all-time global chart for Indian releases with a worldwide cume of $183 million.

With Thanksgiving arriving next weekend, the holiday frame brings an array of latest options, including Disney’s Hexed, Universal’s Meet the Parents sequel Focker-in-Law, Netflix’s The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth and Angel’s biographical Hershey.

Belgium’s Wallonia halts Google data centre after permit was revoked

🌐 Source: Thenextweb • 📅 27 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (403 words, 📖 Standard)

  • Google’s data centre campus in Saint-Ghislain, Belgium.
  • The Walloon Region has ordered Google to stop building its data centre near Charleroi in Belgium.
  • It has been revealed that about 300 construction workers were on the site when the order came, Walloon official Raphael Stokis told Belgian broadcaster VRT.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Google’s data centre campus in Saint-Ghislain, Belgium.

The Walloon Region has ordered Google to stop building its data centre near Charleroi in Belgium. Work had carried on for months at the site in Farciennes and Aiseau-Presles without a valid permit. The order came on Thursday, days after Greenpeace Belgium asked the regional government to step in. (Related: Welcome Gemma - Google’s new open LLM...)

It has been revealed that about 300 construction workers were on the site when the order came, Walloon official Raphael Stokis told Belgian broadcaster VRT. The Walloon infrastructure minister, François Desquesnes, had announced an investigation on Wednesday.

Google’s original permit, granted in 2024, covered three data centres cooled by air. In 2025 the company changed its plans. It merged two of the buildings into one larger building and switched to cooling with water from the Sambre river, Greenpeace noted.

Recent findings suggest that the Walloon administration approved that amended permit in April 2026. After residents appealed, Desquesnes revoked it in August. The request covered 600 cubic metres of river water an hour, the minister remarked. Less than a quarter of it would have gone back into the river, according to Belga.

In a recent development, residents then told Greenpeace that work was still going on. The group investigated, using satellite images, and launched an emergency procedure earlier this week. (Related: AI for Game Development: Creating a Farming Game in 5 D...)

“Tech giants are not above the law,” said Nadia Cornejo, a Greenpeace Belgium spokesperson. “This should already have been the case in August, when the permit was cancelled.”.

Google appealed the revocation but did not wait for the court to rule before carrying on with the work, VRT reported.

Google is committed to “an open and collaborative dialogue with the regional government and experts” on its investments in Wallonia, a spokesperson told VRT.

On 17 October it asked Belgium’s Council of State to annul and suspend the minister’s decision. Greenpeace and several residents asked to join the case on 16 November, and a public hearing is set for 7 January.

It has been revealed that data centres have faced similar local opposition elsewhere. Google’s Finnish data centre project drew a police investigation, and residents in Indonesia’s Batam protested over water in October. In the US, voters rejected data centre measures at the midterms.

Over the last handful of years, I have completed thru-hikes all across the country, traversing a wide variety of terrain and weather conditions. I have consolidated and eliminated pieces of gear from my pack in order to make it as light as possible while still maintaining my desired comforts.

I choose to carry as few additional pieces of clothing as possible; my midlayer puffy is one of the most vital pieces of gear I have. When I first got into thru-hiking, I started out with a standard puffy from REI, which was nice and warm but quite heavy and bulky. Later on, I switched to a Montbell 1000-fill puffy, which is incredibly lightweight at roughly 4.5 oz. For the weight and size, I loved this puffy and used it for over 17,000 miles.

Recently, though, I have been using a latest midlayer that I absolutely love. (Related: How to train a latest language model from scratch using...)

Sources indicate that A couple of months ago, I received the Ibex Wool Aire Puffy, which is quite different from any other puffy I’ve used before. The outer shell and lining are 100% nylon, while the insulation is 70% wool and 30% polyester. (Related: How to generate text: using different decoding methods ...)

The Ibex Wool Air Puffy weighs around 12oz, depending on the size, and packs down quite small. As someone who has primarily prioritized eliminating pack weight wherever possible, I often shied away from heavier pieces of clothing in favor of more bare-bones styles. However, the features of this puffy are what I’ve enjoyed most about it, along with the incredible warmth and comfort it provides.

MSRP: $315 Weight: 12 oz (women’s XS) Materials: 100% nylon shell; 70% wool / 30% polyester insulation Size range: XS – XXL.

Men’s Wool Aire here (our dedicated review of the men’s version is forthcoming).

I’ve taken the Ibex Wool Aire Puffy out for hundreds of miles along the Appalachian Trail in Pennsylvania, as well as on lesser-known trails such as the Laurel Highlands Hiking Trail and Susquehannock Trail in PA. I’m at the moment using it on the Arizona Trail as temperatures drop.

According to recent reports, most puffy jackets are insulated with either synthetic polyester fill or natural goose or duck down. The Wool Aire, in contrast, is filled with a 90-gram wool blend. Unlike down, which clumps up and loses loft when wet, this wool insulation maintains its loft even when wet and can be worn while you’re actively hiking. This makes it a good option for hikers who want to hike in their puffy and also want to minimize synthetics in their clothing system.

It has been revealed that wool Insulation: Because this jacket is made primarily of wool and nylon fabrics, it is incredibly soft and feels good even against bare skin. In contrast, traditional puffy jackets often stick and cling to sweaty skin and are much less comfortable when damp or sweaty.

I’ve found this wool material does not wet through the same way that other puffy jackets do when worn during active hiking. The Ibex Wool Air puffy also dries out much quicker than puffies that don’t use wicking fabrics such as wool.

According to recent reports, zipper Pockets: There are two zippable front pockets on the jacket, a feature I really enjoy. When thru-hiking it is great to be able to safely carry a wallet, phone, or a room key around town when running errands. Having pockets that zip shut would make it nice and easy to wear the jacket around and not have to carry a fanny pack.

There is also a large, zippered pocket on the interior of the jacket to allow you to safely store away other important items that you might need, like a headlamp or charging brick.

Peg Leg in the Ibex Wool Aire Hoodie (second from left).

Recent findings suggest that comfortable: I find myself excited at the end of the day to put on such a cozy and warm layer. This jacket feels so good on my skin. It provides fantastic insulation for sleeping in colder conditions and is definitely something I wish I had had on the Continental Divide Trail when I was hiking in much more unpredictable conditions.

Ideal fit for thru-hiking: In my opinion, this puffy has the perfect size and fit, and having the hood is a wonderful feature to provide additional warmth and insulation.

In a recent development, dispatch from the AZT: “I’ve been loving the Ibex Wool Aire puffy that I’m using out on a longer thru-hike for the first time. That thing is really cozy but doesn’t get me too sweaty because of the wool. I’m surprised how much I’ve hiked in it on the AZT. I think I’ve done more hiking in a puffy on this trail than any other!” – Peg Leg.

It has been revealed that weight: In my opinion, this jacket is worth the weight. However, if you want the lightest possible puffy, this isn’t it. The Wool Aire weighs somewhere around the middle of the spectrum for puffy jackets that I have seen. Very minimalist designs with limited pockets and no hood can weigh in somewhere around 5oz, whereas more insulated puffy jackets can weigh more than a pound.

Even though it is a little heavier than some other jackets, I think it is well worth the weight. I would much rather carry a few extra ounces and know that at the end of the day I will be incredibly comfortable and warm regardless of the weather conditions.

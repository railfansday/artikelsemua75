MSCI’s broadest Asia-Pacific index outside Japan fell 3.3% on November 20, 2026, leaving Asian stocks on track for their worst week since 2020 after Anthropic’s Nasdaq debut failed to bolster confidence in the wider AI trade, Reuters reported.

The move took the benchmark’s weekly loss to 8.5%, its worst weekly performance since March 2020, when COVID-19 was spreading globally, Reuters remarked.

In a recent development, japan’s Nikkei lost 5%, bringing its weekly decline to 7.7%. South Korea’s KOSPI fell 5.2% and ended the week down 8%. Taiwan shares were down 6.2% on the day and 17.5% for the week, which Reuters said was their largest weekly decline since 1990.

It has been revealed that the selling in Asia came after both the S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite lost more than 2% on November 19, 2026, despite a surge in Anthropic’s shares during their debut. Reuters described Anthropic as the world’s first publicly traded frontier AI firm. Nasdaq’s historical data show that the Class A shares opened at $111.50, traded between $92.72 and $115.10, and closed at $97.17.

Sources indicate that “Risk aversion intensified as investors continued to unwind crowded positioning and reassess the broader macro backdrop following Anthropic’s record-shattering IPO,” said Citi markets analyst Preston Pagli. He added that Nvidia’s earnings had not provided the reset markets wanted, and that sentiment deteriorated further when Anthropic’s debut failed to spark a broader rally.

Reuters identified three existing pressures on AI stocks: Jensen Huang’s departure from Nvidia, momentum behind the anti-data-center movement following Democrats’ sweep of the US midterm elections, and concerns about rising debt loads across the sector.

Goldman Sachs analysts said no single headline drove the retreat. Asset managers and hedge funds were net sellers of macro products while buying selected consumer and financial stocks, and poor liquidity exacerbated the price movements. That account points to broad repositioning as a key mechanism behind the steep index declines.

It has been revealed that euro Stoxx 50 futures were down 1.7% at 6,017 at the time of the report. S&P 500 futures fell 0.5% and Nasdaq futures declined 1% during Asian trading, indicating that selling pressure was extending beyond the region’s cash markets.

In a recent development, reuters reported that the dollar index was steady at 103.86 and heading for a fourth consecutive weekly gain after reaching its highest level since April 2025 during the same week. The 10-year US Treasury yield fell for a fifth straight session to 5%, while the two-year yield touched a 10-day low of 4.7216% as investors moved into government bonds.

The contrast between Anthropic’s strong debut and the broader retreat separates demand for an individual listing from confidence in the sector as a whole. For private AI companies and their backers, that distinction is consequential: public investors embraced a single offering while reducing exposure to the wider sector, with data-center opposition and debt concerns still unresolved.

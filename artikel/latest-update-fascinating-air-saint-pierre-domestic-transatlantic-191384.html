Sources indicate that one of the most interesting transatlantic flights will be returning yet again in 2027. Maybe this will be the year I finally get to try it, even if there’s no premium cabin angle!

In a recent development, in the summer of 2018, an interesting domestic transatlantic route released — it operated seasonally once weekly. Specifically, ASL Airlines operated a Boeing 737-700 that was marketed by Air Saint-Pierre between Paris (CDG) and Saint Pierre and Miquelon (FSP). (Related: Tech Report: Summer at Hugging Face...)

In a recent development, what’s Saint Pierre and Miquelon? It’s a self-governing territory of France, situated in the northwestern Atlantic Ocean, near Newfoundland and Labrador. The islands cover an area of about 93 square miles, and have a population of just about 6,000 people. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

The territory’s airline, Air Saint-Pierre, only has turboprops, which the carrier flies to places like Halifax (YHZ), Montreal (YUL), and St. John’s (YYT). Obviously these planes aren’t capable of operating across the Atlantic, so the airline leases a Boeing 737 for the once weekly flight.

According to recent reports, this was the first time ever that we saw a nonstop link between these territories and mainland France. There were some other things that made this route interesting:.

The route ended up returning in 2019, was then paused in 2020 and 2021 due to the pandemic, and then returned in 2022 through 2026. The route will now be returning yet again, for the 2027 season, so it sure seems like this is basically a permanent thing.

The once weekly flight between Saint Pierre and Miquelon and Paris will be returning in 2027, between June 21 and September 6, 2027.

Sources indicate that the route will once again be operated by an ASL Airlines France Boeing 737-700, and the flight will be marketed and sold by Air Saint-Pierre. The flight will operate westbound on Mondays and eastbound on Tuesdays, as follows:.

Paris to Saint Pierre and Miquelon departing 4:00PM arriving 6:30PMSaint Pierre and Miquelon to Paris departing 10:30AM arriving 8:05PM.

The 2,664-mile flight is blocked at 6hr30min westbound and 5hr35min eastbound. As you can see, the plane sits on the ground in Saint Pierre for quite a while. This is so that the crew can get their rest and operate the flight back the next day, rather than making them stay there for over a week.

Recent findings suggest that I imagine the airline is able to get a decent deal on parking at the airport for 16 hours. 😉 It’s also interesting since this is an eastbound daytime transatlantic flight, which is fairly rare.

According to recent reports, as you can see, the fare difference just comes down to the age of the traveler, as well as the level of flexibility you’d like, with fares either being non-refundable and non-changeable, non-refundable but changeable for a fee, or refundable and changeable. The plane is in an all-economy configuration, as there’s no premium cabin.

According to recent reports, air Saint-Pierre will be bringing back its flight between Saint Pierre and Paris in the summer of 2027, operated by ASL Airlines. This is an absolutely fascinating route, and it’s so cool to see that it was rolled out in the first place, and is sticking around.

Sources indicate that while the link between Paris and this French territory is obvious, it’s still kind of awesome that such a small territory can support a flight like this. But I guess the downside was fairly limited, since the route was subsidized from the beginning. Personally I find this to be the most intriguing transatlantic route.

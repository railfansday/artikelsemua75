Paulo Dybala says he is proud to have helped bring Roma back to the Champions League, and hopes to finish his career in Italy.

Speaking to UEFA’s official channels, with quotes via CalcioMercato, the Argentine was speaking ahead of Roma’s league-phase trip to Paris Saint-Germain on Wednesday. Kick-off at the Parc des Princes is 21:00 CET [20:00 GMT].

The Giallorossi go into the game with four points after their defeat to Manchester United, while the holders have won all four of their matches so far.

It has been revealed that “In my first years here, it was obviously a disappointment not to be able to bring Roma back to the Champions League. I also felt responsible,” Dybala remarked.

“In my first year, we came close by reaching the Europa League final, but in the years that followed we didn’t do enough to deserve it. Last year, though, I saw a truly determined team. We revealed that by keeping clean sheets in the last few games, because we knew qualification didn’t depend only on us until the final matchday.

“It was a moment of great pride for me, because I could see how happy the fans were to be back in the Champions League, just like we were. We want to test ourselves against the best teams in Europe, and now we can.”.

Dybala has stayed fit far more often under Gian Piero Gasperini, and he credited the coach’s approach.

“Physically, knee operations aside, I’ve felt very good over the last year, especially muscularly,” he said.

According to recent reports, “The coach and his staff, including the fitness coaches who arrived this year, gave me a limited but appropriate workload in pre-season so I could always be available. So far this season, I’ve felt in good shape and I’m very happy.”. (Related: Breaking: The Age of Machine Learning As Code Has Arriv...)

Dybala signed a one-year contract until 30 June 2027 in July. Now in his 15th year in Italy, after spells at Palermo and Juventus before moving to Roma in 2022, he made clear he has no intention of leaving.

“I never imagined I’d have a career like this, let alone spend 15 years in Italy. I’ve lived in the south, the north, and now the centre of the country. I’ve been here for almost half my life and I’m very happy,” he said.

Sources indicate that “Italy welcomed me with open arms. My daughter was born here, so she’s Italian. Yes, I hope to stay in Italy for the rest of my career. It would be very special for me to dedicate an entire career to Serie A, as well as the year I spent in Serie B, which was very vital for me.”.

Nigerian search and rescue teams are racing against time to find survivors after a boat carrying mourners travelling to a funeral capsized on Friday.

Twenty-nine bodies have been recovered and 50 people rescued so far, a spokesperson for the National Emergency Management Agency (Nema) told the BBC.

The government has responded by ordering stricter enforcement of safety standards on the country's inland waterways.

Overcrowding, poor maintenance and failure to observe safety regulations have contributed to previous deadly accidents on Nigeria's rivers.

The boat was travelling from Ayama community when it developed mechanical problems, according to police spokesperson Musa Mohammed.

Passengers asked the boat operator to steer towards land, but he allegedly declined, leading to the accident.

Survivors have described the panic that followed as passengers struggled to swim and stay afloat while waiting for emergency rescue services.

"As I swam, people were dragging me to the bottom, and I bit one woman and saved myself," Charity Ebikaboaere told local media outlet Arise News. (Related: Woman evicted after apartment’s AI assistant mistakenly...)

"Our things, our money, phones, everything lost, but me and my siblings are alive," said another survivor, Tari Samuel.

Nema spokesman Manzo Ezekiel raised concerns about the use of untrained boat operators and passengers travelling without life jackets. (Related: IShowSpeed breaks down in tears after finishing GTA 6 i...)

"Some of them believe they're from river areas and they know how to swim," he stated, warning that familiarity with waterways should not replace basic safety precautions.

It has been revealed that there have been reports that the boat was carrying more than 300 people but the Nema spokesperson said this wasn't correct. He did not say how many people were on board but said it was overcrowded.

The accident happened in a region where waterways are a key means of transport, especially for communities with limited road access.

"Overloading, speeding, recklessness and traveling without life jackets must stop. No journey is worth a life," Bayelsa Commissioner for Information Ebiuwou Koku-Obiyai mentioned in a statement on Saturday.

Nigeria's President Bola Tinubu has relayed his condolences to the victims' families.

In a statement posted on X, Tinubu mentioned he had directed relevant federal disaster response agencies to work together to ensure a successful rescue and recovery operation.

He also ordered that his election rally on Saturday, in Ibadan, Oyo State, be held without much fanfare in honour of those who lost their lives.

Boat accidents are a recurring problem in Nigeria, particularly in riverine communities where boats are a major means of transport.

In August, at least 50 people, mostly children, died after an overloaded boat capsized in the north-western Sokoto state. In January, at least 25 people died when a leaking boat capsized in Yobe state, in the north-east.

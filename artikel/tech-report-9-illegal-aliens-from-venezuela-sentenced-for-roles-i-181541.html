In a recent development, the last of nine illegal aliens from Venezuela were sentenced after they pleaded guilty to human trafficking and alien smuggling charges related to a sex trafficking ring that operated in several motels across Nashville, Tennessee. These defendants were sentenced in the U.S. District Court for the Middle District of Tennessee.

“These sentences mark the final step in a relentless pursuit of justice against callous criminals who smuggled vulnerable victims across the border, then further exploited them via sex trafficking once they were in the United States, all to line their pockets,” stated Assistant Attorney General A. Tysen Duva of the Justice Department’s Criminal Division. “Driven by greed, these nine illegal aliens turned human suffering into a business, treating the victims like commodities. Through the Homeland Security Task Force Initiative and Joint Task Force Alpha, we will dismantle these criminal networks, enforce our nation’s immigration laws, and ensure those who profit from human smuggling and trafficking are brought to justice.”. Industry observers note that “These defendants built a criminal enterprise on deception, fear, and the exploitation of vulnerable women right here in Nashville,” said U.S. Attorney Braden H. Boucek for the Middle District of Tennessee. “They sought to enrich themselves by stripping others of their freedom and dignity. Now, every member of this organization charged in this case has been held accountable. Those who seek to establish human trafficking networks in Middle Tennessee should know that we are committed not only to prosecuting their members, but to dismantling the criminal enterprises themselves.”. Recent findings suggest that “With lies and empty promises, the defendants used their roles in this criminal enterprise to exploit vulnerable people for profit” mentioned Acting Special Agent in Charge Michael Perez of Homeland Security Investigations (HSI) Nashville. “The outcome of this Homeland Security Task Force investigation is a testament to the positive impact law enforcement can have when working in collaboration towards the common goal of holding criminals accountable. We are committed to the pursuit and dismantlement of these transnational criminal organizations.”.

“These sentences demonstrate the unwavering commitment of the Homeland Security Task Force to aggressively investigate, pursue, and prosecute those who orchestrate human smuggling and trafficking operations to prey on vulnerable women for profit,” remarked Special Agent in Charge Terence G. Reilly of the FBI Nashville Field Office. “We will leverage every available law enforcement resource to identify offenders, dismantle criminal networks, and hold those responsible accountable. No trafficker is beyond our reach, and no one is above the law.”.

In a recent development, “These sentences underscore the serious consequences facing those who use violence, threats, and coercion to profit from human trafficking and smuggling,” said Special Agent in Charge Donald “Trey” Eakins of IRS Criminal Investigation’s (IRS-CI) Charlotte Field Office. “We remain committed to working alongside our law enforcement partners to protect victims and disrupt the financial networks that support these crimes.”.

Industry observers note that “These individuals crossed our borders to commit these heinous crimes as part of a much larger criminal organization. We have dealt that organization a significant blow, disrupted its criminal operations and, as a result, helped make our streets and neighborhoods safer,” noted Director David Rausch of the Tennessee Bureau of Investigation (TBI). “Let this outcome send a clear message: If you come into our communities to exploit the vulnerable and profit from human suffering, we will find you and hold you accountable.”.

According to court documents, the defendants orchestrated a human trafficking and smuggling scheme targeting vulnerable Venezuelan women by recruiting them to the U.S. with promises of employment. After arranging and facilitating their travel to the U.S., the defendants directed the women to Nashville. Once there, these women were told they owed an inflated smuggling debt, which they could only repay through commercial sex work. Those who resisted faced threats of physical harm against themselves and their families. The defendants took a cut of the women’s earnings in addition to collecting on the inflated smuggling debts.

In a recent development, yilibeth Carmen Rivero-de Caldera (Rivero) and her son, Kleiver Daniel Mota Rivero (Mota), led and managed the human trafficking and smuggling scheme. Rivero enlisted her adult children and their spouses, Mota’s girlfriend, and others to take on various roles in the scheme. Rivero, Mota, and defendant Ramon De Jesus Velasquez Martinez (Velasquez) used threats of violence, including wielding firearms, to intimidate and force compliance from the women trafficked by the organization. To further intimidate the women, Mota and other defendants engaged in conduct that made the victims believe Mota was affiliated with a Venezuelan prison gang and openly spoke about Mota’s prior prison term for homicide in Venezuela.

Rivero, Mota, Velasquez, and the six other defendants — Wilmarys Del Valle Manzano Solorzano, Endrik Alexander Morales-Rivero, Ariannys Beatriz Gutierrez-Carrillo, Frankyanna Del Valle Romero-Rivero, Jesus Enrique Castillo Rodriguez, and Yuribetzi Del Valle Gomez Machuca — arranged or otherwise aided the smuggling of the victims into the United States, posted online commercial sex advertisements for the victims, set up commercial sex appointments with men who responded to the postings, and collected the proceeds of the commercial sex acts.

The court sentenced the nine defendants as follows:.

Yilibeth Carmen Rivero-de Caldera: 204 months in prison;.

Kleiver Daniel Mota Rivero: 204 months in prison;.

Ramon De Jesus Velasquez Martinez: 100 months in prison;.

Wilmarys Del Valle Manzano Solorzano: Time Served;.

Endrik Alexander Morales-Rivero: 66 months in prison;.

Ariannys Beatriz Gutierrez-Carrillo: 50 months in prison;.

Frankyanna Del Valle Romero-Rivero: 50 months in prison;.

Jesus Enrique Castillo Rodriguez: 48 months in prison; and.

Yuribetzi Del Valle Gomez Machuca: 60 months.

It has been revealed that in addition to the sentences imposed above, the court ordered that Rivero and Mota pay a total of $171,614 in restitution to four identified victims. The court further ordered that several of the other defendants were jointly and severally liable with Rivero and Mota for various portions of the total restitution order depending on their conduct toward individual victims.

According to recent reports, trial Attorneys Lindsey Roberson, Jessica Arco, and Alexandra Skinnion of the Criminal Division’s Human Rights and Special Prosecutions Section (HRSP) and Assistant U.S. Attorney Katy Risinger for the Middle District of Tennessee are prosecuting the case.

These prosecutions are part of the Homeland Security Task Force (HSTF) initiative established by Executive Order 14159, Protecting the American People Against Invasion. The HSTF is a whole- of-government partnership dedicated to eliminating criminal cartels, foreign gangs, transnational criminal organizations, and human smuggling and trafficking rings operating in the United States and abroad. Through historic interagency collaboration, the HSTF directs the full might of United States law enforcement towards identifying, investigating, and prosecuting the full spectrum of crimes committed by these organizations, which have long fueled violence and instability within our borders. In performing this work, the HSTF places special emphasis on investigating and prosecuting those engaged in child trafficking or other crimes involving children. The HSTF further utilizes all available tools to prosecute and remove the most violent criminal aliens from the United States.

The Nashville HSTF comprises agents and officers from his, the Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms & Explosives (ATF), the FBI, IRS-CI, U.S. Postal Inspection Service (USPIS), U.S. Marshals Service (USMS), U.S. Secret Service (USSS), Drug Enforcement Administration (DEA), and other federal agencies, working in collaboration with our state and local partners, with the prosecutions being led by the Middle District of Tennessee.

Industry observers note that this case was further supported and prosecuted by Joint Task Force Alpha (JTFA), the U.S. Department of Justice’s lead effort in combating human smuggling and trafficking committed by cartels and Transnational Criminal Organizations throughout the Americas. In partnership with the U.S. Department of Homeland Security (DHS), JTFA investigates and prosecutes the most prolific and dangerous human smuggling and trafficking networks that impact public safety and border security. Led by HRSP and supported by other Criminal Division components, JTFA has dedicated prosecutors from U.S. Attorneys’ Offices along the Southwest border, Southern Florida, Northern New York, and Vermont. JTFA partners with U.S. Attorneys’ Offices throughout the country and supports high-priority cases in any district. All JTFA cases rely on substantial law enforcement resources from DHS components, including U.S. Immigration and Customs Enforcement, HSI, and U.S. Customs and Border Protection, U.S. Border Patrol, and the Office of Field Operations. JTFA also partners with FBI and other law enforcement agencies. To date, JTFA’s work has resulted in more than 486 domestic and international arrests of leaders, organizers, and significant facilitators of alien smuggling and trafficking; more than 447 U.S. convictions; and more than 374 significant jail sentences imposed and forfeitures of substantial assets.

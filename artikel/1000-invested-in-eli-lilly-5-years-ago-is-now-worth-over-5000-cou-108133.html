Industry observers note that eli Lilly (LLY -0.30%) is a well-established pharmaceutical organization, known for delivering reliable revenue year after year. Patients need their medicines regardless of the economic backdrop, and this fact offers pharma giants a certain amount of stability. This translates into an element of safety for investors. But Eli Lilly also offers something that many of us associate with other industries, such as technology. I'm talking about growth. The organization has generated double-digit revenue gains in recent quarters thanks to its presence in the weight loss drug space. Lilly sells tirzepatide under the names Mounjaro for type 2 diabetes and Zepbound for weight loss. (Doctors have prescribed either one for the weight loss indication.). It has been revealed that all of this helped the stock climb more than 420% over the past five years, meaning that if you invested $1,000, you would have over $5,000 today. Could it soar that much again by 2031? Let's find out.

It has been revealed that so, first, let's take a look at Eli Lilly's path over the past few years. This pharma giant sells a broad range of medicines across treatment areas, from cancer to migraine and Alzheimer's disease. But the area that has stood out in recent years is weight loss. Lilly's drugs are in the class known as GLP-1. These drugs act on hormonal pathways involved in digestion and therefore regulate appetite and blood sugar levels.

Individuals aiming to lose weight have rushed to get in on GLP-1 drugs since they've proven safety and efficacy and are easy to self-administer. Mounjaro and Zepbound are weekly injections, and Lilly recently won approval for a newly introduced GLP-1 drug in pill format. That product is called Foundayo and may significantly broaden the audience for these types of drugs.

Lilly isn't alone in the market; in fact, it wasn't even the leader in the early days of GLP-1 drugs. Novo Nordisk was the original leader, though Lilly slipped ahead in 2025. Now, the two are vying for leadership in the oral GLP-1 space, as Novo also rolled out an oral drug this year.

Lilly's recent earnings report demonstrates the power of its GLP-1 drugs, with Mounjaro and Zepbound driving a 39% increase in revenue to more than $24 billion.

The story doesn't end here, though. Lilly also has an extensive pipeline of weight loss drug candidates, including one called retatrutide that's in phase 3 studies and may have the potential to deliver greater weight loss. This could complement the existing portfolio, offering a variety of GLP-1 options for different needs.

Industry observers note that now, let's consider whether an investment in Lilly could once again turn $1,000 into $5,000 over the coming five years. This would imply a gain of about 420%, taking the share price to about $7,092 -- and the market cap from $1.2 trillion today to $6.3 trillion. This surpasses the value of today's biggest technology leaders.

Industry observers note that of course, it's possible that over the coming five years, tech players also will gain, seeing their market values march higher. So the market as a whole may see value climb significantly, making the Lilly jump to more than $6 trillion seem less shocking.

In a recent development, that noted, after the S&P 500's 78% increase over the past three calendar years and the double- and triple-digit gains of many substantial stocks, I wouldn't count on the same momentum over the coming five years -- for the general market or for Lilly. This doesn't mean the market or Lilly will drop. I think there's still potential for gains, and Lilly's weight loss portfolio and potentially new releases from the pipeline could drive significant growth.

Though I would be surprised to see another gain of 400%, I think it's possible to see a double- or triple-digit increase from Lilly stock over the next five years -- and that means I consider it a great buy today.

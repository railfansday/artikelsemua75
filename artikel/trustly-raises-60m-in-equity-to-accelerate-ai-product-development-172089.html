Trustly Holding AB revealed in a November 24, 2026 regulatory release that parent company Trustly AB will receive approximately $60 million from a shareholder capital raise, funding a push to develop AI-powered products that turn payment activity into financial intelligence for businesses.

Nordic Capital, Trustly’s principal shareholder, and Alfvén & Didrikson made more than $40 million in irrevocable, legally binding equity commitments disclosed on October 2, 2026. Other existing shareholders, including funds and accounts managed by BlackRock, also participated in the raise. (Related: Breaking: Binding of Isaac fans review bomb DLC after d...)

It has been revealed that the proceeds will support products that combine payment and data services on Trustly’s open-banking payments network across the United States, Canada and Europe. Trustly says its planned real-time intelligence platform is intended to help businesses acquire customers, retain them longer and stay ahead of risk, with each interaction making the next one smarter.

Sources indicate that group CEO Johan Tjärnberg put the existing network at more than 50 million consumers and over $120 billion in annual transactions. That activity provides the payment and data base for the AI strategy: Trustly is building an intelligence layer within its existing payments network, tying the plan to transaction activity already flowing through the business. Sources indicate that the financing follows the second quarter of 2026, when payment activity grew much faster than revenue. Trustly’s August 27, 2026 investor presentation reported total payment volume of SEK 296 billion, up 29% year over year at constant currency, and revenue growth of 9% on the same basis, with volume gains more than compensating for price compression. First-half revenue growth was 11% at constant currency, reversing a double-digit decline in the comparable year-earlier period.

Within that result, Americas revenue rose 24% at constant currency, while European revenue fell 9%, making the Americas the principal regional growth driver in the quarter.

Profitability moved in the opposite direction. Reported adjusted EBITDA, an adjusted earnings measure before interest, taxes, depreciation and amortization, fell to SEK 42 million from SEK 85 million a year earlier. Management attributed the decline primarily to higher US losses after Trustly widened its risk-approval rules, alongside pricing pressure and foreign-exchange effects.

Trustly noted the wider US approval rules produced approximately 40% growth in new US Payin owners in the second quarter compared with the first, alongside higher losses, and that it would continue calibrating approval thresholds to balance growth against loss exposure.

Cash flow was negative for a second consecutive quarter, and the first-half outflow reached SEK 261.7 million. That context sharpens the significance of the upcoming capital: Trustly is funding an intelligence layer on top of a growing payments network while earnings and cash generation remain under pressure. The investment links its AI ambitions to the operational challenge of turning payment data into useful intelligence while containing US losses and pricing pressure.

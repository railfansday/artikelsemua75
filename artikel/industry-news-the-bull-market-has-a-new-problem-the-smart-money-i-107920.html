Companies have shifted from buying back shares to issuing them, adding to the supply that the four-year-old bull market must absorb.

"No matter how you measure it, U.S. corporations have changed their behavior over the past two years, switching from repurchasing in 2024 to issuing today," Acadian Asset Management portfolio manager Owen Lamont wrote in a letter to investors this month. "The question you should be asking is: Why is the smart money selling?". (Related: University launches $5m smart system that checks who is...)

As of late last week, net new issuance of shares equaled 3.1% of total market capitalization, according to a recent Acadian analysis. While net issuance remains below the peaks during the tech bubble and the covid bubble, the analysis shows that the era of repurchases has ended and an era of equity issuance has begun. And more supply came to the market this week with Anthropic's blockbuster IPO. The offering drew strong enthusiasm initially with the frontier AI laboratory selling shares in its IPO at $83 each, above the expected range and raising $81 billion. But the stock is falling on Friday and taking the broader stock market with it.

Some investors for years have cited the shrinking stock market as a reason why share prices can keep increasing. As companies repurchased stock, it shrunk the number of shares available for trading and so it helped boost prices. There was more money chasing fewer shares. Not to mention, it had the effect of boosting earnings per share by lowering the denominator in that ratio. Now that trend has reversed with increased supply acting as a potential headwind on the bull market.

A 2000 paper by Malcolm Baker and Jeffrey Wurgler found that when companies issued more equity over debt, low market returns followed, suggesting that managers timed the market when issuing securities. The Baker-Wurgler equity share, which measures equity issuance as a share of total stock and bond issuance, peaked at about 18% in 2021. It has risen in recent months but remains below that peak, Lamont wrote.

Sources indicate that the Federal Reserve data show a similar trend in the year-over-year change in trailing 12-month gross stock and bond issuance. Debt gross issuance rose first in 2020, followed by equity issuance in 2021. More recently, debt issuance began climbing in 2024, followed by a surge in equity issuance in 2026.

Recent deals show companies tapping equity markets in different ways.

Oracle filed to sell $20 billion in stock through an at-the-market program and issued another $10 billion in common shares in November. In October, Meta priced a $25 billion common-stock offering and $12.5 billion in mandatory convertible preferred shares. Alphabet unveiled an $84.8 billion equity raise in June to help fund its AI infrastructure buildout. SpaceX raised $85.7 billion in its June IPO after underwriters exercised their overallotment.

Recent findings suggest that "Before 2026, firms were net repurchasers of equity, consistent with the idea that they viewed their shares as undervalued. Today, for whatever reason, firms are issuing equity on net," Lamont wrote.

The investor said he cannot rule out that we are in an AI bubble now.

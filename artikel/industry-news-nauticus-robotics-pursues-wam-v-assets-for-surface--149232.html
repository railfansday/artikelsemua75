📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Ocean Power Technologies announced on November 23, 2026 that Nauticus Robotics had signed a non-binding letter of intent to acquire its WAM-V unmanned surface vessel business for $8 million in Nauticus Series C convertible preferred stock at closing, plus up to $2 million in additional cash.

According to recent reports, nauticus’s announcement specifies that the proposed asset purchase includes associated software and intellectual property. The unmanned surface vessels, or USVs, would extend Nauticus’s subsea robotics portfolio into surface operations, with ToolKITT integration intended to enable coordinated operations between WAM-V vessels and Aquanaut autonomous subsea robots.

The contingent cash would comprise 20% of proceeds from Nauticus at-the-market share sales through December 31, 2026, and a 5% royalty on WAM-V revenue. Nauticus president and CEO John Gibson remarked the preferred-stock structure would expand the company’s autonomous robotics capabilities “without impacting current cash reserves.”.

Nauticus describes ToolKITT as modular, vehicle-agnostic software that can work alongside existing vehicle-control software when a vehicle’s software interfaces permit. It is designed for Nauticus and third-party underwater vehicles as well as surface vessels. Current functions center on pilot assistance, software-assisted control and repeatable workflows; obstacle avoidance remains in development, and the product page tells customers to begin integration now and blueprint for delivery in early 2027. Industry observers note that nauticus describes Aquanaut 2.1 as an all-electric, untethered vehicle with ToolKITT-native autonomy for transit, survey and inspection, and lists customer configurations up to a 3,000-meter design rating. The underlying Mk2 platform has demonstrated an operating depth of 2,300 meters, while autonomous manipulation and collision and obstacle avoidance remain future capabilities. The integration goal therefore goes beyond those single-vehicle functions: coordinated WAM-V–Aquanaut operations are an objective of the proposed acquisition. OPT describes WAM-V as a modular, wave-adaptive platform for coastal, littoral, harbor and inland operations and reports more than 80 platforms delivered globally. Its patented articulating suspension and twin-hull configuration support sensor, communications and mission payloads, while modular interfaces allow propulsion and equipment to be configured for mapping, monitoring, inspection, surveillance and data collection.

The parties are targeting signing and closing within 45 days, subject to a definitive agreement, approval by both boards, due diligence, required regulatory approvals, and consents from Nauticus’s lender and preferred stockholders. Nauticus cautioned that neither a definitive agreement nor completion of the transaction is assured.

It has been revealed that OPT indicates it has reduced ongoing operating expenses by 65% as part of its strategic review. Following the WAM-V transaction, it would focus on its U.S. PowerBuoy business, which serves U.S. government customers including the Department of Homeland Security, and on its MERROWS and MDAS software. A separate consulting agreement with Stratum Holdings provides up to $3 million in annual retainer payments to OPT in exchange for U.S. exclusivity while the companies evaluate PowerBuoy technology for powering and connecting offshore data centers.

Recent findings suggest that the proposal would give Nauticus a surface-vessel business on which to pursue shared autonomy, while OPT would retain a narrower business centered on PowerBuoy and software. The next substantive test is whether the parties can secure the required approvals and turn ToolKITT’s cross-vehicle architecture into the coordinated surface-and-subsea operations Nauticus envisions.

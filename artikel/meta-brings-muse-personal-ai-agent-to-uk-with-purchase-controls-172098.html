In a recent development, meta launched its Muse personal AI agent in the UK on November 23, 2026, for users aged 18 and over. The agent can initiative and execute tasks across apps and websites, with British retail and travel connectors, agent-assisted checkout and mandatory purchase approval.

Sources indicate that muse is available on iOS, Android, muse.ai and WhatsApp. It is free for most uses, with optional subscriptions for additional usage, and Meta says it will come to the manufacturer’s AI glasses in the months ahead. Sources indicate that consumers can assign a goal rather than a single question. Muse turns it into a initiative, works across connected apps and websites, and can open a browser, fill in forms or pursue refunds with user oversight. Longer tasks continue after the app closes, with Muse returning when circumstances change or approval is needed. It can also create trackers, itineraries, study guides and dashboards.

The UK partner network supplies the local component of the launch. Dozens of partners include global services such as Spotify and OpenTable alongside Marks & Spencer, Ocado Retail, Trainline, Currys and easyJet. Some integrations let customers connect personal accounts so Muse can reorder a usual shop or retrieve a booking; for others, the agent can navigate their websites from launch. PayPal is listed as coming soon rather than being available at launch. (Related: Restaurant owners left in tears after discovering their...)

Sources indicate that checkout through Link, built by Stripe, generates a unique digital version of a card that only agents using Link can access. Shop Pay works with supported stores using saved payment and shipping details. Purchases require user approval every time, and Muse presents the action before proceeding.

Industry observers note that meta’s companion control guide describes read and write permissions that can be separated, so Muse can read email without being allowed to send messages. Access can be limited by task or duration, changed or revoked, and services can be disconnected. Muse keeps a record of completed and planned actions and approved permissions; owners can watch its browser activity and take over.

Memory can be inspected and edited, users can tell Muse to forget a topic or person, and resetting the agent deletes their Muse data for a fresh start. Credentials are held in a secure store so the model does not see passwords, while the email connector ignores one-time codes and password-reset links.

Sources indicate that meta says conversations and retained Muse information are not shared with its advertising systems. Use of interactions to develop Meta’s AI is on initially and can be disabled; if left on, Meta removes certain details, including names, email addresses and phone numbers, and does not use interactions containing private messages such as emails or Instagram direct messages.

The UK service is powered by Muse Spark 1.3, which Meta AI Research released on September 2, 2026, for agentic and coding work. Meta describes the model as designed for longer-horizon tasks and multiple workflows in one thread, with behavior that includes clarifying ambiguous requests, requesting help when stuck and confirming consequential actions. Those functions provide the model-level basis for Muse’s combination of background work and user intervention.

Meta’s technical safety account claims each Muse runs in an isolated cloud virtual machine, with the agent runtime separated from credential and safety services. A component called Sentinel is the sole permission authority for connector actions and network egress, and the main agent cannot override it. The design assumes that the agent may process hostile data and seeks to contain the resulting damage.

It has been revealed that the safety account also qualifies the privacy boundary: operational policies restrict Meta personnel’s access, but do not prevent Meta from accessing VM data when necessary to support, secure or operate the service. Its planned Confidential VM is intended to cryptographically and verifiably prevent that access.

Meta nevertheless cautions that Muse can make mistakes and that prompt injection — hostile instructions embedded in content the agent reads — remains an open problem. Its architecture is designed to limit damage rather than eliminate risk.

It has been revealed that that residual risk is the central qualification to the UK launch. Muse’s usefulness grows with persistent context and access to more services, while the amount of responsibility it can take on remains tied to scoped permissions, visible activity and explicit human approval.

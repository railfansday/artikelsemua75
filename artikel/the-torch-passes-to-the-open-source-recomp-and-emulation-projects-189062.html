📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

GTA 6 leaker or leakers Cyberleek claim to to have released the main executable file for an alleged Xbox build of the game that they asserted they'd lobbed onto the net last week. In a fresh statement, the leakers alluded to the excutable being used in attempts to create an unofficial PC port of the crimsim, and denied that their use of cryptocurrency is just a grift.

Sources indicate that referring to the alleged executable as "an early Christmas gift", Cyberleek declared in the statement posted to their website that "the torch passes to the open source recomp and emulation projects". As of writing, the legitimacy of the executable doesn't appear to have been independently verified, meaning it could still just turn out to be a pile of malware or something similarly dodgy. Needless to say, given that and the fact software piracy is illegal, not touching it - or any of the other files Cyberleek claim to have posted - with a barge pole is the sensible thing to do. Sources indicate that cyberleek have been bigging up the idea that these files they've been releasing could result in GTA 6 running on PC sharpish, via a modified version cobbled together with generative AI. That might happen, but I'm seriously skeptical that if it were to, it wouldn't just be dodgy jankware than runs like piss, rather than anything that'd compare remotely well to an official PC port.

Thus far, it appears a group of would-be recompilers going by Project Leonida may have got the game to start booting on PC, but they since look to have disappeared and scrubbed their established online presence.

All of this again raises the question of whether the threat of any sort of bodged PC recreation coming out of this alleged executable release may lead Rockstar to narrow the gap between this month's console release and its eventual Windows release, which reports have previously claimed is planned for the opening months of next year.

Sources indicate that returning to Cyberleek's latest statement, the leaker or leakers have also taken the opportunity to insist that their attempts to pump up a crypto coin as part of the leaking is just for practical reasons rather than grifty ones, honest. "Every donation is a vote for more of this," they wrote. "Where the votes go, [Cyberleek] follows. Crypto here is just a tool - there is no other way to move funds without names.".

Sources indicate that rockstar called the initial barrage of leaks laid claim to by Cyberleek "heartbreaking". Along with parent company Take-Two, they began a hunt for the identity of the leakers back in August, but that doesn't seem to to have deterred Cyberleek, if indeed the people leaking under the name are still the same ones as in the summer.

Industry observers note that mmathematician at Anthropic says he has used the organization’s Claude AI model to prove the Riemann hypothesis, one of the most famous unsolved problems in mathematics. Levent Alpöge made the claim in a note on his website on Friday, Bloomberg reported. (Related: Industry News: “Determined” woman completes 7 marathons...)

“I believe I have, working with Claude, discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis,” Alpöge wrote. (Related: We Got Claude to Build CUDA Kernels and teach open mode...)

The hypothesis, first proposed in 1859, concerns how prime numbers are spread out. It is one of the Clay Mathematics Institute’s seven Millennium Prize Problems, each of which carries a $1m prize. Alpöge indicates his proof also covers a broader version, the generalised Riemann hypothesis.

He did not publish a traditional paper. Instead, he released the proof in Lean, a programming language that checks every logical step. The code runs to about 17 million lines, Science reported.

Sources indicate that andrew Sutherland, a senior research scientist at MIT, told Bloomberg he had checked the code on his own computer. He said he had seen at least three other checks by professional mathematicians. Later on Friday, Sutherland put the chance that anything is wrong at roughly zero, Scientific American reported.

In a recent development, however, other mathematicians urged caution. A proof that compiles in Lean still has to state the right problem, and humans have to confirm that it does.

Kevin Buzzard, a professor at Imperial College London, said nobody yet understands what the proof shows, Al Jazeera reported. The proof has not been formally accepted.

Alpöge stated Claude did much of the work.

“This time Claude far outpaced me near the finish line,” he wrote.

Anthropic declined to comment to Bloomberg. The claim came a day after the company’s record stock market debut.

In a recent development, if confirmed, it would be the second Millennium Prize Problem that AI may have helped solve. In September, OpenAI remarked its models had produced a solution to the Navier-Stokes problem.

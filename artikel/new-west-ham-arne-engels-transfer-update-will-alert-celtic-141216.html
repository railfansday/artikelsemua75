Arne Engels is of interest to Stuttgart, even though Celtic only sold the midfielder to West Ham United in the summer, and this could mean a windfall for the Scottish giants.

According to recent reports, west Ham United's stop-start 2026-27 season continued on Saturday with a 1-1 draw at home to Preston North End which saw them drop to tenth place in the Championship while leaving fresh manager Scott Parker still chasing his first win since replacing at the start of November. The Irons are now three points adrift of a play-off position, and they're now five consecutive games without a League win.

Recent findings suggest that successes at The London Stadium have been thin on the ground this season, but one of the players that has shone for them so far has been Arne Engels. The Belgian midfielder signed for the club from Celtic in the summer for £22 million, but such has been the calibre of his performances that he's already starting to draw attention from other clubs despite only having signed a five-year contract with West Ham in August. The Glasgow Times have reported on Monday morning that Arne Engels stay at West Ham United could turn out to be a brief one. They report that the midfielder is of interest to the Bundesliga club Stuttgart amid speculation that one of their midfielders, Angelo Stiller, could be on his way out of the club.

But Engels isn't the only midfielder who could be of interest to Stuttgart as a replacement for Stille, with the Glasgow Times also reporting that the German club could be interested in Caspar Jander of Southampton and Aidan Morris of Middlesbrough.

They report that "Celtic are likely to have included a sell-on percentage in the deal and could be set to benefit from any future move", though there is no clarification of what that percentage might be, while it hasn't been confirmed that such a clause actually exists.

In a recent development, and they also report that the price could become something of a sticking point, given that West Ham paid a Championship-record £22 million for him in the summer, and signed him on a five-year contract. There are clearly disgruntled West Ham United fans whose patience with Arne Engels has already run out, but their exasperation isn't quite borne out by the statistics for him. Fotmob match ratings for the midfielder demonstrate a succession of decent performances this season from the player, even though West Ham's team performances have not delivered the results that supporters would have been hoping for.

But even in the event that Engels were to leave West Ham, it's doubtful that he would be able to play for Stuttgart this season. FIFA rules explicitly state that, while players can be registered to play for three different clubs over a 12-month period, they can only play for two of them, and as Engels' sale to West Ham only went through in the middle of August, by which time he'd already played in two SPFL games for Celtic, he would ordinarily be prevented from playing for Stuttgart as well, though there are exceptions to this rule for clubs who play to different calendars.

Recent findings suggest that the Glasgow Times' reporting confirms that the speculation over Angelo Stiller concerns a potential move next summer, meaning that West Ham would retain his services for the remainder of 2026-27. This would at least mean that they don't have to scramble for a replacement for him themselves during the January transfer window. Whether the news of potential Bundesliga interest in him is connected to another recent announcement, that Engels has changed his agent, is not known.

West Ham United's performances so far throughout 2026-27 have not matched pre-season expectations, so it's unsurprising that fans are turning their ire on their underperforming players. But having already been capped four times by the Belgian national team, it's clear that there is talent in Arne Engels, and perhaps the best way to get the best out of him would be to get back up into the promotion hunt and secure a quick return to the Premier League. Recent results have not shown much indication that this is going to happen, but the possibility of Premier League football in 2027-28 could yet prevent the player's head from being turned by the possibility of a move to Germany.

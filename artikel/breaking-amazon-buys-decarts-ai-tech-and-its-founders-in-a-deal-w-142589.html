Breaking: Amazon buys Decart’s AI tech and its founders in a deal worth about $7.5bn

Amazon has agreed to buy the technology behind Decart’s AI optimisation software and to bring the Israeli startup’s three co-founders and their team into the company. Amazon confirmed the deal on Monday without giving a price. (Related: The Partnership: Amazon SageMaker and Hugging Face...)

According to recent reports, amazon will pay about $7.5bn for the assets, a person familiar with the matter told Matt Day of Bloomberg. It is not buying Decart’s AI models, the person said. News of their talks first emerged in October. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

According to recent reports, the deal covers the Decart Optimization Stack, software that helps AI models run faster and more cheaply across different chips. The co-founders, Dean Leitersdorf, Moshe Shalev and Orian Leitersdorf, will join Amazon with their researchers and engineers. The deal still needs regulatory clearance.

“Every leap forward in AI starts with getting more out of every chip,” mentioned Peter DeSantis, Amazon’s senior vice president for foundational AI models, custom silicon and quantum computing.

It has been revealed that decart has worked with Amazon since 2025, including on its Trainium chips, which Amazon pitches as a rival to Nvidia’s GPUs. Amazon said the technology will help it get more out of both its AI inference and its custom chips.

“Joining Amazon gives our team the scale, the customers, and the infrastructure to pursue that mission faster than we could on our own,” said Dean Leitersdorf, Decart’s co-founder and chief executive.

According to recent reports, amazon is making the deal through Annapurna Labs, its chip development arm, Israeli business daily Globes reported. The payment is in shares and depends on meeting targets over five years, according to Globes. The three founders have committed to stay at Amazon for that long.

Recent findings suggest that the founders hold about 64% of Decart and stand to share $4.8bn, Globes reported. The bulk of that money comes in the later years of the deal.

Anthropic had held talks to buy Decart for about $6bn before it walked away in September.

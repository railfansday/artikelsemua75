Robins ‘aims for the stars’ as Stoke dream of Premier League return

The Championship’s form side climbed to second after holding on resiliently with 10 men to knock Birmingham off the summit.

Sources indicate that there have been false dawns before. Plenty of them. This time last year, with Christmas on the near horizon, Stoke sat second in the Championship table and were beginning to wonder whether they might allow themselves to dream. So hopeless was their subsequent run that by the onset of spring they were forced to consider a potential relegation fight.

In a recent development, experience has taught the benefit of pragmatism, so no one will be getting ahead of themselves just yet. But this weekend was supposed to be the significant test for Mark Robins’s team and few considered they would pass it quite so resoundingly. It has been revealed that facing a Birmingham side who arrived top of the table and with firmly held Premier League ambitions, Stoke dominated for an hour, the hitherto goal-shy George Hirst earning a well-deserved lead to send a bumper crowd into raptures. The hand-wringing that then followed Luca Bombino’s sending-off with half an hour remaining made the victory that followed all the sweeter. The 10 men of Stoke were superb. It was no surprise that it took considerable time to convince those in the stands to depart for home after the final whistle.

You see, it is not just that Stoke are second in the table, kept off top spot by goal difference. It is not even that they are undoubtedly the Championship’s form side, with just one defeat in their past 13 games. It is the whole feeling around the place; the resilience Robins has developed, and the belief that they can take on the significant guns and beat them.

According to recent reports, “We are a work in progress, but today was that step in quality against different opponents. A step up in class,” mentioned Robins. “I thought we were really, really good. The intensity, bravery and the intent that we showed was everything that we asked for. (Related: University launches $5m smart system that checks who is...)

Recent findings suggest that “And, on the back of that, you’ve got the support that are absolutely right behind us. They trust the players. That is a really good feeling. It’s a great feeling for the players. It’s a heady mix, really.”.

Industry observers note that eight years have passed since Stoke were last in the top flight; eight years spent as the Championship equivalent of a sea sponge, doing the bare minimum to survive and letting defeats wash over them while remaining resolutely attached to the rocks of the bottom half of the table. During that time they have ended every campaign no higher than 14th and no lower than 18th. Now, something altogether different is afoot. And this may well be the win that convinces supporters to trust their upbeat instincts.

Industry observers note that there was an unavoidable fact heading into Saturday’s game. Stoke’s four defeats this season had all come when tasked with opponents fighting at the top end of the table in Swansea, Southampton, Wolves and Middlesbrough. They had shown themselves capable against much of the division, but a failure to deliver against another Championship heavyweight would have raised legitimate questions about their realistic aspirations.

In a recent development, the three August defeats at the start of the campaign preceded the departure of their best player, Sorba Thomas. His replacement, Jack McGlynn, has been an inspired signing. That this victory came without the talismanic figure of Ethan Galbraith, who was absent through illness, is considerable.

As well as switching to a three-man defence following those season-opening losses, a key factor in their upturn has been the restoration of a touch of old-school Stoke; a physicality that was frequently evident on a cold Saturday afternoon.

Sources indicate that bobby Madley, the referee, did not always take kindly to such an approach – to the indignation of the home crowd, who frequently made their feelings clear – but there was no complaint over Bombino’s sending-off for a second yellow after a rash challenge that left him head in hands before the card had even been shown. Tears followed on his slow trudge towards the tunnel, but he need not have worried, with Birmingham barely threatening thereafter.

Given just five points separate the top eight in the Championship table, it would come as no great shock to see Stoke drop off at some point. They do not have the financial benefit of recent Premier League parachute payments or big-spending owners, they are not prolific goalscorers, and a look at their expected goals and expected points tallies reveals them firmly in the bottom half of the table. But they are finding ways to win and if McGlynn has a habit of scoring from unexpectedly long distances, perhaps it is because he is good at it.

Before this weekend, Robins had revealed that a top-eight finish was the pre-season target. “You aim for the stars and hopefully you land trying to grab hold of one,” he had said, rather poetically.

If promotion is a “P” they dare not yet speak of, another one – playoffs – is becoming an increasingly realistic proposition. No more sticking to a rock. Swim free and prosper.

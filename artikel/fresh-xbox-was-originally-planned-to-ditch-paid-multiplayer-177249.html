Fresh Xbox Was Originally Planned to Ditch Paid Multiplayer

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that all signs point to the next generation of console hardware being historically expensive. With rising component costs, the PS5 and Xbox Series are going for hundreds more than they did when they debuted in 2020. In that context, it’s frightening to imagine the price of an even more powerful system, with one estimate placing potential manufacturing costs between $1,200 and $1,800.

This dire situation seems to be prompting Microsoft to make major changes to how it will handle the next Xbox, codenamed Project Helix. According to a fresh report from Windows Central, soaring component costs are causing Microsoft to reconsider several core assumptions for its next-generation machine, including a plan to ditch subscription fees for multiplayer games. (Related: Is Buy Now Pay Later (BNPL) Worth It? The Hidden Costs...)

The main reason the tech giant planned to remove subscription fees is that the previous Xbox leadership team, led by Phil Spencer, had envisioned the Helix as a gaming PC that would let you run existing Xbox games. It would have functioned similarly to the Xbox Ally PC gaming handheld, with a controller-focused interface. However, the system would also have let customers exit the Xbox home screen to access a Windows desktop, allowing them to install whatever they want, including Steam. Because the console would function like a PC, former Xbox execs concluded it didn’t make sense to keep charging subscription fees when directly competing against PC games that don’t charge for multiplayer. The company apparently factored lost subscription revenue into the system’s original costs.

In a recent development, however, with Spencer and many other key executives leaving Xbox and an AI-driven RAM scarcity crisis driving up hardware costs, the new leadership team under CEO Asha Sharma is reconsidering these options. Microsoft is reportedly looking for ways to subsidize the system's expensive hardware, such as continuing to charge for multiplayer access.

Recent findings suggest that while Sharma hasn’t confirmed specifics about how plans for the Helix may be changing due to current market conditions, some of her comments offer clues about the system's fresh direction. The current blueprint is that the console will still offer the “ability to play your PC games,” but it’s unclear whether this means customers can access Steam or will be limited to PC games purchased through Microsoft’s storefront. (Related: How to Start a Side Business While Working Full-Time...)

It should be noted that it’s still possible Sharma’s team will carry out the previous executive team’s plan to scrap multiplayer subscription fees, potentially to differentiate Xbox from PlayStation. However, given rising hardware costs and how closely Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella seems to be monitoring Xbox’s financials, that outcome has become much less likely. Sharma previously noted that Xbox's current business was "not healthy" before announcing mass layoffs that will cut 3,200 jobs.

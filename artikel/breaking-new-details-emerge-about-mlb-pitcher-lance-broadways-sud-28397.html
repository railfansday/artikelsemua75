Fresh details about Lance Broadway’s death have emerged following his sudden death at the age of 43.

According to recent reports, three days after the former MLB pitcher turned actor died in Texas, the Dallas County Medical Examiner listed his manner of death as “pending,” Us can confirm. While Broadway’s cause of death remains unknown, the ME indicated that he died “in the stairwell of an apartment complex” off Lemon Avenue, which is located near Dallas-Love Field Airport. NBC News reported on Friday, November 20, that the Dallas police have a case number associated with Broadway’s death, but additional information surrounding his death has not been released.

Us has reached out to the medical examiner’s office for further comment.

Broadway, who played for the Chicago White Sox and Upcoming York Mets, died on Tuesday, November 17.

Jamie Plunkett, a Texas Christian University sports reporter for HornedFrogBlitz.com, announced the news via X the following day, writing, “Some heartbreaking news: Former TCU and DBU pitcher Lance Broadway has passed away.”.

Industry observers note that “Broadway earned 2005 CUSA Pitcher of the Year and First-Team All-American honors from Baseball America, posting a 1.61 ERA and 151 strikeouts in 117 innings in his lone season as a Horned Frog,” Plunkett shared. “Following that season, he was selected 15th overall in the 2005 MLB Draft by the Chicago White Sox. He made his MLB Debut on Sept. 7, 2007, and also spent time with the Mets and Blue Jays organizations.”. (Related: Industry News: Politics Takes A Toll On Your Health — W...)

The journalist concluded, “Broadway also briefly stepped into acting, appearing in the 2013 film Olympus Has Fallen as well as a handful of television and short film projects.”.

Broadway was born and raised in Texas where he attended Grand Prairie High School and Waxahachie High School, before spending two years at Dallas Baptist University.

The right-handed pitcher transferred to Texas Christian University for his junior year, where he was dubbed the Conference USA Pitcher of the Year.

After an impressive season with the Horned Frogs, Broadway was selected by the White Sox as the 15th pick in the 2005 MLB Draft.

According to recent reports, he made his big league debut with Chicago in September 2007 and pitched 27 games for the team. Broadway also pitched for the Mets from 2007 to 2009. He ended his career in the minor leagues as part of the Toronto Blue Jays organization in 2010.

That same year, Broadway took an acting class and found his second act after baseball.

“In 2010 was my last [MLB] season and in every offseason I wanted to get involved with something,” Broadway told Screen Slam’s Shawn Cauthen in 2013 of the career shift. “And I don’t know why, but I just wanted to go take an improv class. The improv class I wanted didn’t start until January 2011, so I asked a friend who told me to go take an acting class. That way, at least I will be in front of people doing something while they watch. So I got into an acting class in November 2010. I loved it and stuck with it.”.

Broadway appeared in his first short in 2011, and two years later he made his major screen debut in 2013’s Olympus Has Fallen.

In a recent development, his credits also include episodes of Lucifer, Agents of S.H.I.E.L.D. and Colony.

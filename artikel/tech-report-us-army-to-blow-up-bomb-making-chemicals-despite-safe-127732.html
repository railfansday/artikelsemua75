Army invokes emergency provisions in Iowa to avoid court order despite worries about health and environment risks.

The US army intends to invoke emergency provisions to avoid a court order that had put on hold plans to detonate several tons of bomb-making chemicals in Iowa, saying that continued delays will make it more dangerous as the weather starts to turn colder.

“To protect its personnel and the surrounding communities, the Army needs to take swift action,” government lawyers told the eight US circuit court of appeals in a filing late on Saturday. “Protection of human health and the environment demand action now, not delay.”.

The army plans to detonate the chemicals by Tuesday.

According to recent reports, the appeals court on Thursday granted a request by environmentalists to put on hold a permit that cleared the way for the detonation in Middletown, a small community about 160 miles (257km) south-east of Des Moines. It was initially planned for Friday, but the army later announced that it would push the operation to Monday after an outpouring of public concern about potential health and environmental effects.

In a statement on Sunday, the Iowa Environmental Council, Sierra Club and Earthjustice questioned claims that all alternatives were given appropriate consideration.

“The Army seemed more concerned over the weekend with finding a way to sidestep the judicial process than on meeting the public’s demands for greater transparency, notice to affected communities, and far stronger protection of people, property, and the environment from serious harm,” remarked Thien Chau, an attorney at Earthjustice.

Government lawyers remarked the army doesn’t need a permit to take emergency action against an immediate threat from explosive material.

They said the standards of a federal law regulating hazardous waste don’t apply in situations when there is an immediate threat to health, safety or the environment as determined by an explosive or munitions emergency response specialist.

Officials contend that the chemicals are unstable, and the risk of an uncontrolled explosion is growing, particularly as temperatures drop. They mentioned there is a risk that the chemicals may explode as temperatures are expected to drop below 30F (-1C) in the coming days.

In March, staff at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant found several dozen barrels of degraded lead azide, which is used in munitions production. Crystallization of the material created an unstable and dangerous situation, prompting the army to assemble experts in munitions chemistry, engineering and environmental safety to come up with a plan.

According to EPA documents, alternatives such as neutralizing and decomposing the drums’ contents with other chemicals were considered. But officials mentioned handling the drums would increase the probability of an accidental, fatal detonation.

The environmental groups disagreed, arguing in their petition that other alternatives were not fully considered. They also criticized officials for not sharing more information with the public.

Nearly 400 people live in Middletown, but thousands more in surrounding areas could be affected by detonated material that could disperse in the air and land on the ground.

Iowans in the region were told to stay inside, keep windows and doors closed, bring pets and livestock indoors, and clean cars, windowsills and outdoor surfaces after.

Sources indicate that “The detonation will result in an air plume with some lead-containing particles which will disperse in the air and deposit on the ground,” an advisory states. “Lead is toxic to humans and animals.”.

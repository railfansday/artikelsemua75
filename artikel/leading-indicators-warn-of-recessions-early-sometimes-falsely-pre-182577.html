It has been revealed that leading economic indicators such as the yield curve tend to turn months or even years before a recession, and sometimes no recession follows. When one flashes, don’t sell your stocks on it. Use the warning as a prompt to check your emergency fund, your plan for a job loss, and your mix of stocks and bonds.

The Conference Board’s Leading Economic Index, which combines 10 such indicators, went nearly two years without a monthly gain after February 2022, and no recession began in that time. The yield curve’s record has the same catch. It inverted 6 to 24 months before each of the nine U.S. recessions from 1955 to 2018, the San Francisco Fed found, and a mid-1960s inversion brought a slowdown but no official recession. Measured as the gap between 10-year and 3-month Treasury rates, it inverted in October 2022 and stayed that way until December 2024, the St. Louis Fed’s data show, and no recession began in that time. Anyone who sold stocks when it inverted missed a rise of about 57% in the S&P 500 stock index by the time it flipped back, not counting dividends. Your job-loss plan should list the costs you’d cut first and how soon to file for unemployment. And your mix of stocks and bonds should leave out money you’ll need within about five years, which belongs in savings.

Start with the emergency fund, since it helps whether or not a recession comes. An automatic transfer from each paycheck to savings builds it without selling anything.

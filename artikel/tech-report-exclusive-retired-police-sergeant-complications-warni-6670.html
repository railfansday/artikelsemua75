EXCLUSIVE: A retired police sergeant with nearly three decades of law enforcement experience warned that she would not bring her own family to Upcoming York City amid Mayor Zohran Mamdani's plans to dismantle a specialized NYPD unit responsible for counterterrorism support and crowd control.

Recent findings suggest that "I wouldn't take my family there right now," retired Sgt. Betsy Brantner Smith, a spokesperson for the National Police Association, told Fox News Digital in an exclusive interview.

Smith's warning follows Mamdani's announcement that the NYPD's Strategic Response Group (SRG), a roughly 500-officer specialized unit involved in counterterrorism, major event security and protest response, will be disbanded as part of a broader overhaul of how the nation's largest police department handles demonstrations. The democratic socialist mayor has defended the decision as fulfilling a campaign promise to separate counterterrorism operations from the policing of constitutionally protected protests.

Critics, however, argue that dismantling the established unit could undermine the coordination and specialized training needed to respond to emergencies.

Smith, who spent 29 years in Chicago-area law enforcement and has experience supervising crowd control operations, mentioned the consequences could extend beyond protests.

"One of the beauties of this strategic response group is that these are a group of officers who are used to working together," Smith said. "They train together. They have specialized knowledge of how to deal quickly and safely with these types of situations, including an active shooter.".

In a recent development, her concerns come as Upcoming York City prepares for the 100th Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade Nov. 26, an event expected to draw enormous crowds of visitors to Manhattan.

Smith warned that breaking up the specialized unit could create vulnerabilities if its responsibilities were redistributed without preserving existing operational relationships.

"First and foremost, I'm concerned that any intelligence may be lost or may be ignored for a large scale event like this," she noted.

NYPD Assistant Commissioner Brad Weeks told Fox News Digital the restructuring will not affect security arrangements for this year's Macy's Thanksgiving Day Parade, explaining that the new First Amendment Support Team and Patrol Support Division will not begin operating until February 2027.

The department also said counterterrorism personnel at the moment assigned to the Strategic Response Group will be transferred to the NYPD's Counterterrorism Bureau, consolidating those capabilities within the specialized bureau.

In response to Fox News Digital's request for comment, Mamdani's office directed the outlet to remarks the mayor delivered during a news conference earlier this week.

"I've long said that counterterrorism and protest response are fundamentally different responsibilities and that Upcoming Yorkers exercising their constitutional right to protest should not be met with the same police response as a terrorism threat," Mamdani said.

The overhaul also imposes fresh restrictions on police tactics during protests, including prohibitions on long guns and tear gas, according to the mayor's office.

Pepper spray will no longer be permitted as a general crowd management tool, while officers assigned to the newly introduced Patrol Support Division will receive specialized training and retain arrest powers when necessary.

A Change.org petition created by the National Police Association calls on Upcoming York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani to restore the NYPD's Strategic Response Group. The petition displayed 1,938 verified signatures in the supplied screenshot. (Courtesy of National Police Association/Change.org).

Mamdani noted the initial changes would take effect in approximately 90 days, with a First Amendment Support Team (FAST) operating within the NYPD's Community Affairs Bureau during the transition.

A separate civilian-led Community Dialogue and De-escalation Division, housed within the Office of Community Safety, is scheduled to launch in 2028.

The mayor mentioned the division would focus on engaging with protest organizers, mediation and preventing tensions from escalating.

Mamdani rejected suggestions that civilians would be expected to confront violent demonstrators without police support.

Recent findings suggest that "Now, police will always be present in the case of any kind of violence," he noted. "So this idea that Fresh Yorkers' safety will be compromised is not true.".

"I would never support changes that diminish the NYPD's ability to protect New Yorkers or that put our officers in a position where they lack the resources necessary to confront a serious threat," Tisch mentioned in the city's announcement.

Mamdani's announcement also drew praise from far-left activist organizations that have long criticized the NYPD's handling of demonstrations.

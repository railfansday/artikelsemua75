You must file a 2026 federal return if your gross income reaches the threshold for your filing status and age, or if a trigger like $400 of net self-employment earnings applies. Otherwise filing is optional, but do it anyway if income tax was withheld from your pay, since only a return gets it back.

According to recent reports, for most filers, the threshold is the standard deduction for their status and age, the amount subtracted from income before any tax is figured. Gross income is all income that isn’t exempt from tax. Wages, tips, taxable interest, and unemployment benefits count, and so does a home sale’s gain, even if you can exclude it from tax. Social Security benefits count only in part, and only once half of them plus your other income, tax-exempt interest included, passes $25,000, or $32,000 on a joint return. Married people filing separately who lived together during the year count that part at any income.

Recent findings suggest that say you’re single, under 65, and earned $12,000 part time in 2026, with $700 of federal income tax withheld. You owe no income tax and don’t have to file, but only a return gets the $700 back. Refundable credits such as the Earned Income Tax Credit also pay out only to filers.

A return filed within three years of its due date still gets its withholding back, so a skipped 2023 return has until mid-April 2027.

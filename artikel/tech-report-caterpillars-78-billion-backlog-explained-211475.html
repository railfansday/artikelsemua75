Looking at Caterpillar's (CAT +0.57%) stock chart lately feels like a market paradox.

Sources indicate that on one hand, the heavy-machinery giant is sitting on a staggering, all-time-high $78 billion order backlog -- nearly double the $39.8 billion list it had a year ago. On the other hand, the stock has been cut roughly in half from its 52-week high, leaving shares trading at a modest 16.5 times forward earnings.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

Industry observers note that so, what gives? First, that $78 billion pile of orders isn't some accounting mirage cooked up by overly optimistic customers. (Related: How we sped up transformer inference 100x for 🤗 API cus...)

According to recent reports, artificial intelligence data centers require colossal amounts of electricity, and hyperscalers are snapping up Caterpillar's industrial gas turbines and massive reciprocating engines as fast as the manufacturer can build them. Power generation retail sales jumped 75% year over year in the third quarter, and customers are already booking production slots out to 2029 and 2030 just to get in line. Industry observers note that even better, Caterpillar's momentum isn't trapped in a strict artificial intelligence silo. Construction equipment sales to end owners climbed 20% in the third quarter -- marking seven straight quarters of growth -- thanks to major infrastructure projects and commercial builds. Even the mining division is seeing healthy order rates for copper and gold operations.

However, management expects 58% of the current backlog to convert into revenue within the next 12 months.

In a recent development, rather than a temporary double-ordering boost, Caterpillar is backing the demand with physical capacity expansions. The company prepares to nearly triple large reciprocating engine production by 2030 along with a $1 billion manufacturing investment in North Carolina.

For long-term investors, the massive backlog provides multi-year revenue visibility as the capacity constraints gradually ease.

Sources indicate that hi GPOD! We’re digging into the archives today for a garden that looks a little otherworldly. Jeff Calton gardens in Northeast Tennessee (Zone 6b/7a), where he grows an incredible collection of pitcher plants (Sarracenia cvs., Zones 5–9). Even better is how he grows them: Jeff sinks containers with minimal drainage into his regular garden beds, creating the boggy conditions these carnivorous plants need while allowing completely different plants to grow right alongside them. Such a clever way to work around varying growing conditions!

White-topped pitcher plant (Sarracenia leucophylla, Zones 5–8) has leaves that are more colorful than most flowers.

Yellow pitcher plant (Sarracenia flava, Zones 6–9) isn’t as brightly colored as some species, but it has a grace and elegance all its own.

‘Scarlet Belle’ pitcher plant (Sarracenia × ‘Scarlet Belle’, Zones 5–10) glows in front of a mixed planting including ‘Golden Duchess’ eastern hemlock (Tsuga canadensis ‘Golden Duchess’, Zones 3–7). (Related: Industry News: Uber Eats driver saves elderly woman aft...)

Close-up of the graceful pitchers of a yellow pitcher plant ready to lure in an insect meal.

The pitcher plants in the back are growing in a sunken bathtub, giving them the wet soil they need, while the sedum and sedges in front have the better-drained conditions they require.

With Jensen Huang back as CEO, Nvidia’s SEC probe puts CFO Colette Kress in the spotlight

  • The Fortune 500 manufacturer announced Monday that Huang had been reinstated as president and chief executive officer and as a member of the board, effective immediately.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that following Jensen Huang’s return to Nvidia, Colette Kress is no longer interim CEO. But as finance chief, a lingering Securities and Exchange Commission investigation could put Kress under scrutiny, raising questions about her future at the AI giant.

The Fortune 500 manufacturer announced Monday that Huang had been reinstated as president and chief executive officer and as a member of the board, effective immediately. He had stepped down on Nov. 12 amid reports of extramarital affairs, including one with a woman some officials believed was a Chinese spy. The White House told Fortune that “President Trump was totally involved” in Huang getting reinstated, and Trump said the U.S. government did not believe Huang’s return posed any national security risk. While the announcement indicated a return to Nvidia’s previous management structure, with Kress back in the finance chief role she’s held for the past 13 years, that may not be as easy as it appears. For one thing, it’s unclear whether the relationship between Kress and Huang has been damaged by the events of the past two weeks. And with Nvidia facing various investigations, Kress could be in the line of fire, depending on the outcome of the probes.

In a recent development, the SEC is investigating Nvidia’s accounting practices, Reuters reported last week. The Wall Street Journal previously reported the investigation, although its precise focus remains unclear. Nvidia didn’t respond to Fortune’s request for comment. An SEC spokesperson remarked the agency does not comment on “the existence or nonexistence of a possible investigation.”.

Industry observers note that an investigation does not necessarily mean wrongdoing has occurred. But the scrutiny could put Nvidia’s financial practices and corporate governance under a brighter spotlight, raising questions about what Kress knew about the firm’s financial risks and when.

According to recent reports, kathryn Harrigan, a professor at Columbia Business School, pointed to Kress reportedly raising concerns internally last spring about Nvidia’s financial backing of loans to AI startups buying its chips. Kress questioned how borrowers were selected and how guarantees were valued, according to reports.

Nvidia has helped AI startups secure financing to buy its chips, creating a circular flow of money: The tech giant supports buyers financially, and those buyers spend the money on Nvidia products. That makes it harder to assess underlying demand for its chips without that support, Harrigan remarked.

According to recent reports, “In my book, Ms. Kress is a person who’s aware of the problem, and good luck to her if it’s an SEC investigation,” mentioned Harrigan, a corporate strategy specialist. “When I served on boards, I’ve gone through that, and it’s just endless depositions, and they never tell you whether they’re satisfied or not.”.

Harrigan added that Huang “had better brush up on whatever he told Ms. Kress back in the spring when they reportedly started to have a disagreement between them about how they were proceeding.”.

Industry observers note that during Nvidia’s Nov. 17 earnings call, Kress also signaled caution about financial commitments.

“We are rigorously evaluating future investments, commitments, and guarantees,” she said, adding that Nvidia would be “disciplined and selective in our support.”.

The way that Nvidia accounted for sales in Singapore has also come under scrutiny. Singapore was one of the manufacturer’s five geographic revenue locations until mid-2025, when Nvidia abruptly changed how it recorded revenue and removed Singapore from the list of locations. Megaspeed, the Nvidia customer suspected of evading U.S. chip export controls and the company at which Huang’s paramour was an executive, is based in Singapore.

Shawn Cole, president and cofounder of Cowen Partners Executive Search, said an SEC investigation involving financial oversight generally falls within the CFO’s purview.

“Nvidia’s potential exposure to securities class action litigation, compounded by alleged gaps in its directors and officers liability insurance, raises serious questions about corporate governance, financial oversight, and risk management,” Cole mentioned.

Kress could face scrutiny over what she knew about these exposures, when she knew it, and her role in evaluating, disclosing, and mitigating those risks, he said.

Sources indicate that huang could also potentially have exposure for all of the same issues, of course. But as evidenced by Trump’s intervention in the board’s deliberations, the CEO has an important ally in his corner that Kress may not have.

Recent findings suggest that research suggests CFOs can face career consequences even when they are not implicated in corporate fraud. In “Fraud Firms’ Non-Implicated CFOs,” published in Contemporary Accounting Research, the authors found that roughly one-third of CFOs in its sample were not implicated in the misconduct, yet these executives experienced higher turnover and poorer subsequent employment prospects than peers at comparable companies—evidence that reputational damage can extend beyond those accused of wrongdoing.

Sources indicate that when Nvidia confirmed Kress’s interim CEO appointment on Nov. 12, she said she was “committed to building on the extraordinary foundation Jensen created over the last 33 years.” At the end of the Nov. 17 earnings call, she added: “Our customers and our partners are with us and employees are committed; we’ve got this.”.

Kress had not publicly expressed interest in pursuing the permanent CEO position. The board had said it would consider internal and external candidates, but Huang’s reinstatement effectively ended that search.

CFOs are increasingly being appointed CEOs, and Kress, who joined Nvidia in 2013, played an extensive role in the manufacturer’s growth, and has vast finance experience at Cisco, Microsoft, and Texas Instruments.

“I’m not sure Kress wants to be CEO, but it is unlikely she will eventually be CEO of Nvidia,” Cole remarked. “Her short stint as interim CEO wasn’t long enough to impress.”.

If Kress decides on her own to leave Nvidia, Cole stated, her next step would most likely be retirement.

“I usually try to steer my students by reminding them that if they take a CFO job, that assumes that they know everything about the finances of the company and how the money gets made,” Harrigan stated.

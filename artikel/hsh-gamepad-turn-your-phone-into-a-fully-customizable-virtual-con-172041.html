v1.0.6 is here, and it's the biggest release since the app launched. This update brings a native Android app (APK), streams your PC audio to your phone, turns your phone into a PC microphone, adds left/right gyroscope steering, and a bunch of quality-of-life fixes that make the whole thing feel like a real product instead of a browser page.

If you're new here: HSH Gamepad is an open-source Windows tool that turns your Android phone into a fully customizable virtual controller — PS4 DualShock 4 or Xbox 360 controller, gyroscope steering wheel, mouse & keyboard, and even a live touch screen for your PC. No app needed on the phone (until now 😄), no cloud, no accounts. (Related: Breaking: My Journey to a serverless transformers pipel...)

This is the headline feature. The whole app previously lived in a mobile browser, which meant a couple of things were awkward: you had to open a URL manually, browser chrome got in the way, and the microphone / gyroscope / vibration APIs needed chrome://flags in Chromium.

According to recent reports, A small quality-of-life detail: pressing Back once closes the at the moment open panel. Press it twice to exit the app.

It has been revealed that 📦 Install note: since the APK isn't from the Play Store, Play Protect may warn you that the app is unknown. Tap Send this time and continue — that's all.

Touch Screen mode now has a speaker button. Stream the audio of whatever is on your PC — including fullscreen games — to your phone.

You also get an output device picker in the Windows app, so you can decide where the sound actually goes: your PC's speakers, the phone, or whatever else you have available.

Your phone's microphone can act as the PC's microphone (player 1 only), which means you can talk in-game — in chat, in voice comms, whatever — from the same device you're using as a controller. (Related: How to train a newly introduced language model from scr...)

There's also an option to keep the virtual microphone always connected, so you don't have to re-arm it every time the app restarts.

In a recent development, from the Windows app you can now open the folder where your layouts and themes live. Copy a layout, rename it, delete it, or send it to a friend — all from your file manager, with no JSON editing required.

This is a small change on paper, but it makes sharing layouts a normal workflow instead of a copy-paste-the-code ritual.

According to recent reports, previously, tilting the phone fed a single axis. Now the phone can steer both the left and the right stick independently: tilt it like a steering wheel and the left stick goes left while the right stick goes right.

The important detail: it works with any controller type, not just the wheel layout.

You also get proper wheel tuning:.

That means a physical wheel can snap to a clean center value instead of drifting around zero.

In a recent development, tap a button three times within one second, then tap the second button — and they swap positions. That's it. No dragging, no JSON, no "why did my layout end up like this".

Touching a button can now vibrate the phone, with adjustable intensity. Off by default, since not everyone wants their phone buzzing during a boss fight.

Every phone in the players list now has its own test button — tap it to see that device's buttons and sticks live.

Recent findings suggest that the touch area bug. The empty corners of the square around the stick, wheel and D-Pad were still capturing touches. That meant buttons sitting next to them — like CAM or L3 — were unreachable, because the invisible square ate the input first.

Industry observers note that now the touch region matches exactly what you see on screen. If it looks like a gap, it's a gap.

Industry observers note that the installer sets up the ViGEmBus driver for you. Windows 7 / 8.1 / 10 / 11 installers are unchanged — same file names as before.

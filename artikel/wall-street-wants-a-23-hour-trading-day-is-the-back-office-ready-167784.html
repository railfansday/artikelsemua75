Global survey finds 40% of firms aim to offer near-23-hour trading by year-end, while 42% have yet to determine how they will cover extended sessions operationally.

According to recent reports, UPCOMING YORK, Nov. 24, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- As U.S. equities move toward a longer trading day, a gap is emerging between the ability to execute an overnight trade and the operations needed to support it. A global industry pulse survey by Broadridge (NYSE: BR), SIFMA and The ValueExchange finds that 40% of firms intend to offer near-23-hour, five-day-a-week trading by the end of 2026, while 42% have not yet determined how they will cover extended sessions operationally. Industry observers note that "An overnight trade does not end when it is executed, firms also need the people, controls and technology to monitor exceptions and support the trade through settlement," stated Quentin Limouzi, Global Head of Post Trade at Broadridge. "Broadridge's systems are operational during extended trading hours. Now is the time for firms to make sure their people, processes and technology are ready to support those trades from execution through settlement. As demand grows, the market won't wait for the back office to catch up.". (Related: Industry News: Welcome spaCy to the Hugging Face Hub...)

"Extended trading hours could give investors more opportunities to respond to events and access U.S. markets," stated Steve Byron, Managing Director and Head of Technology, Operations and BCP at SIFMA. "But a resilient market depends on more than an open trading window. The industry must work together to understand the implications for liquidity, operations and the infrastructure that supports investors around the clock.".

The October 2026 survey of 268 financial institutions points to a central tension: the ability to execute an overnight trade does not necessarily mean the services, controls and operational coverage around it are ready to operate continuously.

Demand is not simply a question of adding more time to the trading day. Respondents identified the ability to trade overnight and to react to geopolitical events as leading objectives. At the same time, only 10% expected liquidity to be adequate for institutional-size execution from day one, suggesting a selective early market rather than an immediate shift of all trading activity.

Firms should assess their readiness across people, processes and technology now, and engage providers to address the operational gaps that extended trading hours may expose.

Industry observers note that bristol Myers Squibb (BMY +0.79%) is a large, well-respected pharmaceutical company. It traces its history back to the mid-1800s. Right now, the company's portfolio of newer drugs, which it calls its "growth portfolio", just generated nearly $7.7 billion in revenue in a single quarter. That's a 12% increase over the same quarter in 2025. But is the stock worth buying? The risk/reward balance looks tilted toward reward for all but the most conservative investors. Here's why. According to recent reports, the success Bristol Myers Squibb is having with its growth portfolio clearly indicates that this pharmaceutical giant hasn't lost its mojo. But investors are still downbeat on the stock (more on this below). So the dividend yield is a lofty 4.3%. The S&P 500 index (^GSPC -3.70%) yields roughly 1.1%, the average healthcare stock yields 1.3%, and the average drug stock yields 1.3%. That above-market and industry yield will likely be very enticing to long-term dividend investors. Meanwhile, the roughly 55% dividend payout ratio leaves ample room for adversity and should mollify all but the most conservative income lovers. (Related: Industry News: My Journey to a serverless transformers ...)

Notably, the dividend has been increased regularly, though not annually, for over three decades. There have been no dividend cuts along the way despite the tech giant working through some difficult periods during that span. That reveals the board of directors is strongly committed to returning value to shareholders through a reliable, slowly growing dividend. Adding to the allure is the fact that the stock's 13x price-to-earnings ratio is notably below the pharma industry average of roughly 28x. The high yield and relatively low P/E ratio will likely be of interest to value investors.

Sources indicate that growth investors will appreciate the above-highlighted success of the firm's growth portfolio. That portfolio is a collection of over a dozen new drugs that, as of the third quarter, account for nearly 60% of the company's drug revenues. That's good news and bad news, however, and it highlights the risk that investors face if they decide to buy Bristol Myers Squibb.

According to recent reports, the healthcare giant's legacy portfolio still accounts for around 40% of revenues, or roughly $5.4 billion. And one drug, Eliquis, is responsible for nearly $4.7 billion of that total. Eliquis is set to see generic competition as soon as 2028. When that happens, the revenue this drug produces will likely start to fall, and perhaps sharply. That is why investors remain downbeat on Bristol Myers Squibb's stock despite the success of its growth portfolio.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

It is reasonable that extremely conservative investors might want to stay on the sidelines until after Eliquis' patent expires. However, it is important to remember that patent expirations are just a normal part of the pharmaceutical business. And given the success of the growth portfolio, Bristol Myers Squibb continues to execute well in new drug development. There's just a mismatch between when patents expire, which happens on a set schedule, and its development efforts, which do not happen on a set schedule.

It has been revealed that in order to buy Bristol Myers Squibb, you need to believe it will get through this period. History suggests it will, and that's backed up by the success of the growth portfolio.

Industry observers note that if you can't handle the uncertainty around the patent expiration of Eliquis, you shouldn't buy Bristol Myers Squibb. However, for most investors, including dividend, value, and perhaps even growth-focused types, the stock is probably worth a deep dive. The market doesn't appear to be giving the organization enough credit for its success on the development front, and that is probably tilting the risk-reward scale toward reward.

It has been revealed that there’s still quite a gender gap in the airline industry, with a disproportionate number of pilots being male, and a disproportionate number of flight attendants being female. Airlines will sometimes have special celebrations with all-female crews, so here’s an interesting twist on that.

Today is (apparently) International Men’s Day. We often see airlines commemorate International Women’s Day, but there aren’t many airlines that celebrate the equivalent for men… well, except Finnair! The airline has today operated its annual flight with an all-male crew. This was the case for Finnair flight AY1331, from Helsinki (HEL) to London (LHR), using an Airbus A350-900. The crew consisted of 10 people, comprised of two pilots and eight flight attendants. The gate agents were also all men. So, why is this key? Finnair highlights how only around 10% of cabin crew at the airline are male, but the manufacturer hopes to see that number grow in the future. I think this is a fun concept, and it would be nice to see a closer to 50/50 gender ratio in both the cockpit and cabin, as it would show that everyone is given a fair chance at such a career.

I think it’s worth acknowledging that at many airlines, hiring of flight attendants is still primarily or exclusively based on gender. Now, the policies do vary significantly by airline, but perhaps an extreme example is India’s largest airline, IndiGo, which only hires female flight attendants. It’s literally written on the application, that it’s a job for “Female Indian nationals.”.

It’s all so strange, because the airline has this super enthusiastic page about the importance of diversity in the workplace, and says “all employment decisions are made solely on the basis of merit and qualifications, without regard to a candidate’s gender…” You mean except the single largest portion of your workforce, which is cabin crew?

What’s interesting is that while those gender restrictions exist in the cabin, roughly 18% of the carrier’s pilots are female, which is way above the global average of 7%. That’s obviously a conscious effort, so I guess the idea is that women can become pilots but men can’t become flight attendants.

In fairness, I suspect part of the motivation here is that IndiGo flight attendants share hotel rooms on layovers, and that is a bit more complicated with mixed genders. Then again, it seems easy enough to just try to pair male flight attendants on trips, so it’s hardly a hurdle that can’t be overcome.

Industry observers note that while we’re used to seeing airlines celebrate International Women’s Day, it’s rare to see an airline celebrating International Men’s Day. Finnair has operated an all-male flight today, intended to highlight how only 10% of flight attendants at the airline are male, and the airline wants to change that. While an unconventional initiative nowadays, I think this is still pretty cool.

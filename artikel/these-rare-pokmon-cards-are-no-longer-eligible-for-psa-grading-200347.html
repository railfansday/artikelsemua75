These Rare Pokémon Cards Are No Longer Eligible for PSA Grading

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that mega Evolution returned to the Pokémon Trading Card Game with the launch of the Mega Evolution series in 2025. In this expansion, collectors can find extremely rare Mega Evolution Pokémon EX cards known as Mega Hyper Rare cards in English and Mega Ultra Rare cards in Japanese. These holographic secret cards show alternative artwork of Pokémon's Mega Evolutions on a beautiful gold-etched display.

Given how rare these cards are, most collectors lucky enough to find them quickly sent them to PSA for grading. Unfortunately, in many cases, this will no longer be possible. On Nov. 25, 2026, the PSA revealed that it has stopped grading English-language Mega Hyper Rare Pokémon cards effective immediately.

Industry observers note that in an official blog post, PSA explains that some English-language Mega Hyper Rare Pokémon cards are defective, and therefore, none can be accurately graded. Card grading experts "identified a manufacturing issue that can cause the gold foil on the front of these cards to deteriorate over time, leaving visible patches or fading." While the severity of the defect varies from card to card, it's impossible to determine whether a card will eventually be affected by it and deteriorate. The issue can arise even if the card is properly stored and encapsulated. (Related: Industry News: huggingface_hub v1.0: Five Years of Buil...)

"A card can begin deteriorating after it’s graded, even with no visible signs at first, so we can’t assign a grade that will reliably hold. Continuing to grade these cards wouldn’t meet the standard of accuracy and consistency that collectors rely on us for.". (Related: Introducing RTEB: A Fresh Standard for Retrieval Evalua...)

Sources indicate that grades already assigned to English-language Mega Hyper Rare cards by PSA will not be revoked and can be submitted for Review and Reholder service, allowing collectors to reevaluate their condition. Cards in transit or submitted for grading will be returned ungraded, and PSA will waive the grading service fee as compensation.

On the bright side, PSA states that Japanese-language Mega Ultra Rare Pokémon cards do not appear to be defective, as they were printed at a facility that uses different techniques, and can be submitted for grading for the time being.

Recent findings suggest that it is at the moment unknown how this will affect card prices. It could go either way. The price of defective cards might rise before the manufacturing error is fixed, or it could fall sharply, as collectors begin their search for a pristine Japanese MUR card or look for an already graded English-language card instead.

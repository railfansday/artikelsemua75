Anthropic falls below its $83 IPO price five days after its record debut

Anthropic shares fell below their $83 IPO price for the first time on Tuesday. The company’s record Nasdaq debut was five days ago. The stock was down 5.4% at $82.78 in early trading, Medha Singh reported for Reuters. It opened at $111.50 on 19 November. (Related: Estate Planning for Early Retirement: The FIRE Guide...)

Sources indicate that later in the morning, the fall had reached 7%, CNBC reported. At Reuters’ price, Anthropic was worth about $1.5tn, roughly 40 times its sales over the past 12 months. On its first day, it touched $2tn. (Related: What Is Considered Upper Middle Class? Income and Net W...)

Recent findings suggest that the stock closed 17% higher on that first day. Since then, a wider tech sell-off has hit the market. It pushed the S&P 500 and the Dow into correction territory, Reuters noted. Anthropic also missed Monday’s AI rally after Nvidia brought Jensen Huang back as chief executive.

“If we do see a slowdown in the data-center buildout, then that obviously can impact the speed at which Anthropic can develop,” Fiona Cincotta of StoneX told Reuters.

In a recent development, reuters pointed to other large tech debuts that lost their early gains. SpaceX’s $85.7bn June listing was the biggest before Anthropic’s. It took about a month to fall below its offer price. Facebook, now Meta, did so on its second trading day in 2012.

“Like most IPOs, it probably came in overpriced and it is now the real price discovery,” Peter Andersen of Andersen Capital Management told Reuters.

Some support could come from index funds. MSCI will add Anthropic to its global standard indexes as of the 30 November close. Index analytics firm Intropic estimates that passive funds could buy up to 30% of the shares offered by the end of the year.

Recent findings suggest that chief executive Dario Amodei and president Daniela Amodei each sold more than 3.37m shares, worth over $280m. They sold them to the IPO’s underwriters a day after the debut, according to a filing late on Monday. The sales were part of the founders’ planned sale, in which all seven co-founders offered 3,378,378 shares each.

Industry observers note that anthropic’s lock-up rules let some employees and insiders sell part of their holdings from the first trading day. Further releases are tied to earnings reports. The underwriters also used their option to buy extra shares. That lifted the offering to $93.5bn when Anthropic closed the offering. Reuters noted that it also removed a possible source of support for the stock.

Recent findings suggest that reuters also listed cheaper open-weight models as a risk, such as those from DeepSeek, Mistral and Moonshot AI. Anthropic has warned that they could pressure its prices. Huang is a strong supporter of open-weight models.

It has been revealed that three brokers started coverage with positive ratings, CNBC reported. Canaccord Genuity rated the stock a buy with a $135 target. Its analysts noted Anthropic is building:.

“One of the most notable enterprise technology franchises of the AI era.”.

Sources indicate that oppenheimer set an outperform rating and a $150 target on Tuesday, citing enterprise AI spending and Anthropic’s compute efficiency. On Monday, Wedbush started at outperform with a $120 target.

“The genie is out of the bottle. And ANT is a strong play on that theme,” the Wedbush analysts wrote.

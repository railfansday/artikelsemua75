By The Rule Breakers Team – Nov 24, 2026 at 6:35AM EST.

Sources indicate that yesterday, CrowdStrike (CRWD -8.92%) agreed to buy Zscaler (ZS +17.67%) in an all-stock deal worth about $43.9 billion. On the same day, it reported the best quarter in its history. Investors focused on the deal, and CrowdStrike stock fell about 9%, while Zscaler jumped more than 17%. This morning, CrowdStrike rose about 1.5% in pre-market trading, even as Nasdaq-100 futures fell about 0.8%.

Team Rule Breakers and Team Hidden Gems have both recommended CrowdStrike for years, and have done well with it.

The first of six Traits of a Rule Breaker Stock is: Top Dog and First-Mover in an Important, Emerging Industry. (Related: Industry News: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

Many of the best stocks of every era lead a fresh industry that matters. Being first and biggest also tends to feed itself, because the leader usually finds it easier to attract top talent and raise money. (Related: Industry News: Welcome spaCy to the Hugging Face Hub...)

CrowdStrike built its lead protecting computers and cloud systems. Its latest quarter showed that lead is still growing. Revenue rose 28% to $1.6 billion, and the company turned a small profit under standard accounting rules. Annual recurring revenue grew 27% to $6.26 billion, the fifth straight quarter of faster growth. Newly introduced recurring revenue jumped 58% to a record $419 million, and management raised its full-year target for that measure.

Sources indicate that members who followed our recommendations have benefited. Rule Breakers recommended CrowdStrike on Aug. 26 and Oct. 14, 2021, and again on Feb. 7, 2023, at split-adjusted prices of $69.69, $69.00, and $28.65. The 2021 picks have roughly tripled, and the 2023 pick has grown more than sevenfold. In Hidden Gems, the team's three picks from 2021 and 2022 have each more than tripled, and the best has grown about sixfold. All six are well ahead of the S&P 500.

Zscaler was an early leader in "zero trust" network security, which checks every connection before letting it through. George Kurtz argues that AI agents now move across networks constantly, so CrowdStrike needs that view too. About 8,000 Zscaler customers don't use CrowdStrike yet, and fewer than 10% of CrowdStrike's customers use Zscaler.

Together, they would start with more than $10 billion in annual recurring revenue. As of Friday's close, their combined market value was more than $265 billion, close to Palo Alto Networks' (PANW +2.94%) $282 billion.

In a recent development, paying in stock means CrowdStrike will matter new shares, roughly 19% more by our math at Friday's prices. Kurtz says the deal will add to free cash flow per share right after it closes, but that won't happen until the second half of fiscal 2028. Merging two large platforms is hard, and some customers may hesitate to buy so much from one vendor.

In a recent development, valuation can be another concern. In August, Team Hidden Gems trimmed CrowdStrike across more than 15 services, citing the stock's rich price and how large the position had grown. In Hidden Gems itself, the team sold its November 2024 recommendation, locking in a total return of about 176%. It kept its other stakes and its long-term conviction. Team Rule Breakers didn't trim.

Sources indicate that the Motley Fool has long suggested holding stocks for at least three to five years. Our 2021 picks have just passed five years, and they're still compounding. If the Zscaler deal works, CrowdStrike could lead a bigger and more important industry, security for the AI era.

Investors who share that view may see yesterday's drop as a chance to add, though watching how the integration goes is a perfectly reasonable choice too.

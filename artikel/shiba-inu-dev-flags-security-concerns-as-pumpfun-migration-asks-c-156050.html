Long-time Shiba Inu community member and SHIB developer, Mazrael, has reacted to Solana-based memecoin platform Pump.fun's recent wallet login changes, which are sparking concerns about private key security. (Related: Tech Report: Developer builds a ‘Discord kitten’ that’s...)

In a new announcement, Pump.fun stated that external wallet sign-in is being retired on November 30, 2026. Starting November 30, 2026 (15:00 UTC), Pump.fun on the web will only allow users to sign in with an email account or a Google, Apple, or GitHub account instead of an external wallet such as Phantom, Solflare, or Ledger. (Related: Build A Racetrack codes (November 2026)...)

In a recent development, pump.fun reassures users that nothing will happen to their funds or account, as their wallet address, positions, callouts, followers and username remain unchanged. The only change is how users sign in.

Owners who sign in to their accounts with Phantom or any other wallet are urged to take action before the November 30, 2026 deadline.

According to recent reports, pump.fun outlined a number of steps for owners on how to switch their sign-in; of particular concern is the step that asked users to enter their wallet details and paste their private key when prompted. Pump.fun noted that this step encrypts the key in the user's browser and that the key is end-to-end encrypted by its login provider, Privy, allowing users' wallets to continue working for everything else.

Mazrael reacted to the development, drawing attention to the potential security implications for users.

pumpfun is retiring external wallet logins on web starting Nov 30. From then on you sign in with Google, Apple, email or GitHub. To keep your existing account..... they want you to paste your private key into their Privy flow 😬 Also note their own terms says they won’t ask for private keys...

The standard security practice across the cryptocurrency industry is that exchanges, wallet providers and other platforms do not ask users to disclose their private keys or recovery phrases. Such warnings are routinely issued to protect users from unauthorized access to their funds.

The Pump.fun wallet login change process appears to contradict this widely promoted security principle, Mazrael noted.

Private keys are highly sensitive credentials that give holders direct access to their cryptocurrency wallets. Whoever gets their hands on them might be able to access those wallets and move the funds. In general, it is recommended never to share private keys with individuals, support agents or third-party services.

I rather like these extended post-election activities. Every few days, another seat in Congress flips from R to D, and all the joy and wonder comes flooding back again. Up in Alaska, Democratic candidate Mary Peltola was (finally) declared the winner of the Senate race. She also managed to clear the 50 percent mark, which means that the race didn’t have to go into the land of ranked-choice voting, which would have resulted in Republicans birthing bovines all over Alaska and the Lower 48.

For the record, Peltola’s total votes exceeded those of both Dan Sullivans combined. Whoever deployed the JFK strategy failed against the power of fish, family, and freedom. And, truth be told, Peltola, thus far, has been a committed Blue Dog. In her one term in the House, she was one of the strongest Democratic supporters of some conservative immigration policies. She also lined up against Washington, D.C.’s independence in sentencing criminals. But, major tent and all of that.

It has been revealed that meanwhile, in the national capital, Representative Hakeem Jeffries easily won the endorsement of the Democratic caucus to be the next Speaker. Recent findings suggest that the question remains open as to whether Jeffries—and, to a more critical extent, Senator Chuck Schumer—recognize the power they’ve been given by the midterm elections, a necessary precondition to putting that power to actual use. The iron will never be hotter.

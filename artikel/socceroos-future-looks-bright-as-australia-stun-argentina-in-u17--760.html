Sydney FC prodigy Akol Akon has demonstrated why he’s one of the most exciting talents in Australian football, leading the Joeys to a shock 2-0 victory over Argentina to launch their U17 World Cup campaign in style. (Related: Course Launch Community Event...)

The brilliant young winger had his shot deflected for an own goal on the hour and then produced an audaciously cheeky finish 11 minutes later to seal the opening-day win on Friday (AEDT) as Carl Veart’s side unveiled their return to the global boys’ competition for the first time in seven years.

Industry observers note that against one of the powerhouse football nations, Australia had their backs to the wall, having to make 31 clearances as Argentina enjoyed two-thirds of the possession and 22 shots on goal. But the Joeys fought back after the break, spearheaded by Akon, who hailed their second-half heist as “amazing”. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

The 17-year-old livewire provided the real star quality, and it was soon evident why the Sky Blues had wanted to keep him in Australia for their A-League Men campaign instead of releasing him for the tournament; a decision that triggered widespread backlash before being reversed.

Akon caused havoc from the start down the right flank, his neat through ball in the fifth minute almost resulting in a goal for Central Coast Mariners’ Luke Becvinovski.

The teenage sensation then struck after the break following another dangerous foray down the right that ended with his shot across goal deflecting off the hapless Alvaro Guich and into the net.

In the 71st minute, Mariners midfielder Oli O’Carroll expertly picked off an Argentina attack before Brisbane’s Emmett Shaw released Akon with a slide-rule left-foot pass, the winger going on to dummy the goalkeeper before slotting into an empty net.

It only got worse for Argentina when substitute Benjamin Vallejos got sent off for a reckless challenge in stoppage time.

In a recent development, but it wasn’t all about Akon, a considerable talent who’s already made his national U23 bow. At the other end, Charlie Wilson-Papps, the young goalkeeper who’s on the books at Brighton and Hove Albion, made half-a-dozen fine saves and kept a clean sheet.

“I do it for my teammates, I do it for my family at home, so to make them proud is is the most important thing,” said Akon as he clutched the player-of-the-match trophy afterwards.

According to recent reports, akon’s only worry came with the video checking of his goal. “Ah, that was nerve-racking, man,” he mentioned. “Because at 1-0, it was a very scary game. So to be able to seal it like that, the coach was telling me it was a goal.

“The first goal? It wasn’t really my goal, but I just wanted to do the Tim Cahill, start off the tournament like that. So, yeah, the knee side had to come with it.

“Now, the group, we’re really confident, really confident in our ability, and I think that highlighted today.”.

Next up on Sunday for the Joeys will be Denmark, who lost 4-0 to Mozambique in their Group C opener on Thursday.

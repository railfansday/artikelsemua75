There are always going to be a massive number of deaths in any Hunger Games movie, as that is simply the nature of the franchise. However, the newest installment, The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping, actually has significantly more deaths than the prior Hunger Games movies (except The Mockinjay - Part 2 when there is an all-out war, but those many deaths are largely not depicted on screen) because this Hunger Games is the Second Quarter Quell, in which 48 told tributes were reaped. Given that, there were always going to be, at minimum, 47 deaths in this Hunger Games.

Adding to that number, though, were two deaths that take place before the Games even begin and several that take place afterward, all having to do with punishing Haymich Abernathy for his rebellious behavior in the Games. However, unlike in the book, most of the tributes go unnamed and/or get very little screen time. By the first fight's conclusion in the arena, in fact, 20 are already dead. With so many deaths, many of which occur off screen and are never extrapolated upon, there are really just 12 key deaths that happen in the movie. Here's how each of those major characters die before the end of Sunrise on the Reaping.

Recent findings suggest that woodbine Chance is the first death in Sunrise on the Reaping, and he is actually why Haymitch ends up in the Hunger Games. Woodbine is the final name called during District 12's Reaping, but he decides to run off rather than be taken to the Capitol for the Games. As a result, he is immediately shot, leading his mother to run to him. In a devastating scene, Lenore Dove tries to interfere on the mother's behalf, and when Peacekeepers go to attack her, Haymitch steps in. Because of this, he is made Woodbine's replacement in the Hunger Games. (Related: Industry News: Community Evals: Because we're done trus...)

According to recent reports, louella, or Haymitch's "sweetheart," as she's also called, has a tragic death, despite her never entering the arena. Specifically, just as in the book, in the movie, Louella dies as a result of an accident during the chariot ride, wherein the horses become scarred from objects being tossed onto the track and ultimately run off, crashing into other chariots. In the book, Haymitch is described as being drenched in her blood, but the movie is not nearly as graphic. Still, her death leads to Haymitch doing his clap in front of President Snow—his first open sign of defiance that seals his fate. (Related: Trump calls Putin and Xi friends. According to recent reports, the second District 12 tribute to die is Wyatt Callow, the boy whose family has made enemies in the district because they take bets on the Hunger Games. However, he eventually wins over his fellow District 12 tributes, and it's gutting that he dies, especially because it happens so early in the Games. In the book, Haymitch doesn't see this transpire, but he later learns that Wyatt died trying to protect Lou Lou. In the movie, audiences actually see Silka kill Wyatt as he runs to defend Lou Lou (who does get away, although her death soon follows).

It was always clear that Lou Lou was going to die in the arena, but the remaining District 12 tributes at least wanted to keep her as safe as they could and, ideally, prevent a death that was too painful. Ultimately, Lou Lou dies because she breathes in poisonous pollen from the plants in the arena. There is a slight change between how this is done in the movie and how this is done in the book, though. In the book, Lou Lou has a pump attached to her that sends drugs into her as the Capitol sees fit. Haymitch uses this to mercy kill her in the book. In the movie, he instead watches as she dies.

In a recent development, ampert's death is absolutely devastating, especially because it's established that he was put in the arena (and marked for death) in order to punish his father, Beetee. Beetee even tells Haymitch earlier in the movie that he knows Ampert is doomed to die, and he simply asks that Haymitch not allow him to suffer. Unfortunately, try as Haymitch might, Ampert does suffer before his crushing death, as he's swarmed by ravenous squirrels who essentially eat him. In the book, he's described as being only a skeleton, whereas in the movie, he's gravely injured by the squirrels but he does not lose his skin.

Industry observers note that panache long seemed like the greatest threat in the arena, and he and Silka no doubt were, but Panache is taken down before multiple other characters. After an altercation between Haymitch and two other tributes, during which Haymitch actually takes them both out, Panache moves to kill Haymitch. Thankfully, Maysilee is nearby and uses poisoned darts to shoot Panache from afar, leading to his death. In the book, it's revealed that Maysilee actually fashioned the poisoned darts herself, using poisoned berries she found in the arena, although in the movie, that detail is left out.

Easily one of the saddest deaths in Sunrise on the Reaping is that of Maysilee Donner, who becomes a sister to Haymitch throughout their time in the arena. In the book, Maysilee's death comes about in large part due to her decision to kill Gamemakers who enter the arena during the Hunger Games. However, the movie doesn't show that scene, instead positioning her death as something that happens to punish Haymitch. In either case, though, Maysilee dies after a pack of vicious birds swarm, ultimately pecking her to death as Haymitch helplessly looks on.

Industry observers note that in both the book and the movie, Wellie is one of the Newcomers, making her an ally to Haymitch Abernathy. In fact, in the book, Haymitch even actively hopes that she is going to be the victor after Maysilee dies. Sadly, that isn't ultimately Wellie's fate. Instead, she is killed by Silka as one of the final deaths in the arena. In the book, this is actually all the more horrifying, as Silka actually decapitates Wellie and then holds up her head. In the movie, Silka is still the one to kill Wellie, but she does so off-screen, using Wellie's bell to lure Haymitch to where they are.

Silka is one of the other ruthless, incredibly dangerous careers (although, notably, her story is one of the many changes in Sunrise on the Reaping, as she's actually shown crying in the book, but that was cut out of the movie), and she becomes the final person standing other than Haymitch. The two have one final fight, in which it seems like Silka is going to win—at least, it would, if audiences didn't already know that Haymitch is the victor—as she cuts a deep gash in Haymitch's stomach. However, when she throws her axe at Haymitch, he is able to use the force field to his advantage, sending the axe flying back at her, killing her.

According to recent reports, although Sunrise on the Reaping contains many heartbreaking deaths, there are perhaps few, if any, more gutting than the deaths of Ma and Sid because it is just outright cruelty, hatred, and proof that President Snow will do whatever he wants that lead to their deaths. Specifically, after surviving the arena, Haymitch finally makes his way home to District 12, only to discover his home engulfed in flames. He tries desperately to run to Sid and his Ma, but he's held back and informed that it's too late. In the end, Sid and Ma are buried in one casket, as they were found holding onto each other.

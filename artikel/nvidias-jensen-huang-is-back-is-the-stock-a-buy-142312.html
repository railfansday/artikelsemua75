Many Nvidia (NVDA +31.21%) watchers and investors just got exactly what they wanted: The artificial intelligence (AI) chip giant reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive officer, president, and member of the board. Huang had resigned less than two weeks ago following allegations of two affairs -- one with a person U.S. intelligence called a spy and another with an Nvidia executive.

According to recent reports, an outpouring of support followed, including a petition to bring Huang back. Nvidia's stock had lost 24% since his resignation, extending an October decline. AI stocks in general have come under pressure periodically this year amid concerns about the depth of spending on infrastructure -- and whether it's sustainable. But the biggest drag on Nvidia stock was clearly Huang's departure.

Industry observers note that with news of his return, the stock soared 30% within the first half hour of trading. Is this hot AI stock, which still faces certain headwinds, a buy? Let's find out. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

Jensen Huang. Image source: Nvidia.

First, a summary of the news that led to Huang's resignation -- and his surprise return. Nvidia's board on Nov. 8 confirmed an investigation into reports that a foreign intelligence effort had targeted Huang. This followed allegations that Huang had an affair with Alice Huang (not a relative), whom U.S. intelligence called a spy. Huang, in an email to staff, apologized for being unfaithful to his wife and expressed his commitment to Nvidia and his support for the investigation.

Days later, The Newly introduced York Times reported that Huang had a second affair, with an Nvidia employee, and Huang resigned soon afterward. The board named finance chief Colette Kress as interim CEO and started a search for a permanent leader. (Related: Latest Update: How to train a newly introduced language...)

Recent findings suggest that investors worried about Nvidia's future without the leadership of Huang, seen as a visionary who has driven the company's successes and become almost the face of AI around the globe. Though Nvidia has a multi-year roadmap and contracts in place through the end of the decade, investors still questioned whether the company truly could excel without Huang at the helm. An open letter to bring Huang back garnered more than 200,000 signatures, including those of leading tech CEOs such as Elon Musk of Tesla and Space Exploration Technologies and Shopify's Tobias Lütke.

Nvidia today confirmed the return of Huang "effective immediately." This followed a series of interviews with him, "after which the board was satisfied with respect to Mr. Huang's candor and ability to lead Nvidia." Nvidia said the decision also came after receiving "input from senior levels of the United States government." Based on this, the board considers that Huang's return doesn't represent a national security risk.

So, is Nvidia a buy now that Huang is back? The board's investigation -- carried out by an independent committee -- into Huang's interactions with Alice Huang will continue until conclusions are reached. This remains an element of uncertainty.

However, the board's move to reinstate Huang, partially based on some input from the government, is encouraging.

As for the company's earnings picture, it remains bright, with revenue and profit soaring in the third quarter. The release of the Vera Rubin platform has been smooth, and the company predicts 75% revenue growth for the next fiscal year based on strong visibility. Now that Huang is back, concerns about the future strategy have lifted.

Meanwhile, the stock, even after today's explosive gains, is dirt cheap at only 17x forward earnings estimates.

Of course, after the recent allegations involving Alice Huang, it's possible that Nvidia will come under greater government scrutiny, or that sales of chips to China may face further regulation. This could lead to some volatility or even downward pressure. Even with these challenges, though, the company's AI leadership and innovation, as well as its solid earnings track record, should make it an ongoing winner in the AI revolution. That's why, with Jensen Huang back in the driver's seat and these valuation levels, the stock is a buy -- for investors who can handle some risk and volatility.

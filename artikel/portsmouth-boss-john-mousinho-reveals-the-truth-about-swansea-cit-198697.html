Portsmouth boss John Mousinho has shot down the rumours linking him with the vacant Swansea City job.

The Swans have been looking for a fresh head coach for a week now, after Vitor Matos left the club in favour of a move to Ligue 1 outfit Strasbourg, and Mousinho opened up as one of the favourites to succeed him. (Related: Introducing RTEB: A Fresh Standard for Retrieval Evalua...)

Pompey have gone from strength to strength under the 40-year-old, earning promotion to the Championship and then battling bravely against the drop in their first couple of seasons back at the level.

This year, Mousinho has taken them to new heights, with the South Coast side one of the more organised clubs in the division defensively, and that identity has led them to the top-10 as we enter December. Indeed, they're just one point behind the Swans, who occupy the final play-off spot.

It's been an exciting project at Fratton Park over the past few years, and one that Mousinho isn't ready to give up just yet. (Related: Industry News: huggingface_hub v1.0: Five Years of Buil...)

Mousinho has overseen plenty of famous days at Fratton Park in the nearly four years he's been in charge at the club, one of which came on Tuesday night when Pompey beat West Ham United 1-0.

That further reaffirmed that this season should be one where they're looking up at possibly gatecrashing a play-off party, rather than worrying about dropping back into League One, but losing their head coach would bring about some concern at Fratton Park.

Luckily enough, speaking to The News, Mousinho has removed any doubt that he'd be leaving for South Wales.

Sources indicate that "It’s nothing to do with me!" he said when asked about being one of the favourites to succeed Matos. "There’s been absolutely no contact between myself and Swansea, between my agent and Swansea — there’s nothing in it whatsoever.".

It has been revealed that "There’s nothing in it, honestly, there’s nothing. You may sometimes think my answers are clever and trying to avoid it (the question). But there’s nothing there, and there will be nothing which comes out of that.".

This isn't the first time that Mousinho has been linked with another Championship club in the past six months, as Bristol City were legitimately interested in bringing him in over the summer, before pivoting to Michael Skubala.

There was a point where it may have been believed that Portsmouth had gone as far as they could have gone after finishing on 54 and 55 points in their two seasons back at Championship level, and the worries of Mousinho leaving for a more attractive project were real.

But the 40-year-old's call for owner Michael Eisner to invest more in the club both on and off the pitch back in late April to become a more competitve second-tier club was answered, and the results in the first 17 games of this season have shown that Mousinho isn't just a head coach who specialises in keeping teams up.

Now, Mousinho's name is being thrown around clubs with more lucrative finances and legitimate promotion credentials, which is a testament to the work he's doing to get the former Premier League side closer to their past glory.

The Pompey boss can see the potential in the South Coast side and is, at the moment at least, keen to see it through.

Swansea were already sinking a little bit in the final weeks of the Matos reign, losing four of their final five games under him and relinquishing top spot in the process.

With just one point in two games under interim boss Ashley Williams, albeit against difficult opposition in West Brom and Middlesbrough, that's now just four points picked up from the last 21 available.

Next up for the Swans is the biggest game in their calendar, as they welcome bitter rivals Cardiff City for the first South Wales derby in over a year, with the Bluebirds spending last season in League One.

Brian Barry-Murphy's side were well beaten by Preston North End on Tuesday, so Swansea will be looking to take advantage of a side perhaps low on confidence, but they'll likely be doing so with Ashley Williams still in the dugout.

Rosenborg boss Freyr Alexandersson has emerged as a possible Matos replacement, but only time will tell if he, or someone else, is in situ by Sunday, as the Swans look to get back to winning ways in the South Wales derby.

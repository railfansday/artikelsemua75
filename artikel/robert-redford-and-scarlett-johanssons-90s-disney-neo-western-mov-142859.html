Robert Redford And Scarlett Johansson's '90s Disney Neo-Western Movie Is Worth Revisiting

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that nicholas Evans' "The Horse Whisperer" was a publishing phenomenon in 1995. The novel is about a young girl who's involved in a horseback riding accident that kills her best friend and their horse, leaving her and her own horse seriously injured. When she falls into a deep funk, her mother whisks her off to rural Montana. There, they try to heal her horse emotionally via a horse whisperer. And while she is married, the mother finds herself falling in love with the handsome, deeply sensitive animal shrink, something which, according to many critics, transformed the narrative into a shamelessly sentimental romance à la "The Bridges of Madison County.".

In a recent development, the novel sold millions of copies, which meant a film adaptation was inevitable. Like "The Bridges of Madison County," the pedigree didn't make it immediately attractive to A-list directors. But Robert Redford saw something more in the material and took it on as his first directorial effort since his brilliant 1994 drama "Quiz Show." For the first time in his filmmaking career, Redford took the lead in his own movie. He also assembled a sensationally talented cast that included Kristin Scott Thomas, Dianne Wiest, Sam Neill, Chris Cooper, and Cherry Jones. For the role of the young girl, he and the casting team of Ellen Chenoweth and Gretchen Rennell went with up-and-comer Scarlett Johansson, who gave a moving performance that wound up being her breakthrough role.

Recent findings suggest that "The Horse Whisperer" isn't quite on the level of the movie version of "The Bridges of Madison County" (a lovely film that Clint Eastwood saved from production limbo), but the screenplay credited to Eric Roth and Richard LaGravenese is nuanced in a way that maybe the novel isn't. And though it's not one of Redford's best movies, it's a strong piece of work that deserves more attention. (Related: Understanding BigBird's Block Sparse Attention...)

It has been revealed that "The Horse Whisperer" holds a 72% Fresh critical approval rating on Rotten Tomatoes and grossed $187 million at the worldwide box office upon hitting theaters. That was a decent result for Touchstone Pictures, but it didn't come close to matching the runaway success of its source material.

Robert Redford rarely failed to strike up chemistry with his leading ladies ("The Great Gatsby" being a notable exception), so it's hardly a shocker that he's perfectly paired with Kristin Scott Thomas. This is not, however, a heated attraction, as Redford's character is still mourning the end of a previous romance where the woman chose the city over him and the country. Thomas is from the city, too (and, again, married), so he's worried that another heartbreak is around the corner.

Industry observers note that as for Scarlett Johansson, she captures the grief of a young woman whose life has been irreparably changed. Her leg was partially amputated, which she fears will make her undesirable to romantic partners as she gets older. This gives rise to the most overpoweringly emotional scene in the movie when, after a row with her mother, Johansson's character tearfully asks, "Who's going to want me?" Industry observers note that "The Horse Whisperer" is available to rent via streaming, but the film is badly in need of a digital restoration. Hopefully, Disney will commission one at some point, although I'm not sure if the movie is a popular enough library title to merit such treatment.

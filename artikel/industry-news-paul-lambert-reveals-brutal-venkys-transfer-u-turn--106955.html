The former Blackburn Rovers manager Paul Lambert has explained the circumstances which surrounded his departure from the club at the end of the 2015-16 season, and it doesn't shine a positive light on the owners.

The ownership of Blackburn Rovers by the Indian chicken magnates Venky's has seldom been uncontroversial, but an interview with one of the club's former managers has now shone light on the circumstances surrounding his departure from Ewood Park, and even though this happened more than a decade ago, it doesn't paint a particularly flattering picture of the owners. In a recent development, paul Lambert joined Blackburn as a replacement for Gary Bowyer following Bowyer's dismissal in November 2015, which came with the team in 15th place in the Championship table. But Lambert didn't last very long in the position, leaving the club at the end of the season after activating a release clause in his contract. (Related: Build A Racetrack codes (November 2026)...)

Little more information over what had actually happened was revealed at the time, but the interview fills in the gaps and reveals that it was the result of a dispute over the sale of striker Jordan Rhodes that resulted in the manager leaving after spending just eight months at Ewood Park.

As reported by the Blackburn Telegraph, the former Blackburn Rovers manager has confirmed that he left Rovers at the end of the 2015-16 season following a dispute over what happened to the proceeds of the sale of striker Jordan Rhodes.

Lambert explained: "We thought we were going to get a bit of a war chest to crack on for the following season, because the remit was to keep Blackburn in the league and then rebuild. We went in there and were doing pretty well, and then around November time, I got told we had to sell Jordan Rhodes. I thought, ‘Well, he is our best striker, he scores the goals for us.’".

The manager confirmed that he asked about what would happen with the proceeds of this sale, and was told that it would be spent on squad-building: "So I stated, ‘Where does the money go?’ And they went, ‘Well, it will come back to you.’ It never came back.".

Lambert didn't get the answer that he was expecting from the club about the funds from the sale when it completed, either: "Around February I went to see one of the guys and stated, ‘Listen, I am going to leave now because this isn't going to work.’".

Lambert mentioned that he was talked around to staying until the end of the season by this member of the club's senior management: "He said, ‘Please, Paul, stay until the end of the season.’ I thought we'd have got money to kick on, but there was never any money coming.".

The picture that he painted of the club at that time wasn't flattering in the slightest: "I didn't want to put myself through that, and I wasn't enjoying it anyway. There was no enjoyment in it. Things were not getting done, there was no investment coming in, players were getting sold, and there was so much going on behind the scenes that I thought I may as well leave.".

The position regarding Jordan Rhodes by the time of the January transfer window was complex. The striker had been a record club signing when he arrived from Huddersfield Town in August 2012, costing them £8 million. But by the summer of 2015, Rhodes was a wanted man, and Middlesbrough made a bid for him described as being "in excess of £10 million", which left the striker unhappy.

Speaking to ESPN after the rejection of the offer, Rhodes remarked: "When I left Huddersfield I felt the timing was right in the next stage of my career and, having seen Middlesbrough's performances last season in reaching the playoff final and their ambition this summer in terms of acquisitions, I would have liked to have been able to talk to them and I am unhappy at not being given that opportunity.".

By the time that Lambert arrived at Ewood Park, then, the fact that Rhodes was coveted by other clubs and wanted to leave Ewood Park was common knowledge. But the fact that his eventual sale to Middlesbrough wasn't tied up until the very, very end of the following January's transfer window meant that there was no time to bring in a straight swap replacement. Rovers had quite a busy January transfer window, but they certainly didn't spend the millions that Lambert believed he was told would be made available.

In a recent development, blackburn Rovers were in 15th place in the Championship at the point of Paul Lambert's appointment, and they finished the season in exactly the same position in the table. He was replaced in the summer by Owen Coyle, but he only lasted until February 2017, by which time Rovers were in serious trouble near the bottom of the table.

It has been revealed that tony Mowbray was appointed as his replacement, but the club were still relegated at the end of the 2016-17 season. Whether they were better off with or without Paul Lambert is something of a moot point, but Blackburn certainly paid a price for losing the goals of Jordan Rhodes.

Thanks for all the responses, really appreciate it.I’m 62 years now, I have done savings and investments in total stocks market index fund and international index funds for 25 years . I am not sure how to do tax loss harvesting, which funds to sell first before I call it quits at age 67, should I withdraw tax sheltered first or taxable. Make a trust and how to transfer funds to the kids, without estate taxes.How to combine the withdrawals to complement the social security.Your questions are a bit complex and seem more related to taxation that what to invest in. Many "wealth managers" or "Financial Advisors" don't give great advice about taxes and typically give you general advice and say they aren't tax professionals. The correct tax answers depend a lot on your specific investments and income sources. To give good advice they should look at your previous tax returns and ask a lot of questions. There are planners that do focus on taxes and would be savvy enough to move you to a different index fund if the ones you have chosen are not tax efficient or are in a non optimal place (e.g. stocks and maybe municipal bonds should be in taxable, bonds and cash in IRA, most aggressive investments in Roth). There are a lot of circular issues with taxes when you hit certain thresholds so it can be difficult to plan. Managing your income to get ACA healthcare subsidies is another factor to think about if you retire before Medicare age and don't have a retiree medical plan.This forum also gives good tax and income management advice and you can certainly learn here how to optimally place your assets, how to manage your income sources to minimize tax and not miss out on the 0% brackets, and how to tax loss harvest. You probably don't need a trust unless you will end up with over $15M of assets or you have an estate tax in your state with a much lower threshold. Trusts can be a hindrance and need to be updated periodically as laws and circumstances change. That costs you money each time...Thanks for the wonderful advice.

In a recent development, nvidia closed Friday at $125.53, down 47.5% from its October high, which works out to roughly $2.7 trillion of market value gone in seven weeks. The tech giant beat earnings last week and guided revenue higher, and the stock still fell 23% across the five sessions.

The Nasdaq dropped 10% on the week, its worst since 2020, erasing its entire 2026 gain and shedding about $8.7 trillion since October 6. The S&P and Dow both closed in correction, AMD lost 20.5%, and CoreWeave now trades below its IPO price. Bank of America’s fund manager survey showed investors cutting tech exposure by the most since 2006, with 52% expecting at least one major hyperscaler to announce a capex cut inside six months.

Polymarket traders put 54% odds that the AI bubble will burst in 2026.

It has been revealed that crypto rode the same elevator down. Bitcoin hit roughly $55,100 Friday night, its lowest level since 2024 and marking a latest cycle low, 56% off last October’s peak. Fear and Greed printed 9 and ETFs bled $1.79 billion on the week. It’s now tracking toward two consecutive down years, which has never happened before.

Industry observers note that then came the weekend bounce. Bitcoin reclaimed $60,000 and sits over $61,000 this morning; ETH is back above $1,710, and even Anthropic, down 9% Friday, quotes near $90 on Hyperliquid’s perp, still above its $83 offer. Two explanations landed over the weekend.

According to recent reports, U.S. President Donald Trump posted Sunday that the market is down $6 trillion since the Democrats’ “rather strange” win on November 3, that the Nasdaq is down 20%, and that it’s “100% due to” Democratic policy. Looks like no Trump bailout is coming anytime soon.

Elon Musk’s version is more useful. He warned Saturday that the cash shortage next year is “going to be nuts,” because even a few months of lag between paying for compute and earning revenue off it means hundreds of billions in incremental working capital. Then the question: where will that come from?

Industry observers note that that’s a funding problem rather than a valuation one, and it lands harder coming from him. SpaceX reportedly sold $32 billion of bonds last week to buy Nvidia chips, so he’s describing a squeeze he’s already borrowing into.

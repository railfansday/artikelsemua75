UBS's crisis planning has won qualified support from market regulators, which called its plan "executable" in principle. But Switzerland's biggest bank still faces a major fight over sweeping intends that would force it to hold more capital against its foreign subsidiaries.

FINMA, the Swiss markets regulator, remarked Wednesday that UBS's resolvability — or its ability to fail safely, without wreaking havoc on the economy — has improved since it integrated rival Credit Suisse, with progress made in implementing the emergency plan across group-wide resolution planning.

It has been revealed that the preferred resolution strategy — involving prepares to allow for recapitalization and restructuring through the use of loss-absorbing bail-in bonds — is "executable," FINMA said. The regulator formally approved UBS's recovery blueprint for the first time since its ​Credit Suisse integration.

How UBS would withstand a future crisis remains in sharp focus among Swiss authorities ever since the lender's emergency takeover of stricken rival Credit Suisse in 2023.

UBS is now the country's only global systemically crucial bank, and the Swiss government wants it to hold stronger capital cushions, specifically in relation to its foreign subsidiaries, which would help it withstand another banking collapse.

UBS has pushed back against the overhaul, saying the measures are disproportionate and would damage its international competitiveness, forcing it to hold an extra $16 billion in capital.

A parliamentary showdown now looms, with the National Council, Switzerland's lower house, scheduled to debate the proposal Dec. 16.

It has been revealed that on Tuesday, the lower house's economics committee backed intends to force the bank to support its foreign subsidiaries with 90% high-quality core Common Equity Tier 1, or CET1, capital. The Council of States, the country's upper house, approved the 90% proposal in September.

According to recent reports, the Swiss government had earlier proposed a 100% threshold. UBS wants it to be no more than 50%.

Recent findings suggest that UBS CEO Sergio Ermotti told CNBC in an interview Oct. 28 that 90% would not allow the company to be a "competitive player" long-term. But he would not be drawn on speculation that the measures could push the bank to relocate out of Switzerland.

In a statement, the bank stated the economics committee's recommendation would herald further excessive tightening of Swiss capital requirements, "which are already among the most stringent globally.".

FINMA stated in its Nov. 25 update that the goal is to avoid government solvency support and emergency law.

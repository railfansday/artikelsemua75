Breaking: Audi Files Patent Application for Physical Buttons That Fold Out From Behind a Touchscreen

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Physical buttons have a lot going for them. They’re easy to find by feel, satisfying to use, and generally pretty good at staying wherever the automaker installed them. Well, it seems Audi has an idea that would change that last part: it involves hiding the buttons behind a touchscreen, then deploying them when needed.

As CarMoses first discovered, Audi has filed for a patent application that describes a motorized switch bar, which pivots out from behind the display’s lower edge. The screen stays fixed, while the physical controls swing into position beneath it. Interestingly, when stowed, the bar apparently has an on-screen counterpart with the same functions lined up in the same order, so those who don't want physical buttons can avoid them entirely. (Related: Show, Don't Certify: How to Prepare for Your First Data...)

For those who do want the physical buttons, there are plenty of reasons to get excited. First, Audi states in its patent documents with the German government that the switches can deploy at startup, via voice controls, or via gestures. Moving the controls from the screen to the physical switches also provides more total screen room for consumers. (In one example, the physical bar deploys when a movie opens, leaving more room for the video.). (Related: Grandma who accidentally invited a stranger to Thanksgi...)

Ultimately, this could be a great way to meet the demands of more people. Designers love a clean dashboard without physical buttons and switches. Plenty of drivers really love having that switchgear. This system allows owners to pick whatever they want on the fly.

Recent findings suggest that the timing makes plenty of sense too. Audi has openly mentioned that it views moving toward more mechanical controls as the right direction. Of course, adding extra complexity to the once-simple idea of buttons also can increase the cost down the line when it comes time for repairs, but even so, this idea is certainly a fascinating solution to a very modern problem.

A newly introduced report indicates that the number of female victims of violence rose in 2025, with minors also "significantly affected.". (Related: How to train a newly introduced language model from scr...)

The Federal Criminal Police Office's (BKA) new report, "Gender-Specific Crimes Against Women," published on Tuesday, indicated increases in all categories of crimes against women last year.

According to the figures, 54,675 women and girls were registered as victims of sexual offenses in 2025, 2.3% more than the year before. Nearly half of the victims were children.

In 2025, 292 people were killed during instances of domestic violence, including 209 women and girls. (Related: Industry News: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

Of that number, 130 were killed by a partner or ex-partner.

The number of female victims of digital violence rose by 15.7% to about 31,200, while the number of female victims of human trafficking increased by 17.7% to around 700.

The figures include only those cases known to the police.

"The statistics in the situation reports alarmingly demonstrate how commonplace violence against women is in Germany," stated Minister for Women Karin Prien.

Sources indicate that "Behind every statistic lies immeasurable suffering for those affected. This cannot continue," Prien remarked.

Seven former Volkswagen executives and employees are in court today in a fraud trial relating to the emissions scandal known as "dieselgate.".

The case before the Braunschweig Regional Court comes more than 11 years after the emissions scandal came to light and is the fifth and final of the criminal proceedings related to the matter.

The defendants are accused of failing to act in their positions of management to halt the continued production and marketing of vehicles, while allegedly knowing that illegal defeat devices were being used to change the outcome of emissions tests.

The matter is expected to run until December 2028, with more than 150 hearing dates scheduled.

A German documentary about Berlin filmmakers who hunt down a man who stole their graduation film has won an International Emmy.

"Der talentierte Mr F" (The Talented Mr F), bagged the prize in the Arts Programming category at the awards gala in Latest York.

The documentary centers around film students Moritz Henneberg and Julius Drost who discover their animated short film "Butty" online, but with someone else credited as its creator.

It turns out that a young man from West Virginia in the US claimed the work as his own, even changing its title and appearing on talk shows promoting the stolen film, winning 12 awards in the process.

The Berlin duo managed to travel to the US and duped the man into meeting with them, where they then confronted him.

"We were mainly just happy for our film that it was somehow finding an audience and doing well in the US," Henneberg told German news agency dpa.

A latest survey has revealed a growing sense of pessimism among Americans and Germans when it comes to the state of relations between the two countries.

The survey conducted by Pew Research Center and the Körber Stiftung found a cooling of attitudes, with one-third of Americans saying the US-Germany relationship "is bad.".

Some 62% of Americans stated the relationship was good, down from 71% in 2025 and 84% in 2024.

Meanwhile, Germans "remain deeply skeptical of the countries' current relations: 21% say they are good, and 77% say they are bad," the Pew Research Center remarked, adding that sentiments were similar to what they were early in the first Trump administration.

The Germany survey found a general trend of Germans being "down on the West".

According to recent reports, among the findings, 61% of Germans felt they could not rely on the US "nuclear umbrella" to provide protection in the event war. Nearly 70% said they wanted a French or British nuclear protection arrangement.

About two-thirds of those surveyed opposed Germany taking on a military leadership role, while 35% were in favor.

Around 60% of Germans mentioned Western unity — or "the concept of political and cultural linkage between the US and Europe — no longer exists.".

According to recent reports, the US findings came from a survey involving 1,014 adults conducted between September 18-24, 2026. The German findings were from a survey involving 1,501 eligible German voters between September 15-28, 2026.

German President Frank-Walter Steinmeier is in Ghana's capital, Accra, on Tuesday, on the second stop of his three-nation tour of African states.

Steinmeier is due to get the sixth German-African Business Summit started in Ghana along with President John Mahama.

Representatives of companies and business associations from Germany and Africa will be attending.

The previous summit took place in Kenya two years ago.

Steinmeier is hoping to encourage German companies to become more involved in African markets that could help patch up declining business in traditional markets such as the United States and China.

According to figures from Germany's Federal Statistical Office, German foreign trade with Africa grew by 10.5% in 2025, reaching a record value of €63.4 billion ($68.3 billion).

Steinmeier was in Ivory Coast on Monday, and after Ghana he is scheduled to travel to Kenya for the last leg of his trip.

Germany's largest labor union IG Metall and the country's largest automaker Volkswagen (VW) are embarking on a first round of talks in the northern city of Emden amid protests and threats to strike.

Volkswagen has noted it wants to place German workers' ‌pay in ​2027 and 2028 on ice, while also cutting a range of benefits. It is also looking to tinker with the union's 35-hour week, asking staff to work longer on the same pay when needed.

VW is facing increasing competition from China and has told its German workforce that domestic production costs have become too expensive.

According to recent reports, "The VW board is stabbing its employees in the back. Volkswagen is facing a critical test, the extent of which the employers apparently still don't grasp," said chief negotiator Thorsten Gröger, IG Metall district manager for Lower Saxony, last week.

IG Metall has warned that strikes could begin from January 1, with the union saying that VW's restructuring push puts it in violation of a 2024 deal which included 35,000 layoffs in return for employment and investment guarantees.

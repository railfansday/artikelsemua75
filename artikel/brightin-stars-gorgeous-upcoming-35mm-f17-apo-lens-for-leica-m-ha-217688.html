Brightin Star’s Gorgeous Upcoming 35mm f/1.7 APO Lens for Leica M Has Arrived

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, brightin Star first unveiled its upcoming APO 35mm f/1.7 ASPH. lens for Leica M-Mount cameras at CP+ in February and then launched it on Kickstarter in August. As of today, it’s available to everyone, and it’s still as handsome as ever.

Sources indicate that the Brightin Star 35mm f/1.7 APO ASPH. is a full-frame, manual-focus lens with a native Leica M-Mount design. However, as evidenced by the fact that Brightin Star offers it in kits with E, Z, RF, X, L, and even GFX adapters, it’s easy to adapt to other camera systems. Although Brightin Star does not state the lens’s exact image circle when used with its GFX adapter, the tech giant says it covers the 44 x 33-millimeter sensor inside GFX system medium-format mirrorless cameras. (Related: Porting fairseq wmt19 translation system to transformer...)

Recent findings suggest that although rangefinder-coupled with close focusing down to 0.7 meters (2.3 feet), the lens can focus as close as 0.3 meters (0.98 feet) when used in live view or adapted. Brightin Star notes that 100 degrees of focus throw covers infinity to 0.7 meters, while an additional 207 degrees covers the relatively narrow distance from 0.3 to 0.7 meters. That 307 degrees of total focus rotation is quite a lot, and Brightin Star describes it as “cinema style.”.

According to recent reports, the 35mm f/1.7 prime is a compact, relatively lightweight lens. It measures just 43 millimeters (1.7 inches) long and weighs around 260 grams (9.2 ounces). It accepts 39mm front filters. Part of Brightin Star’s new “Apex” series of lenses, the 35mm f/1.7 APO ASPH. promises precision build quality and high-end optical performance.

According to recent reports, “We believe a truly exceptional M-mount lens should never have to choose between heritage and innovation,” Brightin Star states. “The Apex Series was created with this belief at its core. It preserves the timeless, understated character of classic M lenses while bringing together advanced optics, precision mechanics, and uncompromising standards of craftsmanship.”.

Sources indicate that on the topic of image quality, the lens features 11 elements arranged across seven groups. There are some notable elements inside, including three double-sided aspherical lenses, three high-refractive pieces of glass, and an ED element. There are also two floating lens groups that move independently during focusing, which the manufacturer claims better controls distance-related aberrations and reduces spherical aberrations at close range. The lens also promises minimized focus breathing. (Related: Hugging Face Reads, Feb. 2021 - Long-range Transformers...)

Industry observers note that as evidenced by the “APO” in its name, Brightin Star indicates this is an “apochromatic” lens. While there’s no precise, industry-standard definition for what level of performance that requires, Brightin Star promises minimal chromatic aberration. An apochromatic lens focuses red, blue, and green wavelengths of light at the exact same point on the image plane. The closer a lens gets to achieving this ideal, the fewer chromatic aberrations it exhibits, all else being equal.

The Brightin Star MF 35mm f/1.7 APO ASPH. lens is available now for $1,099 for Leica M. An adapter adds $30, pushing the price to $1,129.

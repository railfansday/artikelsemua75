Industry observers note that back in June, I started rethinking my bearish outlook on artificial intelligence (AI) voice chatbot specialist SoundHound AI (SOUN -10.31%). I suggested investors should keep an eye on the company. (Related: Industry News: “Determined” woman completes 7 marathons...)

In a recent development, since then, its stock has fallen 54%. Over the past year, it's down 75%. Boy, am I glad I didn't recommend buying it in June! (Related: Breaking: AI for Game Development: Creating a Farming G...)

But at just $3.10 per share (at the time of this writing), has SoundHound's stock price dropped to the point that it's now a buy? It could be ... if SoundHound can make this one thing happen by 2027.

Industry observers note that what's so strange about the stock's recent performance is that trailing-12-month (TTM) revenue has continued to rise even as the stock price has fallen. Q3 revenue hit a record $62.4 million, bringing TTM revenue to $223.6 million.

Meanwhile, SoundHound's shares peaked in late 2024 at $24.23 per share. They're now sitting at about $3.10 per share, a 52-week low.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

According to recent reports, management expects revenue to continue improving, with guidance of $350 million to $400 million for 2027. But there's more to that number than meets the eye.

According to recent reports, soundHound is still digesting its recent acquisition of LivePerson, an enterprise digital messaging platform with significant debt on its balance sheet. SoundHound used an equity raise to retire that debt completely.

In a recent development, livePerson brought an extensive patent portfolio and existing contracts with major Fortune 100 companies. SoundHound is now integrating LivePerson's messaging infrastructure with its own OASYS agentic voice AI platform to offer an omnichannel customer engagement system that "operates natively across voice, web, mobile, SMS, and social channels.".

As if this transition weren't enough, SoundHound is also switching to a recurring-revenue, hosted-solution model. This kind of transition is usually a long-term positive for a firm, but can result in lumpy revenue during the transition period.

It has been revealed that another variable is the high amount of churn among LivePerson customers prior to the acquisition. Plus, because the acquisition didn't close until Sept. 4, LivePerson only contributed about $14 million to SoundHound's Q3 revenue. That means SoundHound's organic revenue growth was just 15.2% year over year (YOY), which is much lower than Q2's 45% growth and Q1's 52% growth.

For SoundHound to turn around its slumping stock price, it needs to show it can do more than just achieve revenue growth. It needs to make meaningful improvements in the quality of its revenue.

Cost reductions and synergies with LivePerson will play a role. Management believes it can identify $80 million in annualized cost reductions that will take effect by the end of the calendar year. But the company has also noted that it is exiting some legacy contracts with negative growth margins, which will result in the loss of some lower-quality revenue.

According to recent reports, ultimately, SoundHound's 2027 revenue guidance implies 68% revenue growth from the past year. The LivePerson acquisition will likely get the company most of the way there. What investors should instead be watching is whether SoundHound's margins and quarterly cash burn improve, how much of its existing revenue is converting to annual recurring revenue, and how well it retains and upsells LivePerson's customer base.

If SoundHound AI can show meaningful revenue quality improvement by 2027, it will look like a steal at its current share price.

Time is as cruel to sci-fi television as it is to humans. As more time goes by, it becomes hard not to notice how sci-fi reveals that were once considered masterpieces are slowly decaying into tomorrow's relics. Good sci-fi storytelling alone remains timeless. However, from a visual and legacy standpoint, many TV shows of our time struggle to sustain what they started.

Fortunately, Prime Video's The Expanse is a rare exception.

It has been revealed that perhaps in a decade, even the visuals from The Expanse will start feeling dated. The CGI from earlier seasons already seems a little rough around the edges. Yet, overall, The Expanse can still stand well against some of the biggest major-screen productions. The reason being that its visual language emphasized grounded physics and industrial grit instead of extreme photorealism. (Related: How to train a newly introduced language model from scr...)

When it comes to The Expanse's themes, it feels more relevant than ever. Its portrayal of manufactured class divides, information war, and humanity's unchecked ambition reflects real concerns surrounding today's world. Beyond its themes and visuals, however, there is another aspect that ensures the Prime Video sci-fi show ages like George Clooney.

Way too many sci-fi shows have landed in the ever-growing graveyard of the streaming era's cancelations. This has happened to a point where many viewers have even given up on getting too invested in shows. After all, one can never know when the proverbial plug will be pulled.

The Expanse, too, almost became a victim of the same trend. Despite earning immense acclaim after landing on Syfy, it was canceled after just three seasons. History could have repeated itself. The Expanse could have become another forgotten sci-fi series that fell short of reaching its full potential.

However, this is when fan-campaigning and Amazon eventually saved the day. (Related: The Partnership: Amazon SageMaker and Hugging Face...)

Sources indicate that more often than not, fan campaigns go unnoticed. When the economics stop making sense, streaming services and networks care little about fan campaigns from cult audiences. In The Expanse's case, though, fans did everything from flying a place over Amazon Studios to launching a miniature Rocinante into near-space.

In a recent development, as a result, The Expanse was saved by Amazon, and Jeff Bezos himself unveiled its return. He mentioned (via Forbes),.

It has been revealed that "I just got word that The Expanse is saved. The show is extraordinary, and these guys are unbelievably talented.”.

While Prime Video, too, could not eventually sustain the show's run beyond season 6, it is hard not to be grateful that The Expanse got to last so long. Some of the best aspects of the series emerge after season 3, which shows how it would not have been half as good if Prime Video had not given it a second chance.

Recent findings suggest that unlike many canceled sci-fi shows, The Expanse has a reliable 6-season arc with a solid legacy. It continues to age so well because it got the opportunity to reach its full potential.

According to recent reports, lined up with the same team as The Expanse, Prime Video is adapting Daniel Abraham and Ty Franck's The Captive's War book series. Naren Shankar of The Expanse fame is returning as the showrunner, while director Breck Eisner is also set to helm the series. Reports suggest that even Daniel Abraham and Ty Franck​​​​ are actively involved in the series' writers' room.

In a recent development, with so much going in The Captive's War's favor, it is hard not to hope that it will get the same treatment as The Expanse. Unlike The Expanse, The Captive's War only consists of a trilogy of books. Owing to this, if its creators adapt only one book per season, the show could wrap its run in just three seasons.

However, given how unpredictable streaming cancellations have become, one can only pray that it will last long enough to cover its entire source material. Given the strength of its production team and source material, though, The Captive's War could have a strong three-to-four season run before concluding its story.

If all goes well, the upcoming Prime Video sci-fi series could also match The Expanse's legacy and maybe even pave the way for its return after season 6.

In a recent development, the Expanse is a sci-fi series set in the distant future where humanity has spread out across the solar system, but the alliances between the three most potent governing bodies have reached a state of cold war. A mixed-up crew finds themselves at the center of a dangerous intergalactic conspiracy that threatens to bring war to the colonized worlds.

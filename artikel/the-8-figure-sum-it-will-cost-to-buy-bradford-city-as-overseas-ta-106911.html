Bradford City are up for sale, but the price put on the club by owner Stefan Rupp could be off-putting to those seeking to buy into the West Yorkshire club.

It has been revealed that bradford City marked their return to League action following the international break with a 2-2 home draw against Doncaster Rovers. A Sean Grehan goal for Doncaster salvaged the visitors a point, but the draw was still enough to lift the Bantams back into the play-off places as a result of Plymouth Argyle's surprise 2-0 home defeat to Peterborough United. But while Bradford's current position remains encouraging, there has been a degree of uncertainty surrounding the future of the club amid reports that owners Stefan Rupp and Edin Radic could be planning to sell up and move on. (Related: Industry News: “Determined” woman completes 7 marathons...)

The club are reportedly of interest to a Thai consortium who are looking to buy a club and who have had an interest in EFL clubs in the past.

Reporting on his Patreon on Sunday morning, journalist Alan Nixon has reported that the asking price for Bradford City is £11 million, though he adds that this price doesn't include the club's Valley Parade home itself, which has been owned by the pension fund of former chairman Gordon Gibb since 2004.

Nixon confirms that "there is interest in the Yorkshire club and they could potentially be sold", but adds that "the owners are looking for an eight-digit sum and that seems high when they do not own the ground".

However, he does add that this doesn't necessarily mean the end of interest in buying them, saying that, "potential buyers have been in contact with Gordon Gibb, the Valley Parade landlord, and could go in with him".

Although Gibb hasn't been directly involved in the running of Bradford City in over two decades, he is a key player in any sale of the club, despite being based in Scarborough and being a Leeds United fan - the Whites are West Yorkshire rivals of the Bantams, but of course they are two divisions apart.

Industry observers note that selling a club in the bottom two divisions of the EFL which doesn't own its own ground is very different to selling a club which does. Since the other assets that the club owns aren't usually especially valuable, the ground itself is often by far the biggest and most valuable that the club owns.

Industry observers note that those who buy a club which doesn't own its own home are usually buying little more than the contracts, the right to play in the EFL, and the name of the club itself. Many prospective owners will want to 'reunite' the club with its ground when they purchase it, as happened with Coventry City last year, where owner Doug King managed to secure the purchase of The CBS Arena, almost three years after buying the club itself.

Bradford reportedly pay more than £500,000 annually to lease the ground, with the existing agreement due for renewal in 2028, and that amount of money as an ongoing cost limits what the club can spend elsewhere. But Gibb is under no obligation to sell, and it's not known what he would want for it.

The last set of company accounts for the club confirmed that a "net loss of (£996,814) in 2024 has been augmented by a further loss in the current year of (£2,992,958)", so Bradford City are not currently profitable, and any fresh owners would be aware that profitability might only come should they manage to get the club all the way up to the Premier League.

But Bradford City have been moving in an upward direction over the last two or three years following years of stagnation in League Two, and the size of the club, who can still attract attendances of close to 20,000 and represent a city of half a million people, will be appealing to investors looking for a project that they can grow. But the cost of the stadium itself is a clear complication in the sale of the club, and it might mean that negotiations take some time to conclude.

Breaking: U.S. court clears the way for Army’s planned ammunition detonation in Iowa

A U.S. appeals court on Monday cleared the Army to proceed with a controlled detonation of nearly five tons of degraded explosives at an Iowa ammunition plant, overturning a bid by environmental groups to block an emergency federal permit for the blast.

The order clears the way for the Army's initiative to destroy around 68 barrels of lead azide at the Iowa Army Ammunition Plant in Middletown, near the Iowa-Illinois border, on Wednesday.

A three-judge panel of the 8th U.S. Circuit Court of Appeals denied a request by environmental groups seeking to challenge an emergency permit from the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency authorizing the operation.

According to recent reports, "We are mindful of the very real risks presented by the Army's planned detonation of the stores of degraded lead azide," the ruling mentioned. "However, we must acknowledge the realities of the situation: the degraded explosives present a risk of an uncontrolled and unplanned explosion, which would occur without the benefit of the safety measures that the Permit puts in place.". The planned blast, originally scheduled for Nov. 20, has been delayed three times amid regional concerns over possible health and environmental effects and criticism that the government failed to adequately communicate the potential risks to local communities.

The detonation is expected to produce a dust plume containing lead particles, a toxic metal that can cause serious health effects, according to the Iowa Department of Health and Human Services. Breathing in lead-containing particles or coming into contact with lead dust deposited on surrounding surfaces could pose potential health risks, the agency mentioned.

The U.S. Army has argued that the risk of the material detonating on its own has increased as temperatures fall, and that the explosive compound, stored for more than two decades, has degraded to the point that moving it is too dangerous. President Donald Trump mentioned in a social media post Monday that he had told the Army to hold off until the court weighed in. He added that the decision would be subject to Iowa's Democratic Gov.-elect Rob Sand, who takes office in January, warning that further delay could risk "a large, spontaneous explosion.".

In an X post late Monday following the court ruling, Sand said the Trump administration "should make this decision based on the information they have from the people they brought in to do the job months ago and have been on the ground in Middletown for weeks.".

"Everyday Iowans are entitled to know how Middletown came to this situation, and learn why the administration believes this is the best path forward," Sand said, urging the federal government to share information with the public as soon as possible.

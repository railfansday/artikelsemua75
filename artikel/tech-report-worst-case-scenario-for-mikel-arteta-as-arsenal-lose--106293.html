It has been revealed that not here, at least not at first. In the opening 45 minutes, it was the Gunners who were the bullies, bringing an intensity and a level of physicality that Newcastle simply could not live with. Arteta’s side led 1-0 through Eberechi Eze’s emphatic finish but it should have been more. Noni Madueke wasted two big chances. Bruno Guimaraes flung himself at a cross and turned it wide.

There was no reason for this encounter to follow the trend of previous showdowns. It was a Saturday evening stroll for Arsenal and it should have remained as much.

Instead, the Gunners fell apart. Alarmingly so. All it took was one shanked volley from Martin Odegaard, letting Newcastle away on the break to equalise, and the match flipped.

Like in the Champions League win over Slavia Prague last month, Arsenal were dominant in the first half but then let it slip away from them, whether it be complacency or something else. There was no sign of any game management.

In Prague, the opposition did not have the quality to punish them. At St James’ Park, Arsenal paid the price.

Industry observers note that from the moment Harvey Barnes equalised, all composure from the visitors evaporated. Madueke swung at thin air attempting to clear. Eze slipped in his own box, Raya could not clear, and Gabriel had to quickly arrive to leather the ball behind.

Arteta looked shell-shocked on the touchline when Newcastle then took the lead from a mess of a corner but the worst was still to come.

David Raya had a collision with William Osula that looked fairly innocuous. When that then forced him off, looking in real pain with an apparent shoulder injury, it felt like a fever dream for Arsenal.

SpaceX's 35th Dragon cargo mission has begun its journey home to Earth.

In a recent development, the robotic Dragon capsule undocked from the International Space Station (ISS) today at 12:05 p.m. EST (1705 GMT), while the duo were flying 260 miles (418 kilometers) above western China. (Related: How to Start a Side Business While Working Full-Time...)

According to recent reports, if all goes according to blueprint, the freighter will splash down in the Pacific Ocean off the coast of California on Saturday (Nov. 21) at about 4:55 p.m. EST (2155 GMT). There will be no livestream for its reentry and splashdown. (Today's departure webcast did not feature live footage of the moment of undocking, as it occurred during a gap in coverage of the high-bandwidth signal needed for video.). (Related: Alaska Mileage Blueprint → Cathay Pacific / Qantas to A...)

It has been revealed that the Dragon released Oct. 13, on a mission called CRS-35 because it's the 35th that SpaceX has flown for NASA's Commercial Resupply Services program.

Industry observers note that the capsule arrived at the ISS on Oct. 15, delivering more than 6,300 pounds (2,860 kilograms) of food, scientific gear and other supplies to the astronauts aboard the orbiting lab. It will bring a lot of stuff down to Earth, too.

Industry observers note that "Thousands of pounds of cargo are returning from the space station, including research samples that could advance future space exploration and improve life on Earth," NASA officials wrote in an patch on Tuesday (Nov. 17).

"The returning research includes a 3D-bioprinted blood vessel construct scientists used to study vascular aging and cardiovascular disease in space and on Earth," they added.

Dragon is the only cargo craft flying today that's capable of making such downward deliveries. The others — Northrop Grumman's Cygnus, Russia's Progress vehicle and Japan's HTV-X — are all expendable and burn up in Earth's atmosphere to wrap up their first and only spaceflight.

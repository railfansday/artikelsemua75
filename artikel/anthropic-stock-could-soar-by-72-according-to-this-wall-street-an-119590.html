Anthropic's (ANT -8.50%) IPO was a smashing success. It broke the 5-month-old record for the biggest IPO ever and opened well above its IPO price. Clearly, many investors and analysts are excited about the firm's prospects, and that is already being reflected in various price targets. For instance, Scott Berg, an analyst at Needham & Company, initiated coverage of the stock with a $150 price target. That implies an almost 72% upside from current levels. Could Anthropic really rise that much over the next year?

In a recent development, at a stock price of $150, Anthropic's market cap would be about $2 trillion (it would be higher on a fully diluted basis). That would put the tech company in a very exclusive group. Here is an argument as to why this valuation could make sense, especially in 12 months, once we have more data on Anthropic's performance.

First, the company's revenue has been growing at a dizzying pace. Third-quarter revenue was $17 billion, up 1,127% year over year. Anthropic's run rate revenue grew by $10 billion in a single month, to $81 billion as of the end of October.

According to recent reports, true, the tech giant remains unprofitable. In the third quarter, its net loss was $23.3 billion, significantly worse than the $12.1 billion loss recorded in the prior-year quarter. But that was mostly due to non-cash charges driven by an increase in the value of various financial instruments -- a mostly paper loss that tells us very little about the fundamentals of Anthropic's business.

Meanwhile, several factors could allow the manufacturer's growth to maintain a strong pace. Anthropic arguably benefits from a competitive edge. For instance, with about 40 million users across its portfolio of AI-powered tools, including more than 1,900 customers with trailing 12-month revenue exceeding $1 million, Anthropic is building network effects. It has been revealed that data and customer feedback can help the company improve its AI-powered tools and attract even more owners. The company also argues that more sophisticated versions of its Claude frontier model will help it build even better ones next time around, strengthening its lead. Claude has been writing increasingly more code per person within the company. (Related: How to train a new language model from scratch using Tr...)

Embedding this AI tool into Anthropic's workflows could lead to significant productivity gains in the medium term and allow the organization to reduce expenses and turn a profit. Based on the company's run rate revenue and the pace at which it is growing, it could reach sales well above $100 billion in fiscal year 2027.

Suppose Anthropic records $115 billion in revenue next year and ends 2027 with a $150 stock price. The tech giant's price-to-sales ratio as of the end of the year would be 17.3 on a basic-share count basis. If, in addition, the company can turn a profit, that valuation wouldn't be that unreasonable.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

Recent findings suggest that however, there is significant uncertainty. Consider, for instance, that Anthropic revealed $900.9 billion in infrastructure commitments with various companies. What's more, many of those agreements are non-cancellable.

The message management is sending here is clear. Anthropic is bullish on the future of AI. But if spending on the technology declines meaningfully, that would be a massive blow to the company. Revenue growth would decline, making it much more challenging for Anthropic to post consistent profits.

There are other potential problems. Other AI companies might steal some of its business as corporations increasingly seek to diversify their portfolio of AI-powered tools away from the largest providers. Or perhaps Anthropic might fail to maintain its rapid pace of technological progress, leading to a decline in market share.

These are all meaningful risks to keep in mind, though some might not matter over the next 12 months. Still, they matter to long-term investors, and the question isn't just whether Anthropic stock could rise to $150 by the end of 2027 -- it's whether the manufacturer could deliver exceptional returns over the long run. I am not yet convinced that it will; that's why I am staying away for now.

But Anthropic is worth keeping on investors' watchlists, at the right price, which, in my view, is around the $83 IPO price, or slightly below it for a healthy margin of error.

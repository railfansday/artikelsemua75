If you're all about the NCIS shows, NCIS: Sydney is the only one of the bunch held over for a winter premiere date. Back for its fourth season, the spin-off is the first outside of the U.S., taking place in Australia. The Naval Criminal Investigative Service (NCIS) has joined forces with the Australian Federal Police (AFP) there to look into crimes involving military personnel.

Filmed in Australia, you get a different perspective with this show, and an international vibe. It was originally meant to be exclusive to Paramount+ in the U.S., but airing on CBS opens up the option to watch via network TV, too. It's actually the first Australian show to air in the U.S. during the primetime hours. And it's done just as well as the others in the franchise, including the original that remains one of the greatest CBS indicates of all time. It seems like its predecessors, NCIS: Sydney is here to stay for the long-haul.

It has been revealed that wait, isn't Survivor on right now? It was indeed part of the CBS fall 2026 schedule, but as die-hard fans know, the long-running reality competition has always aired two seasons per year, resulting in more than 50 seasons now through its 26-year and counting run. Jeff Probst continues to host the award-winning show that brings together people from all walks of life and all ages to compete for a million-dollar prize. But it's not easy, because they will have to outwit, outplay, and outlast the competition in both physical and mental challenges.

Industry observers note that as a formulaic show, you can guess what to expect for the next season, but there are some interesting changes. First, the new season, which will premiere in late February, will increase the episode run-times from the already increased 1.5 hours to two hours per week. The finale will remain at three hours plus an aftershow. The season will take place in Fiji once again and the journey will last 26 days. There won't be any returning players this time. But one thing we can be sure of is that there will be an eclectic mix of personalities and players to root for or against as you get to know them and their strategies.

Sources indicate that we don't know a ton yet about Stacks of Cash aside from that it will be a game show hosted by Mike O'Malley, best known for playing Burt Hummell in Glee. The first season will be 10 hour-long episodes following six contestants who compete by answering quiz questions. They win giant shapes when they get them right, each with a cash value on it. From there, they use a giant claw to lower the shapes into an area where they are trying to build a multi-shaped tower. If the tower falls, they're out, however. So, they must build carefully.

Of course, the shapes range from the simple rectangular and square ones that Tetris players love to weird triangles that could throw a wrench into your potentially toppling tower. The series has already debuted in Europe, so this American version won't be anything new if you've seen other ones. If you love game indicates, this one sounds like loads of fun.

Remember Undercover Boss? The reality series based on the British one of the same name hasn't been on for years, but it's coming back with Drew Carey as the host. The show has never historically had a host, but centers around a company's owner, CEO, or other high-ranking individual. The person dons an elaborate disguise then goes undercover in their own company to discover problems and observe hard-working (or not so hard-working) employees on the ground level. The employees know they are being filmed, but they think it's for a documentary of some kind about career changes or entry-level jobs. The bosses get an awakening about what it's really like to do the grueling jobs and sometimes customer-facing jobs that are essential to their companies.

It has been revealed that known for the often ridiculous make-up and wigs, which have been parodied on Saturday Night Live, Undercover Boss is still deeply heartwarming. At the end of every episode, the subject meets with each employee they worked with, reveals their true identity, and delivers life-changing news. It might be a promotion, upskills and training, a bonus, maybe even time off for a vacation with their family. But they also discuss changes they will make to improve the operations and address problems that were inadvertently revealed to them or they witnessed first hand. It will be interesting to see how and if the show changes in its reboot and with a host at the helm.

Kathy Bates has already dominated Hollywood with her illustrious movie career, and now she's doing the same for the small screen in this legal drama. She plays Madeline "Matty" Kingston in Matlock, who goes by the surname Matlock when she applies for an entry-level job at a law firm. She's a talented lawyer with a successful career behind her, but they don't know that. She has ulterior motives, trying to uncover what information was hidden that she believes could have saved her daughter's life.

The series, one of the greatest procedurals that isn't about cops or doctors, may carry the same name as the iconic one from the '80s, but it's only in meta fashion: Matty uses this name because she is a fan of the show, which existed in the fictional world of the series as well. Coming back for its third season, Matlock is shaping up to be one of the best new procedurals of this decade.

Industry observers note that A newly introduced show in the line-up, Einstein stars Matthew Grey Gubler as Lewis Einstein. He's a Princeton University professor who gets caught up with the law and pays his dues by using his genius to help them solve crimes. It's a story we've seen again and again in procedurals, the consultant with a special skill or genius-level IQ. But this one has an interesting twist because Einstein is literally descended from the genius Albert Einstein: he's his great-grandson.

The series, based on the German series and movie of the same name, also stars Melissa Fumero as Detective Teresa "Teri" London, who isn't quite sure about working with Einstein, and Aunjanue Ellis-Taylor as Captain Marcia Frost, a veteran officer who can't wait to retire. Network procedural fans will love the casting of Tony Shalhoub, who himself has played a detective with a quirk in Monk, as Lewis' father Jack Einstein.

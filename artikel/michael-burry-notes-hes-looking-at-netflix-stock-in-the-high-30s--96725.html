With the stock trading near $58 as of this writing, a price in the high $30s would cut about another third off the shares.

It has been revealed that I don't think investors have to wait for that. At today's price, Netflix already looks cheap for a firm whose profits are still climbing.

Burry has been circling Netflix for months. In July, he figured that Netflix at $67.75 per share traded at about 1.6 times its intrinsic value, which comes out to a value of roughly $42 a share by his math. He also said then that the stock would have to drop to the low $50s to get closer to an ideal buying opportunity.

So, the high $30s isn't a random number. It would let him buy under his own estimate of what the manufacturer is worth.

It has been revealed that his concern is how long Netflix's content keeps its value. In July, he compared the streaming giant to Walt Disney and its Pixar studio, whose content he called evergreen. Much of what Netflix makes, he suggested, is OK for now but doesn't improve with age.

Sure, that's a fair worry about any business built on hit highlights. But it's a long-term debate. In the meantime, Netflix's profits keep growing, making the high $30s look like a really pessimistic price.

Call the high $30s $38 a share. On average, analysts expect Netflix to earn about $3.80 per share in 2027, so a $38 stock would cost about 10 times next year's expected earnings. At around $58, shares trade at about 15 times next year's expected earnings.

By comparison, the S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) traded at 17.7 times its expected earnings for the next 12 months on Nov. 5, according to FactSet.

Noted another way, Netflix stock already costs less than the average stock relative to its earnings. Burry's price would put it at a discount of over 40% to the market's price-to-earnings ratio.

Recent findings suggest that for $38 to be a market-average price, Netflix would have to earn only about $2.15 per share. It earned $2.53 per share in 2025 (split-adjusted too), and its earnings per share have grown year over year in every quarter of 2026 so far.

The high $30s would only make sense, then, if Netflix's profits were about to shrink.

They don't look close to shrinking. Netflix's operating margin was 26.7% in 2024 and 29.5% in 2025, and the business is targeting 31.5% for 2026. Its third-quarter operating margin hit 34.6%. And management is guiding for a 26% margin in the fourth quarter, above the 24.5% Netflix reported a year before, even with costs from newly declared job cuts. All told, Netflix states its outlook implies operating income growth of over 20% this year.

In a recent development, also, Netflix keeps returning cash to shareholders. It spent $4.8 billion buying back its own stock in the third quarter alone, and it still had $22.3 billion in repurchase authorizations left.

Sales growth, though, keeps slowing. Revenue growth has fallen every quarter this year, to 11.8% year over year in the third quarter from 16.2% in the first. And management expects around 11.5% for the fourth quarter, while it has lowered its 2026 ad revenue outlook to about $2.7 billion from about $3 billion.

But a business still growing sales by double digits while raising its operating margin looks unlikely to wind up earning less than it did in 2025.

Could Burry still get his price? He might. Netflix stock is already down over half from its June 2025 record, and a stock that's fallen this far can keep falling.

Industry observers note that but waiting for the high $30s is arguably a bet that Netflix's earnings stall. At around 15 times next year's expected earnings, Netflix stock already prices in a lot of caution, I think. I'd consider buying shares now rather than waiting for Burry's number.

Sources indicate that earlier this month, Costco Wholesale (COST +1.46%) declared a special dividend of $30 per share, payable on Dec. 4 to shareholders of record as of the close of business on Nov. 24. That payout will be double the $15 per share special dividend that shareholders received in 2024. (Related: How to Start a Side Business While Working Full-Time...)

But rather than being a reason to buy the stock on its own, these intermittent bonus distributions point to a broader case for holding Costco as a long-term investment. (Related: See where votes are being counted in Senate and House m...)

The special dividend payout is a way to reward its shareholders. But it's also an indication that Costco has more money than it can usefully deploy to grow the business at the moment, a situation many companies would like to be in.

In a recent development, for Costco, that situation has resulted from an increase in its income from membership sales, strong renewal rates, expansion, and improved profitability, which are all solid reasons to consider owning the stock. In its fiscal 2026 fourth quarter, which ended Aug. 30, Costco reported membership income growth of 7.3%. That’s the revenue stream that makes the entire business work. Its product sales have extremely thin margins; Costco makes the bulk of its money on the membership fees, so growth on that line indicates healthy demand from customers wanting to access Costco's warehouses.

In North America, Costco has a 92.3% membership renewal rate, while global renewals overall are just under 90%. This gives it a predictable high-margin revenue stream, and it also means Costco doesn't have to constantly replace large numbers of members who cancel. It also demonstrates that, despite concerns about rising grocery and household costs, most members still see enough value in the warehouse giant to keep their memberships. And if the economy were to enter a downturn, Costco offers the kind of deals on bulk goods that people particularly desire when their budgets get tighter.

NASDAQ · As of 2:13 PM ET 11/20/26.

It has been revealed that costco can also generate more revenue and translate it into profitability by opening upcoming warehouse stores. It chooses its locations selectively, as evidenced by the fact that it only opened 25 new warehouses in fiscal 2026. That selectivity is a positive, as expansion continues to drive membership fee growth without tying up all the cash Costco generates, giving it greater financial flexibility.

It has been revealed that finally, the company can pay out this latest special dividend because it's simply turning more of its sales into profits. For fiscal 2026, Costco reported $9.2 billion in net income, a nearly 14% increase from the prior year.

Costco's strong business model is what is allowing it to pay out that $30 special dividend, and it is one of the best consumer staples stocks on the market. But the clear strength of that business model has led the market to price high expectations into its stock.

Trading at a forward price-to-earnings ratio of 48.6, there's little room for Costco to deliver any underwhelming or mediocre results in its earnings reports. I still like Costco as a long-term portfolio addition, but investors should buy in with the understanding that the company has high expectations to live up to.

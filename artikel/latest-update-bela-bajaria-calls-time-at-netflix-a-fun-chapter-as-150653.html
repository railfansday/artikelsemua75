Former Netflix chief content officer Bela Bajaria addressed her exit from the streamer in her first public comments about her abrupt departure last month.

Speaking to The Hollywood Reporter ahead of the 2026 International Emmys, where she’s set to be honored with the Founders Award, Bajaria called her decade at Netflix “such an amazing, fun chapter” and shared she was “proud of so many of the things” that she and her team had accomplished.

“I’m excited about what’s next, and I think this time is good for a little rest after a lot of global travel,” she told THR on the red carpet ahead of the awards show, adding that she’s “really open” to whatever the future holds. Bajaria was ousted from her role at Netflix on Oct. 15, with former Fox and Disney exec Peter Rice taking her place as chief content officer.

The former chief content officer previously spoke with THR at the Saban Community Clinic Gala at the end of October but wasn’t yet ready to speak about her time at Netflix. Still she offered, “the outpouring that I’ve received in the last couple of weeks has been so beautiful and amazing. I’ve been in television for 30 years, and at this point I have worked with what feels like nearly every single person from agents and producers to writers and actors. I love it. I’m so grateful for the career I have had to date, with lots more to come.”.

Speaking to THR at the International Emmys, Bajaria also weighed in on the recent departures of a number of top female executives amid ongoing layoffs and restructuring efforts, including Pam Abdy at Warner Bros. and Cindy Holland at Paramount.

“I think in general, in every industry, we just have to make sure that you have leaders that are doing a great job and really care about the group and are making those decisions,” Bajaria said. “And it matters who’s in those roles, and I think we should always just be aware of that.”.

She added that it was particularly notable to think of who’s in leadership roles and making decisions and “people’s backgrounds” when you’re “greenlighting” movies and TV shows.

Still she’s looking back on her time at Netflix with gratitude and excitement for what’s next.

It has been revealed that “I’m so proud of being part of something that transforms television and film and connecting with audiences around the world,” she told THR. “I’m just really excited to be here to celebrate a long career at this point and that’s really what it’s about. Let’s see what’s next.”.

Bajaria, who joined Netflix after serving as president of Universal Television at NBCUniversal, left the streamer as it shed 1,000 employees, roughly six percent of its workforce, including execs Doug Belgrad and, days after news broke of Bajaria leaving, film boss Dan Lin. The layoffs came as the manufacturer seeks a reset amid slowing viewership growth.

When she left Netflix, co-CEO Ted Sarandos stated of Bajaria, “Bela has built and shaped a truly global Content organization, hiring brilliant people and empowering them to make good decisions. I am incredibly grateful to her for everything she’s done.”.

He added in a staff memo, “I know this is a big change in a year that has already had its share. But I also see an opportunity to take our slate to the next level, especially in [the U.S. and Canada] where we have an outsize impact in creating big, buzzy hits that our members love and drive conversation around the world.”.

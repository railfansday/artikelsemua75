Alphabet priced C$6 billion of Canadian-dollar senior unsecured notes on November 19, 2026, and filed the final pricing terms with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission on November 20, 2026. Settlement is scheduled for November 24, 2026.

The pricing divides the debt into four fixed-rate tranches, with higher coupons and yields on the longer maturities:.

The pricing sheet lists Moody’s Aa2 and S&P AA+ ratings, both with stable outlooks. RBC Dominion Securities, Scotia Capital and TD Securities are joint book-running managers, with CIBC World Markets as co-manager.

Alphabet designated the proceeds for general corporate purposes, which may include repayment of outstanding debt; it did not earmark them for AI. Industry observers note that across Alphabet, capital spending is running ahead of operating cash generation. Third-quarter purchases of property and equipment were $51.4 billion, above $42.3 billion of operating cash flow, leaving negative free cash flow of $9.1 billion under Alphabet’s definition of operating cash flow minus capital expenditures.

For the first nine months of 2026, property-and-equipment purchases totaled $132.0 billion, compared with $63.6 billion in the same period of 2025. Those figures place the Canadian borrowing alongside a substantial increase in investment across the company.

It has been revealed that alphabet separately disclosed $28.7 billion of net proceeds from senior unsecured notes issued in the third quarter. For the first nine months of 2026, proceeds from debt issuance, net of costs, were $85.2 billion, showing that substantial borrowing preceded the Canadian offering.

In a recent development, long-term debt stood at $125.1 billion on September 30, 2026, up from $46.5 billion at December 31, 2025. Cash, cash equivalents and marketable securities totaled $265.1 billion, making the fresh notes an addition to funding capacity within a large investment program rather than an emergency liquidity raise.

The prospectus also points to substantial infrastructure commitments beyond those already recorded. Alphabet’s preliminary prospectus supplement disclosed $130.5 billion of future payments under leases primarily related to data centers that had not commenced as of September 30, 2026. Those leases were not yet recorded on the consolidated balance sheet, and a portion will represent finance lease obligations.

The Canadian notes add long-dated funding without creating a dedicated AI pool. The central issue for subsequent results is whether operating cash generation catches up with capital spending and the coming data-center commitments, or whether debt and other external financing take on a still larger role in Alphabet’s AI expansion.

World of Warcraft: Forever has turned me into a massive, friendly golden retriever that says hello to everybody

It has been revealed that this is Terminally Online: PC Gamer's very own MMO column. Every other week, I'll be sharing my thoughts on the genre, interviewing fellow MMO-heads like me, taking a deep-dive into mechanics we've all taken for granted, and, occasionally, bringing in guest writers to talk about their MMO of choice.

World of Warcraft: Forever's done a lot of things for me over the past couple of weeks—it's given me a deeper appreciation for oldschool MMO quest design, it's wowed me with some genuinely impressive latest lighting technology, and it's… ugh, it's made me so friendly.

Mind, I wouldn't say I'm unkind when I play MMOs, but I am a solo player by habit. I'll have a guild, friends I roleplay with, sure. But I'm very rarely striking up conversations with people in-game—all of that typically happens in Discord. And yet, here I am in WoW forever, trying to emphatically start conversations with goddamn everyone I meet. (Related: Developer builds a ‘Discord kitten’ that’s actually an ...)

And it's in part because of how much of a bastard the game is. I don't think Classic is a particularly difficult videogame—I've even gotten my warrior from level 1 to, at the time of writing, 56 as fury, a spec typically considered trash for levelling. And it's been fine, I've just minded my pulls and levelled first aid.

In a recent development, but it is a known fact that vanilla WoW quests are just easier when you group up with people. Part of this is because players are a force multiplier—many classes have group buffs, and not having to worry about health with a healer present means that you can pull swathes of enemies and melt them down easily. (Related: GTA 6 players report game-breaking crashes on PS5 and X...)

And so I've been doing that. I've been grouping with people, and they've been grouping with me, and… oh, god, we've been talking to each other.

Recent findings suggest that I don't know if I just happen to have a lucky propensity for drawing in nice folks, but my levelling journey has been dappled with some of the chillest people you'll ever meet. People hanging around to help you finish quests they've already completed is so common it's almost standard.

In one instance, a shadow priest carried me through multiple elite quests in Stranglethorn Vale—they needed to do 'em too, sure, but they actively went out of their way to show me where I could get a couple.

Recent findings suggest that there is a real, tangible sense of camaraderie in the game right now, one that's yet to be grinded to the marrow by the efficiency-laden tasks of Mythic+ or a seasonal system. But it's also one borne of how long everything takes. When you've already spent 10 minutes jogging to a quest, what's another five to help a stranger out?

Recent findings suggest that it is, to put it plainly, the opposite of what I'd expected after years of playing far more modern MMOs. I'm even way ahead of the curve of the general population level-wise, and I'm still meeting these relaxed, generous folks. People who'll offer up scraps of a treasure map that's dropping from the pirates we're grinding for free, rather than being a mercenary about it and selling them on the auction house.

Moreover, it is infectious. I've been handing mana potions to people. I've been sticking around to help out with quests—in a recent Zul'farrak run, a dungeon I was only doing twice because I'd missed that it was required for the Sunken Temple questline, our tank had simply atrocious luck collecting troll tempers for a quest (you need 20 of them, and they don't drop often).

Not just myself, but another player in the same situation all stuck around for about 20 minutes just to help them out. When they thanked us, I, saccharine and glowing with the spirit of a reformed Grinch, said: "No worries, bud, if you wanna live in a village you've gotta be a villager sometimes.".

It has been revealed that in short, Forever's turned me into a substantial, fluffy, golden-haired dog. The kind you meet in a park that bounds up to you with its stupid tongue wagging out. All that oldschool game design and camaraderie has, as it turns out, been a complete antidote to my hermetic ways. And while my street cred as a tough, solemn badass might never recover, I'm glad to be taking my time.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Police are investigating the tragic death of Alexander Haskin.

Industry observers note that the 38-year-old—who appeared as a background actor on shows like You and The Rookie—was pronounced dead Nov. 15 shortly after he was pulled from the ocean outside the Santa Monica Pier in Los Angeles, the Santa Monica Police Department confirmed to The Los Angeles Times. According to recent reports, police noted they received a report at 4:06 p.m. Nov. 15 that a man was in the water. Harbor guards responded and removed Haskin from the ocean, hoisting him onto the pier deck, where the Santa Monica Fire Department paramedics continued lifesaving measures, police spokesperson Lt. Lewis Gilmour told the Times. (Related: Latest Update: Does Fluoride In Drinking Water Affect I...)

Despite their efforts, Haskin was pronounced dead shortly before 5 p.m.

The actor’s cause of death was deferred, pending further investigation, according to Los Angeles County Medical Examiner’s Office records obtained by the Times.

Meanwhile, Gilmour confirmed an investigation into Haskin’s death is underway, though he noted a preliminary investigation found no suspicious circumstances.

Plus, police confirmed that Haskin had been alone in the moments before entering the water, despite reports that he had been in an altercation with another person.

“The evidence indicates the man was alone and did not interact with anyone in the period immediately before the incident,” Gilmour told the Times. “Security footage corroborates that finding.”.

It has been revealed that following Haskin’s death, his dad Dana Haskin told the Times that his son had seemed in good spirits when they last spoke on Nov. 13. He also shared that Haskin had sent him a short text the morning of his death, though he did not disclose the message’s contents.

He also remembered his son—an Air Force veteran and personal trainer—as a “multifaceted guy” who loved music, fitness and acting.

It has been revealed that “He was a happy guy,” Dana Haskin told the Times. “He liked to joke around, and he was always getting in trouble, but in a good way.”.

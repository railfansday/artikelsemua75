Sources indicate that openAI reported on November 24, 2026 that its GPT-6 family can design protein structures and amino-acid sequences using reasoning and general-purpose coding tools. In laboratory work with research partner Onava, some proteins based on GPT-6.2’s designs revealed evidence of binding across five targets.

The research examines whether one general model can handle work usually divided among specialized protein systems. Those systems accept particular inputs and constraints, so newly introduced requirements can demand software changes or additional training. OpenAI’s approach instead uses natural-language instructions to specify details such as where a small protein called a minibinder should attach, its structural class, and the number of helices or strands it should contain. In the first laboratory experiment, a pre-release version of GPT-6.2 received three-dimensional structures of target proteins with the desired attachment sites marked. It wrote code to generate candidate minibinder shapes without specialized protein models. SolubleMPNN then proposed amino-acid sequences for those shapes, and other specialized systems helped select candidates predicted to fold correctly and bind at the intended site before Onava made and tested them. OpenAI’s laboratory results show evidence of binding for 35 of 322 tested minibinders, or 10.9%, with at least one hit for each target. The same account cautions that earlier RFdiffusion experiments produced higher hit rates and that comparisons depend heavily on how strictly candidates are filtered and how binding is defined.

In a recent development, A separate experiment withheld original amino-acid sequences and asked GPT-6.2 to redesign them from three-dimensional minibinder structures. For three unpublished structures that GPT-6.2 had itself generated, 14 of 107 proteins made from its fresh sequences, or 13.1%, showed binding. OpenAI presents the unpublished starting structures and substantial sequence differences as strong evidence that the capability extends beyond memorization.

The antibody experiments focused on redesigning the loops that help an antibody attach to its target. OpenAI reports that 128 of 182 loop designs, or 70%, across six small VHH antibodies targeting IL-4Rα indicated signs of binding. Separately, 179 of 272 fragments derived from five therapeutic antibodies, or 66%, showed binding to their original PD-1 or PD-L1 targets after roughly half the amino acids in each of three binding loops were changed.

Recent findings suggest that openAI says an internal research model followed instructions for structural class and the number of coils and strands with near-perfect fidelity in three-dimensional designs. In a preliminary analysis of 30 motif-scaffolding challenges, where the model had to build a protein around a fixed arrangement of key amino acids, 43.7% of evaluated designs from its latest internal model met the structural success criteria, compared with 27.8% for RFdiffusion. ProteinMPNN supplied sequences and ESMFold predicted their folds for screening, so the rates reflect computational checks rather than laboratory demonstrations of function.

OpenAI’s other evaluations produced varying margins over specialized systems. Across 200 challenges requiring a specified overall protein fold, 18.9% of evaluated designs from the latest internal model passed the computational criteria, compared with 17.1% for Proteina. In a small-molecule docking evaluation, predictions closely matched experimentally observed positions and orientations in 93% of scored GPT-6.2 cases and 96% of cases for the latest internal model, compared with 50% for DiffDock-L.

Whole-protein structure prediction remains the clearest weakness. OpenAI reports average TM-scores of 0.16 for GPT-5.6, 0.26 for GPT-6.2 and 0.38 for its latest internal model, compared with 0.93 for ESMFold2. A TM-score of 1 denotes a perfect structural match, while scores above 0.5 generally indicate that a prediction captures the overall fold.

Sources indicate that the campaign also relied on Boltz-2, OpenDDE and ESMFold2 to filter candidates. The results therefore do not establish that GPT models can generate latest minibinders end to end without specialized assistance. OpenAI cautioned that unusually strong inverse-folding results may partly reflect memorization or artifacts left by structure-generation processes, while public antibody-docking structures may have appeared in training or resembled training examples.

Recent findings suggest that binding is only an initial requirement for a useful research tool or medicine. Further work would need to establish specificity, stability and biological activity, along with safety and efficacy for therapeutic applications. OpenAI’s stated next steps include finding binders for harder targets, designing for biological effects, selectivity and manufacturability, expanding to RNA and small molecules, and evaluating biosecurity risks from possible misuse of the same capabilities.

According to recent reports, the reported results position GPT-6 as a flexible design aid whose outputs still depend on specialized filtering and laboratory validation. Their practical value now rests on whether prompt-controlled candidates can consistently become selective, stable and biologically active molecules rather than merely bind their targets.

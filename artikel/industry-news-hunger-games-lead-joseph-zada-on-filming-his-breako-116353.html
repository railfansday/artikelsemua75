Industry observers note that when it comes to taking on the leading role in one of the most beloved film (and literary) franchises, sometimes you just have to jump right in. At least that was the case for Joseph Zada, whose first day on set of “The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping” was spent filming the opening cornucopia scene where nearly 50 children are released to fight to the death.

According to recent reports, it’s quite the introduction for Zada, who stars as a young Haymitch Abernathy, the victor of the 50th Hunger Games and the future mentor to Katniss Everdeen (Jennifer Lawrence) and Peeta Mellark (Josh Hutcherson). (Woody Harrelson played Haymitch in the 2010s movies.) When Haymitch is selected for the Second Quarter Quell, the odds seem stacked against him. He should be celebrating his 16th birthday, but instead, he’s fated to enter the deadly arena as one of 48 tributes — double the usual number for Panem’s annual Hunger Games. There’s a certain amount of pressure that comes with stepping into such an iconic role, but Zada relished being thrown into the chaos. The first five weeks of filming were dedicated to shooting inside the arena — a “genius” scheduling choice, the 21-year-old actor tells Variety — because it let Zada fully immerse himself in the environment that would change Haymitch forever.

“It was really good for stepping into the character’s body, and I started mainly working by myself and living in my character by myself before I had to go on and work with veterans in this crazy cast,” Zada says, referring to the film’s star-studded ensemble that includes Glenn Close, Ralph Fiennes, Jesse Plemons and Oscar winner Kieran Culkin. “I was able to just get on set and be confident and work with the younger actors first before I had to go into the bigger scenes and heavy dialogue.”.

In a recent development, starting with such an intense sequence wasn’t an issue for Zada; he leaned into the movie’s intensity from the moment he auditioned, which occurred before Suzanne Collins’ novel was published. Zada was filming Netflix’s “East of Eden,” but he flew home to Australia to put together his tape. The instructions were simple enough: improvise a scene for a younger version of Woody Harrelson’s Haymitch. But Zada has never been one to take the easy route. In a recent development, zada wrote four scenes that required using a drone and finding a stand-in for Maysilee, the character ultimately played by Mckenna Grace. He also enlisted help from his father and brother, who are also actors. The art of elevating an audition has become something of a family tradition for the Zadas, whether casting directors ask for it or not. (“They usually don’t,” Zada quips.). In a recent development, “I didn’t go to acting school, but I have my own acting school at home with my older brother and my dad, where we did like four years of auditions,” he explains. “A standard audition is like propping yourself up against a white wall, and then just shooting and reading the scene, but we always just made a meal out of everything. We’d turn it into a little short film.”.

Zada had just two weeks to submit his audition tape, but, seeing as he was then focused on the Florence Pugh-led Netflix series and didn’t want to miss his chance to play Haymitch, he decided to take a risk.

In a recent development, “I had a two-week deadline, and then I just told them that they could have it in five weeks, because I had a whole plan for it,” he notes, laughing. “Took that risk, and it paid off!”.

Zada has been a fan of “The Hunger Games” for as long as he can remember, so joining the franchise was an opportunity he (clearly) didn’t take lightly. To prepare, Zada read Collins’ manuscript several times and, although he also studied Harrelson’s performance in the movies, he made a conscious effort to not focus too heavily on replicating his predecessor’s specific mannerisms.

“I was just more worried about the voice, and then I think everything else was going to fall into place if I just played the truth of the scene,” Zada states. “The scenes are so beautiful, and I had the book to constantly refer to. I just trusted that it would all work out, and the fans would enjoy it.”.

Zada was also tasked with executing some of the film’s physically demanding action scenes. Between the combat, explosions and other deadly threats, there’s no shortage of danger surrounding Haymitch. One particular explosion sequence, which features a dramatic slow-motion shot, ultimately became Zada’s favorite moment to watch back.

It has been revealed that “It’s ridiculous!” he laughs. “I look like a fucking action star! It’s just not real. Like I’m watching it, and that is not me. There’s no way that I’m doing that right now. I look so cool!”.

Another particularly intense sequence was an underwater scene that Zada filmed on his final day of production. To prepare, Zada spent three weeks taking scuba lessons, training on the weekends while filming during the week. For the scene, he would descend underwater with an oxygen tank; then, when he heard “Action,” the crew would remove his air supply and activate the jets. It took some trial and error to determine how much force was needed to pull off the right look.

Sources indicate that “One time, they made the jets too heavy, and I flew off it and actually hit a pole,” he recalls. “They had to get someone to come down and give me air. Pretty scary!”.

Sources indicate that readers of Collins’ novel know that Haymitch experiences considerable trauma after the games, too, which plays out over the film’s final 30 minutes. Zada filmed at least one emotionally demanding scene a week and claims it was “unavoidable” for the experience to take a toll on him as an actor.

“Halfway through my week, I’d just be extremely depressed for an entire day,” he recalls. “It was really a lot.”.

Then, during reshoots, Zada had to revisit those taxing moments over nearly three consecutive days, sometimes shooting as many as 10 scenes in a single day. Even when a scene amounted to only a brief moment in a montage, the actor had to find his way back into the same devastated headspace.

Sources indicate that “I don’t understand actors that are able to switch in and out of it. I have to kind of stay in it for a while before [the scene] and, inevitably, after,” Zada states. “Right now, in my career, I’m not really scared of just going to those places a lot.”.

But even with the physical danger and emotional demands, the experience was well worth the risk for Zada, who hopes to approach every project with the same level of commitment.

According to recent reports, “I just want to throw myself completely in the deep end with every project,” Zada claims. “It helps so much, so much having that in the arena, because that’s just my character the whole time. He’s really going for it, and I was like, ‘There’s no way for me to do this believably unless I’m throwing myself down that mountain or really pushing myself to the edge of death and injuring myself every day.’ There was no other way for me to do that and make it believable.”.

Not many people can understand what it’s like to star in an immensely popular book-to-screen adaptation like this, but Zada got to meet one of them at “The Hunger Games” prequel’s NYC premiere last Monday. Of course, Harrelson, Jennifer Lawrence and Josh Hutcherson were there, but so was Walker Scobell, who plays the titular demigod in “Percy Jackson and the Olympians.” Fans quickly recognized the similarities between the two curly-haired protagonists fighting for a better world — not to mention that Season 3 of the Disney+ show shared a release date with the “Hunger Games” prequel.

“I’m excited to see what Walker’s done with the latest season,” Zada states about his Hollywood compatriot. “He’s a fantastic young actor, and I’m happy that we’re grouped together.”.

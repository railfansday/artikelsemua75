November’s full moon on Nov. 24 is the first of three supermoons in a row, and it will pass close to the Pleiades

The November 24, 2026 Beaver Moon will be the first of three consecutive supermoons, with views of the Pleiades, Mars and Jupiter before dawn.

The full Moon of November 24, 2026, will be a supermoon, the first of three consecutive supermoons that run through January.

In a recent development, the Moon turns full at 9:53 a.m. EST (14:53 UT), the U.S. Naval Observatory calculates. That is midmorning on the East Coast, so the best views come before dawn and again that evening.

Before sunrise, the nearly full Moon will pass close to the Pleiades star cluster, while Mars and Jupiter shine in another part of the sky.

Sources indicate that the Moon’s orbit is slightly oval, so its distance from Earth changes. At its closest point, called perigee, it is about 226,000 miles (363,300 kilometers) away, and at its farthest about 251,000 miles (405,500 kilometers).

It has been revealed that A supermoon occurs when a full Moon coincides with that closest approach, NASA explains. The word is not an official astronomical term and typically describes a full Moon within at least 90 percent of perigee.

Such a Moon can appear up to 14 percent bigger and 30 percent brighter than the faintest full Moon of the year.

Industry observers note that “It might be hard to detect a supermoon visually, but it does have an effect on Earth,” the space agency’s page states. A Moon at perigee can cause higher tides than usual. (Related: Industry News: Hyperparameter Search with Transformers ...)

Measured against an average full Moon, the gain is smaller: up to 7 percent larger and 15 percent brighter, the Royal Observatory Greenwich notes. (Related: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Faster Language M...)

When it turns full, the Moon will be roughly 224,200 miles (360,800 kilometers) from Earth. That is around 12,400 miles (20,000 kilometers) closer than average, by the Greenwich astronomers’ figures.

Recent findings suggest that perigee itself arrives about 30 hours later, on November 25 at 4:01 p.m. EST. The Moon will then be 223,400 miles (359,567 kilometers) away, a government almanac from Land Information Fresh Zealand demonstrates.

December’s full Moon falls on December 23 at 8:28 p.m. EST. Perigee follows about seven hours later at 221,800 miles (356,903 kilometers), the closest of the three.

The third full Moon comes on January 22, 2027, at 7:17 a.m. EST. Perigee occurs the previous afternoon, at 222,200 miles (357,530 kilometers).

According to recent reports, measured at perigee, November’s supermoon is therefore the most distant of the trio. It follows the Beaver Moon of November 2025.

It has been revealed that U.S. Naval Observatory rise and set tables put moonset in Upcoming York at 7:05 a.m. EST, 12 minutes after sunrise. In Los Angeles, the Moon sets at 6:49 a.m. PST.

Early risers should look low in the west, toward Taurus, the constellation that holds the Pleiades.

Industry observers note that the International Occultation Timing Association lists that date among the Moon’s 2026 passages over the cluster. In an occultation, the Moon moves in front of a star and hides it.

The listing does not rank it among the year’s best passages. Bright moonlight will also make the cluster’s faint stars harder to pick out.

Higher up, Mars and Jupiter have been neighbors all month. They appeared about 1.2 degrees apart on the mornings of November 15 and 16, slightly more than two full Moon widths.

In a recent development, jupiter is the brighter of the two, and Mars has a reddish tint. Binoculars or a low-powered telescope, under 40x magnification, give a closer look.

The planets have since drifted apart and stand high in the south before dawn, near Regulus, the brightest star in Leo.

Such Mars and Jupiter pairings recur every 2 to 2.5 years on average, by the Royal Observatory’s count. Mars circles the Sun faster and laps Jupiter in the night sky.

Recent findings suggest that the second chance comes at dusk. “Look east around sunset to watch it rise,” NASA’s November skywatching guide advises.

According to recent reports, “It may look even bigger near the horizon, but that’s the Moon illusion,” the guide adds. The effect is common to any full Moon.

Moonrise in Upcoming York is at 4:16 p.m. EST, 16 minutes before sunset. Los Angeles sees the Moon come up at 4:43 p.m. PST, two minutes ahead of the Sun’s setting.

Bill Ingalls, then NASA’s senior photographer, shared supermoon photography tips in 2016.

According to recent reports, “Don’t make the mistake of photographing the moon by itself with no reference to anything,” Ingalls noted. “I’ve certainly done it myself, but everyone will get that shot.”.

He suggested including a local landmark to give a photo a sense of place. For phone cameras, he advised holding a finger on the Moon to lock focus, then sliding it to adjust exposure.

In a recent development, beaver Moon is a nickname for November’s full Moon. The name draws on several traditions and folklore in Native American and European culture.

Industry observers note that one explanation is that beavers spend November fortifying their dams and stocking food for winter. The month was also the season to trap the animals for their thick, winter-ready pelts.

“There is disagreement over the origin of November’s ‘Beaver Moon’ name,” the Royal Observatory’s monthly highlights state.

The word “supermoon” was coined in 1979, the same 2024 NASA post says.

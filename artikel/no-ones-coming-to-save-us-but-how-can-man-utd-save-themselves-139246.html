Manchester United lost 4-0 at Liverpool in their opening match under Mauricio Pochettino.

Manchester United's Premier League trip to Anfield was described by some as a free hit for upcoming head coach Mauricio Pochettino.

The theory was expectations for United were so low as they travelled to Liverpool that a positive outcome would be a huge bonus, and anything else was expected.

But Pochettino did not look like a man without pressure after 15 minutes of what turned into a 4-0 defeat.

Recent findings suggest that by that point, his side were already 2-0 behind and down to 10 men. Liverpool's fans were gleefully singing about the Argentine being "sacked in the morning", even though he was only a sixth of the way through his first game in charge.

A day later, United supporters began filtering through Manchester Airport on their way to Lisbon for a Champions League match that marks the next stop in what has turned into a desperate campaign.

The prevailing mood was one of resigned acceptance punctuated by dark humour around what a season in the Championship might look like, and whether Manchester City might be there with them.

United's England midfielder Kobbie Mainoo summed the situation up when he spoke to MUTV after the final whistle at Anfield.

"We're at the lowest we've ever been and no-one's coming to save us," mentioned the 21-year-old.

"There are no helping hands coming, so we've got to work together, lean on each other, keep working and hopefully it will come good.

"It's all about character now.".

Pochettino has signed a two-and-a-half-year contract.

United are giving little away about their aims for the January transfer window.

Depending on finances, a striker and left-back are priorities but they still have nine games to play before they can make any changes to their squad. And even then, it is not certain to what extent it can happen.

Among those fixtures are home games against Coventry, Nottingham Forest and Sunderland.

Industry observers note that newly promoted Coventry are four points ahead of United, who are second from bottom. Forest and Sunderland are in the two positions immediately above the Old Trafford outfit. (Related: Latest Update: How to train a newly introduced language...)

A trip to Crystal Palace - the one side below United, and the other Premier League club to replace their coach during the international break - looms on 13 December.

After Wednesday's encounter with Sporting, Pochettino's side have one Champions League game before the end of the year - against RB Leipzig on 8 December.

According to recent reports, any solutions to be found to the current mess over the next five weeks have to come from within. If they don't, light-hearted discussions about a first relegation since 1973-74 under Tommy Docherty start to get serious.

"They have got enough in that squad to get themselves out of the bottom three," Ashley Young - a Premier League winner with United - told Match of the Day.

In a recent development, "They have not been showing that, but Pochettino has only just come in. He is going to want to get his team set up in a way that they are hard to beat. Right now, they do not look like they are hard to beat.

Sources indicate that "It doesn't look like there is that togetherness. He has got to find a way to get that out of them. I am sure he will be able to do that when he has been longer in the job.".

Man Utd shouldn't worry about relegation right now - Young.

Pochettino shut down a question about United's terrible recent defensive record when he spoke to the media immediately after the game at Anfield.

Recent findings suggest that "Tell me only about the circumstance today," he said. "I wasn't [here] in the past.".

That is true - and may also have been a useful diversion exercise in avoiding a dissection of a defensive unit he is still getting to know.

According to recent reports, however, Pochettino knows United have kept one clean sheet all season - against Azerbaijani champions Sabah FK in September. He will also know United have now conceded 19 goals in their past six games.

Pochettino's big decision at Liverpool was to pick Matthijs de Ligt in the centre of a three-man defence ahead of Harry Maguire.

"He played in the Champions League you know, before the internationals," mentioned the former Tottenham, PSG and Chelsea boss.

It was not clear when Pochettino meant.

Sources indicate that in the 4-1 loss at Como on 21 October, De Ligt made his first appearance in 11 months following major back problems that eventually required surgery, largely because Leny Yoro was suspended and Lisandro Martinez was injured. He then played for two minutes against Roma on 3 November, when he was thrown on as then manager Michael Carrick searched desperately for a winner.

Most goals can be broken down into a number of mistakes that compound to make an eventual negative outcome.

According to recent reports, team positioning has been mentioned as a reason De Ligt found himself exposed under a high ball at Anfield. However, his failure to deal with it presented Alexander Isak with a clear run at goal and prompted Yoro to make the tackle that led to his controversial dismissal, which Pochettino felt shaped the 82 minutes that followed.

But United have been conceding bad goals all season. Numerous players have been culpable.

Sources indicate that there is a limit to what Pochettino can do in the time available. However, he wants to maximise what he can do.

Whereas Carrick tended to let his starting players have a day off after matches, the newly introduced regime has decided a warm-down session at Carrington is the better option.

And defensive organisation was surely one of the key points his coaching team had on their list of things to work on when the players began preparations to face Sporting on Wednesday.

In his recent interview with Sky Sports, United chief executive Omar Berrada mentioned the club needed to be "disciplined" in the transfer market and avoid spending huge sums on players who would not necessarily make the squad better.

“The only Claude you need is Monet,” the sign reads. It’s part of the latest wave of messages in SAM’s “Dear Seattle” campaign, which has spent the fall urging people to look up from their screens and see something in person.

Earlier billboards around the city told passersby to “come stare at something that isn’t your inbox” and noted that “even the algorithm thinks you should get out more.” The final batch pushes the AI jokes further, including one asking for “a little more art and a little less artificial” and another promising, “Your phone doesn’t have to know.”. Tech billboards are a fixture in the San Francisco Bay Area, where AI startups cover Highway 101 and city streets with ads that often mean little to anyone outside the industry.

Recent findings suggest that they’re less common around Seattle. Anthropic put up a substantial orange Claude billboard over Aurora Avenue in 2024, and this spring Bellevue startup Summation put one up along SR 520 because “startup energy should be more visible around here,” its CEO told GeekWire.

Recent findings suggest that “In a city where so much of our lives happens on screens, we’re making a playful case for the things you can only experience in person,” Kat Bryant Flaherty, SAM’s director of communications, stated in a statement. She listed “the scale and texture of an artwork, a film with a room full of strangers, making something with your hands, or catching up with a friend over a meal.”.

The campaign is tied to changes at the downtown museum itself, where SAM is adding more public space and more reasons to linger, including hands-on art making and music in the Studio at SAM and the newly opened Bar Dojo restaurant.

The campaign runs through the end of the year, with more than 200 ads across the region — more than 20 billboards plus spots on light rail and digital kiosks.

SAM’s campaign is about the same size as the one Bellevue startup Statsig ran in the Bay Area in 2024, with more than 200 billboards and transit posters, but pointed in the opposite direction.

SAM is offering buy-one-get-one-free tickets in December with the code “LOVESAM,” good at both the downtown museum and the Seattle Asian Art Museum in Volunteer Park, to encourage people to visit together.

“We’re hoping to grab attention, spark curiosity, and remind Seattle that there’s a lot happening at SAM worth leaving the house for,” Flaherty said.

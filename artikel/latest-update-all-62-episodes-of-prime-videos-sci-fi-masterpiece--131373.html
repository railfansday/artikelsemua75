Industry observers note that all 62 episodes of Prime Video's acclaimed sci-fi masterpiece deliver gripping interplanetary warfare, extraordinary characters, and epic storytelling. Released back in 2015, this dystopian sci-fi series spanned six seasons on Prime Video until it concluded in 2022, based on a series of celebrated novels that began with 2011's Leviathan Wakes. Praised for its elite world-building, science-backed mechanisms, and great visuals, this is one sci-fi series that all fans of the genre must check out, as all 62 episodes are truly perfect from start to finish.

Sources indicate that the Expanse is a true sci-fi masterpiece on Prime Video, given its 95% Rotten Tomatoes score across 62 episodes and 6 seasons. The series delivers peak dystopian sci-fi on streaming and still sets the gold standard for the genre 11 years after its premiere on Prime Video. Critics and audiences agree that it is, somewhat quietly, one of the greatest sci-fi series of the 21st century, with The Ringer saying of its final season, "For all the worthy comparisons to Game of Thrones, there's one thing The Expanse achieved that its HBO counterpart couldn't: This show stuck the landing." The Expanse has one of the most intriguing premises with a massive scope in all modern sci-fi set between conflicting communities on Earth and in outer space. With each competing for political power and cosmic influence, The Expanse is set several centuries in the future, when humans have colonized several areas of the solar system, including Mars and the Asteroid Belt between Mars and Jupiter. Industry observers note that when the series begins, Earth remains the most dominant force within the solar system, but a newly advanced society and military presence on Mars threatens to disrupt that order. Earth's key vulnerability, however, is its dependence on off-planet resources from far-off colonies.

Much like the distribution of power between nations on Earth, colonies each import, export, and trade resources, which become the driving forces behind power and control in this newly introduced dynamic. This is one of the ways that The Expanse feels reality-based and plausible given the circumstances, while also maintaining modern science elements that never lean too heavily into the fantasy genre.

Sources indicate that even the battles that take place in outer space are based on realistic physics, separating it from other epic space franchises such as Dune and Star Wars. This emphasis on political considerations and resource allocation resembles the most crucial and basic foundation of human civilization and conflicts between world superpowers.

While The Expanse carries a vast scale across planets and colonies, the heart of the series throughout all six seasons is the crew of the Rocinante spaceship. Featuring members from Earth, Mars, and the Belt, the crew forms an unlikely bond and allegiance despite their cultural differences, which forms the emotional core of the series. Several key figures include James Holden (Steven Strait), an ex-military officer and captain of the ship, Naomi Nagata (Dominique Tipper), an engineer from the Belt, Amos Burton (Wes Chatham), a mechanic from Earth, and Alex Kamal (Cas Anvar), a pilot from Mars.

These four begin on the Canterbury, a freighter ship transporting water from Saturn's rings to the Belt, which is destroyed in season 1. James, Naomi, Amos, and Alex must work together to survive and discover who destroyed the Canterbury and why. As their journey continues, they eventually come into possession of a Martian gunship they rename the Rocinante, a reference to the famous 1605 novel Don Quixote. All this only scratches the surface of the characters and story in The Expanse, which has a fresh prequel series set to be released in 2027. It's truly a modern sci-fi masterpiece that is perfect from start to finish across all 62 episodes on Prime Video.

50 Years Ago Today: The Beach Boys Play Pivotal ‘Brian’s Back’ Concert in Cincinnati

It has been revealed that “Brian is the Beach Boys, and we’re his messengers,” Dennis Wilson told People magazine in its August 1976 cover story about the band. “We’re thrilled to have him back.”.

It has been revealed that just a few years earlier, the idea of a 1960s has-been group appearing on the cover of a mainstream magazine like People would have seemed absurd, about as likely as Herman’s Hermits or the Dave Clark Five having a major resurgence. But the Beach Boys were the beneficiaries of an enormous nostalgia wave that swept America in the mid-1970s, giving us American Graffiti, Happy Days, Sha Na Na, and Grease. The Beach Boys weren’t able to generate much interest in their newly introduced work, but their 1960s compilation Endless Summer hit Number One in October 1974. As a result, they dumped most of the recent material from their live repertoire and began playing arenas and even stadiums. “We knocked them dead every time we went out just playing the hits,” Al Jardine told Rolling Stone in 2025. “It allowed us to grow musically at home while we could continue to entertain. It was a nice balance.”. (Related: Industry News: “Determined” woman completes 7 marathons...)

But by 1976, as Endless Summer mania began petering out, the group needed a newly introduced gambit to keep drawing the major crowds. That’s why band manager Steve Love decided to rouse Brian Wilson from his bed and get him back on the road for the first time since 1964, generating a groundswell of interest in the band.

Recent findings suggest that they promoted the “Brian’s Back” campaign with an NBC television special put together by Lorne Michaels and the original Saturday Night Live team, which included a legendary sketch where Dan Aykroyd and John Belushi storm into Brian’s bedroom as the “Surf Police” and force him to surf for the first time. Annie Leibovitz shot a classic photo of Wilson standing on the sand that day in his blue robe, which appeared on the Nov. 4, 1976, cover of Rolling Stone.

Much of the press outside of Rolling Stone presented Wilson as a goofy eccentric rather than a genuine genius. “Brian is like an amiable child (violence was never one of his symptoms) trying to relearn the simple social graces,” People wrote. “As Brian told Mike [Love] lately, ‘I’m tired of being a failure.'” Editor’s picks The 250 Greatest Albums of the 21st Century So Far The 100 Best TV Episodes of All Time The 500 Greatest Albums of All Time 100 Best Movies of the 21st Century.

According to recent reports, that’s why he decided not only to join the tour but also to produce a new Beach Boys record for the first time since Pet Sounds. The result was the underwhelming 15 Major Ones, which is thin on new originals and heavy on classic covers like Chuck Berry’s “Rock and Roll Music,” the Dixie Cups’ “Chapel of Love,” and Fats Domino’s “Blueberry Hill.”.

In a recent development, brian Wilson was an infrequent presence throughout their demonstrates in the summer of 1976, but fully committed to a winter tour in November and December that followed a two-month hiatus from the road, giving him time to record The Beach Boys Love You. The run of dates began Nov. 22 at the Riverfront Coliseum in Cincinnati, Ohio. Tickets ($7.50 in advance and $8.50 on show day) were unreserved throughout the entire venue, which encouraged fans to line up all day to secure choice spots.

Industry observers note that (Three years later, 11 Who fans were trampled to death when doors opened at this same venue. As a result, Cincinnati outlawed “festival seating” at concerts. The ban wouldn’t be lifted until 2002, when Bruce Springsteen and the E Street Band played an arena show at the U.S. Bank Arena on the Rising tour.).

The Beach Boys show opens with “Wouldn’t It Be Nice” and “Palisades Park,” before Mike Love welcomes “my cousin, the one and only, the magnificent, Brian Wilson!” The crowd roars as he briefly stands up from his perch behind the piano, and then they kick into “Little Deuce Coupe,” “Darlin’,” “In My Room,” and “God Only Knows” while the crowd squeals with delight. “Brian was introduced early on in the first set, and most of the crowd stood to acknowledge one of the giants in the history of rock,” noted a review by Dennis Freeman of the News Record. “He didn’t do much, sat impassively behind his piano, but it was key to the group that he was with them again.”.

It has been revealed that after warming the crowd up with oldies, they shifted the show to the present with Al Jardine’s “Back Home” from 15 Substantial Ones. It goes over particularly well with this crowd since it’s all about going back to Jardine’s birthplace of Ohio. “Well, I’m going back this summer to Ohio,” he sings. “I’m going to seek out all my friends I’ve always known/I’m going back to that farm that I remember.” (The song fell out of the band’s rotation in 1978 and wasn’t brought back until a Jardine and the Pet Sounds Band concert in Warren, Ohio, earlier this year.).

“How would you like to hear a couple of new ones we haven’t even done for the public yet?” Mike Love asks the crowd after “Back Home.” “These aren’t even released yet. They will be out in January on our new album. Keep it quiet and don’t go singing to too many groups since they might rip it off. The first one we’re going to do is called ‘Love Is a Woman.’ It sends chills up and down where I won’t tell you. The other one is a song about being on an airplane, so we call it ‘Airplane.’ So listen real good. We hope you like it.”.

Sources indicate that nobody in the arena had any idea they were witnessing a historic moment. This was the first time anyone outside the tight Beach Boys circle had heard even a note of music from The Beach Boys Love You. And even though many fans likely used this as an opportunity to run to the bathroom or grab a beer, the group did a magnificent job on both. “Airplane” is especially transcendent. (Sadly, they’d only play Love You songs a handful more times, and they wouldn’t go near any of it after 1979. It wasn’t until Al Jardine’s inaugural tour with the Pet Sounds Band in 2025 that Love You finally got the treatment it deserved in front of live audiences.).

They win the audience back with a trio of 1960s songs (“Catch a Wave,” “Heroes and Villains,” and “Surfin’ USA”) before introducing the crowd to a local woman named Joellyn Lambert, a former taxi driver who drove Jardine through town in 1970 and inspired his song “Susie Cincinnati.” They recorded it for 15 Significant Ones and placed an ad in the local paper prior to the show seeking her out. “Attention!!!” reads the notice. “The Beach Boys are looking for the lady cab driver named Suzi who picked them up six years from the airport. Group wants the person to be guest at their November 22 concert at Riverfront Coliseum.”.

Lambert was stunned when the Cincinnati Post tracked her down and arranged the visit. “I still can’t believe this is really happening,” she told the paper. “I’m now stopped on the street and asked if I’m really ‘Susie Cincinnati.'”.

The deep cuts continued with “It’s Okay,” “A Casual Look,” “Feel Flows,” and “Everyone’s in Love With You” before they closed out the night with a long run of surf-era hits that finished with “Fun, Fun, Fun.”.

“We danced, clapped and sang our way through classic after classic, each song bringing back memories of summers past,” wrote Freeman in the News Record. “‘Surfer Girl’ led into ‘California Girls’ and climaxed with ‘Good Vibrations.’ The audience was pretty young, mainly 18-year-olds, and it was fascinating to note the songs which were greeted with the most applause were the group’s early pre-Beatles hits.”.

“It shows the timelessness of the Beach Boys’ music,” he continued. “It also indicates that rock critics are wrong that time is up for the group. Even if the apparent re-emergence of the group as a creative force is short-lived, be assured the people will want to hear and see the Beach Boys for a long time to come.”.

They would indeed. But what they didn’t want to hear or see was basically any song they recorded after “Do It Again.” Those fans were in luck since the Beach Boys essentially abandoned any pretense that they were anything but a perpetual-motion 1960s jukebox not long after the 1976 Brian’s Back tour. The idea of seven songs from a fresh album and another two from an album that wasn’t even released yet, as they did in Cincinnati, soon became unthinkable. It has been revealed that the Beach Boys returned to Cincinnati on Oct. 10 of this year for a show at the PNC Pavilion at Riverbend. Love is now the final legit member in the touring lineup, and this was a deeply upsetting concert for many fans since it was the first one where he was unable to stand or even sing. He’s been battling a severe health ailment for at least the past year and hasn’t been seen since a show the following night in Nashville.

According to recent reports, whatever happens going forward, the News Record was prophetic back in 1976 when it said “people will want to hear and see the Beach Boys for a long time to come.” Does that mean they’ll become a zombie group like Foreigner or the Four Tops that continues to tour without any original members? Could Jardine finally be given the chance to front his own version of the Beach Boys? Are their children going to take over? There’s simply no way to know at this point. But it’s very hard to imagine a summer passing without some version of the Beach Boys on the road.

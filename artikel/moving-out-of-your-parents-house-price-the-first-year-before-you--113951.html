📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that moving out of your parents’ house costs more than the rent, so price the whole first year before you commit. Count the cash it takes to move in, add up a month of rent and bills, and check that total against your take-home pay. Then save one to three months of that total before you sign a lease.

Moving in can cost several thousand dollars, most of it due before you get the keys.

According to recent reports, most landlords want the first month’s rent and a security deposit that’s often the same amount, and some ask for the last month’s rent too. The Federal Trade Commission says a utility can ask a upcoming customer for a deposit as well. Ask whether the utility will take a letter of guarantee from a parent instead, a written promise to cover your bill if you fall behind. In a recent development, next, price a normal month: rent, utilities, internet, renters insurance, groceries, getting to work, and your phone if you’re coming off a family blueprint. Check that total against your take-home pay, the amount that reaches your account after taxes and deductions, not your salary.

Say you take home $3,200 a month, rent is $1,050, and the other bills come to $950, so a month costs $2,000 and leaves $1,200 for loan payments, savings, and everything else. Moving in takes about $3,500, which covers the first month’s rent, a deposit of the same amount, $200 to set up utilities, $900 for secondhand furniture and kitchen gear, and $300 for the move. A three-month cushion adds $6,000, so the goal is about $9,500 saved before you sign.

The cushion covers a car repair or a gap between jobs, so neither goes on a credit card. Make it at least a month of rent and bills, and three if your hours or pay vary. If the numbers don’t work yet, a roommate or a cheaper area lowers your rent, and the deposit and cushion you need fall with it.

Work out your own target before you tour a single apartment. Then pay yourself first until you reach it. Move part of every paycheck into savings the day it arrives, before you spend on anything else.

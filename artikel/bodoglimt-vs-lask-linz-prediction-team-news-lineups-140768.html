Hoping to pick up where they left off prior to the international break, LASK Linz will travel to face Bodo/Glimt at Aspmyra Stadion for a Champions League league-phase showdown on Tuesday.

Glimt are hoping to climb into the playoff spots, while the Black-Whites are looking to push closer to the top eight.

Kjetil Knutsen's Bodo/Glimt have endured a difficult European campaign to date, winning just one of their four Champions League outings, beating Borussia Dortmund 1-0 in mid-October.

On November 3, Glimt were held to a 0-0 stalemate by Ligue 1's Lille, blanking for the second time in the league phase as part of a concerning trend that has seen the team muster the joint third worst goal tally in the competition, with only PSV and Slovan Bratislava netting fewer than their four.

That frustrating draw has left Knutsen's side 25th in the Champions League table with just four points, though they are level with all of the clubs up to 20th-placed Roma.

Additionally, Bodo/Glimt are a mere two points shy of their opponents, and will be optimistic about their chances of climbing up into one of the playoff qualification spots in midweek.

With that aim in mind, fans of the hosts will be buoyed by their team's record on home turf, where they have lost just one of their 15 outings across all competitions since early May, winning 13 times in the process.

Meanwhile, Dietmar Kuhbauer's LASK kicked off their league phase with two defeats, but have bounced back in impressive fashion to record consecutive wins ahead of Tuesday's clash.

Last time out in the Champions League, the Austrian champions hammered Slovan Bratislava 4-0 in Linz, racing into a 3-0 lead by half time courtesy of a brace from Kasper Jorgensen and a follow-up by Robert Ljubicic.

That victory came as part of a run that has seen Kuhbauer's men net at least four goals in three of their past five matches, and considering they have scored 10 in UEFA's premier club competition, they are likely to pose a threat in midweek.

On the other hand, LASK have conceded nine times across their four Champions League games so far - the joint second worst total in the top 18 - and they will need to be wary of surrendering chances on the road.

The visitors arrive 16th in the table with six points, a tally that puts them two points clear of 25th-placed Bodo/Glimt and three short of eighth-placed Liverpool.

Bodo/Glimt will be without midfielder Hakon Evjen, who unfortunately suffered further damage to his cruciate ligament while recovering from a previous knee injury, and could reportedly be sidelined for the rest of the season.

In his absence, Patrick Berg, Sondre Fet and Sondre Auklend look set to start in the centre of the park, platforming a front three of Jens Petter Hauge, Ole Didrik Blomberg and Andreas Helmersen, while August Mikkelsen is dealing with a groin problem.

Elsewhere, backup goalkeeper Isak Sjong is a doubt, though Nikita Haikin should be ready to line up between the sticks on Tuesday.

As for LASK, they are missing injured left-back George Bello, so expect to see Yvan Dibango and Kasper Jorgensen operate as wing-backs, either side of a three-man defence featuring Xavier Mbuyamba, Andres Andrade and Alemao.

The visitors also have question marks about the fitness of right-sided attacker Moses Usor, and if he is unavailable, Christoph Lang should join Samuel Adeniran up top in midweek.

Bodo/Glimt may have found wins hard to come by in the Champions League, but they are unbeaten on home turf in the competition and could prove stubborn opposition once again.

LASK will be confident going forward, though their defensive frailty could cost them at least two points on Tuesday.

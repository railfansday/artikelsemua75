📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

CrowdStrike and Zscaler announced a definitive acquisition agreement on November 23, 2026, under which CrowdStrike would acquire Zscaler in an all-stock transaction valuing Zscaler at an enterprise value of approximately $43.9 billion. The deal would add network controls and AI-agent connection security to CrowdStrike’s Falcon platform.

Each outstanding Zscaler share would be exchanged for 1.135 CrowdStrike shares, implying approximately $260.41 per Zscaler share based on CrowdStrike’s November 20, 2026 closing price and a roughly 34% premium to Zscaler’s closing price on that date. Both companies’ boards unanimously approved the agreement. Existing CrowdStrike and Zscaler shareholders are expected to own approximately 84.1% and 15.9%, respectively, of the combined firm after completion. The acquisition would take CrowdStrike into network security, reflecting a model in which people, applications, and AI agents connect directly to cloud services and their traffic does not return through the data center. Firewalls and VPNs built to guard data centers cannot see, inspect, or stop that traffic. Zscaler’s Zero Trust Exchange sits inline between users, agents, and applications to enforce zero-trust controls on every connection. It has been revealed that that position would complement CrowdStrike’s controls over endpoints, cloud workloads, identities, and data. After closing, Falcon would add Zscaler’s Zero Trust SASE, Security for AI, and Data Protection offerings, placing controls both where an agent runs and where its connections travel. CrowdStrike CEO and founder George Kurtz summarized the change: “The network used to connect people to places. Now it connects agents to data.”.

Recent findings suggest that the plan also joins the companies’ telemetry. Falcon Next-Gen SIEM would unify device and network data in one security dataset, while Falcon Flex would let customers add either manufacturer’s products under a single contract. CrowdStrike says Falcon generates trillions of endpoint and cloud-workload events daily, Zscaler processes more than 750 billion transactions per day, and the combined data would let its SafeMind frontier security models train on the adversary’s full attack path.

The companies report that their existing technology alliance spans more than 2,000 shared customers and 16 integrations, while approximately 8,000 Zscaler customers remain outside CrowdStrike’s customer base. That gap creates a cross-selling base for Falcon. Zscaler protects half of the Fortune 500 and operates its distributed inline security cloud across more than 200 service locations.

The companies project more than $10 billion in combined annual recurring revenue at closing, including Zscaler’s $3.9 billion as of its fiscal first quarter of 2027.

Industry observers note that crowdStrike estimates that the acquisition will expand its total addressable market by more than $185 billion in calendar year 2029. That figure describes potential demand rather than current revenue.

CrowdStrike targets approximately $1 billion in annualized run-rate revenue synergies by the end of the first 30 months after closing and $500 million in annualized run-rate cost synergies within 18 to 24 months. The revenue benefits would come from deeper customer relationships, cross-selling and upselling, and integration with Falcon Flex; cost savings would primarily come from optimizing data-center infrastructure, vendors, and broader operations.

Recent findings suggest that the transaction is expected to close in the second half of CrowdStrike’s fiscal year 2028, subject to Zscaler shareholder approval, required regulatory approvals, and other customary conditions. Both companies will operate independently until closing. Zscaler founder, chairman, and CEO Jay Chaudhry and his affiliates, representing approximately 34% of Zscaler’s outstanding shares, have signed voting agreements supporting the transaction.

After closing, Chaudhry is expected to continue leading the Zscaler business and join CrowdStrike’s board of directors.

The proposed combination would move customers from integrations between separate products toward one data layer and one contract. Its security value rests on whether that shared dataset can give CrowdStrike a broader view of AI-agent connections and attack paths, turning the companies’ complementary control points into more effective protection.

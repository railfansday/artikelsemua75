The KBW Bank Index, a 24-member gauge of substantial U.S. banks, has lost as much as 7.7% since Monday, putting it on course for its steepest weekly drop since April 2025, according to Bloomberg.

According to recent reports, shares of Morgan Stanley $MS and Goldman Sachs $GS each fell more than 10% this week, placing them among the index's worst performers, according to Bloomberg. In a recent development, financial stocks slid on Thursday after JPMorgan $JPM Chase, Visa $V and Mastercard $MA disclosed that an attack targeting one of their vendors had exposed certain customers' details, according to Bloomberg. That followed earlier disruptions this week, including an outage at the National Security Agency's internal communications network and a "technical matter" at JPMorgan, according to Bloomberg.

In a recent development, "Bank stocks are getting hit by a confluence of macro concerns rather than anything fundamentally newly introduced with the banks themselves," Bloomberg Intelligence analyst Herman Chan mentioned, according to Bloomberg. "The broader risk-off move in technology hasn't helped either, particularly for capital-markets-oriented banks.".

Chan also pointed to inflation and the Federal Reserve. "Persistent inflation has put higher-for-longer rates back at the forefront, with Fed officials raising the possibility of a 50-basis-point hike in December," he remarked, according to Bloomberg. "Renewed geopolitical tensions around Iran are adding another layer of uncertainty and could complicate the inflation outlook through energy prices.".

Industry observers note that the KBW index formed a "death cross" on Thursday, a bearish technical signal that may point to more short-term weakness, according to Bloomberg. Bank stocks have given back all of the advance they had built up since January.

Recent findings suggest that the sector was already under pressure before this week. In October, the index was headed for a sixth straight weekly loss and had fallen 13% from its August record, putting it in correction territory. Worries about how artificial intelligence will disrupt financial services were weighing on the stocks then.

Sources indicate that the wider market has also dropped. Through Thursday's close, the Dow Jones Industrial Average was down 6.2% for the week, while the S&P 500 and Nasdaq $NDAQ had each lost 5% as technology stocks sold off.

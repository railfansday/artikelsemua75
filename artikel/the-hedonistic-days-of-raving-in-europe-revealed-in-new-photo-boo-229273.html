A latest photography book offers a glimpse into Europe’s underground rave culture at the turn of the millennium, documenting the free-party movement through the eyes of two photographers who were part of the scene.

Never Alone 1997–2004: Raving in Europe, by Cheyenne Clementi and Valentina Morandi, features 160 original analog photographs made between 1997 and 2004. The images document a period when free parties were spreading across Europe, offering an alternative to mainstream society through music, community, and a shared rejection of conventional lifestyles. Sources indicate that rather than documenting the movement as outsiders, Clementi and Morandi were active participants in the culture. Their photographs provide a firsthand account of a counterculture built around emotion, affection, and togetherness, at a time when music and personal connections — rather than social media and mass communication — brought people together. The book spans 196 black-and-white pages and brings together previously unseen photographs that capture the atmosphere and values of the era. Its accompanying text is written by Tobia D’Onofrio, author of Rave New World, who explores the history and significance of the European free-party movement.

“Rave was born as a disappearing ritual in which the art of invisibility was practiced, and parties were temporarily autonomous and free zones that appeared and disappeared into thin air within a few hours,” claims D’Onofrio.

Recent findings suggest that “1997–2004 can therefore be considered the golden age of the so-called illegal free parties and of rave culture. It was the moment of the phenomenon’s expansion across Europe… the rave scene had been criminalized to the point that in 1994 the Criminal Justice Act in England outlawed free parties. At that point, the energies shifted to the rest of Europe, and Italy immediately became the scene of legendary parties.”.

The book examines how rave culture developed into more than a musical movement. It represented a rejection of conventional working and social structures, embracing communal living, constant movement, and a different relationship with time and space. Free parties became meeting places for people united by anarchic sensibilities and a desire to escape everyday routines.

In a recent development, clementi and Morandi both attended the same high school in Milan before pursuing separate careers. Despite taking different paths, both remained dedicated to photography, eventually bringing their shared rave-scene experiences together in Never Alone.

The book is available online and through specialist bookstores.

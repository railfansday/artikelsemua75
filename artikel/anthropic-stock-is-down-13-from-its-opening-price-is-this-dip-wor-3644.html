Anthropic (ANT +17.07%) stock had a surprisingly lackluster first day of trading. While its opening price of $111.50 per share was well above its $83 per share IPO price, and it briefly headed toward almost $115, the stock began to slide within minutes. By the close of trading, it was at $97.17 per share — 13% below the opening price. Given that performance, investors may want to consider buying the stock, albeit with a more cautious approach.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/19/26.

Admittedly, many stocks gain ground quickly at their debut. This is especially true of well-known businesses like Anthropic, whose Claude AI Assistant has become a top choice for many customers. (Related: How we sped up transformer inference 100x for 🤗 API cus...)

Moreover, at a market cap of $1.76 trillion as of the time of this writing, Anthropic is now trading at a price-to-sales (P/S) ratio of around 50.

Sources indicate that although that amounts to a significant premium, investors might be willing to pay it given the company’s  1,127% revenue growth in the third quarter. Assuming Anthropic’s rapid growth continues for a few more quarters, the stock could actually be a bargain.

However, investors need to remember that the purpose of IPOs is to raise money, meaning they tend to occur well into bull markets. This often sets such stocks up for steep declines when the broader sentiment turns bearish, which is why these stocks can end up trading below their IPO prices for extended periods.

Industry observers note that additionally, the sell-off that took place after its brief early surge suggests that investors have less appetite for this SaaS stock than they did for Space Exploration Technologies(SPCX -3.53%). SpaceX debuted in June and surged by as much as 49% above its IPO price in its first few trading days before pulling back to well below that level over the following month. As of the close on Thursday, SpaceX was trading at around $137 — just above its IPO price, and well below its first trading price. It has been revealed that the problem with Anthropic stock is that its near-term path is uncertain. As of the time of this writing, it is unclear whether Anthropic will dive below its IPO price or gear up for a recovery.

Indeed, its rapid revenue growth and relatively low valuation relative to its sales growth outlook suggest that investors should consider buying some shares. Yet considering its volatility, as well as the commonly poor performance of IPO stocks, investors who want to add Anthropic shares to their portfolios should probably do so slowly and build their positions incrementally.

An investigation into the workplace behavior of Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang, who resigned recently after a series of extramarital affairs came to light, has uncovered decades of harassment, verbal employee abuse and "cultish" behaviour at the manufacturer which has long played a major role in PC gaming.

Following Huang's departure, The New York Times has published a report in which "more than a dozen" current and former Nvidia employees discussed their experiences with the former CEO. Speaking on conditions of anonymity, employees alleged Jensen Huang would verbally berate them, reportedly going as far as to say he'd rip their arms off, rip their heart out and stomp on it, or rip their head off and "pee on your brain". The report includes numerous examples of Huang's alleged abusive behaviour, which apparently stretches back years before Nvidia became the one of the most valuable companies in the world - years before the AI boom. It's claimed Huang on one occasion went around a room of managers telling them he'd kill them. Then, when he reached a person in their 70s, mentioned everyone hoped they'd drop dead.

It has been revealed that huang allegedly referred to these bouts of public humiliation as a "rite of passage" at Nvidia, when questioned by an internal HR employee whom he later fired for not being a "right fit". Huan's stance was apparently to test whether a person could handle the culture there, which is described in the report as "cultish". It's claimed Huang's influence at Nvidia has meant other senior managers have followed his lead, including his daughter Madison, Nvidia's chief of staff, who it's said often yells at staff, including executives more highly ranked than her.

A spokesperson for Huang denied the allegations in response to a request for comment by the New York Times. The spokesperson, John Fisher, said the claims in the report were "fabrications that are now impossible to disprove specifically, and the thousands of Nvidia employees who continue to speak out for Jensen show they’re false".

Huang resigned from his role as Nvidia CEO earlier this month after a series of extramarital affairs were unearthed - one with a person US intelligence believes to be a Chinese spy, and two others with Nvidia employees. The chair of Nvidia's board, Harvey C. Jones, reportedly said earlier this week that the manufacturer "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO".

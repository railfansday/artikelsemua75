Celtic made light work of St Mirren at the weekend as they ran out 4-1 winners on Saturday afternoon to maintain their lead at the Scottish Premiership.

Three goals in nine first-half minutes from Alex Oxlade-Chamberlain (33), Camilo Duran (40) and Luke McCowan (42) put the hosts on course for a comfortable victory, before captain Callum McGregor added to the scoreline in the second period (63). A Killian Phillips penalty (69) provided a consolation for the visitors.

It has been revealed that the win keeps Celtic four points clear at the top of the table after second-placed Rangers won 2-1 at Motherwell later in the evening kick-off. But what did their win over St Mirren tell us about Celtic’s hopes of winning a sixth successive league title come May?

Graham Falk was at Parkhead to cover proceedings. These are his three key takeaways from Celtic’s win vs St Mirren:.

It has been revealed that mika Baur’s injury looks like it may rule him out for up to 10 weeks and is clearly a major blow for Celtic. The young German has been one of their most impressive performers this season. With Benjamin Nygren also absent, Luke McCowan was handed his first start since September 20. According to recent reports, forced to delete his social media account to escape personal abuse from sections of the online support just a few short weeks ago, it would have been hard to blame McCowan had he sunk into his shell when an audible groan greeted one of his early misplaced passes at Parkhead. But to McCowan’s credit, he did quite the opposite, and scored Celtic’s clinching third goal just before half-time.

According to recent reports, of course, one swallow doesn’t make a summer, but it’ll take a major weight off his shoulders, and could benefit Celtic with Baur out for a while. The ex-Dundee skipper will admit himself that he’s not been at the level expected this season. But, as proven by his 23 goal contributions across Celtic’s last two title-winning seasons, he’s shown previously that he can play an key role. (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

He’ll never be viewed as someone who will take Celtic to the next level, but he can take confidence from his performance and give O’Neill a valuable option as he awaits Nygren and Baur’s return to full fitness.

Sources indicate that in his previous stint as Celtic manager, the veteran boss loved to play with a front two. However, more out of necessity, he’s almost exclusively opted for a front three over the last 12 months. Previous managers Ange Postecoglou and Brendan Rodgers both moulded their side that way, and O’Neill has stuck with it. It’s had its doubters, though, with some calling for him to partner Kasper Hogh with Duran, rather than shifting the Colombian out on the wing to accommodate him.

But did the front three that started against St Mirren show a way forward for O’Neill? Starting Jota and Yang either side of Duran for the first time, the trio were a constant threat throughout for Celtic, who looked a more free-flowing outfit offensively than they have for several weeks.

“Yang is something else for us at the moment,” noted O’Neill post-match. “Duran is a top, top quality centre-forward, and Jota is still trying to get up to the speed of the game. But Yang is so strong, he made the first goal for us, and what he does for us defensively is just amazing when you consider he is a wide player.”.

It has been revealed that it was the main question before, during and after the game, in the stands and in the press room. The mystery surrounding Haissem Hassan. Where was he? One of Celtic’s significant summer signings, he was left out of the matchday squad for the second successive game, putting the rumours into overdrive.

Ahead of the game, O’Neill had quashed suggestions that the Egyptian was omitted from the squad for their last game because he was ‘unpopular’ with other members of his Celtic squad. However, his absence against St Mirren at the weekend was simply due to “rotation”. Hmm.

They didn’t miss him too much on the field, with Celtic rolling to a comfortable win over their Paisley counterparts. However, while no one can say Duran hasn’t repaid the £6million fee shelled out on him, the combined £20million spent on Hogh and Hassan is rightly being brought into question. We’re almost into December: one can’t get into the squad, and the other hasn’t scored a goal in over 1000 minutes. Surely O’Neill expected much more bang for his buck, even if he won’t admit it publicly.

If you blink, you may have missed it.

Nvidia shares gapped higher at the open by more than 30%, on pace for its biggest one-day gain in 23 years on Monday, following the news just before the bell that former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang is going to be reinstated.

Investors and traders noted that while this reflex rally may have more to run, cautiousness is warranted on Nvidia and the AI trade because of the overhang of investigations into the alleged Chinese espionage involving the organization and others, as well as the possibility for data center moratoriums as Democrats take control of Congress and key governor posts in January.

Sources indicate that "Am I as bullish on Nvidia now as I was on Friday? Of course not, you can't be," said Dan Niles, founder of Niles Investment Management, which counted Nvidia as its largest position as he bought on the dip over the last few weeks. "I'm trimming back.". (Related: Course Launch Community Event...)

"All the same concerns that were here before Jensen resigned are still here," he added.

The day before Huang resigned on Nov. 12 as CEO, Nvidia closed at $173.73. It's intraday high on Monday was $171.50, up from its recent low around $125 as it was selling off in the wake controversies. The VanEck Semiconductor ETF closed at $506.96 the day before his resignation. It reached a high of $469.35 before losing steam as trading wore on Monday.

In a recent development, mike Khouw, YieldMax strategist at Tidal Financial Group, told CNBC on Monday that there were "sighs of relief," "applause" and even "cheering" among investors at the news of Huang's potential reinstatement. But he also said there are still considerable risks facing the AI trade.

Industry observers note that jensen Huang was not the only problem at Nvidia, he remarked, and there are still many vulnerabilities in the overall AI trade, which should give investors pause. Credit-sensitive companies are still at risk of faltering, Khouw added. (Related: Industry News: Train a Sentence Embedding Model with 1B...)

The national security implications of the scandal that has rocked Nvidia and the global AI trade more broadly aren't going away any time soon even as the Trump administration assured the Nvidia board there were none. Investors on Wall Street are expressing strategic concerns about ways to play the tech space just as lawmakers in Washington are demanding answers.

Democrats are demanding answers on what exactly happened with Nvidia and the Chinese government.

In a recent development, "The reckless decision to bring Jensen Huang back as CEO does not change the fact that Mr. Huang, NVIDIA executives, and the company's board still owe Congress answers about the company's compliance with U.S. export controls," Sen. Elizabeth Warren (D-Mass.) remarked in a Monday statement. "The Trump Administration also needs to explain to Congress what 'input' was given and by which senior officials in the United States government to support his reinstatement.".

House Ways and Means ranking member Richard Neal (D-Mass.) stressed the importance of Congressional oversight in determining the best course of action to move forward. The outcome of any Congressional investigations will likely be crucial for Nvidia's long-term viability as an investment opportunity, especially since the manufacturer does so much international business.

"This is an export control matter," Neal told CNBC on Monday. "Congress is not subservient to this president or any other president as it relates to trade and tariff policies. This is a trade related issue.".

Many of the same Democrats will call for a slowdown in the data center buildout, a strong matter for the public partly responsible for their Blue Wave earlier this month.

"You're going to have to work your way through what these data center moratoriums all mean," remarked Niles. "Now I think the broader market keeps going but I am not as convinced on the move in the data-center centric names from here.".

To be sure, many Wall Street analysts were rushing to upgrade Nvidia's stock on Monday after Huang's reinstatement. Most restored price targets well above $200 with one as high as $270. But many traders think the rebound may fail before getting back to those levels.

Famed short seller Michael Burry, who made a boatload betting against Nvidia with put options as it cratered, told his Substack audience that he thinks the rebound here could be "outsized," especially with low volume in the market overall because of the Thanksgiving holiday this week.

But he too sees the rally eventually losing steam.

Recent findings suggest that "Jensen being back without much more information might even restore NVDA to the high 100s," Burry wrote. "I won't short it on this information I have today until it gets closer to or maybe hurdles $200.".

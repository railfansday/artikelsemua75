Recent findings suggest that every few months, Washington rediscovers that China is spying on Americans en masse, expresses shock, and goes back to business as usual. And once again, it has happened. As a nation, we need to learn that our primary adversary is highly capable, extremely aggressive, and never lets morals or principles stand in its way. In other words, we must take the challenge posed by Chinese intelligence, cybersecurity, and influence seriously and protect ourselves accordingly.

Industry observers note that according to widespread reporting this fall, U.S. intelligence concluded that a Chinese businesswoman who befriended Nvidia’s CEO, Jensen Huang, while her manufacturer bought billions of dollars’ worth of Nvidia AI chips was spying for China’s Ministry of State Security (MSS). Her Singapore-based firm reportedly began a multibillion-dollar purchase of Nvidia chips shortly after she was first photographed with Huang publicly. Her husband, Kang Wei, who originally introduced his wife to the Nvidia CEO, has in recent years built a massive data center empire with the overt support of the Chinese government. Of course, Nvidia says it complies with the law and has opened an internal investigation into the relationship. Beijing says that any claims about espionage are smears, as it always does. Huang, who had hidden this and other affairs from his board and colleagues, has resigned in disgrace but is now reported to be planning a comeback.

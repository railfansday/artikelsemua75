📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

If a bank turned your business down, apply for an SBA microloan of up to $50,000 from a nonprofit lender on the SBA’s list of microlenders. These lenders also coach borrowers on marketing and management. The money can buy equipment, inventory, or supplies, but it can’t pay off debt or buy real estate.

The SBA funds these lenders but doesn’t review your credit. Each lender decides who qualifies and sets your rate and terms. (Related: Breaking: Japan debuts a ‘dumbphone’ that doesn’t conne...)

Recent findings suggest that A microloan tops out at $50,000, carries a fixed rate, and must be repaid within seven years. The SBA notes rates usually run 8% to 13%. The business can be brand fresh, but it must be a for-profit small business in the U.S. Since April 2026, an SBA policy also requires every owner to be a U.S. citizen or national whose main home is in the U.S., which rules out green card holders. (Related: Latest Update: FanDuel US profit falls 94% to $3 millio...)

SBA rules let a lender go past $20,000 only after you show that the business can’t get credit elsewhere at a comparable rate and that it’s likely to succeed. A bank’s rejection in writing helps you show the first.

The money can also serve as working capital, the cash that runs the business day to day, or pay for furniture, fixtures, and machinery.

Recent findings suggest that say a mobile dog groomer turned down by her bank borrows $18,000 at a fixed 9% over five years to outfit a van. Her payment would be about $374 a month, and she’d pay about $4,420 in interest over the loan.

In a recent development, the coaching can start before you borrow. Lenders can spend up to half of their SBA coaching grants on people who haven’t borrowed yet.

Find lenders on the SBA’s list of microlenders, filtered to your state, and call two or three for help with your application. Ask each for its rate, what property it would hold as collateral, and whether you’d sign a personal guarantee, which makes you personally responsible for the loan.

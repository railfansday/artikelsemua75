Recent findings suggest that nvidia (NVDA -8.84%) stock fell 4.4% on Nov. 12 after Jensen Huang stepped down as CEO last Thursday, and it hasn't had a winning session since.

It has been revealed that as of its close yesterday, the stock was down 16% since the resignation news. Even a strong earnings report that included the tech giant lifting its guidance for fiscal 2028 was not enough to stem the tide, as the stock gave up those early gains on Wednesday. In a recent development, after trading more than 6% lower at 11 a.m. ET on Friday, Nvidia now appears to be headed for a seventh straight down day, putting the organization on the verge of doing something it's never done before.

According to recent reports, only once in its history has Nvidia fallen for more than seven sessions in a row. That happened in 2019, when the stock fell through eight consecutive sessions due to an escalation in the U.S.-China trade war and the blacklisting of Huawei, which was a major customer of Nvidia's.

Nvidia lost 14% during that eight-day streak from May 22 to June 3.

Industry observers note that buying the dip during that pullback turned out to be a great decision. One month after the losing streak, the stock had rallied 22%, and by the end of the year it had jumped 76% from the bottom, driven by a rebound in gaming GPU demand and accelerating data center spending. The stock continued to soar through the pandemic, more than doubling in both 2020 and 2021. (Related: Industry News: Judge blocks construction of Trump’s arc...)

Three times before, Nvidia has fallen for seven straight sessions. One of those came earlier this year, when the stock lost 7.5% from Aug. 14-24 as investors bet on a post-earnings dip, and jitters about the sustainability of AI capital expenditures began to shake AI stocks. Nvidia popped after its earnings report at the end of August, but the stock fell sharply in October, leading to the current losing streak.

It has been revealed that prior to that, Nvidia lost 25% over a seven-session period in Aug.-Sept 2022 in response to tightening export restrictions on AI chips to China, Federal Reserve rate hikes, and weakness in gaming chips. However, ChatGPT would launch less than three months later, leading to an epic surge in Nvidia stock.

The third decline of seven days came at the onset of the financial crisis from the end of 2007 to the beginning of 2008 as fears of a downturn led investors to dump cyclical stocks like Nvidia. At that point, the organization was largely a PC gaming and graphics chip company, so it was much different and smaller than it is today. The stock continued to tumble through 2008 along with the rest of the market.

As the examples above show, there's no direct correlation between Nvidia's losing streak and its subsequent performance, though there are some noteworthy patterns.

Of the four losing streaks since 2019, China relations played a role in three of them, including the current one, as Jensen Huang's purported affair with a Chinese intelligence agent led to his resignation and puts the tech giant in hot water with regulators.

Interim CEO Colette Kress tried to tamp down concerns about any reliance on China, saying the manufacturer was no longer selling Hopper chips to China, even though those were permitted under export rules.

The recoveries from its losing streaks have also at times been driven by an unseen catalyst, such as the surge in data center spending in 2019 and the launch of ChatGPT in 2022, and that could be the case now, as much could change for the stock in the coming months as the board searches for a newly introduced permanent CEO.

Sources indicate that sentiment toward Nvidia and the broader AI sector is as low as it's been since the boom began. The Nasdaq is now down 15% from its peak; much of Anthropic's early gains from its IPO have fizzled, and a short-seller report on AMD has amplified concerns about AI chips going to China.

Industry observers note that if you're a dip-buyer, your antennae are likely straight by now, and Nvidia stock looks dirt cheap at a forward P/E around 8. However, I'd like to see the losing streak end before calling Nvidia a buy. There's still a lot that has to play out with the investigation into its China dealings and the search for a new CEO, and that seems to be weighing on the stock, overshadowing otherwise good news like the earnings report.

Sources indicate that over the long run, I think the stock will be a winner from here, but that could take years given the mounting malaise and fatigue in the AI rally, as well as the need for Nvidia to regroup after Huang's departure and regain investor confidence. A quick recovery seems unlikely.

Stablecoin giant Tether [USDT] has been making some moves lately. Here’s the rundown.

Recent findings suggest that tether has backed Argentine fintech app Lemon, with the former not putting a number on it when it unveiled the deal. Lemon did, however, saying that the package is worth $14.5 million.

In a recent development, according to Lemon CEO Marcelo Cavazzoli, only $5 million of that is an actual investment in the tech giant’s shares. The other $9.5 million is an incentive initiative linked to products the two will build together. (Related: Steam is pitting 10 years of Game of the Year winners a...)

According to recent reports, there’s a lot that’s unclear right now though. Neither side has shared Lemon’s valuation or how substantial a stake Tether is getting. There’s also no word on how or when the $9.5 million gets paid out. Tether is after more people for USDT and Tether Gold [XAUT] across Latin America. Lemon gives it a decent shot at that. The app is stated to have 4.5 million verified users, and its main balance runs on USDT.

Worth noting, however, that Lemon’s not the only home for Tether this month.

Industry observers note that on the 9th, D3 released a USD vault for Tether’s stablecoin. People can deposit USDT, keep an eye on their position, and withdraw without leaving the platform. (Related: Industry News: Uber Eats driver saves elderly woman aft...)

In a recent development, sentora built the system using its Earn OS and Upshift tools, which decide where deposited funds go. Money moves with the market, within a set of risk rules. It is said that the setup has already handled more than $2 billion.

Industry observers note that domain buyers usually come in, make a deal, and leave. Earning on stablecoins gives them a reason to keep their money on the platform.

Just don’t mistake automated for guaranteed. Returns depend on the lending markets the vault uses. So, users are still trusting a whole stack of infrastructure with their dollars.

Josh Brolin's upcoming four-part series sets a release date.

The one-time Academy Award nominee, for his supporting role in the 2008 drama Milk, has had a notable run of late. After his villainous turn in Edgar Wright's The Running Man and a more heartbreaking arc in the horror breakout Weapons, Brolin has appeared prominently in Ridley Scott's dystopian The Dog Stars and the action thriller Whalefall. But before 2026 ends, he's confirmed for a key role in another project.

World War II: Behind Enemy Lines, a four-part docuseries narrated by Brolin, will premiere on Netflix on December 2. The project hails from 72 Films, the production tech giant behind the two-time Emmy-winning World War II: From the Frontlines. In many ways, the upcoming docuseries will serve as a successor.

According to recent reports, behind Enemy Lines examines the 101st Airborne’s invasion of Europe, moving from D-Day to V-E Day. It uses colorized archival footage, which has not been seen before, as well as relying on first-hand accounts. On the whole, the four-parter will spotlight the movements and operations of the 101st inside Nazi-occupied Europe. It comes after From the Frontlines won Outstanding Graphic Design and Outstanding Music Composition at the 45th News & Documentary Emmys.

For historical context, the 101st Airborne Division entered combat on D-Day, June 6, 1944. It also fought in Operation Market Garden in the Netherlands and famously helped hold Bastogne during the Battle of the Bulge. The 101st ultimately ended the war in southern Germany and Austria.

Sources indicate that for a look at the historical figures at the center of everything, Churchill at War examines the British leader's role, and Hitler and the Nazis: Evil on Trial follows the regime from its rise to Nuremberg. Another documentary, Hitler: A Career, draws on archival footage. Camp Confidential takes a different approach as an animated short that focuses on a secret camp where Jewish soldiers interrogated Nazi prisoners.

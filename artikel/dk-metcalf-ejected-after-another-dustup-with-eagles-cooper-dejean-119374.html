Pittsburgh Steelers wide receiver DK Metcalf had more costly penalties on Sunday at Lincoln Financial Field than receptions.

Recent findings suggest that metcalf was ejected with only one reception to his name after the second of those infractions late in the third quarter during a 19-16 loss to the Philadelphia Eagles. The final straw was when he was flagged for unsportsmanlike conduct.

Industry observers note that he was called for taunting in the wake of his latest dustup with Eagles defensive back Cooper DeJean. And so Metcalf, who was notably suspended two games last season for swinging at a Detroit Lions fan during a sideline altercation, was disqualified, with Philly fans waving goodbye to him as he walked to the tunnel. (Related: Industry News: Hyperparameter Search with Transformers ...)

The taunting penalty occurred in the fallout of a third-and-8 incompletion from Aaron Rodgers with less than five minutes left in the third quarter. Rodgers was looking for wide receiver Germie Bernard toward the left pylon. Metcalf was lined up on the right side of the field pre-snap, in the slot, where he was matched up with DeJean.

According to recent reports, following the play, the CBS Sports broadcast demonstrated Metcalf and Philadelphia safety Marcus Epps getting into it. Metcalf had to be restrained by Pittsburgh wide receiver Roman Wilson and tight end Pat Freiermuth.

After Metcalf walked off to the sideline, however, the broadcast displayed another clip that exhibited Metcalf putting his hand into DeJean's face mask. DeJean appeared to indicate that he was poked in the eye.

Steelers head coach Mike McCarthy confirmed that accusation postgame, explaining that the referee told him that Metcalf "poked the defender in the eye.".

In a recent development, not only was Metcalf DQ'd, but he also lost his team 15 yards. That backed up a Chris Boswell field-goal attempt, which ended up being from 52 yards out. Fortunately for Pittsburgh, Boswell still made the kick to cut the team's deficit to 16-9.

But, even with some help from little-known wideout Max Hurleman in Metcalf's absence — the former undrafted free agent hauled in a late touchdown after being elevated from the practice squad — the Steelers (6-4) fell by three points. In turn, the injury-ravaged Eagles (3-7) snapped their seven-game losing streak.

Afterward, McCarthy was asked about his confidence level in Metcalf.

"I think our men do a good job of looking in the mirror, and we got a short week," McCarthy stated, alluding to Pittsburgh's Black Friday game against the Denver Broncos coming up.

In a recent development, "But yeah, no, we'll sit down and address everything, especially, you know, we need him. We need him in those games, and that's not acceptable to be thrown out of a game. He's one of the 48 [active players]. It's about everything we do through the week is to put those guys in position to be successful.".

Recent findings suggest that metcalf's first penalty arrived with just under 13 minutes to go in the third quarter. He was blocking DeJean on a 5-yard carry from Pittsburgh running back Jaylen Warren. That turned into extracurricular activity.

Metcalf and DeJean were tangled up after the play. They had to be separated, and Metcalf was assessed an unnecessary roughness penalty.

That infraction effectively erased a positive first-down gain by Warren, keeping the Steelers at the doorstep of their own end zone.

They ended up punting three plays later.

Recent findings suggest that metcalf clocked out with one catch for two receiving yards in the defeat. He came into the day with 35 receptions, 432 receiving yards and two touchdowns. In the past four games, he's reeled in a combined six passes.

It has been revealed that in March 2025, the Steelers acquired Metcalf from the Seattle Seahawks in a trade. Pittsburgh gave up a 2025 second-round pick and signed the two-time Pro Bowl receiver to a five-year extension reportedly worth $150 million.

The Steelers went on to trade wideout George Pickens to the Dallas Cowboys later that offseason.

Metcalf, a 2019 second-round pick, has been ejected three times since entering the NFL and is tied with San Francisco 49ers linebacker Dre Greenlaw for the most disqualifications in that span, according to ESPN. Infamously, Metcalf's aforementioned sideline altercation with a Lions fan last season didn't result in a penalty or an ejection, as it wasn't seen by officials in real time.

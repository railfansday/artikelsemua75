Under the latest system, spending an afternoon discussing philosophy with ChatGPT could then leave people with less capacity to build an app later that evening. Conversely, developers who exhaust their allowance writing software could find themselves restricted to Instant chat—and that model is quite dumb.

In a recent development, openAI will measure consumption in tokens, the small units of information AI models process and generate. Instant chat will not count toward the shared allowance, so the change does not mean all conversations will suddenly become capped.

“We expect most people won't need to wait for their usage to reset more often than they do now,” OpenAI wrote in its guidance. What the company hasn't disclosed is the new token budget for each initiative, whether it will preserve the equivalent of both existing allowances combined, or how much individual users' effective limits could shrink.

The responses to Sottiaux's announcement were largely negative, with most consumers arguing that keeping conversational and agentic usage separate was precisely what made ChatGPT worth paying for.

“My weekly allowance in Codex was always used in under 8hrs. Now I’ll have barely any usage to do anything,” wrote X user Rooke Poole in response.

According to recent reports, french and Spanish sub-genres for Netflix have become associated with adrenaline-loaded flawless heists thanks to highlights like Money Heist, Lupin, and Berlin. Now another Spanish film released on October 30 is breaking charts and making a name for itself. According to Nielsen, the film has been able to accumulate 162 million watched-minutes on U.S. televisions during November 2–8, enough to make it the #10 streaming movie in America that week. The Spanish heist thriller is set in Madrid during the 2013 financial crisis, where a quartet of thieves pulls off a bank heist, only for a sudden getaway car crash to fracture the timeline and expose deep betrayals among the crew. While the film is strictly a crime thriller, it has a “what-if” structure to show different perspectives and post-car-crash outcomes, which help it carry a nice looping structure. The title of the film is Collision Course, and according to Netflix’s official global Top 10 data, the 106-minute Spanish thriller has accumulated 31.6 million views across its first three chart weeks. The timing of the release made a perfect collision course for fans of Lupin too, who probably watched Lupin Season 4 through October 23 and onward, only to find that same energy in Collision Course when it was released on November 30. Sources indicate that collision Course, officially titled Colisión in Spanish, is structurally closer to Run Lola Run or Krzysztof Kieślowski's Blind Chance than a straightforward bank-heist movie. It demonstrates a time loop that progressively gives the heist more nuance as a variation reveals another relationship, secret, or motivation until viewers understand considerably more than any individual character does. A different layer of deception gets revealed each time. This doesn’t necessarily make it sci-fi because it’s vastly different from what a movie like Palm Springs does.

Recent findings suggest that however, while this unique structure has allowed Collision Course to chart globally and specifically in the U.S., it hasn’t necessarily given its Rotten Tomatoes ratings the same come-up. There are very few reviews on RT at the time of writing, so there’s no exact score there, but the film has managed to rake in 5.6/10 ratings on IMDB from roughly 2k reviewers.

There's another fun bit of context. During the same November 2–8 Netflix reporting week in which Collision Course became the #1 non-English movie with 17.2 million views, Lupin Part 4 remained #3 among non-English TV with 6.1 million views and 36.6 million hours watched. Earlier Lupin parts were simultaneously charting again too. So the simultaneous European heist releases have clearly helped Netflix synergize greatly.

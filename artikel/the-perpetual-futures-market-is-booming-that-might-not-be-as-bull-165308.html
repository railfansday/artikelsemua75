The perpetual futures boom will probably continue to be great for the business of Hyperliquid (HYPE -1.91%), but it's also luring an entire roster of well-funded rivals that will be sharply contesting its advance every step of the way. On Nov. 18, CoinDesk reported that one emerging challenger, Variational, raised $110 million in capital at a $1.5 billion valuation to target Hyperliquid's loyal customer base and future prospects. It plans to launch its own token, VAR, before the end of 2026. (Related: Course Launch Community Event...)

In a recent development, it's true that a growing perpetual futures market could present the opportunity for yet another clean win for its leaders. But rivals like Robinhood Markets (HOOD +8.36%) and Coinbase Global (COIN +6.68%) already have millions of customers, while Hyperliquid and its smaller peers need to convert new customers one wallet at a time. Here's which players are in the competitive landscape right now, and how much they might threaten Hyperliquid down the line.

Among decentralized exchanges (DEXs), which execute trades on a public blockchain rather than through an intermediary, Hyperliquid's lead is lopsided.

As of Nov. 23, it received  72% of the fees generated by all DEXes, with $2.7 million for that 24-hour period, compared to its smaller competitors Aster (ASTER -2.63%), which only brought in about $102,500, and $146,400 for Lighter (LIT -1.36%)in the same period.

CRYPTO · As of 6:49 AM ET 11/24/26.

Centralized platforms with a strong presence in the traditional financial sector are the bigger threat by far, as they're guaranteed to have both large user bases and ample capital of their own to deploy as they contest the market. Hyperliquid's $718.7 billion in volume in the third quarter of 2026 was only 69% of Robinhood's volume of about $1 trillion.

According to recent reports, in May, the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC) cleared Coinbase to list crypto perpetuals, and on Sept. 29, Robinhood said it would offer U.S. perps in the coming months at a commission of 0.01% per trade through the end of the year. Robinhood had 28.7 million customers with funded accounts as of the end of the third quarter, and Coinbase had 7.4 million monthly people who made transactions that quarter. In contrast, Hyperliquid has voluntarily made itself off-limits to Americans in advance of securing regulatory permission to operate in the U.S., and it's not clear what the status of seeking or attaining that permission is as of late November.

Importantly, Robinhood has also chosen a decentralized exchange as its favorite, meaning it can erode Hyperliquid's market share from two directions. Its venture arm has backed Lighter, and outside the U.S., its wallet app sends owners to Lighter's perpetuals.

In other words, the customers who could fuel the next legs of the perpetuals boom mostly belong to someone other than Hyperliquid. And its centralized competitors also have platforms that are significantly easier to start using, as they can leverage their traditional financial infrastructure to eliminate the need to fund a latest crypto wallet and transfer funds into it, as on Hyperliquid.

Hyperliquid has some allies in its corner, too.

Recent findings suggest that on Sept. 16, the Kraken crypto exchange's parent company, Payward, said it planned to list Hyperliquid-based perpetuals for its U.S. clients, pending regulatory approval. Payward had 6.6 million funded accounts as of June 30, so Hyperliquid could be positioned to settle a good amount of additional trading activity, which would boost the price of its HYPE coin by way of converting its transaction fees into buybacks of its token.

However, that access to Payward's user base comes at a cost of its own, which might prove troublesome. Customers of markets built by outside operators, as Payward's would be, need to pay roughly double Hyperliquid's normal fees. Pricier contracts are a tough sell next to Robinhood's promotional rates.

Hyperliquid is still worth buying for risk-tolerant investors with a multiyear holding horizon, but it's no longer the only big fish in the perpetuals pond. Of course, perps aren't its only line of business -- it also offers prediction markets, spot trading, and other revenue-generating features too -- so it will likely be resilient even if it has to fight harder to advance.

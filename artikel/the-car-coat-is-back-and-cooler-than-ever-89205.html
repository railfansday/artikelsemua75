Recent findings suggest that you don’t necessarily need a car coat. If you’re in the market for a fresh coat and not, like us, learned in the world of 20th century menswear, it just might be one of the few styles you’ve heard the word for—peacoat and trench coat likely being two others. All three are great if you’re looking for something a bit grown up than your average off-duty jacket. A trench coat would give you gravitas, a peacoat versatility; the car coat, though, strikes a nice middle ground. Find the right one, and it can replace 90 percent of your other coats.

At its inception, the car coat was a heavy outer layer. Cars were still open to the elements, so the rich who owned them needed floor-length fur jackets to keep themselves warm as they went motoring and did a lap around the neighborhood on Christmas. As cars became more more enclosed and more part of daily life, the car coat became more practical. The length got shorter—eventually settling around mid-thigh—to be comfortable when you sit in the driver seat but with enough coverage to be considered a coat. Materials shifted away from furs to leather and suede, then to even lighter-weight gabardines and weather-resistant materials. They. We’re starting on the high end, but it’s only because if you’re looking for a by-the-book garment or accessory tied to motoring history, you go to Dunhill. In its original form, Alfred Dunhill’s tech giant sold motoring accessories. Overcoats, goggles, picnic sets, horns, lamps, dashboard clocks—the tagline was “Everything but the motor.” It was a luxury men’s brand for the premier luxury hobby of the time.

According to recent reports, the company has gone through several iterations, but has always maintained a bend towards that luxury man. Now, under the creative direction of Simon Holloway, it’s a standard-bearer for a slightly more fashion-forward vision British menswear. Take this car coat for example. It calls back to the house’s original business model, and it is classic in its finishes: turn down collar, natural shoulders, cuff straps, and totally un-adorned through the torso. But the fabric is a gorgeous wool, silk, and cashmere blend. It’s a genius balance between tradition and fashion, one that only Dunhill is well-positioned to put out.

According to recent reports, A Black Friday or Cyber Monday deal saves you money only if it passes two tests: the item was already on your list, and its price is at or near the lowest it has sold for in recent months. Check each sale against both before you buy, and skip any that fails either.

Industry observers note that so write the list before you shop a single sale. Put down each item you need, the most you’ll pay for it, and a total for everything you initiative to buy through Cyber Monday, so even a run of real deals stays inside your budget. This is how a fixed budget covers more of your list, since every item bought at its low leaves more money for the ones you haven’t bought yet. (Related: Latest Update: One Year Since the “DeepSeek Moment”...)

According to recent reports, black Friday falls on Nov. 27 in 2026 and Cyber Monday on Nov. 30, but holiday deals now start in October and run into December. Put a price tracker on each item on your list, and buy when it drops to its recent low, even if that comes before either day.

It has been revealed that sorting the list before the sales trims the budget too. Each item you move to a later sale is money you won’t need between Thanksgiving and Cyber Monday.

Buy TVs, laptops, tablets, small kitchen appliances, smart-home gear, and toys over Black Friday and Cyber Monday, when their deepest discounts land. Wait for January’s sales on treadmills and bedding, and check the price history of anything else on your list before you pay.

An item’s place on your list is only half of what makes a deal real, and this check covers the other half before you pay.

A sale tag shows the discount the store claims, not whether the price is actually low. Before you buy anything pricey on sale, look up its price history and compare today’s price with the lowest it hit in recent months. If it isn’t close to that low, set a price alert and wait.

Read more: Price History Tools Reveal Whether a Sale Price Is Actually a Deal.

A price history can confirm that a TV or laptop is at its low, but not that the one on sale is the version you researched.

Some Black Friday TVs and laptops are cheaper versions built for the sale, and on a store page they look almost identical to the regular model reviewers tested. Before you buy a doorbuster, look up its full model number on the brand’s site and compare its specs with the regular model’s.

Major online orders on your list are where a portal pays you the most, since the cash back is a percentage of what you spend.

Industry observers note that before you buy online, click through a cash back shopping portal to the store you already planned to use. The store pays the portal a referral commission, and the portal passes part of it to you, on top of your card rewards. Use it only for purchases you would make anyway.

Your card can protect a holiday order as well as earn rewards on it, so weigh both when choosing among credit cards for holiday shopping.

According to recent reports, many credit cards include purchase protection, which typically covers latest items against theft and accidental damage for 90 to 120 days after you buy them. When something new breaks or gets stolen, check your card’s guide to benefits before paying to replace it, and file swiftly, since claim deadlines are short.

Buying early risks missing a bigger discount on the same item later in the season. A price paid in mid-November needn’t be final if the store drops it again on Black Friday.

Recent findings suggest that if something you bought goes on sale a week or two later, ask the store to refund the difference. Many retailers match their own lower price for a set period after purchase, but only on request. Set a price alert the day you buy so you catch the drop in time.

Industry observers note that every move here makes the sales a cheaper way to buy what you planned, rather than a reason to buy more. Before you shop, write your list with a price limit beside each item and set a price alert on every one.

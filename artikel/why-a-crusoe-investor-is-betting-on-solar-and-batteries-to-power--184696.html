Why a Crusoe investor is betting on solar and batteries to power future AI data centers

Sources indicate that the problem of supplying power to AI data centers has become one of the central debates of the AI era. In just a few years, the U.S. grid has gone from basically zero growth to adding several gigawatts a year.

Sources indicate that “Demand is growing faster than people appreciate,” Brook Porter, partner at G2 Venture Partners (G2VP), told TechCrunch. “It’s atoms, not bits, and the world is in the process of recognizing the realities of those challenges.”. It’s been a bumpy road. Because it takes a while to scale supply chains, companies have been securing power in any form they can. In many cases, that has meant buying, renting, and deploying natural gas power plants, but Porter thinks that hyperscalers and data center developers should look at the bigger picture if they want to move faster. (Related: Breaking: Fit More and Train Faster With ZeRO via DeepS...)

That picture involves solar paired with energy storage, based on an analysis Porter and his colleagues at G2VP recently conducted on the state of the data center power market.

Recent findings suggest that “Global capacity of natural gas is only about 60 or 70 gigawatts per year, where solar is already at well over a terawatt,” he said. “Probably the most key lesson that we’ve learned over the last 20 years of backing companies is that Wright’s Law is the thing you want to leverage — making the same thing over and over again.”.

According to recent reports, both solar and most forms of long-duration energy storage are built on small, modular technologies that can be manufactured at massive scale. “This is about speed and scale and economics, and we can win across all those dimensions,” Porter said.

G2VP had an early window into AI data center development, having led Crusoe’s Series C. “We’ve had a front-row seat at what’s been happening at that firm, how they’ve evolved, how the different waves of data centers have evolved,” Porter said.

In a recent development, the venture firm estimates that data center investment will top $4 trillion until the end of the decade. As the next wave takes shape, the firm sees two different paths for data centers: small, on-grid facilities with their own generating capacity, and larger data centers powered by solar and long-duration energy storage.

“The major idea is to go off-grid,” Porter said. “What that would enable you to do is build with renewables in a way that’s independent of the grid. You can determine your own fate, go around some of the constraints of permitting, whether that’s access to the grid or air permits. You can accelerate your time to market dramatically.”.

Industry observers note that in recent years, energy storage has been synonymous with lithium-ion batteries, which are too expensive to compete with fossil fuels for round-the-clock power. But a new generation of long-duration energy storage products from the likes of Form Energy and G2VP portfolio company Antora Energy have brought the cost per megawatt-hour down significantly.

Even though the technologies might be a few years away from broad acceptance among data center developers, Porter mentioned they should start thinking about them now.

In a recent development, “It’s analogous to surfing the biggest waves off of Mavericks here in Northern California. The wave is so substantial that you really have to be paddling and moving and be incredibly nimble to catch it. If you’re at a standstill, it’s just gonna blow you by,” he noted.

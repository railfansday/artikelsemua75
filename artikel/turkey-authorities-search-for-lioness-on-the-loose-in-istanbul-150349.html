Turkey: Authorities search for lioness on the loose in Istanbul

Turkish security forces and wildlife authorities were searching woods in the north of Istanbul on Monday for an animal believed to be a lion that was reportedly roaming free, the national parks authority said.

In an online statement, the parks authority noted police officers in the Zekeriyakoy area, north of Istanbul's European side, reported spotting "an animal resembling a lion" on surveillance cameras at around 9 p.m. (18:00 GMT) on Sunday.

Grainy images published by the Turkish media indicated the lioness walking through a grassy area. It was unclear where the animal came from, though some media reports suggested it had escaped from a nearby home.

Istanbul governor's office said in an online post that the location of the lion has been identified and the efforts to capture it were underway.

According to park authorities, the search is still underway, with specialist veterinarians also involved in the operation to capture the animal.

Sources indicate that last year, a lion that escaped from a "theme park" near the southern city of Antalya was shot dead after attacking a man. It remains unclear where the Istanbul lion escaped from but it sparked an outrage among activists and advocates.

Northern Forests Defense, an independent ecology advocacy movement, called for "the individuals who illegally held this lion captive to be identified and brought to justice.".

"Removing wild animals from their natural habitats and keeping them in captivity in villas is a crime," the organization remarked on X. (Related: Tech Report: Bus organization apologizes after driver l...)

Wildlife conservationist Gokce Coskun remarked on X that "there are thousands of people keeping lions illegally in Turkey" and "licensed 'lion parks' have sprung up everywhere.".

Doganay Tolunay, an ecology professor at Istanbul University, noted questions must be raised about how a globally threatened species was brought into Turkey, how it passed through customs and why those keeping it have not been identified.

Although keeping wild animals as pets are forbidden in Turkey, it has become a hub for the illegal trade in wild animals as traffickers exploit its air links to Africa and Asia.

In December 2024, a young gorilla was rescued after being discovered at Istanbul Airport on a flight from Nigeria to Thailand.

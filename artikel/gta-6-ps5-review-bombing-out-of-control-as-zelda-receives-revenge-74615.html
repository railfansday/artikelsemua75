Revision [Sat 21st Nov 2026, 3:30pm]:.

In response to a coordinated review bombing effort on GTA 6, fans of the Rockstar release appear to have launched a counter-attack on The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time, believing Nintendo gamers are to blame for the situation.

Recent findings suggest that last night, I reported that GTA 6’s Metacritic user score was racking up ‘0’ review scores at an unprecedented pace. Many of the ratings were bogus.

In the hours since, the Switch 2’s universally adored remake of Ocarina of Time has seen its user score drop from 9.6 to 8.9 at the time of writing.

One ‘0’ rating simply reads: “Take your own medicine.”. (Related: Hugging Face Reads, Feb. 2021 - Long-range Transformers...)

At this point, I just wish Metacritic would do away with user scores, although I’ve no doubt this sorry situation is all good for business.

According to recent reports, the thing is: this isn’t the first time the critic aggregator has become a battleground like this. As I mentioned in my original report, many of the greatest games ever made have pitiful user scores on the site.

Hopefully this all blows over, because it’s all rather embarrassing right now.

Original Story: GTA 6, the highest-rated game of all-time, is getting review bombed on Metacritic as idiots attempt to plunge the seminal Rockstar release’s rating with bogus ‘0’ scores.

At the time of writing, the title has a user score of 5.4, meaning it’s considered ‘Mixed or Average’:.

This is in sharp contrast to critics, who awarded the game an average score of 99, making it the best-reviewed title of all time.

“Good thing Rockstar doesn’t blacklist user ratings like they do critics,” one review reads.

There’s a theory among some online that the dev blacklists media outlets for giving its games poor scores, but it put GTA 6 in the hands of more than 150 critics, so I don’t buy that for a single second.

Many of the ‘0’ reviews all touch on the same thing: 30fps is “unplayable”, the series has gone “woke”, and the “story is horrible”. (“I never smiled one time,” writes one seemingly perpetually miserable user.).

It has been revealed that there are some legitimate criticisms among these; one person writes they’re not a fan of the life sim elements and prefer the “power fantasy” of older games. That’s fair, but it doesn’t make it a ‘0’.

In a recent development, metacritic reviews have always been a disaster really, because you don’t need to actually own a game to rate it. Many of the best titles ever released have dreadful user scores on the site.

Sources indicate that on the PS Store, though – where you actually have to spend money on the game to score it – the current rating sits at 4.84 based on 170k reviews. So that’s probably a better reflection of how the community feels. (Related: Transformer-based Encoder-Decoder Models...)

My take on all this is that humans are predictable creatures: wherever there’s something popular, you’re probably going to find a group of people eager to tear it down.

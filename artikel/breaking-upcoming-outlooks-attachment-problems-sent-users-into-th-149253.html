📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Microsoft recommended Classic or the web client while two rounds of fixes restored normal service.

A recent enhancement broke attachment handling for some Fresh Outlook users, prompting Microsoft to recommend switching to the web or Classic version of its email client.

Recent findings suggest that things are going swimmingly for New Outlook, the email client that Microsoft would very much like users to migrate to. A code change meant that for some users, opening or uploading email attachments failed. It took the manufacturer two attempts to fix the problem.

It has been revealed that microsoft confirmed there were complications on November 20, as reports that customers were having problems with attachments began rolling in. By the end of the day, the company had added inline images in email to the litany of woe and "paused the rollout of a recent client patch that may be contributing to attachment failures.". (Related: Build A Racetrack codes (November 2026)...)

The advice for affected owners was to switch to the web version, or use Outlook Classic to open or upload attachments and view inline images.

It has been revealed that the good news was that Microsoft produced a fix. The bad news was that this fix only resolved problems with Office attachments and inline images, leaving other attachment types affected, according to incident notice EX1561765.

The Windows behemoth tried again with a "supplemental fix" and reported the remaining problems resolved on November 21. (Related: Towers of Fortune codes (November 2026)...)

Users needed to close and reopen Upcoming Outlook for the fix to take effect.

Microsoft mentioned it had tested the fixes before deployment. Administrators might reasonably wish the original upgrade had received the same attention. The preliminary root cause was a "recent update to the code which supports embedded web content unexpectedly caused impact.".

The clock is ticking for Outlook Classic. Microsoft is clear that Fresh Outlook is the future, though Classic Outlook will remain available until at least 2029. The fact that the manufacturer had to suggest that affected owners fall back on the old warhorse to perform that rarely used email activity – attachment handling – is less than confidence-inspiring.

Still, by the time Outlook Classic is finally put out to pasture, we're sure Microsoft's habit of sending out broken updates will also be a thing of the past. Right? ®.

According to the report, which was released by the university’s researchers Thursday, more than a third of civil suits filed in Santa Clara County, California, and San Francisco County this year show signs of AI use. The researchers noted that this doesn’t include debt collection cases. (Related: Tech Report: University rolls out $5m smart system that...)

In a recent development, among other findings, Stanford’s researchers noted that pro se litigants had higher AI adoption rates. In Santa Clara County, the share of self-represented complaints that showed signs of AI use increased from 19% in 2024 to 72% in 2026. Meanwhile, the share of attorney-filed complaints increased from 5% to 34% during this period.

According to recent reports, researchers also found that the use of AI varies by case type, with employment and business complaints in California showing the highest rates. They noted AI use also varies throughout court filings, with more appearances in the statement of facts and causes of actions sections.

“The sharp rise we document here corroborates that AI in the law is a question of how, not whether,” Stanford’s researchers wrote in the report.

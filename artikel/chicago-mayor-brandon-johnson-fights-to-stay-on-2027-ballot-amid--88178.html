Sources indicate that johnson needs 12,500 valid signatures to qualify for Chicago's Feb. 23, 2027, mayoral ballot. Hearing officers at the Chicago Board of Elections are reviewing more than 25,000 signatures challenged by supporters of perennial mayoral candidate Willie Wilson.

According to recent reports, johnson's campaign notes 6,718 signatures were not challenged and projects that he would finish with 14,788 valid signatures if the first day's 32% validation rate holds. That would put him 2,288 signatures above the requirement, but the figure is the campaign's estimate, not a final board tally. The objectors say early rulings are favoring their challenge, and the review remains unresolved.

The turmoil inside City Hall accelerated after the sudden departure of Chief Financial Officer Ashlee Gabrysch, who stated continuing in the role would "compromise my integrity and professional obligations." Johnson later said she "wasn't the right fit.".

"It’s yet another high-level change in the mayor’s administration and is a sign of continuing turmoil and a lack of direction at a very key time for the city, especially for him heading into a mayoral election," David Greising, CEO of the Better Government Association, told CBS Chicago after Gabrysch's abrupt exit.

A survey of 1,108 adults by NORC at the University of Chicago, conducted Sept. 13-23, found that 22.2% approved of Johnson's job performance, while 52.9% disapproved and 24.8% had no opinion. The survey predates both the current petition review and Gabrysch's departure.

Johnson's proposed $17.6 billion 2027 budget is intended to close an $882.4 million gap, but a 28-member City Council bloc told him it did not see the votes needed to pass his tax proposals.

Standard & Poor's ultimately placed Chicago's BBB general-obligation rating on negative CreditWatch, citing the budget stalemate and management instability. The agency said there was at least a 50% chance of a downgrade within 90 days, which could raise the city's borrowing costs.

The City Council eventually voted 45-5 on Wednesday to authorize an independent review of three years of financial reporting for possible material or intentional misstatements.

Industry observers note that tuna salad typically already comes with plenty of protein and flavor, but boosting these virtues can give you something even more delicious and nutritionally dense. This is especially true if you use boiled eggs, an easy-to-prepare ingredient that's highly compatible with whatever recipe you prefer.

Exact needs vary based on body type and medical history, but most adults need a minimum of about 0.36 grams of protein per pound of body weight daily. Beginning a recipe with canned tuna, which hovers around 22 grams of protein per 3-ounce serving, is a good start, but it can be better. A single boiled egg contains roughly 6 grams of protein and is rich in umami, no matter what texture you cook it to. The whites add a lovely, soft chew to counteract the density of fish meat, while the yolks themselves sort of disappear into the mayonnaise to boost its richness. In a recent development, if you use the best mayo for tuna salad — Kewpie, which is packed with MSG — you'll get an extra umami bomb. Between the fish, egg, and MSG, you have all the makings of a hearty, satisfying meal, whether you eat it straight or heat it up in a melt. The end result is something that tastes more decadent than it does nutritious, full of velvety textures and rich flavors that make it seem like you spent quite a bit of time perfecting your recipe. Combine it all with minced veggies and pickles, and you get something comprehensively nourishing that's ready in a flash.

Eggs can be so much more than simply hard-boiled, so you've got plenty of options when it comes to the exact tastes and textures you want your tuna salad to have. Some bulk out a bowl, some focus on the sauce, and still others can add delightful fresh textures, but all are quite easy.

If you want something with an unobtrusive texture but a great amount of flavor, then boil your eggs for a jammy yolk. The runnier center diffuses through the mayonnaise, infusing it with flavor before it coats every piece of fish. If you're looking for something different, you can pan-fry hard-boiled eggs to crisp up the exteriors and add a touch of roasted flavor to a comparatively fresh and cool dish. If you use a flavored oil, like chili oil or one infused with herbs, this is an easy way to add new seasonings to the overall dish. In a recent development, eggs can either introduce a dichotomy of textures or disappear into the meal, depending on your preference. Leaving your eggs whole or cut in half lets you spoon them into individual bites, preventing your meal from being too one-note. On the other hand, finely dicing them into crumbles is the best way to get their flavor into every nook, cranny, and ingredient. Using an egg slicer can give you the best of both worlds, creating thin slivers of white while the yolk dissolves into the mayonnaise.

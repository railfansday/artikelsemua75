Microsoft Vice Chair and President Brad Smith offered a polite but pointed response to President Trump’s seemingly impromptu threat to tax U.S. companies that build data centers abroad: most of them have to be where their customers are. (Related: Tech Report: How we sped up transformer inference 100x ...)

Speaking on CBS News’ “Face the Nation” on Sunday morning, Smith remarked the president is “making an vital point,” and that there’s room to talk about keeping the training of the most advanced AI models in the U.S., much like the push to make more chips here. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

But he said most data centers run everyday cloud services, such as streaming video, video games and business software, and need to be close to the people using them.

“You can’t support Helsinki from Upcoming Jersey any more than you can keep the lights on in Helsinki from an electricity power plant in New Jersey,” he said.

A tax on foreign data centers could have significant financial implications for Microsoft, which says it has more than 600 data centers in 34 countries.

Trump made the statement Friday at a White House press conference, saying companies would face a “tremendous tax” if they build data centers outside the U.S.

Recent findings suggest that his reason: “We’re not allowing data centers to go into other countries because China will take all of them, and we’re not allowing that to happen. And we’re going to put tremendous penalties on companies that try and do it.”.

.@POTUS: "Whoever wins SI, wins… We're not allowing data centers to go into other countries — because China will take all of them, and we're not allowing that to happen." pic.twitter.com/FiH1LfAQDu.

He gave no rate or timeline and acknowledged that he hadn’t told his own staff. “That’s a breaking story,” he said. “I haven’t even said that to my people. But I’m telling them now — they’re here.”.

Trump had just returned from the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit in Shenzhen, China, where he met with Chinese President Xi Jinping and Russian President Vladimir Putin.

Sources indicate that his one example was an unnamed company that wants to build in Finland. Google declared a €13 billion investment in Finland in September, covering data centers and clean energy projects. Margaret Brennan, host of the CBS program, remarked he might have meant a Chinese project.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, who appeared earlier in the same CBS broadcast, remarked he hadn’t spoken with the president about the proposal, but added that Trump is “very concerned that we don’t do a substantial technology transfer offshore.”.

Smith also fielded questions on other topics in his “Face the Nation” appearance.

TikTok’s owner: Brennan asked about TikTok’s Chinese owner, ByteDance, which is in advanced talks to spend about $500 million more on Microsoft’s Azure cloud, mostly to use OpenAI’s models, The Information reported last week.

Industry observers note that rep. John Moolenaar, R-Mich., chair of the House Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party, stated Microsoft should not sell AI tools to ByteDance, called it unacceptable for a Pentagon contractor, and asked the company for a briefing.

Smith noted that TikTok’s U.S. business is now a separate manufacturer, and mentioned Microsoft expects to answer the committee’s questions on Capitol Hill.

White House ballroom: Microsoft committed $10 million to Trump’s ballroom project, according to an internal donor list reported by the Washington Post. Brennan asked why, and whether Microsoft is worried about scrutiny from a Democratic-controlled Congress.

Industry observers note that smith remarked Microsoft takes the same approach with every administration, regardless of party. “We partner where we can. We stand apart when we should,” he said.

He put the ballroom donation in the first category, and mentioned the manufacturer “didn’t know, obviously,” that the project would involve tearing down the East Wing. In the second category, he pointed to lawsuits Microsoft has filed against the current administration, as it has against previous ones, to stand up for challenges it considered important.

It has been revealed that outlook outage: Smith reiterated Microsoft’s apology for the Nov. 12 global Microsoft 365 and Outlook outage. He said the outage wasn’t caused by AI or a cyberattack.

Speaking more broadly, he mentioned much of the recent wave of tech failures can be seen as “AI ripple effects” — the result of AI making software bugs easier to find, which has companies racing to patch systems faster than they can test the fixes.

(GeekWire has covered that angle in depth, including the surge in AI-discovered bugs, Microsoft’s final outage report and Oren Etzioni’s column on the conspiracy theories.).

According to recent reports, smith mentioned the patching race won’t be finished anytime soon. “The whole information infrastructure of the world is in effect being upgraded right now,” he said.

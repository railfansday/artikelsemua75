It has been revealed that rocket Lab will add another satellite to a growing Japanese Earth-observation constellation today (Nov. 26), and you can watch the action live.

An Electron rocket carrying a radar imaging satellite for Tokyo-based Earth-observation manufacturer Synspective is scheduled to lift off from Rocket Lab's New Zealand launch site today, during a window that opens at 7:00 p.m. ET (0000 GMT and 1:00 p.m. on Nov. 27 in New Zealand).

It has been revealed that you can watch the mission, called "Night Owls Never Sleep," via Rocket Lab starting about 30 minutes before liftoff. Space.com will carry the company's stream if, as expected, it's made available.

Synspective is building a network of "Strix" spacecraft equipped with synthetic aperture radar (SAR), which uses radio waves to generate detailed images of Earth's surface in both day and night lighting, as well as through cloud cover. It has been revealed that today's launch will be Rocket Lab's 14th for Synspective to date, and there are a lot more coming. At the end of September, Rocket Lab unveiled a deal with the tech giant for an additional 20 Electron missions between 2028 and 2031 — the largest commercial launch contract in the rocket's history. That agreement brought Synspective's total number of contracted Electron flights to 47.

Synspective aims to eventually establish a constellation capable of imaging locations anywhere on Earth at intervals of just a few hours.

If all goes to blueprint today, the Electron's kick stage will deploy the Strix satellite into low Earth orbit, 569 kilometers (353.6 miles) above our planet.

Sources indicate that "Night Owl Never Sleeps" will be the 101st mission to date for Rocket Lab. Ninety-two of them have been orbital flights with Electron; the other nine have been missions with HASTE, a suborbital variant of Electron that allows customers to test sensors and other equipment in the hypersonic flight environment.

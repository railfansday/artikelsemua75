Industry observers note that in its ruling that Myanmar committed state-sponsored genocide against the Rohingya people in 2016 and 2017, the United Nations’ top court indicates that Facebook played a role in helping Myanmar’s military spread hate speech against its Muslim minority. The ruling from the International Court of Justice determined that Facebook posts made by the country’s military “dehumanized” the Muslim minority “and at times encouraged and legitimized violence against them,” as reported earlier by Reuters. According to recent reports, the decision that Myanmar committed genocide comes after The Gambia sued the country in 2019 over the military’s violent “clearance operations” targeting the Rohingya Muslims. The military relied heavily on Facebook, which was the most popular platform in Myanmar at the time of the attacks, with a 2022 report from Amnesty International finding that the platform “proactively amplified and promoted content on the Facebook platform which incited violence, hatred, and discrimination against the Rohingya.” Despite numerous warnings from human rights activists, Amnesty International mentioned Facebook failed to stop the spread of hateful content in Myanmar. (Related: Latest Update: Should I Quit My Job? A Decision Framewo...)

The UN is now backing up these findings. It cites a report from the Independent Investigative Mechanism for Myanmar (IIMM), which indicates that a post made by the military ahead of its August 25th, 2017 massacres on the Rohingya people “unleashed a torrent of public vitriol,” with over 200 comments calling for Rohingya people “to be shot, killed, exterminated or permanently removed from Myanmar.”.

In a recent development, facebook later removed military-linked pages containing thousands of posts with anti-Rohingya hate speech published between July and December 2017, according to the IIMM. The company admitted in 2018 that it didn’t do enough to prevent the incitement of violence in Myanmar, and later introduced a “corporate human rights policy” that includes an annual report about its impact on human rights. The Verge reached out to Meta with a request for comment but didn’t immediately hear back.

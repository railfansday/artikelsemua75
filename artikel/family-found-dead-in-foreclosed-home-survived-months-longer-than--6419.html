Latest evidence suggests that at least one member of a family found dead inside a foreclosed-upon Connecticut home survived longer than previously thought—paving the way for transfer of title to a new owner.

The bizarre case hinges upon whether the home's owners died before or after the foreclosure was recorded. If the owners were already dead at the time of the filing, the foreclosure may be deemed illegitimate, and a search for rightful heirs would begin. Edward Marchion stumbled into the mystery in June, when he purchased 7 Stanwich Lane, a four-bedroom, three-bathroom home in Burlington, CT, in a foreclosure auction for $525,000. The 2,800-square-foot home sits on more than 2 acres and comes with a hot tub, patio, and deck, according to the listing.

In a recent development, A few days after placing the winning bid, Marchion finally entered the property and came across a grisly sight. The skeletal remains of the property’s previous owners, Sally Anne Cash and Paul Cash, and their adult son Brian Cash, were found inside.

Marchion then asked a judge to halt the closing on the property to determine the timeline of the Cashs’ deaths.

According to recent reports, in a July 2 filing in the state Supreme Court, Marchion’s lawyer John C. Lewis III asked the court to delay closing until the Connecticut State Police could determine whether the Cash family died before or after the foreclosure action.

"From a title perspective, unless it can be determined that the Defendants were alive on the date the lis pendens was recorded (August 6, 2025), then the foreclosure action is defective," wrote Lewis.

Now, months later, newly introduced evidence from the family’s probate attorney and state medical examiner’s office demonstrates that the family’s deaths likely took place long after the foreclosure paperwork was filed, paving the way for Marchion to receive title to the home.

According to recent reports, paul and Sally Ann Cash purchased 7 Stanwich Lane in 2019. At the time, they took out a $385,000 mortgage on the property. But court records filed by their mortgage servicer showed that the couple stopped making monthly mortgage payments in December 2024. Shellpoint Mortgage Servicing filed a notice of foreclosure on the property on Aug. 6, 2025.

Recent findings suggest that when police entered the property this past June, they discovered Paul and Sally Ann in a second-floor bedroom and Brian Cash in a bedroom above the garage. All three were found in sleeping bags and wearing multiple layers of clothing.

The home was packed with clutter and garbage bags, but authorities stated the only food found in the house was powdered milk.

The family was known to have been extremely reclusive and the home’s interior reflected prolonged isolation and deprivation.

The exact cause of death is still being investigated, though authorities believe that either malnutrition or hypothermia were factors.

In late October, Shellpoint attorney Christopher Throgmartin filed a petition to request that proceeds from the sale of the property to Marchion be used to pay outstanding property taxes that accrued while the investigation was taking place. His petition also requested that Marchion be able to access the property in order to winterize and secure it at is own expense.

Two days later, the surviving relatives of the Cashes signed documents waiving their rights to 7 Stanwich Lane, further paving the way for the sale to Marchion to move forward.

Then, in an affidavit filed on Nov. 10, Connecticut State Medical Examiner Dr. Mary Kate Schwerdt testified, “The condition of [Sally Ann Cash’s] internal organs, and the level of decomposition is consistent with a death that occurred after August 6, 2025.”.

Dr. Melissa Pasquale, who completed the autopsy of Brian Cash, testified that he also died sometime after August 6, 2025. The Medical Examiner’s office believes that Paul Cash died first.

Mary Anne Charron, the probate lawyer appointed as estate examiner for Sally Ann and Brian Cash by the Farmington Regional Probate Court, filed an affidavit stating that “In the course of the investigation, I discovered numerous electronic devices, including multiple laptops, flash drives, hard drives, several phones, a camera, as well as thousands of writings which appear to be journal entries, many of which include dates and time of entry.”. Charron said she found entries believed to be Sally Ann’s, dated August, September, October, and November 2025. She also found outgoing text messages dated from September and October 2025. The last known phone activity Charron documented was dated Dec. 16, 2025, roughly a year after the Cashes stopped paying their mortgage.

"Based on the dates of the journal entries, text chains and screenshots on cell phones, as well as correspondence with the Medical Examiner regarding the level of decomposition of the bodies, it is apparent that Sally is the surviving spouse," Charron wrote in the court filing.

According to recent reports, rather than trying to predict whether the AI bubble will burst, I'd suggest running some retirement simulations to see how different asset allocations would affect your retirement.You could model your current 60/40 allocation and compare it against 50/50 and 45/55 allocations. You could also examine what happens when retirement begins just before a major market downturn. This is particularly important because of what's known as sequence of returns risk.Since it's impossible to predict the future, the important question isn't if or when the market will crash. It's whether your retirement proposal can withstand a significant downturn, and whether changing your asset allocation improves your long-term prospects.I'm the developer of WARPSimLab (https://warpsimlab.org), a free, open-source retirement simulator. WARPSimLab is being discussed on Bogleheads here: viewtopic.php?t=473760. TPAW, mentioned earlier in this thread, and Bogleheads' Retiree Portfolio Model (RPM) spreadsheet are other retirement planning tools worth considering.WARPSimLab includes sequence of returns risk analysis, Monte Carlo simulations, and Historical Window simulations. These tools are useful for evaluating the risks in your retirement portfolio. It also models the cash flow and portfolio impacts of working part-time for a few years instead of retiring immediately.A simulation won't predict the future, of course, but it can give you a much better understanding of the risks you're taking and help you make a more informed decision.Statistics: Posted by alanne — Sun Nov 22, 2026 11:06 pm.

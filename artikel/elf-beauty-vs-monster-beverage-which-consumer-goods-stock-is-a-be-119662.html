In a recent development, investors choosing between growth and stability often weigh the explosive potential of cosmetics against the steady demand for energy drinks. This comparison evaluates whether e.l.f. Beauty (ELF -5.01%) or Monster Beverage (MNST -0.57%) is the better buy.

e.l.f. Beauty disrupts the traditional makeup industry by offering high-speed product innovation and prestige-quality items at accessible prices. Monster Beverage dominates the beverage coolers with its diverse energy portfolio and global scale. Both companies have delivered impressive long-term results, but they offer distinct risk and reward profiles for retail investors. These retail relationships account for nearly 76% of net sales, and such customer concentration adds a layer of risk to the business. It primarily distributes its beverages through an extensive network of bottlers, relying on Coca-Cola (KO +0.94%) as its primary global distribution partner. The company currently serves a growing number of international markets across 80 countries, leveraging this massive network to maintain its market lead.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached close to $9.6 billion. This reflects revenue growth of approximately 20.2% over the previous year. Net income for the period was nearly $2.2 billion, resulting in a healthy net margin of approximately 22.7%.

Recent findings suggest that e.l.f. Beauty faces intense competition in its industry from multinational firms such as Coty (COTY -2.47%) and L'Oréal (LRLCF -1.17%). Because it sources the majority of its products from third-party manufacturers in China, the firm is exposed to trade policies and tariff-related risks. It must also manage rapid growth and system integrations while remaining dependent on a limited number of major retail customers, such as Target and Walmart.

In a recent development, monster Beverage relies heavily on its distribution relationship with Coca-Cola, which is vital to its international performance. The manufacturer faces significant competition in the energy drink category from PepsiCo (PEP +1.11%) and other global beverage giants. Additionally, it is subject to risks related to ingredient pricing, supply chain disruptions, and evolving government regulations regarding energy drink labeling and health standards.

In a recent development, e.l.f. Beauty right now appears more attractively valued than Monster Beverage based on both its lower Forward P/E and its lower P/S ratio.

What sets e.l.f. apart is selling prestige-quality makeup and skincare at mass-market prices, and it introduces newly introduced products faster than the bigger beauty companies can respond. That's why I'd buy it for 2027. Growth is strong, and rhode, the skincare brand founded by Hailey Bieber that e.l.f. bought in August 2025, gives it a bigger foothold in skin care. Shoppers who keep buying small treats when budgets tighten also help the case. I'd treat this as a starter position to watch through 2027, not a stock to hold blindly. The December quarter is the first fully organic comparison, so it will show how fast e.l.f. grows once rhode sits in both periods. Beauty runs on trends, and rhode depends heavily on one founder, so a fast brand can cool swiftly. The debt from its acquisitions is manageable, but it limits e.l.f.'s room if growth slows. Keep the position smaller than you'd give a steadier name. Then watch whether growth holds without new deals, whether margins stay healthy, and whether new launches keep landing. If all three hold, build the position over time. If they don't, you've risked a small stake and not your conviction.

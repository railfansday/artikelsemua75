📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Forty-six years ago today, the Supreme Court issued its ruling in Stone v. Graham, holding that a Kentucky statute requiring a copy of the Ten Commandments to be posted in public school classrooms violated the First Amendment’s establishment clause because it “has no secular legislative purpose.” That 1980 ruling is back in the spotlight this term, as the court considers whether to take up challenges to similar Ten Commandments laws in Texas and Louisiana.

On Monday, the Supreme Court denied review in dozens of cases, including two DEI-related disputes – the first, a challenge from University of California scholars to cuts in research funding; the second, a challenge from California doctors to diversity-related requirements for continuing medical education courses – and a Second Amendment clash over Maryland laws prohibiting guns in “sensitive places.”.

The court also invited the United States solicitor general to share the government’s views on a trio of cases brought against the Taliban by victims of terrorist attacks.

In a recent development, get two or three itemized bids on the same finished proposal, then set your renovation budget at the bid you pick plus 10% to 20% for hidden problems like rot or old wiring. To stay under it, make every choice before demolition, get each change priced in writing, and pay from savings where you can.

Of the homeowners who renovated in 2025, 37% went over budget, the 2026 U.S. Houzz & Home Study found. More than half of them blamed products or services that cost more than they expected, and 35% had picked pricier materials than they first planned.

A budget built from a finished, priced plan makes both causes less likely. The bid prices what you’ll actually build, and the extra 10% to 20% is there for what nobody can see until the walls are open.

Build the budget in this order:.

It has been revealed that say a couple’s chosen bid for a bathroom remodel is $18,000. They set the budget at $21,000, a $3,000 cushion, and when the crew finds rotted subfloor that adds $1,900, they finish $1,100 under budget without borrowing. Cash saved ahead in a high-yield savings account is the cheapest way to pay, since a personal loan or a loan against your home’s equity adds interest.

Then keep the plan from changing. Before demolition, choose every fixture and material, so you never pick in a hurry once the walls are open. Put each change in writing with its price before the crew starts it, and compare your spending with the plan every week.

Keep the cushion for hidden problems like that rotted subfloor, and pay for any upgrade you want midway by cutting a nice-to-have.

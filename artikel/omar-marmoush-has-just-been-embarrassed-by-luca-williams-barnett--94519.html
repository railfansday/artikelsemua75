Tottenham Hotspur fans have been wanting it to happen for a while, and it finally did on Saturday afternoon.

Ever since teenage sensation Luca Williams-Barnett consistently outshone many of his international teammates during Tottenham's pre-season campaign, there has been a clamour for him to get more first-team minutes, and starts.

Spurs' struggles in the early weeks of the campaign clearly made that difficult, and while he has been in and around the first-team and has had some minutes here and there, Roberto De Zerbi gave the 18-year-old his first opportunity from the start against Ipswich Town at the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium. (Related: Stop treating agents as one-off projects and start runn...)

De Zerbi revealed post-match that he decided 10 days ago that the wonderkid would be in the starting XI against the Tractor Boys, with the decision certainly helped by the injury to Mathys Tel.

But this wasn't a case of Williams-Barnett only getting the opportunity because nobody else was available, with Savio, Dejan Kulusevski and Omar Marmoush all starting on the bench against Gary O'Neil's men.

He has clearly impressed De Zerbi on the training pitch, and the Italian has just been waiting for the right moment to blood him from the start. (Related: Breaking: Distributed Training: Train BART/T5 for Summa...)

Williams-Barnett for Tottenham vs Ipswich.

The teenager didn't let his manager down after starting on the left either, as he took just 15 minutes to open his account for the club when he ghosted in at the far post to finish Pedro Porro's cross from close range.

The attacker's afternoon went from good to great as he then assisted Dominic Solanke for the hosts' second goal after the break, following a cross from the left.

Speaking about his performance after the game, De Zerbi noted:.

According to recent reports, luca Barnett is a special player. I'm not surprised, because his talent is bigger than you think. He played very well. I think he's lucky, because he's playing in a top club with top teammates.

"Because yesterday evening, when I gave the first XI, when I said the first XI to the players, I said, 'remember the first game, the debut of you, and help the kid tomorrow, because he's one of us, and if he plays well, we have more chances to win the game'.

Sources indicate that "He's very young, but at the same time, he's a mature guy. He understands the right behaviour to keep in the dressing room, and he's older than his age.".

Omar Marmoush was one of 10 latest arrivals at the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium during the summer, and he was probably one of the most exciting additions out of all of them.

The 27-year-old was prolific for Eintracht Frankfurt previously, and while he failed to replicate those same numbers during his spell with Manchester City, there was still a feeling that he has something about him.

After all, that is why Spurs agreed a season-long loan deal with City in the summer, with an obligation to pay £60m for him at the end of the season.

So far though, Marmoush isn't showing anything to suggest he's worth anywhere near that fee, as he struggles to find his place in this Tottenham team.

With Richarlison cast aside and Dominic Solanke struggling for fitness and form, Marmoush has been given his opportunity as a centre-forward on many occasions, but he has largely been anonymous or ineffective.

His other two main positions in a De Zerbi set-up would be behind the striker or on the left, but he is now competing with James Maddison and the likes of Mateus Fernandes for the former.

His chances of starting on the left are fading too, with Williams-Barnett giving De Zerbi a newly introduced selection conundrum following his stunning display against Ipswich.

De Zerbi already has the likes of Mathys Tel and Savio who can play there, but now Williams-Barnett has staked his own claim.

If we're being honest, the teenager indicated more in 64 minutes than Marmoush has all season, and if he had hopes of nailing down a regular spot in the starting XI at the Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, they have just taken a huge hit.

In a recent development, newly introduced York State Senator Jeremy Cooney has filed a bill that would bring prediction markets into the state's tax base instead of banning them. The proposal puts a state lawmaker at odds with New York's own lawsuit against Kalshi.

Revenue raised under Jeremy Cooney’s FUTURES Act would flow into a Futures Fund for Early Education to pay for universal pre-K.

Industry observers note that the timing gives the bill extra weight. Attorney General Letitia James had sought to stop Kalshi at a virtual hearing held the day the bill was filed. Governor Hochul and James declared on July 31, 2026, that Fresh York had sued KalshiEX LLC for running an illegal gambling operation through its prediction market. According to recent reports, cooney’s bill states that CFTC-registered markets “may operate within the state of New York lawfully.” That language sets it against the state’s executive branch. It also marks a shift from his earlier bill, S8889, introduced in January. That version would have required operators to get a license from the Department of Financial Services. “I’m placing a bet on the future of UPK in New York,” Cooney said in his release. (Related: Building for an Open Future - our new partnership with ...)

According to recent reports, licensed mobile sportsbooks face a far heavier tax than the bill proposes. The gap could widen in practice. Operators would be allowed to deduct some operating expenses from taxable revenue, which sportsbooks cannot do.

Industry observers note that the scope is narrow. The bill covers sports contracts only, so election, economic and culture markets fall outside it. Its official summary also covers the use of prediction markets in election violations. Elected officials and some state employees would be barred from trading.

Recent findings suggest that the consumer rules borrow from regulated betting. Operators would have to verify identity and age, run self-exclusion programs, and offer deposit limits and cooling-off periods. They would also file a yearly consumer-protection audit with the attorney general. The minimum trading age, however, would be left to federal authorities instead of Fresh York’s sports-betting minimum of 21.

According to recent reports, age also came up in court. The state argued that Kalshi lets younger users bet. Justice Crane asked the manufacturer to consider safeguards for users aged 18 to 20. Kalshi’s lawyers said the company could look at limiting access to its contracts for those users in Fresh York.

Latest York is not alone in treating prediction markets as a taxable business. North Carolina’s new law recognizes the CFTC’s exclusive federal authority over prediction markets. It taxes platforms at 6% of net trading-fee revenue from state residents.

Oregon may go higher. Senate Majority Leader Anthony Broadman told OPB he will probably seek a rate above North Carolina’s, perhaps 8%, in the 2027 session. That money would go toward wildfire costs. If more states follow, platforms such as Kalshi and Polymarket, along with their licensed sportsbook rivals, would face higher costs.

In Fresh York, S10748 was sent to the Senate Rules Committee on the day it was introduced, and it remains pending there.

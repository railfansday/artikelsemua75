Goodbye to metasearch having its own ad budget and scorecard. Google lets hotels place ads in its price-comparison results, but is changing how they place those ads. Will the upcoming process make ad buys harder to scrutinize? (Related: My Journey to a serverless transformers pipeline on Goo...)

Google is retiring its standalone hotel ad campaigns and folding paid booking links into its main search advertising product, a shift that changes how hotels budget for, bid on, and measure metasearch (meaning, the platform's price-comparison listings). (Related: Hyperparameter Search with Transformers and Ray Tune...)

Recent findings suggest that the advertising line item hotels have budgeted for years for "Google Hotel Ads" must now be bundled into all of their work on Google Search. That leaves hotel commercial chiefs with a question: what counts as success once metasearch and paid search share an ad campaign?

A Google spokesperson told Skift the change brings bidding, reporting, and tools such as AI Max, its suite of automated campaign features, onto one platform. The update reflects only how advertisers buy ads, the spokesperson stated, so free booking links are untouched.

A Google migration guide that appeared in recent days notes that hotel ad campaigns will be converted automatically into "Search campaigns for Travel" in the firs.

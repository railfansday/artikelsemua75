Conservative leader Kemi Badenoch said the deal "isn't fair" - and Reform's Nigel Farage called it a "betrayal of the British people".

Legislation to implement the deal is expected to be introduced in the UK Parliament next week.

Burnham arrived in Brussels to finalise the latest stage of the government's post-Brexit "reset" of relations with the EU, which originally began under former prime minister Sir Keir Starmer.

The agreements announced on Friday included scrapping most of the checks on British food exports, in return for the UK following some EU regulations, from 2028.

The UK government believes the changes will cut costs for businesses and could bring prices down for shoppers, while critics argue the change will make the UK a "rule taker" from Brussels rather than a rule maker.

According to recent reports, the new youth experience scheme will allow up to 100,000 18 to 31 year olds from the EU to travel, work and study in the UK for up to three years. The same number of young Britons will be allowed into Europe.

The two sides want to ensure that opportunities under the annual scheme will be available to young people by no later than January 2030.

A further six months is available to discuss a deal on tuition fees, which could prove to be a potential stumbling block.

There have been calls from the European side for fees to be reduced for EU students at British universities. The joint statement notes any agreement would also be for UK students in the EU.

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen noted she is "confident" a solution will be found, with Burnham adding: "Me too.".

On immigration, the UK and EU have committed to working closely to tackle migrant smuggling and human trafficking. They also expect their law enforcement agencies to share more data.

Burnham noted "significant progress" has been made to help "disrupt the gangs who are making those dangerous crossings across the English Channel".

The UK-EU statement also mentioned talks on linking the EU and UK emissions trading systems have concluded and they aim to apply the agreement from 2028.

Burnham argued that polling suggests the UK public "want closer ties" with the EU and the deal "delivers that".

Sources indicate that he added: "Beyond that we do need to consider what is the right long-term relationship. Incremental steps only take us so far.".

The prime minister said his government will publish a 10-year strategy next month, adding it will set out its ambitions for reindustrialisation, growth in "all parts of Britain" and opportunities for young people.

Burnham said these ambitions are "connected to our long-term relationship with our nearest partner".

He remarked over the coming months before the next UK-EU summit both sides will "seek to conduct an informal brainstorming process" to look at what those options are.

Burnham added: "What we are announcing today is probably the limit of what we can achieve in this Parliament, given what needs to be done to implement it.

"But we want to inform the Parliament after this one, so that decisions can be taken about that long-term relationship.".

Labour's 2024 general election manifesto pledged there would be "no return to the single market, the customs union, or freedom of movement", and Burnham has committed to this.

The prime minister noted the "British people will be the boss of what happens next", with the next election needing to be held by August 2029.

Von der Leyen, who remarked the summit was a "turning point" for the EU-UK relationship, said "we are open for brainstorming".

Badenoch mentioned the deal was not fair because UK farmers would now have to follow rules made by Brussels and more young people will come to the UK to "compete with our young people who are struggling to get jobs".

Farage said Friday was a "very good day for the EU", adding: "A good day to try and humiliate the one member state that actually had the courage to say, 'We don't need to be part of you, we can govern our own country'.".

In a recent development, in a social media post, Farage, who was a leading figure in the 2016 campaign to get Britain out of the EU, added: "Like every prime minister before him, Burnham is a total fraud. Only Reform will stop the endless betrayal of the British people.".

Liberal Democrat leader Sir Ed Davey stated there needed to be more than "tweaks" to the UK's relationship to the EU.

"A youth mobility scheme is a major step forward, but it's nowhere near enough. We need a whole new deal that actually works for Britain, and fast.".

Bachelor Nation alum Jason Tartick is planning to make this holiday season one to remember after announcing his engagement to Kathryn Hurley earlier this year.

“We are hosting for the first time this Christmas,” Tartick, 38, exclusively tells Us while promoting his “Trading Secrets” podcast‘s partnership with PayPal. “[Kathryn is] an exquisite Christmas decorator. Looks like the North Pole in our house right now.”. (Related: The Partnership: Amazon SageMaker and Hugging Face...)

It has been revealed that in July, Tartick revealed that he’d recently popped the question while they were on vacation in Europe after dating for just under a year. (Tartick was previously engaged to fellow Bachelor Nation star Kaitlyn Bristowe, though they broke up in August 2023 without ever tying the knot.).

The reality star revealed to Us earlier this month that he and Hurley are in the process of planning a destination wedding as a unique way to celebrate their nuptials with “the people that are your life’s work, the people you love the most, in one place.”. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

Before any vows are exchanged, Tartick and Hurley are getting their families together for the first time to celebrate the most wonderful time of the year.

“‘I’m just so excited to have the family at our house and host everyone. And it’s just been a really substantial year. It’s been such a big year for me,” he now tells Us. “I mean, this is the first year that I’ve bought my first house. We moved in together. We got engaged. We’re hosting our first holiday. We’re making our own traditions.”.

He teases, “Next year at this time, we’ll be married, and I’m so grateful. Life has got a funny way of working out, and it always does work out. I’m so happy about that.”.

The personal finance adviser’s “Trading Secrets” podcast is teaming up with PayPal this year to offer tips on holiday shopping, reveal what’s worth the splurge, what’s overrated and how to make the most of your holiday budget without sacrificing the fun.

One crucial piece of spending advice that Tartick recommends is for shoppers to use their customer rewards to their advantage at this time of year.

“I think about rewards like they’re essentially payment tools,” he notes. “They’re going to give you more value out of purchases you’re already planning to make. So one of the things that you’ll see is you want to be aware of what’s going on with the discounts at the retailers that you proposal to shop with. So you can utilize the discounts at the retailers that we’re all going to see right now. You can then be intentional with where you actually make the purchase.”.

Tartick encourages holiday shoppers to set a reasonable budget ahead of time — and try not to let gifting guilt influence any last-minute purchases.

According to recent reports, “When it comes to the holiday season, we get stuck into this obligation purchase time. And where is the cutoff for when we say the gift is made from intention or it’s made from, ‘Oh I feel as though I have to do this,’ so I would tell everybody is: set a budget, make a list before you shop, before you start shopping with all those people that you know without certainty,” he adds. “You are absolutely going to find a way to give to them, and take care of that list first, because we see through all the studies and statistics, so many people make these last-minute obligation purchases, these last-minute check-the-box purchases that might not necessarily be part of their plan, and that’s the biggest concern in November, December. People go and stray away from their plan.”.

Holiday shoppers also routinely feel pressure to take advantage of online sales even when the items on offer aren’t necessarily “must buy.”.

“I think the biggest thing I want everyone to ask themselves is before they hit buy, whether it’s for themselves or for someone else, ‘Would you still buy it if it wasn’t on sale?'” he tells Us. “There are a lot of marketing tactics out there that are set and very. There’s so much time spent to create FOMO and urgency and quick purchases. And I want everyone to just say, ‘Would I buy this if it wasn’t on sale?’ If the answer is no, take a step back.”.

Tartick urges buyers to consider whether they may have “raises and maybe bonuses” on the horizon all while being “strategic with flexible payment options.”.

“You are just being proactive with your planning, and you know that there’s going to be a cash inflow coming,” he notes. “I’m a significant fan of being strategic with flexible payment options and just planning ahead for the big purchases. So if you know something big is coming now, and you know that with cash flow coming in January, February, you’ll be able to budget for it. Budget for those for the larger expenses, and then you could use PayPal’s Pay in 4 — it’ll let you split that cost into four interest-free payments — so you can spread out the cost of that interest-free and your cash flows coming in.”.

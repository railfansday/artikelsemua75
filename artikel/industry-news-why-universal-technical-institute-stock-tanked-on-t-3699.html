By Eric Volkman – Nov 19, 2026 at 7:46PM EST.

Universal Technical Institute (UTI -8.48%) released its final set of earnings for its 2026 fiscal year, and probably wishes it hadn't. The vocational education specialist fell short of analyst estimates, and its guidance also left something to be desired. Investors expressed their displeasure by trading the stock down by almost 9% that day.

After market close on Wednesday, UTI revealed that its fourth-quarter revenue was $235.9 million, up more than 6% year over year. That was on the back of a 6%-plus rise in the average number of full-time students over the year, to 26,251.

Net income took a different trajectory, sliding by almost 4% to slightly over $18 million, or $0.32 per share. (Related: Breaking: How to generate text: using different decodin...)

That top-line figure was slightly higher than the average analyst estimate of just under $235 million. Pundits tracking UTI stock were also modeling a slightly higher earnings per share (EPS) figure of $0.33. (Related: The Reformer - Pushing the limits of language modeling...)

Industry observers note that UTI is in the midst of implementing its multi-year "North Star Strategy," aimed at transforming its historical focus on training for certain vocations into a learning institute for a variety of them. It attributed the dip in profitability to expenses related to this effort.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/19/26.

UTI also proffered revenue guidance for the current fiscal year of $925 million to $945 million.

Industry observers note that however, those expenses -- which include spending on four planned upcoming campus openings -- will continue to impact the bottom line. Management is guiding for annual net income of $0 to $5 million ($0 to $0.09 per share), which would be well down from fiscal 2026's $33.6 million. It's also notably below the $0.73-per-share consensus analyst estimate (ditto for UTI's revenue guidance, which whiffs on the $971 million collective pundit forecast).

It has been revealed that UTI's expansion plans are ambitious and, in my view, achievable. Demand for vocational education is high given the shortage of skilled professionals in such trades, so this feels like the opportune time to build out an educational business. I think UTI has a brighter future than the Thursday market reaction would indicate.

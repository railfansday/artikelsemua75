It has been revealed that gary Larson’s comic strip The Far Side broke the mold of traditional newspaper comics by not having a recurring cast of characters. Peanuts had Charlie Brown, Snoopy, Linus and the rest of the gang. Garfield had Garfield, Odie, Jon, and some others. Calvin and Hobbes had Calvin, Hobbes, and Calvin’s parents. But The Far Side introduced fresh characters and scenarios every single day.

However, there were several different types of characters Larson returned to again and again, like cows, dinosaurs, scientists, and others. One of the most comedically lucrative subjects Larson brought back repeatedly were cavemen, who he often portrayed as prehistoric idiots tangling with dinosaurs and other primitive beasts. Larson returned to cavemen so often that he had a variety of different running jokes reserved just for them. Many of his cavemen comics were about the discovery of fire, while he also had a whole series of others about the invention of the wheel. My personal favorite Far Side cavemen gags, though, were the ones which depicted them with early inventions and innovations, like the first game of checkers or the earliest means of transportation. In a recent development, this is one of those Far Side comics that is so brilliant on its own that it requires no caption to explain the joke. The idea that the first symphony was a single note is so stupid and funny, especially since the cavemen have fairly modern-looking instruments to play the music.

Gary Larson’s depiction of early peer pressure feels like an insane twist on the D.A.R.E. messaging from the 1980s and 1990s. Will that innocent caveman be able to stand up against the influences of his ne’er-do-well peers? Honestly, this needs more than a single panel. We need an entire after-school special about this.

I love the idea that checkers developed from a game which merely had two squares. Larson was so brilliant at taking everything down to its most absurd conclusion and this is a great example of that.

Recent findings suggest that this is another great example of Larson’s absurdist humor. Does it make any sense that the first microscopes would be gigantic? Not really, but it sure is a funny visual.

My favorite detail in this comic strip is just how long the trail is behind the cavemen. Not only is that one caveman’s head in the dirt, but he's been pushed for miles at this point.

Recent findings suggest that necessity is the mother of invention, and this comic strip plays with the idea that the caveman built a car first, then invented the wheel to go with it. It’s one of those Far Side comics that is both remarkably dumb and remarkably smart all at once.

It has been revealed that this comic strip was published more than three years after the previous one, but it sure feels like they go together. Gary Larson did a whole lot of wheel-oriented caveman jokes and this is maybe the best. Just imagine what happens to the caveman riding the wheel once it begins going downhill. It will obviously be very unpleasant (if it doesn't immediately kill him).

Even millions of years ago, plumbers were exactly the same as they are today. I do wonder what he’s going to use that wrench for.

The all-time funniest caveman gag about early inventions has got to be the one with the caption “Early piñatas.” With a stick and a blindfold, piñatas are already a pretty dangerous activity, but fill the piñata full of rocks and they make for a disaster, especially if you win.

Philippe Laffont built his hedge fund, Coatue Management, on his ability to identify major technology trends and the companies that stand to benefit most from them ahead of the crowd. In his latest update for investors about the outlook for the public market, he laid out a bull case for agentic AI. (Related: The one thing Putin, Trump and Xi agreed on — fewer lim...)

In a recent development, it's easy to assert that the ability for AI agents to complete tasks for anyone could be a major transformational technology, but figuring out which companies are best positioned to profit from that transformation is a much tougher task. Laffont doesn't leave investors hanging, though. He identified two that he views as likely winners from the agentic AI era: Meta Platforms (META -5.93%) and Intel (INTC -7.97%). (Related: Breaking: Japan launches a ‘dumbphone’ that doesn’t con...)

Here's the case for both and whether the stocks are worth buying right now.

Meta launched its Muse consumer AI agent in September, and the app saw near-instant success. It counted 10 million customers within a month and a half of its release, according to CEO Mark Zuckerberg.

It has been revealed that laffont and his team at Coatue describe the shift from Meta's feed-based apps to Muse as a shift from monetizing attention to monetizing intent. While Meta has managed to collect huge amounts of data on its consumers that make it unparalleled at monetizing attention, explicit intent is much easier to monetize — just ask Google or Amazon.

Industry observers note that meta's initiative is to take a small cut from every transaction that Muse processes on behalf of its people. Laffont estimates that in 2031, agentic AI commerce could account for $2.5 trillion in global gross merchandise volume. At a 10% take rate for AI agents, that's $250 billion in potential revenue, more than Meta's total revenue over the last 12 months.

It has been revealed that meta's advertising business continues to grow as well, as advances in AI make its feeds more engaging and its ads more relevant to the people they are displayed to. That's evidenced by the increase in both price per ad and total ad impressions in each of the last 12 quarters. Coatue's analysts believe annual ad revenue per daily active user will climb from $68 this year to $153 by 2031.

With Meta's stock trading for about 21.5 times forward earnings estimates, it looks like a bargain if it delivers that expected revenue and earnings growth. In the third quarter, Laffont increased Coatue's Meta position by 79% from the prior quarter, boosting it to 6% of the hedge fund’s public equity portfolio. The early successes of Muse could push him to buy more.

Industry observers note that training AI requires enormous amounts of data to be handled by arrays of parallel processors — the most common of which are GPUs — and a smaller share of CPUs (central processing units). The GPU-to-CPU ratios in AI data centers built for training were recently around 8-to-1.

However, handling the workloads for agentic AI will require servers with many more CPUs. Agentic AI processes require the systems to determine all the steps needed to complete a task and execute them in order. That's a job for a CPU, not a GPU. According to experts, data centers built for agentic AI will need to have equal numbers of GPUs and CPUS, and Laffont believes Intel is well positioned to benefit as a result.

Sources indicate that while we've already seen the huge inflection in GPU demand, this intensifying wave of CPU demand is just getting started, Coatue's analysts argue. As more tasks are handed off to agents, CPU demand will skyrocket. They see the total addressable market for server CPUs climbing from $40 billion in 2026 to $300 billion in 2031.

Sources indicate that of course, Intel isn't the only tech giant competing for share in server GPUs. It faces competition from several large chipmakers, including AMD, Qualcomm, Arm Holdings, and even Nvidia. Nonetheless, it's showing strong progress capitalizing on the opportunity so far, with data center revenue climbing 78% year over year in the last quarter, even as it remains supply-constrained.

Intel holds an even larger share of the PC CPU market, which has faced challenges as a result of rising memory prices. Management expects PC unit sales to decline 20% in the second half of 2026 and another 10% in 2027. That will weigh on the company's growth until memory prices come back down, considering PC sales still account for more of Intel’s revenue than data center CPUs.

Intel's stock price has fallen quite a bit since it peaked this summer. Laffont thinks the pullback is a buying opportunity. In the second quarter, he initiated a position of over 12 million shares for Coatue, and added 3.6 million more shares last quarter. With the stock now trading well below the price it fetched over the summer, he may be in the market to buy more. And considering the massive market opportunity ahead of the company, its current valuation of about 40 times forward earnings appears to be a fair price for the stock, even as Intel faces stiff competition in data centers and headwinds in the PC market.

JetBlue Doubles Down In Fort Lauderdale With New Flights To Grand Cayman & Honduras In 2027

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that jetBlue Airways revealed two newly introduced daily international services from Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood International Airport (FLL) on Thursday, connecting South Florida with Grand Cayman and Tegucigalpa in Honduras starting in February 2027. The airline notes its latest move will expand its international network at the airport to 20 destinations, strengthening its position as a gateway to Latin America and the Caribbean.

Industry observers note that these latest additions follow Spirit Airlines' collapse in May, which created substantial opportunities at its former Fort Lauderdale stronghold. JetBlue has moved aggressively to capture displaced demand, with an analysis of Department of Transportation (DOT) data revealing that the airline's international passenger traffic is growing even faster than its added capacity. It has been revealed that the first new service will launch on February 23, connecting Fort Lauderdale with Owen Roberts International Airport (GCM) in Grand Cayman. Two days later, JetBlue will begin flying to Palmerola International Airport (XPL) in Comayagua, serving the Honduran capital, Tegucigalpa. Both routes will operate daily using Airbus A220 and A320 family aircraft, with introductory one-way fares starting at $99 and $183, respectively. In a recent development, the Honduras route will be JetBlue's first service to the Tegucigalpa area, complementing its existing San Pedro Sula operation. Daniel Shurz, JetBlue's Senior Vice President of Network Planning, emphasized the airline's commitment to expanding international connectivity and providing more options for travelers visiting friends and relatives.

"We're grateful for the way customers across South Florida continue to embrace JetBlue, and we're committed to delivering even more of the destinations and travel options they're looking for. With fresh international service to Honduras and Grand Cayman, we're strengthening Fort Lauderdale's role as a key gateway to Latin America and the Caribbean.".

It has been revealed that grand Cayman represents a return following JetBlue's short-lived service introduced in December 2025. The airline will also offer temporary daily flights to Port of Spain between January 29 and February 15 for Trinidad and Tobago's Carnival. Following the recent launch of new routes to Barranquilla and Cali, Colombia, JetBlue expects to exceed 150 daily departures at FLL during winter peaks, its largest schedule ever.

Industry observers note that jetBlue's international expansion at Fort Lauderdale has been substantial. An analysis of DOT T-100 International Segment data (available through August) shows the airline offered almost 1.7 million international seats in the first eight months of this year, up 33.4% year-on-year. The growth reflects an increasingly diverse network, with new and restored routes complementing established Caribbean and Latin American services.

Much of the additional capacity stems from six international routes rolled out or restored in December 2025, including Aruba, Cartagena, Grand Cayman, Liberia, San Pedro Sula, and Sint Maarten. Meanwhile, Guayaquil, Guatemala City, and Santiago have also seen significant growth, showing how JetBlue has strengthened previously limited operations.

However, growth has not been uniform. Montego Bay experienced a 33.9% capacity reduction, while Kingston, Cancún, and Punta Cana also recorded declines. The expansion has also coincided with international route reductions elsewhere in its network. The net result is that JetBlue is not only growing its FLL international network but also diversifying it and concentrating capacity where opportunities are strongest.

It has been revealed that although JetBlue's international expansion at FLL was already underway before Spirit's demise on May 2, the vacuum left by its former competitor has given it the space to accelerate that growth. To assess performance after Spirit's exit, Simple Flying zoomed in on DOT T-100 data for May-August, compared with the same period last year.

In a recent development, the findings are striking. JetBlue has increased its international seat capacity by 33.0%, yet passenger traffic surged by 45.8%, lifting average load factors from 79.4% to 87.0%, an improvement of 7.6 percentage points. For context, Delta Air Lines averages about 85% load factors on its international routes, and at 87%, Fort Lauderdale is the top-performing base in JetBlue's network.

This clearly demonstrates that JetBlue has not yet reached the limits of demand at Fort Lauderdale, because even as it adds more capacity, it is attracting even more passengers and filling a greater proportion of those seats. And there is the knock-on effect: Growing international passenger volumes also increase ancillary revenues, with JetBlue reporting 20% year-on-year loyalty revenue growth in Q3 2026, alongside a 55% increase in co-branded credit card acquisitions in South Florida.

A larger international network will certainly reinforce those trends. However, the airline's longer-term success will depend on translating this rapid passenger growth into stronger yields and sustainable commercial returns.

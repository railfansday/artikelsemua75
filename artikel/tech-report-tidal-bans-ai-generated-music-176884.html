📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The music streaming service Tidal mentioned Tuesday that it would ban tracks it believes are completely AI-generated starting next month, a move its leader believes “will resonate with both artists and listeners alike.”.

Recent findings suggest that the service will remove the AI tracks starting Dec. 9, making Tidal the first music streaming platform to ban AI music entirely. It comes nearly five months after Tidal noted it would demonetize any AI-powered song, force those artists to label their tracks as either AI-generated or AI-assisted and ban AI songs that were associated with “fraudulent activity,” a category that includes songs impersonating established artists and efforts to “deceive listeners.” Related Stories Amazon Debuts $30 Streaming Bundle That Includes Starz, BritBox, AMC+, MGM+ and PBS Masterpiece In One Place Celebrity Streaming and Commerce Platform TalkShopLive Expands to TikTok Ahead of Black Friday. “But that’s enough, as 43% of tracks distributed to us in the last 90 days have been detected as AI-generated. Something more drastic needs to be done,” wrote Robert Anderson, the Tidal Lead at parent organization Block, in an X thread on Tuesday. “We’ll begin removing all wholly AI-generated tracks from our catalog, and you’ll see them completely gone by the beginning of the year.”.

Industry observers note that anderson remarked that 96% of all AI-generated songs receive zero plays. The move will cleave the platform of 29 million tracks, Anderson added, and have it reject between 80,000 to 85,000 songs a day. (Related: Industry News: Average Retirement Savings by Age: Are Y...)

“We encourage our peers to follow suit,” he wrote.

The move comes as competitors such as Spotify and Apple Music continue to test approaches to combat the rise of AI-generated tracks proliferating on their platforms, many of which the services have labeled some version of “slop” or “spam.” Apple Music stated it would also begin labeling AI-generated or AI-assisted tracks on its platform, while Spotify said it would label artists it deemed AI creations as an “AI Persona.”.

In a recent development, spotify mentioned in a blog post last month it also removed 50 million slop or spam tracks from owners’ recommendations this year, which came after it removed 75 million of those songs from its catalog entirely. The revelation came among a number of updates regarding Spotify’s efforts to combat AI spam.

In a recent development, “This work isn’t finished,” Spotify added. “AI moves fast, and bad actors adapt just as fast. But as AI makes music more abundant, we believe listeners will value human artistry even more. Our job is to make Spotify the place they find it.”.

Model ML raises $250m at a $1.5bn valuation for bankers’ AI agents

Model ML, a London startup that builds AI agents for bankers, consultants and investors, has raised $250m at a $1.5bn valuation. General Atlantic and QED Investors co-led the Series B round, the firm said on Tuesday. (Related: How to train a latest language model from scratch using...)

Recent findings suggest that evolution Equity Partners, Airtree, Forestay Capital, Y Combinator, FT Partners, Latitude, Inception Fund and 13Books Capital also took part. The round takes Model ML’s total funding to $350m. FT Partners led its $75m Series A in November 2025, and HSBC Asset Management invested in August. (Related: Leveraging Pre-trained Language Model Checkpoints for E...)

Industry observers note that in the third quarter, Model ML’s annual recurring revenue grew 12 times year on year and its daily active customers rose 26 times, the company noted. It will use the money to grow its research and engineering teams in London and Newly introduced York.

In a recent development, brothers Chaz and Arnie Englander founded Model ML in 2023, Bloomberg reported. They previously co-founded Fat Llama, a rental marketplace that Sweden’s Hygglo bought for $41.5m in 2022, TechCrunch reported.

Each user gets an agent that runs on the firm’s own systems and data. When news breaks on a company a banker covers, the agent judges whether it matters and prepares the analysis in the firm’s formats. It works in tools such as Excel and PowerPoint to build pitch decks and due diligence reports.

In a recent development, “AI should do more than give you another answer or notification. It should understand your work and get things done,” noted Chaz Englander, Model ML’s co-founder and CEO.

It has been revealed that model ML sends each task to the AI model that suits it best. A growing share runs on its own fine-tuned open-source models. On the work they handle, these cost 2% as much as frontier models and are in most cases five times faster, the tech giant said. It added that customer data is never used to train AI models.

Banks have tied job cuts to AI. Barclays said in October it would cut 5,000 jobs, and Standard Chartered prepares to cut 7,800 jobs in back-office roles by 2030. Englander played down the idea that Model ML is mainly a way to cut costs.

“The average age of a Model ML user is 33 years old, not 23 years old,” Englander told Bloomberg. “These tools are no longer productivity and efficiency tools. They’re revenue generative.”.

Revenue is split fairly evenly across banks, consulting firms and asset managers, he said. Model ML’s rivals include Newly introduced York’s Rogo, which raised $160m at a $2bn valuation in April. OpenAI and Anthropic also sell AI tools to financial professionals.

The Beauty of the Game: Artist Paul Pfeiffer and Collectors Larry and Allison Berg on the Evolving Crossover Between Art and Sports

🌐 Source: Artnews • 📅 20 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (225 words, 📖 Standard)
💡 Key Takeaways

The convergence of art and sports has become too prevalent to ignore. During Art Week NYC, a citywide festival organized by ARTnews, Artforum, and Art in America, ARTnews convened a panel of people who brought different perspectives to this upcoming development: Larry Berg, co-managing owner of the Los Angeles Football Club and the incoming commissioner of Major League Soccer; his wife Allison Berg, an art collector who, with Larry, founded the A&L Berg Foundation to support early-career arts professionals and artists whose work explores social practice (the Bergs helped the Los Angeles County Museum of Art acquire Fútballet, a sculpture by Lyndon J. Barrois, Sr. that brings soccer and art together); artist Paul Pfeiffer, who has long used video, photography and sculpture to transform familiar images from the world of sports and entertainment, making us reexamine fame, crowds, and the power of spectacle, and who was recently appointed the first artist-in-residence at Barclays Center in Brooklyn. My colleague at PMC, Scott Soshnick, editor-in-chief of Sportico, co-moderated.

At our talk on Friday, November 13, at the PMC Studio in Midtown Manhattan, which you can watch in the video above, we discussed how sports is bringing a upcoming audience to art; the connections between team ownership and art collecting; how art can function in a stadium setting; and the beauty of the game.

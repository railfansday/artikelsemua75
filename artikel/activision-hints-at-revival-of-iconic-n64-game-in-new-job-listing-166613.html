A newly posted Activision job listing has revealed that the mega-publisher might be planning to revive one of gaming’s most iconic character duos in a big way, after listing it alongside its other major active franchises like Call of Duty, Crash Bandicoot and Halo.

It has been revealed that the company name-dropped Banjo-Kazooie (thanks, Reddit) in the post beside its hugely successful IPs—an inclusion which came as a surprise to fans, considering the Rare property has been long dormant. It immediately ignited speculation online that Activision could be preparing a newly introduced major outing for the much-loved pair after years of collecting dust in the company’s trough of original characters. Sources indicate that out of all the franchises listed in the post’s mission statement, Banjo-Kazooie is the only one without an active game or modern entry, making its inclusion stick out like a sore thumb. Titles mentioned like Halo, Age of Empires and Sea of Thieves are all receiving major live-service support or have confirmed active projects, whereas Banjo-Kazooie hasn’t seen an original entry since 2008’s Nuts & Bolts. The company name-dropped Banjo-Kazooie in the post beside its hugely successful IPs … igniting speculation online.

Industry observers note that that game received mixed player feedback upon its release regarding its shift away from being a traditional 3D platformer to becoming a vehicle-building and mission-based game. Since then, the franchise has been on ice with little, if any, news of what might be coming next.

Sources indicate that the pair’s most recent outing was as DLC characters for Super Smash Bros. Ultimate, and they saw a nostalgic return through Nintendo Switch Online ports. But no original content has been developed for the IP in years, and the game’s original developer, Rare, is now under the Xbox / Activision umbrella of studios.

Industry observers note that the Project Manager job listing notes the upcoming role will feature defining and driving “the publishing product strategy” for premium console and PC titles—verbiage which certainly indicates that Banjo-Kazooie might be getting a new push on modern platforms. But whether it’s just a red herring, or Activision trying to show off the feathers in its cap, fans are unsure.

Recent findings suggest that under the leadership of XBOX’s CEO Asha Sharma, the gaming giant has been shifting its focus towards nostalgia plays—much like other entertainment mediums, from TV to movies. There’s a lot of money to be made in the wistful feelings of rosy days gone by, and Sharma’s announcements have made it clear that the company prepares to work hard to earn that slice of the pie.

XBOX is at the moment celebrating its 25th anniversary, and has made clear it wants players to fondly remember their years of gaming on the platform by making classic boot-up sequences available on modern Xbox Series X/S consoles.

Under the leadership of XBOX’s CEO Asha Sharma, the gaming giant has been shifting its focus towards nostalgia plays.

It is also attempting to revive some of its most successful IPs from days gone by, such as starting fresh with a reported Halo reboot after gutting original dev 343 Studios with lay-offs. Development of the next Halo project will be under Activision, leaving the direction unknown. The company will also launch a reboot of its fantasy RPG series Fable early next year—the series’ first mainline entry since Fable 3 in 2010.

Going forward, XBOX hopes to “meet players where they play” with its next-generation hardware, codenamed Project Helix. The platform is being designed to run high-end PC games natively, blurring the lines between console and gaming PC. The manufacturer is aiming to launch Project Helix at the end of 2028 with an eye-watering price tag that makes a PlayStation 5 look cheap.

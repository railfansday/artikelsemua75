The FA may need to consult the government over the law on the operation of drones after Manchester United's training session was recorded.

That is the opinion of former head of PGMOL, Keith Hackett, speaking exclusively to Football Insider.

The drone captured 12 minutes of footage as it flew high above fresh manager Mauricio Pochettino's training session on Thursday.

The operator has since apologised and claimed that no laws were broken, with Man United opting against pursuing legal action.

But the Red Devils are reportedly willing to do exactly that if a similar event takes place, and internally believe that, even if drone laws were not breached, several other laws were. (Related: Industry News: Trump calls Putin and Xi friends. They h...)

Last month also saw an incident take place with a drone, with Arsenal's Carabao Cup tie with Fleetwood Town interrupted for over eight minutes after one was spotted flying above the stadium.

The footage, published on Thursday and later removed, indicated Pochettino and several of his players and staff training. (Related: Latest Update: Two weeks later, Senate vote count is co...)

The Argentine is set to take on Liverpool in the first game of his tenure on Sunday, and will have been incredibly frustrated to have had some of his preparation posted for their opponents to see.

The FA kicked Southampton out of the Championship play-offs at the end of last season when they were discovered to have been spying on their opponents' training sessions.

Although the drone operator in this instance has claimed to have had no relation to Liverpool, the seriousness of the incident is clear.

"The drone operator has apologised and in the statement has stated that no law was broken," Hackett told Football Insider.

"When a drone is flying over players, there is a danger to players if it fails and drops to the ground.

"Having fined and suspended the Southampton manager [Tonda Eckert] for Spygate, I question whether the FA need to consult with the government to determine if the law needs to change.".

While Thursday's incident has ultimately had few consequences, there will be fear of copycat drone operators.

Sources indicate that "They sent me the footage yesterday but the vital part of this is that it shouldn't be legal," Liverpool manager Andoni Iraola said. "It happened to United but it could happen to me.

"Yesterday when [television cameras] had access to our training and the cameras recorded 15-20 minutes of our training, you know who trained and who didn't. It's not the matter of the advantage.

"It's the fact that someone can do this. There's a reason we train behind closed doors because there's somethings you have to respect. I don't think it's nice for any club, honestly.".

Sources indicate that "I don't know what the solution is obviously but technology is growing. The ability to get into systems is growing at a really rapid rate and we need to be very aware of that but I cannot tell you what the solution is that to be fair," Arsenal boss Mikel Arteta said.

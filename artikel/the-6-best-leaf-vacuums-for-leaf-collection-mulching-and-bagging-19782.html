Industry observers note that A leaf vacuum is a handy tool for collecting all the foliage that lands on your lawn each autumn. Instead of using your leaf blower or rake to reach the mess around your landscaping, behind patio furniture, and up against retaining walls, a leaf vacuum simply inhales everything in its path. It’s an especially nice lawn care upgrade if you plan to bag all your leaves, since essentially all leaf vacuums double as leaf mulchers, shredding their contents into mulch for convenient storage and efficient composting. Sources indicate that when you’re picking out a new leaf vacuum, you need to understand what they can and cannot do. A leaf vacuum is not a replacement for a rake or a leaf blower. It won’t suck up every leaf in sight like you would dirt off your living room carpet.

If you have a lot of leaves to collect, the vacuum can suck them up and mulch them effectively. However, most leaf vacuums cover only a small area at a time, so spending a little time raking or using a leaf blower to gather your leaves makes a big difference. Your leaf vacuum will swiftly clear large leaf piles and hard-to-reach pockets of debris, gobbling them up and breaking them down into mulch.

To clear everything without gathering leaves first, you’ll need a walk-behind leaf vacuum, which you can run over your lawn like a lawn mower to collect leaves.

According to recent reports, these offer convenience, but their suction power is limited. They're easy to start and require little maintenance. However, corded models are limited to the 1500 watts available from a standard electrical outlet, and cordless models are limited by their rechargeable batteries’ output.

With those limitations, they cannot match the capability of a gas-powered model. An electric model might struggle to break down heavier material like wet leaves, twigs, and acorns, while a gas-powered leaf vacuum can handle whatever you throw at it.

Industry observers note that your range is also limited by either the length of a power cord or the runtime of a rechargeable battery. Given those limitations, electric leaf vacuums are ideal for smaller, more contained jobs, like gathering leaves that have collected on a back porch or navigating through landscaping.

Sources indicate that they offer far more power than their electric counterparts, letting you suck up bigger, heavier clumps of leaves and debris like acorns and twigs. They’re also less prone to clogging. Their large engines make them heavier than electric models, and they typically use pull starters, so they aren't as easy to start as electric models. They also require some seasonal maintenance: you may need to mix fuel and periodically change the oil. The extra work is worth it, though, if you have a larger yard with a lot of landscaping to clear and leaves to bag.

According to recent reports, as a leaf vacuum sucks up lawn debris, it shreds everything, creating more room in its collection bag. Your leaf vacuum's mulch ratio tells you approximately how many bags of uncut leaves will fit when mulched. For example, a leaf vacuum with a 12:1 ratio can chop 12 bags of whole leaves into a single bag. A higher mulch ratio generally means the vacuum can grab more leaves before you need to unload it. It also generally means you’ll need fewer bags, which helps if you’re planning to put everything on the curbside for pickup.

That noted, the mulch ratio isn’t a perfect measurement. The amount of material that fits in the collection bag also depends on the bag size, which can vary from model to model. Also, keep in mind that the ratio applies to the collection bag, not a standard garbage bag size. Don’t assume that a leaf vacuum with a 16:1 mulch ratio will turn the 16 bags on your curb into one.

You also need to consider whether you plan to recycle those chopped leaves as mulch to nourish your lawn and garden. (Which we absolutely recommend.) If you plan on using your shredded leaves as mulch or compost, look for a model with a higher mulch ratio (10:1 or higher). Shredding your leaves into smaller pieces helps them break down into compost more effectively.

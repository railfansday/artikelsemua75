According to recent reports, we’ve just seen American formally retire its Boeing 777 first class, and with that, no airline in the United States has a “true” international first class anymore. It’s the end of an era, because going back many decades, premium products were largely pioneered by US carriers. So I think this raises the question — why can US carriers not make first class work in a way that’s economically viable, especially as they keep talking about how premium they are?

Recent findings suggest that before we get into this, let me make sure we’re all on the same page when it comes to terms. This post isn’t about the premium cabin on domestic flights, which is often referred to as first class. Instead, it’s about international flights that have a cabin above business class, when it’s marketed as first class. Sources indicate that airline executives are divided on the merits of having an international first class cabin. Some insist that there’s no need for such a cabin, as business class has improved so much. Meanwhile others absolutely see merit to such a product. According to recent reports, what’s interesting is that for the airlines that succeed with first class, the single biggest market — and single biggest point of sale — is the United States. So how is it that carriers like Air France, Lufthansa, SWISS, etc., can make money offering first class to the United States (primarily targeting Americans), but US airlines seemingly can’t?

The US has way too many people with tens or hundreds of millions of dollars, or even billions of dollars. There are plenty of people who would pay to fly private, and would pay for Air France La Premiere first class, but would never consider paying the high price for first class on a US carrier, because it’s just not worth it.

It’s no surprise that first class at US carriers has historically failed. In many ways, it reflects the failures of the industry from decades past. In other words, first class has long been thought of as “upgrade class,” rather than something actually worth paying for. US carriers have managed to change that narrative on domestic flights, but not so much on international flights.

Industry observers note that at this point, no airline from the United States offers first class on international flights anymore. For airlines that can’t stop talking about how unbelievably premium they are, I find that to be a pretty perplexing reality. I know that many people have just accepted “oh, a good business class is the future,” but I have a different take.

I think no US carrier actually being able to offer a globally competitive product is a failure in strategy and creativity. They have access to the world’s most lucrative customer base, and other airlines can seemingly make money serving US customers with a first class product. The issue is, US carriers have long not actually given people a reason to fly first class, all while offering too much capacity.

Personally I think there would be a lot of merit to an international first class product that’s boutique, and is operated in the right markets. That’s not to say that I think it’ll happen — I don’t — but I do think it could succeed. The only real hurdle to overcome would be how to ensure good service….

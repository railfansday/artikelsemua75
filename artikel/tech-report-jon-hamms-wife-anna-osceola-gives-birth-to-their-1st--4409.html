Jon Hamm and his wife, Anna Osceola, are reportedly parents after welcoming their first baby.

Us reached out to reps for Hamm for comment.

News broke in August 2026 that Osceola was pregnant after she was spotted showing off her baby bump in a bikini on a then-recent getaway to the Hamptons.

Recent findings suggest that “Jon has always wanted to be a dad with Anna. Having a family was something that was really important to both of them from the beginning,” a source exclusively told Us later that month. “They’ve talked about this chapter for a long time and were always on the same page about wanting children, so for it to actually be happening now feels incredibly special.”.

According to the insider, the Your Friends & Neighbors star was “over the moon” about becoming a dad and had been “so attentive and excited about every little milestone” throughout Osceola’s pregnancy.

“They’re both soaking it all in and are just so excited to become parents,” the source added to Us. “Their relationship has only gotten stronger, and they feel really lucky to be going through this together.”.

Hamm and Osceola met in 2015 while working together on Mad Men. While Hamm starred as Don Draper on all seven seasons, Osceola joined the show for its final season, playing a receptionist named Clementine.

Sources indicate that despite working together on the hit period drama, Hamm and Osceola’s bond didn’t turn romantic until 2020, three years after they were briefly linked in 2017. Us confirmed in February 2023 that Hamm proposed to Osceola. The duo tied the knot later that year.

“Everybody I love was there. It was so emotional, and it felt right. I was like, ‘OK, I guess this is a thing,’” Hamm told The Hollywood Reporter in 2024 of their wedding day. “We did it [where the Mad Men finale was shot in California], which was also amazing. Again, it was like a reset, a reboot, a reframe, a reimagining, nine years later, almost to the day. So, it’s been great.”.

At the time, Hamm also expressed his hopes to expand their family after previous indecision about becoming a father.

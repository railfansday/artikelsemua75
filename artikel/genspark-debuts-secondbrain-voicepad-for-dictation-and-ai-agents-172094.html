Sources indicate that genspark introduced SecondBrain VoicePad on November 24, 2026, a five-key desktop controller for Windows and macOS that dictates text, opens applications and triggers AI workflows. The organization noted preorders were available, with the first production run scheduled to ship in January 2027.

Industry observers note that the 85-gram device has a CNC-machined aluminum body, dual microphones and a Bluetooth connection. Each programmable key can start voice input, open an installed app such as ChatGPT, Claude or Notion, run Genspark’s Meeting Notes agent, or execute shortcuts including Enter, Copy and Paste. Recent findings suggest that smart Actions chain several steps to one key. Genspark’s example opens Claude, accepts a dictated question and submits it with one press. A double-tap on a Meeting Notes assignment starts recording, followed by a summary when the meeting ends.

Sources indicate that the official product page lists approximately 20 hours of use per charge and a magnetic mount with an included metal plate. The device can also record without a computer, giving it a function away from the desktop setups that host its other features. (Related: How to Budget for a Wedding Without Going Broke...)

Recent findings suggest that genspark indicates the dual microphones can capture normal speech or whispers in loud or shared offices. It compares average typing at about 40 words per minute with natural speech at about 160 to illustrate the potential benefit of dictation, while noting that individual speeds vary.

The Kickstarter project page describes VoicePad as a hardware controller with no AI models of its own. A key press sends voice to Genspark’s existing Speakly app or Genspark AI workspace for edited dictation, translation into English, Ask Genspark, and meeting notes and memos in SecondBrain.

Sources indicate that voicePad listens only when a key is pressed, with no wake word or always-on listening. Meeting notes and memos are saved to the user’s SecondBrain account. Genspark cautions that speech recognition and translation can make mistakes, especially in noisy rooms or with uncommon names and terms, so text is placed where it can be reviewed before sending.

It has been revealed that the official FAQ notes built-in memory supports offline meeting and voice-memo recording and playback. Voice typing, translation, meeting notes and AI agents require an internet connection.

VoicePad’s retail price is $169. A separate $9.99 reservation, refundable before a Kickstarter pledge, holds a $99 VIP price and free shipping; it does not count toward the $99 pledge. The VIP terms add priority shipping and 2,000 Super Agent credits, while the standard allowance is 1,000 credits for the first year.

No subscription is required. Every VoicePad includes unlimited voice typing for the first year, followed by 4,000 free words per week, plus 300 minutes of meeting transcription with AI summaries each month. Credits are used only by Genspark’s AI agents.

The FAQ says shipping will follow pledge order, with the first production run allocated to Kickstarter backers. VoicePad’s central trade-off is that recording can work independently, while dictation, translation, meeting notes and agents still require connectivity and remain within Genspark’s software and service allowances. The device extends the company’s AI workspace into a dedicated peripheral, but the capabilities that distinguish it depend on the platform around it.

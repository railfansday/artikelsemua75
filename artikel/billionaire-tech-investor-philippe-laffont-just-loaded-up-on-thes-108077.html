Billionaire tech investor Philippe Laffont of Coatue Management was busy rebalancing his portfolio in Q3. Interestingly, four of the hedge fund manager's biggest buys, all of which were more than $1 million in the quarter, were in the application layer space, not hardware. His largest purchase in the quarter was in Salesforce (CRM -1.47%), followed by Microsoft (MSFT -2.77%), Meta Platforms (META -5.93%), and Shopify (SHOP -6.25%). Let's look at what may have attracted Laffont to these AI stocks.

According to recent reports, given Laffont's buys in the quarter, he seems particularly bullish on AI agents. If this is the case, his buy of Salesforce makes a lot of sense. The manufacturer has worked diligently to build the foundation to become a leading agentic AI company in the enterprise space. (Related: Build A Racetrack codes (November 2026)...)

In a recent development, AI agents are only as good as the data to which they have access. Salesforce created its Data 360 solution, which used zero-copy technology to retrieve data from both within an organization and from outside storage places, like cloud providers and data warehouses, without the time and cost of moving it. It then acquired Informatica to clean and organize this data. Since then, the company has aggressively acquired companies like Fin, Qualified, Cresta, and Braze to improve its front- and back-end for AI agents.

In a recent development, while Microsoft's growth is being driven by its cloud computing unit, Azure, the company has also been making a major push with its Copilot AI assistant and is seeing a lot of success. Last quarter, Copilot saw an eightfold increase in customers to more than 50,000 users, as it is becoming one of the go-to providers of enterprise agents.

The company also shifted to a hybrid seat plus consumption model, which is driving growth for both Copilot and GitHub. Microsoft said this pricing strategy shift is boosting average revenue per user growth, as well as increasing its total addressable market. It noted last quarter that agentic AI consumption features at GitHub have more than doubled since it changed its pricing model.

Overall, given how ingrained Microsoft is with enterprise productivity tools, the company looks set to be a major AI software-as-a-service (SaaS) winner.

Meta has been one of the best companies at employing AI to drive growth in its core business, but it's the introduction of its Muse AI agents that could be the real game-changer for the social media firm and its stock. AI is already driving strong ad revenue at its social media sites by feeding users more content and ads they are interested in, leading to a higher ad load and stronger ad prices. Now, its AI agents add another potential big revenue stream for the company.

According to recent reports, while Meta's consumer AI agents can help individuals perform a lot of different tasks, the growth driver for Meta will be in facilitating e-commerce purchases. Here, the organization will generate revenue either through affiliate or transaction fees. It's a huge market, and Meta's massive user base puts it at a big advantage to capture a nice chunk of the agentic AI commerce market.

Industry observers note that keeping on the theme of agentic commerce, Laffont was also busy buying shares of Shopify. The e-commerce software company is one of the best ways to play this theme. It co-developed the Universal Commerce Protocol (UCP) with Alphabet (GOOGL -2.70%) (GOOG -2.59%) and others and uses that protocol for its AI-ready database called Shopify Catalog. This makes it ideal for agentic commerce, and catalog-based searches converted to sales at double the rate of ordinary searches last quarter.

At the same time, its Sidekick AI assistant has been a hit with merchants. Daily active users soared 3.7 times year over year last quarter, and it recently launched a newly introduced online store builder to help merchants better personalize their digital stores and build them more quickly.

Overall, Shopify looks positioned to be one of the biggest winners for agentic commerce.

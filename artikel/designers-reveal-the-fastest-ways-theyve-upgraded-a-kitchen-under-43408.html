Industry observers note that as you whip up a weeknight meal or prep for a dinner party, you might find yourself daydreaming—and envisioning the kitchen you wish you had. While your current cook space might be perfectly functional, it just doesn’t have the aesthetics you long for.

Sources indicate that alas, the kitchen is widely regarded as the most expensive room to renovate. As Houzz reports, the average cost to remodel a kitchen is currently $26,500. But if you’re happy with things like cabinets and appliances (or a renovation is far off in the future), and you want to focus on just the visuals, you can give the space a glow-up with as little as $100. It can indeed be done, and ahead, designers are sharing their best budget-friendly upgrades.

Industry observers note that rugs are such thrift-able items. A good rug is basically a necessity in a kitchen, and it can easily be swapped with a latest one that refreshes the visuals. (Related: Industry News: Director of Machine Learning Insights...)

Brooke Mennen-Talsma, principal interior designer at Brooke MT Interiors, shares that in the above kitchen she designed, she added a thrifted runner that she discovered for just $40, adding a focal point to an arched pantry.

She says to not underestimate the power of secondhand, and a lot of her favorite pieces in her portfolio are thrifted items.

Sources indicate that switching in fresh hardware for your cabinets is something that can be done in an afternoon, and it can be gentle on the wallet, too. You can also choose to add backplates to your existing hardware, as Sherrell Neal of Sherrell Design Studio recommends. She calls it a “quick upgrade that can stay under $100,” depending on the size and number of cabinets.

While latest lighting in a kitchen can swiftly add up, you can add light in other, more economical ways.

Recent findings suggest that for instance, as Kira Young, founder and principal designer at High Tide Design, claims, you can simply go with a “cute countertop lamp with a pretty little shade.” She advises specifically purchasing a rechargeable lamp, with no messy cords and no need for an outlet. It can also be easily moved anywhere.

Industry observers note that homeowners are increasingly opting for collected, and even pleasantly cluttered, looks in their homes. But if you really want your kitchen visuals to look polished and pleasing, Heather Kirk, principal interior designer at Kirk Riley Design, suggests highlighting a “hero piece,” or a favorite item that shines and steals focus.

Think strong statement pieces that offer both beauty and function, “like a cheerful tea kettle on the stove or a dramatic footed bowl on the counter,” as Kirk recommends.

According to recent reports, emily Spanos, creative director of Emily June Designs, says that in one kitchen she designed, she painted only the lower cabinets a bright turquoise and kept the walls and upper cabinets a soft white. Paint is one of the cheapest ways to promptly upgrade a home, and it’s something that can effectively be used in the kitchen.

Industry observers note that “Painting one element costs a fraction of painting everything, but still highlights what you want the eye to fall on,” Spanos says. “A gallon of paint and a weekend’s work keeps you under budget.”.

In addition to going with lower cabinets, you could consider painting an island, pantry door, the trim, or the inside of a glass-front cabinet.

To allow your eye to have multiple things to admire in your kitchen, go with a “layered look,” as designers call it. Think: linens, decor, rugs, and artwork.

Spanos indicates that she has accomplished this look for under $100 in a few projects she’s worked on. In one art-filled home, she carried the creative feel into the kitchen by displaying art on a couple shelves above the sink area. In another kitchen, she laid down a black-and-white geometric rug for visual interest, which tied into the matte black hardware.

She states, “A few quality tea towels, a small rug or runner, and a piece of art can all be found affordably, especially if you shop vintage or, even better, your own home.”.

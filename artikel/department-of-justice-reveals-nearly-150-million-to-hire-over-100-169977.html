Department of Justice Reveals Nearly $150 Million to Hire Over 1,000 Law Enforcement Officers and Deputies

Through the Office of Community Oriented Policing Services (COPS Office), the Department of Justice today declared it has awarded nearly $150 million under the 2026 COPS Hiring Program (CHP). CHP is a competitive grant program that provides funding directly to state, local, tribal, and territorial law enforcement agencies to pay for the entry-level salaries and fringe benefits of newly hired and rehired full-time sworn officers/deputies to make America safe again.

According to recent reports, CHP is a flagship program in the COPS Office, which has a long history of promoting police partnerships. Through CHP, this Administration supports our nation’s front-line heroes by providing them with needed resources and expertise to enhance policing strategies and tactics. The express purpose of CHP is crime reduction through effective common-sense policing.

According to recent reports, “Under the leadership of President Trump, violent crime has cratered to record lows,” stated Attorney General Todd Blanche. “We were honored to host President Trump at the Department of Justice today to announce, among other actions, $150 million in grants for state and local law enforcement through the COPS Hiring Program. These funds will put more officers on the beat and keep the American people safe.”. (Related: Industry News: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

According to recent reports, the CHP program focuses on advancing administration priorities. Funding was prioritized for agencies that focused on: Violent Crime, Squatting and Encampment Enforcement, Homeland and Border Security, Nuisance Abatement and Quality of Life, School-Based Policing, and Combating Domestic Terrorism. Additional priority consideration was given to agencies that commit to ending deadly sanctuary city polices, agencies that participate in the Homeland Security Task Force, and agencies that are within rural jurisdictions. Of the selected law enforcement agencies, one hundred and fifty-three recipients will use the funding to focus on violent crime, 40 will focus on school-based policing, 36 will seek to address nuisance abatement and quality of life, 17 will focus on other innovations in common sense policing, 16 will focus on homeland security and seven will focus on squatting and encampment enforcement.

According to recent reports, the demand for CHP funding remains extremely high among law enforcement agencies nationwide. The COPS Office received 551 requests for funding and was able to award 269 agencies, approximately 49%. More information on the 2026 CHP awards can be found here https://cops.usdoj.gov/chp-award.

The COPS Office is the component of the U.S. Department of Justice responsible for advancing the practice of community policing and the Administration’s priority of Making America Safe Again by supporting the nation’s state, local, territorial, and Tribal law enforcement agencies through information and grant resources. To date, the COPS Office has been appropriated more than $21 billion to advance community policing including grants awarded to more than 13,000 state, local, and Tribal law enforcement agencies to fund the hiring and redeployment of more than 141,000 officers.

Industry observers note that loyalty Lobby flagged Accor’s pre-Black Friday subscription sale. Combined with a weakened Brazilian currency this creates an opportunity.

Accor is much bigger than its presence in the United States would have you think. The French hotel firm finished September with 5,857 hotels and 885,219 rooms. They operate in more than 110 countries, with a huge presence in Europe and extensive coverage in Asia and Australia.

Recent findings suggest that their brands include Fairmont, Raffles, Sofitel and MGallery at the luxury end, along with Swissôtel, Pullman and Mövenpick. Then there are Novotel and Mercure, and the budget ibis brands. U.S. options include Fairmont Austin and Raffles Boston.

I took advantage of the Bilt status match I wrote about this summer, which offered up to Platinum. (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

Accor points are worth 2 euro cents apiece toward eligible hotel stays. The familiar redemption is 2,000 points for €40. Using November 18 exchange rates, that’s about $43.87, or 2.19 U.S. cents per point. (Related: How to train a latest language model from scratch using...)

There’s no hotel award chart. An expensive room requires more points. But you can apply points to paid rates without hunting for a limited supply of standard award rooms, and a lower room rate means fewer points needed also.

Reward points normally expire after 12 months without eligible earning activity. A qualifying points credit extends the whole balance, even if you earn just one point. Partner earning can keep the account alive without another hotel stay. An active ALL Signature subscription also protects Reward points from expiration.

In a recent development, here’s the calculation using the published monthly prices multiplied by 12, less 20%, and November 18 exchange rates. Dollar amounts are rounded.

Your card’s conversion rate will also differ slightly, and be sure to use a card without foreign transaction fees.

The Explorer plan is the better pure points purchase. You’re buying hotel credit for about 29.8% below its redemption value, compared with 23.8% for Absolute. Absolute buys more points and more status credit, but each redeemable point costs more.

You can stack this with ALL Accor+ Explorer, a separate paid membership offering 15% off eligible rates at participating hotels worldwide, plus benefits concentrated in Asia Pacific. Accor uses “Explorer” for both a Signature subscription tier and a different membership. Apparently someone thought that was a good idea.

Recent findings suggest that buying discounted points and then spending them against an eligible discounted room rate is where this gets better. Combining a 15% room discount with points purchased 29.8% below face value produces about 40.3% off the underlying room price, before the separate membership cost and the rewards you’d otherwise earn.

There are limits to that stack:.

In a recent development, signature’s monthly Status points are separate from its redeemable Reward points. Absolute supplies 1,000 Status points a month, or 12,000 over a full year, versus the 14,000 needed for Platinum. Those points count in the calendar year they’re credited and reset at year end.

There’s also an escape valve if your hotel prepares change: Accor points transfer to airlines.

Industry observers note that many other partners offer a worse ratio. British Airways, for instance, takes 4,000 Accor points for just 2,000 Avios. Use Iberia for Avios rather than accepting half as many through British Airways.

In a recent development, at the Explorer subscription price above, you’re acquiring points for about 1.54 cents each. That’s also the acquisition cost of a mile through a 1:1 transfer. But once you have the points, moving them to an airline means giving up about 2.19 cents each in hotel credit.

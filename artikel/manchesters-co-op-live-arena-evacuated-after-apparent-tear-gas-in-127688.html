Incident occurred near the end of Sunday night show by US rapper Don Toliver, with one person taken to hospital.

Police have evacuated Manchester’s Co-op Live arena after a substance believed to be teargas was let off in the crowd during a Don Toliver concert.

Greater Manchester police noted the incident occurred near the end of the US rapper’s show on Sunday evening.

According to recent reports, greater Manchester police stated concertgoers had been safely evacuated, with several people being assessed on site for side effects as a precaution and inquiries under way to determine what the substance was. No serious injuries have been reported.

Assistant chief constable Steph Parker said in a statement: “Our priority was to ensure the safety of every person that was attending the concert. (Related: AprielGuard: A Guardrail for Safety and Adversarial Rob...)

According to recent reports, “Several people were treated on site, and one has been taken to hospital at this time. Anyone who has left the event and since become unwell is advised to attend their local hospital. (Related: Latest Update: One Year Since the “DeepSeek Moment”...)

“We understand the concern that this incident will have raised – particularly those with loved ones at the venue last night – and we’d like to thank everyone for their patience as we dealt with it.

“There were over 12,000 people in attendance during this concert who were safely escorted from the arena so they could make their way home.”.

She continued: “Our declaration of a major incident has allowed us to ensure emergency services and partner agencies can work together swiftly and effectively to coordinate a safe response.

“We will now begin to fully understand what this substance was and how this incident came about.”.

The north-west ambulance service stated one person was taken to hospital and more than 50 were assessed by clinicians.

The service stated in a statement: “We deployed several emergency crews, including our Hazardous Area Response Team, to support the safe assessment and treatment of people at the scene.

It has been revealed that “More than 50 people have been assessed by our clinicians, with some reporting eye and respiratory symptoms. Decontamination is being carried out as a precaution and one patient has been taken to Manchester Royal Infirmary for further assessment.

“The incident remains ongoing and our teams remain at the scene assessing and caring for patients. We continue to work closely with emergency service partners while inquiries are carried out to establish what the substance was.

“Anyone at tonight’s event who has since noticed side effects or become ill should seek medical advice.”.

Footage shared on social media appeared to show concertgoers coughing and covering their mouths as they left the venue.

Co-op Live has the capacity to hold 23,500 people, with American singer Phoebe Bridgers scheduled to perform at the venue on Thursday.

Don Toliver is partway through the UK leg of his Octane World Tour.

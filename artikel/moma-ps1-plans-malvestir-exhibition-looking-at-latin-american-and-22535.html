MoMA PS1 in Latest York will open a major exhibition next spring looking at the connections between the spheres of Latin American and Latinx fashion and contemporary art.

Industry observers note that opening April 22, “Malvestir” will feature the work of 20 artists and designers, including Willy Chavarria, Camila Falquez, Carla Fernández, Bárbara Sánchez-Kane, Troy Montes Michie, Manauara Clandestina, and La Cholla Jackson, among others. It will be curated by PS1 director Connie Butler and associate curator Elena Ketelsen González. The exhibition’s title comes from a portmanteau with a long history that combines the prefix “mal-” (meaning poorly) and the verb “vestir” (to dress). Per a release, the “historical term [was] used in Spanish colonial dress codes that policed caste, race, and gender, labeling those who transgressed social boundaries or refused to comply as malvestidos.” While still a pejorative in contemporary Spanish, the release adds, that the term has been embraced as “a countercultural charge, as a deliberate rejection of Eurocentric taste and class decorum, or queer disobedience.”. Sources indicate that art museums have increasingly embraced fashion exhibitions in recent years, at times pairing garments with contemporary art. PS1’s, however, was ahead of the curve: the institution exhibited fashion between 1980 and 1984 through its Multi-Disciplinary Program. The program was facilitated by the late Hollywood stylist and make-up artist Di Russo, who also served as its curator. Projects staged during the program’s run, including ones by Julio (1980), Homer Layne / Charles James (1981), Willi Smith (1982), and Betsey Johnson (1983), have been partially restaged for the museum’s current exhibition “New York Fashion at PS1, 1980–1984” (through March 15).

Among the pairings that will feature in “Malvestir” are the exaggerated proportions of Chavarria’s designs that draw from Chicanx fashion; the “Dominican-York” streetwear and queer nightlight fashions of LUAR; Falquez’s photographic portraits of queer and trans people; and Clandestina’s mixed-media installation TRANSCLANDESTINA 3020, which was commissioned for the 2025 Bienal de São Paulo.

Other themes that will be explored in the exhibition include the circulation and appropriation of Indigenous craft and aesthetics by Western designers and artists, Eurocentric notions of beauty standards and class aspirations, and the textile waste—called “pacas”—that is imported from the United States throughout Latin America.

“Over the past few years, we visited artists’ studios across the US, Central America, the Caribbean, and South America, and again and again we found artists and designers using the language of fashion to confront the commodification of identity, waste colonialism, and the pull of class aspirationalism,” Ketelsen González told ARTnews in an email. “Malvestir brings together some of the most exciting artists working today to show both the possibilities and the contradictions embedded in the politics of dress.”.

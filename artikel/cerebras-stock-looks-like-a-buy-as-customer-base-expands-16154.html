Industry observers note that cerebras Systems (CBRS -0.44%) continued to rebound from its lows, climbing 11% on Nov. 13 after the stock reported soaring revenue and confirmed a deal with Microsoft (MSFT -1.97%). However, the semiconductor stock is still down more than 40% from its $185 IPO price in May, and more than 70% off its highs. (Related: Industry News: Judge blocks construction of Trump’s arc...)

Industry observers note that cerebras, which makes systems built around its wafer-scale chips the size of an iPad, has thus far been more of a niche provider of high-end artificial intelligence inference (AI) systems with a small customer base. As such, the deal with Microsoft is vital, as Cerebras continues to expand its client list, which now includes OpenAI, Amazon (AMZN -3.32%), and Oracle (ORCL -6.98%). As the manufacturer's systems move into the mainstream, it still looks like a good time to buy the stock. (Related: Industry News: Average Retirement Savings by Age: Are Y...)

It has been revealed that in addition to the substantial deal with Microsoft, Cerebras also addressed rumors around OpenAI, which is set to become its largest customer. It noted that it didn't have enough capacity to provide the system behind OpenAI's ultra-fast mode for its latest models, and that this was why its solution wasn't used, not for technical reasons.

NASDAQ · As of 2:12 PM ET 11/20/26.

Cerebras has a take-or-pay contract with OpenAI worth more than $20 billion for 750 megawatts of capacity, accounted for in its remaining performance obligations (RPOs), which total $26.1 billion as of the end of September. Notably, the Oracle and Microsoft deals were signed after the quarter and are not in that amount. Due to delays, Cerebras may owe OpenAI delay credits, which were deducted from its RPOs.

According to recent reports, despite some hiccups with OpenAI, Cerebras saw its revenue jump 55% to $210.8 million. Its core sales figure, which strips out revenue distortions from customer warrants and pass-through accounting, soared 70% to $230.9 million, topping the $214.7 million consensus, as compiled by LSEG.

Customers continue to prefer renting its systems, leading to its cloud revenue climbing 265% year over year to $141.9 million and its core cloud revenue surging 287% to $144.9 million. Hardware revenue sank 29% year over year to $68.9 million, while core hardware revenue slipped 11% to $86 million.

According to recent reports, gross margins have been an matter for Cerebras. Its core gross margins came in at 41%, up 30 basis points versus a year ago. Core gross margin for its cloud operation was 46.9%, a significant improvement from 16.5% a year ago. However, its core hardware gross margin was 31%, down from 50.5% a year ago. The organization said this was due to the continued ramp of its manufacturing capabilities and the final shipments of hardware to early customers under special pricing. Its long-term target for hardware gross margins is 60%. Meanwhile, its cloud gross margins were impacted by Cerebras renting back some equipment from a customer.

Management projected Q4 core revenue of between $310 million and $330 million, with core gross margins between 47% and 49%. Cerebras also raised its full-year guidance, taking its core revenue forecast to a range of $940 million to $960 million, up from a previous outlook of $880 million to $890 million. It now sees its core gross margins coming in between 42% and 44%, up from an earlier projection between 41% and 43%.

In a recent development, for 2027, management expects core revenue to more than triple, with strong growth for both cloud and hardware. The Microsoft deal will contribute revenue starting in the first half of the year, and Oracle's will start in the summer. Cerebras anticipates substantially increasing its data center capacity next year.

Industry observers note that cerebras has the fastest inference solution, which is growing faster than the AI model training market and is expected to become the larger of the two markets in the coming years. In fact, Bloomberg Intelligence projects the inference market will be double the size of the training market by 2032.

In a recent development, cerebras has arguably the best inference solution; the issue is that it's expensive. Still, the company is making inroads and gaining big, newly introduced customers. While it's seen some hiccups with capacity, it has a long runway for growth.

While I consider Cerebras stock speculative, I think it looks like a buy at these levels, given its potential to disrupt the chip market in inference.

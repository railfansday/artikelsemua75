Although one ABC legal drama from the 2010s featured some of the wildest plot twists in TV history, the decline of this genre in the years that followed highlights a sad truth about TV’s future. As far back as 1957’s 12 Angry Men, 1959’s Anatomy of a Murder, and 1962’s To Kill a Mockingbird, the legal thriller has been a staple of cinema. It was not long before TV caught up with this trend.

Perry Mason and Matlock were both early pioneers of the small-screen legal drama format, with Ally McBeal’s success reigniting interest in the genre in the ‘90s. As popular as these highlights were, it wasn’t until Grey’s Anatomy’s iconic producer Shonda Rhimes took to the genre in 2014 that the legal drama’s potential for shocking plot twists truly reached its full potential.

Industry observers note that the same year that Rhimes’s still underrated political drama Scandal started its seven-season run, the producer helped green light creator Peter Nowalk’s mystery/legal drama genre blender. Running for 90 episodes across six seasons on ABC, How To Get Away with Murder followed Viola Davis’s law professor Annalise Keating. Also working as a practicing defense attorney, Annalise found herself caught up in the investigations of not one, but two related murders in season 1 of the series. What followed was a soapy, campy thrill ride that took the courtroom dramatics of reveals like The Lincoln Lawyer and fused it with the twisty, explosive plotting of Scandal. While How To Get Away with Murder’s competitors, such as The Good Wife and Suits, opted to focus mainly on the personal lives of their lawyer protagonists and the cases they took on separately, the ABC show blended these together.

Industry observers note that how To Get Away with Murder’s first audacious move was the decision to plant Annalise herself in the center of a murder investigation, then add another murder into the mix in short order. This set a high bar for the ABC series, as How To Get Away with Murder needed to provide steadily wider plot twists to keep viewers invested after this shocking start. Fortunately, the series was up to this task. (Related: Industry News: Using & Mixing Hugging Face Models with ...)

It is no exaggeration to say that How To Get Away with Murder perfected the art of the plot twist, starting out with a tone that was already somewhat larger than life and growing steadily sillier as the show continued. This might sound like a condemnation, but the delicate tonal balance required to gradually shift a show’s vibe is surprisingly tough to get right.

A lot of crime dramas and legal thrillers, like Netflix’s chilling Adolescence or the tragic true drama dramatization When They See Us, start serious and stay that way. Some, like Ryan Murphy’s instantly infamous All’s Fair, aim for an arch, playful tone, but end up feeling like they have no stakes thanks to their absurdity. Within this framework, the artful nature of How To Get Away with Murder’s twists becomes more apparent.

How To Get Away with Murder pulled off an impressive sleight of hand by starting out only slightly silly and over-the-top, as this left the show feeling glamorous but grounded. This ensured that How To Get Away with Murder’s increasingly shocking and silly plot twists never felt like a massive betrayal of the show’s original tone, unlike the many shows that start out much more realistic and end up far sillier.

It has been revealed that since teen indicates like Riverdale and the seven-season ABC mystery Pretty Little Liars began as believable small-town stories, their later seasons felt cartoonishly bizarre and detached from reality. However, How To Get Away with Murder’s earliest episodes were already just about heightened enough to justify the show’s later excesses, making even its wildest twists feel grounded within the show’s reality.

Fantasy Football Week 12 Panic Meter: Emeka Egbuka isn't in a slump anymore — this is who he is

We're past the point in the fantasy football season where we can afford to be patient. It's playoff crunch time, and for a lot of fantasy managers, every lineup decision over the next few weeks could be the difference between making the postseason and watching from the sideline.

Recent findings suggest that there's no time to sit around waiting for players to figure it out. We need production, and we need it now.

Let's get into the Week 12 Panic Meter.

According to recent reports, I know the noise around Kenneth Walker III is loud right now. Rookie Emmett Johnson has been sensational in the games Walker has missed, and fantasy managers are wondering if we're witnessing a changing of the guard in Kansas City.

Recent findings suggest that the word out of Kansas City on Monday, via Eric Bieniemy, is that Walker remains the starter when he's available. When K9 is on the field, K9 is the lead ball-carrier in this offense.

In a recent development, that "when he's available" part matters, though. Walker was listed as a non-participant on Monday's estimated practice report with a groin injury. Keep an eye on his status heading into Week 12 on Thursday night against the Bills.

Here's what I will say: Johnson has earned a meaningful role in this offense. Think of him as a souped-up, "Super Saiyan" version of Jerick McKinnon. He deserves to be on the field, and his involvement shouldn't disappear when Walker returns. I can absolutely see Johnson settling into an 8-to-12-touch-per-game role, which could take something off Walker's ceiling. (Related: Latest Update: Dividends Aren't What You Think The...)

In a recent development, but the way this Chiefs offense is humming right now, both on the ground and through the air, there's room for both backs to be productive. When Walker gets healthy, this has a chance to be one of the best backfields in the NFL, if not THE best.

If Walker is healthy and active, he's in your lineup. Don't let Johnson's emergence talk you out of starting K9. Also, don't let Walker being active deter you from flexing Johnson.

Panic level: No Panic (for me)/Total Panic (if you're still a believer).

It has been revealed that emeka Egbuka has now gone 16 consecutive appearances without reaching 65 receiving yards, dating back to last season. He's seen five targets in each of his last two games, totaling 56 receiving yards. Meanwhile, rookie Ted Hurst III just found the end zone twice for Tampa Bay. (Related: How to Budget for a Wedding Without Going Broke...)

This is a far cry from the receiver who lit up the first half of his rookie season.

In a recent development, so why am I not panicking? Because I've already adjusted my expectations. Egbuka isn't a WR1 or even a high-end WR2 at this point. He's a WR3 with the potential to deliver WR2 or WR1 production in any given week. If that's how you're valuing him, nothing from Week 11 should shock you.

No panic doesn't mean he's a must-start. He's a WR3 or flex option, and there are going to be weeks when you have better options on your roster.

Now, if you're still holding onto the version of Egbuka we saw during the first half of his rookie year, you should be in total panic territory.

Recent findings suggest that sixteen appearances isn't a slump anymore. That's who he's been.

Panic level: Some Panic (bordering on Total).

Trey McBride probably belongs in total panic territory, but the sheer volume of opportunities he's getting makes it difficult to put him there.

According to recent reports, the Cardinals are down to their fourth-string quarterback in journeyman Josh Johnson. There was legitimate concern about what that would look like against Kansas City on Sunday, but McBride still managed to draw a season-high 14 targets, turning them into nine receptions for 54 yards.

Sources indicate that fourteen targets for 54 yards. That's where the concern lies.

There isn't much happening downfield for McBride in this version of the Arizona offense. It's to get the ball in his hands as quickly as possible and hope he can make something happen after the catch. Touchdown opportunities are going to be difficult to come by, and the Cardinals still haven't settled on who will start at quarterback moving forward.

The volume is enough to keep McBride in your lineup, but expecting him to provide the type of positional advantage we've become accustomed to is probably unrealistic right now.

It has been revealed that close to total panic? Absolutely. Am I recommending that you bench Trey McBride in a 1-TE league? No.

It wouldn't be a Panic Meter without a Latest York Jets player, and unfortunately, we have to talk about Garrett Wilson once again.

It has been revealed that at the start of the year, I thought this would be the season Wilson finally got north of 15 half-PPR points per game. Instead, we're looking at another season in that 13-14-point-per-game range.

In a recent development, this isn't a talent issue. We may have seen the best version of the Jets offense on Sunday, and that's exactly the problem for Wilson's fantasy outlook.

Breece Hall and Braelon Allen combined for 36 carries and 161 rushing yards. Geno Smith was incredibly efficient when asked to throw the football, completing 17-of-21 passes with three touchdowns and no interceptions. Wilson played 90% of the offensive snaps and was still the team's leading receiver.

Wilson's involvement isn't the issue. There just aren't enough passing opportunities when the Jets are leaning on what I consider one of the best 1-2 punches at running back in the NFL.

If the Jets can run the football effectively, limit what they're asking Geno to do and capitalize on his passing opportunities, why would they change that?

It works for the Jets. It caps Wilson.

He's still an every-week fantasy starter for most managers, but he's a WR2 with upside rather than the high-end WR1 many of us hoped he would become this season.

Industry observers note that after sleepwalking through much of the season, the Indianapolis Colts finally did what a lot of fantasy managers wanted to do weeks ago. They'd had enough of Daniel Jones, released him and handed the keys to Anthony Richardson Sr.

Does that make this offense better? Probably not.

Richardson hasn't proven that he's the answer to the Colts' passing game, and he's stepping into an offense that's already without two notable pieces.

No Josh Downs. No Quenton Nelson. No Daniel Jones. No problem, right?

In a recent development, where things could improve is in the running game. Richardson's ability to keep the football on read-option plays gives opposing defenses something else to account for and could open up rushing lanes for Jonathan Taylor. Of course, that same rushing ability could also cost Taylor carries and touchdown opportunities around the goal line.

Taylor and Tyler Warren aren't necessarily in the same situation, though.

Recent findings suggest that taylor's workload had been declining as rookie Seth McGowan earned more opportunities. McGowan's hamstring injury could push some of that volume back in Taylor's direction, although we don't have a clear timeline for McGowan's return.

Industry observers note that JT has been borderline unplayable lately and continues to slide down my weekly rankings. There is at least a realistic path for him to get back on track if the volume returns and Richardson's presence helps the rushing attack.

It's much more difficult to see that path for Warren. In four games since returning from injury, Warren has caught 11 of 25 targets for 85 yards.

Maybe Richardson has been sitting on the bench all this time thinking, "When I get my opportunity, I've got to get the ball to Tyler Warren.".

That's blind hope, faith and optimism at this point.

Warren is going to be outside my top-15 tight ends for Week 12. If there's a dependable option on your bench or somebody available on the waiver wire, make that move.

A quarterback change doesn't automatically fix a bad offense. Taylor has a path back to fantasy relevance through volume, but neither player deserves automatic consideration in your starting lineup right now.

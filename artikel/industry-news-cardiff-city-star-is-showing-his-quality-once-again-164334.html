According to recent reports, cardiff City have been one of the Championship’s most exciting teams to watch this season, despite results not going their way. Brian Barry-Murphy’s side have been very unlucky at times, and have some exceptional talents amongst their squad, including Jack Moylan who is earning a lot of the plaudits.

However, Rubin Colwill is beginning to show why he could become such an important player for Cardiff City, with his recent performances for club and country suggesting he is hitting form at exactly the right time.

The 24-year-old enjoyed an impressive international break with Wales, providing an assist in their 3-2 defeat to Portugal on 17 November. Making his first start under Craig Bellamy, Colwill helped set up David Brooks’ equaliser and received a standing ovation when substituted after 83 minutes.

That performance followed an encouraging cameo against Norway, but perhaps the biggest positive is that Colwill has carried his international momentum back into Championship football. Despite Cardiff losing 2-1 at Norwich City this weekend, the attacking midfielder was arguably their standout performer.

His underlying numbers suggest these performances are not simply isolated flashes of quality. According to FotMob, Colwill has registered three assists in nine Championship appearances this season, creating 12 chances and four big chances in 557 minutes.

His chance creation ranks in the 96th percentile among attacking midfielders and wingers, an impressive return that highlights just how much he can offer Cardiff in the final third. He also ranks in the 85th percentile for touches and 79th for shot attempts, demonstrating his willingness to get involved and influence attacking situations.

His two assists against Burnley in Cardiff’s thrilling 5-4 victory on 7 November further demonstrated his creative potential. The Wales boss highlighted a significant change in his habits, including his work rate, positional discipline and understanding of when to drop deeper. Those improvements could help Colwill become a more complete attacking midfielder. His eight goals and six assists during Cardiff’s 2025/26 League One promotion campaign already demonstrated his attacking ability, but his current performances suggest there is another level to reach.

Sources indicate that cardiff still need to improve their away form, having failed to win on the road in the Championship this season. However, Colwill is providing genuine reasons for optimism.

With Preston North End visiting on 24 November, he has an immediate opportunity to turn individual momentum into another positive result for his club.

The Senate advanced the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026 (BAAJA) on a major bipartisan vote Thursday night. At the 30,000-foot level, the sweeping package would accelerate environmental review of projects, limit legal challenges, protect approved projects, expand the electric grid and streamline energy development in all fields — like oil, gas and renewables.

"This is a major step toward making it faster and more efficient and easier to build infrastructure projects, certainly without giving up any of the strong protections for our environment," Senate Environment and Public Works Committee Chair Shelley Moore Capito, R-W.Va., mentioned when the bill was introduced in September.

The bill also tackles an issue that was front and center for voters across the country: data centers.

It has been revealed that sen. Martin Heinrich, D-N.M., the Democratic lead on the legislation, pushed for stronger language in the bill that would shield ratepayers from higher costs that stem from data center development and their usage of electricity.

That comes after Republicans tried to ram through similar safeguards before the election with a bill from Sen. Jon Husted, R-Ohio, that Democrats argued lacked real enforcement teeth.

In a recent development, "This process will mean that we develop responsibly. It will mean more jobs. It will mean cheaper energy," Heinrich said. "It will mean that we actually require big load centers, whether that's a factory or a data center, to not pass those costs on to the American consumer by statute, not a suggestion, an actual law.".

The provision covers data centers with a demand of at least 20 megawatts of power and would require them to cover the cost of additional power for them to run, financial guarantees covering costs to build or upgrade facilities, to pay the tab if they leave an area for unpaid infrastructure costs, allow states to charge higher rates to reduce other customers’ bills and tack on power supply conditions.

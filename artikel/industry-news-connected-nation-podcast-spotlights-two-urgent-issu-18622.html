Industry observers note that ep. 57: The program that keeps schools and libraries online is in danger.

In a recent development, the federal E-Rate helps connect more than 100,000 K-12 schools and libraries across the U.S. Now its future is uncertain.

Industry observers note that joseph Wender, Executive Director of the Schools, Health & Libraries Broadband (SHLB) Coalition, explained the Fifth Circuit ruling that puts the $8 billion-a-year program at risk. The court found that USAC, which runs the Universal Service Fund, was improperly set up.

Wender also covered the FCC's proposal asking whether E-Rate should end, and what schools and libraries are telling him.

"They're saying E-Rate cannot go away. It just cannot," Wender said of the schools and libraries SHLB represents. "That if it goes away, it will blow a hole in the budgets of schools and libraries.".

Listeners who want to help protect the program can visit saveourerate.com.

According to recent reports, ep. 58: We asked Google about data centers, and a Google exec answered.

Data centers are evolving almost faster than the rulebook can be written. Google is helping write it.

According to recent reports, gino Tozzi, who leads data center quality and commissioning at Google, explained DCE 9000, the first quality standard for data center infrastructure. Google and the Telecommunications Industry Association (TIA) are building it with more than 90 companies, and it's expected in early 2027 (Q1).

Tozzi also explained why some suppliers face as many as 50 audits a year, and what really goes into building a data center.

It has been revealed that "We need to go at hyper speed because we need it fast," Tozzi stated of developing the standard. "We couldn't afford to wait three or five years.".

Recent findings suggest that as Jensen Huang returns to his post as Nvidia’s CEO with support from the Trump Administration, the Department of Commerce has added Megaspeed to its Entity List, which blocks trade with the company, citing national security risks. As reported earlier by Semafor, Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick had faced increasing pressure to list the company after news reports revealed Huang’s affair with Alice Huang, a former Megaspeed executive, and that US officials suspected her of being a Chinese intelligence asset. The Commerce Department stated in a filing that it’s adding Megaspeed and its affiliates in Malaysia and Thailand to the list “because of the significant risk of these entities being or becoming involved in the diversion of advanced computing commodities to unauthorized end uses or end people.” It has been investigating Megaspeed since 2024 for allegedly smuggling advanced AI chips to China, where companies are banned from buying them directly. The Upcoming York Times mentions how a Commerce Department official’s visit to a Megaspeed datacenter in Malaysia found servers packed in boxes instead of being used, adding to the smuggling suspicions.

The Times also notes that the Commerce Department will now be responsible for enforcing trade restrictions on Megaspeed, after facing accusations that Lutnick was too close to Huang and Nvidia, and failed to adequately police its chip sales to China.

The DOJ mentioned last week that it has taken over the Commerce Department’s investigation, but it’s still “ongoing.” The department also recently added a rule aimed at blocking a loophole that could allow Chinese firms to access banned AI chips remotely. According to the DOJ, Nvidia added Megaspeed to its no-sell list last year, after the Commerce Department requested Nvidia’s records on its “direct or indirect” sales to the company.

Oliver made decisions that both sets of fans won't be happy about, but the travelling supporters at Stamford Bridge may have been the most aggrieved.

Emmanuel Emegha opened the scoring for the hosts in the 25th minute before Noah Okafor equalised soon after.

Dominic Calvert-Lewin and Brenden Aaronson also got on the scoresheet in the West Yorkshire outfit's 4-1 victory, and here Football Insider look at some of Oliver's contentious decisions from the game.

In the sixth minute of the game, Ao Tanaka went in with a crunching challenge on Romeo Lavia in Chelsea's defensive third.

Oliver awarded the foul in the Blues' favour, but he could've been harsher with his punishment.

The Japan international went high and strong, with his knee making direct contact with Lavia's hamstring in the follow-through.

While it was early in the game, Tanaka was arguably lucky not to have gone in the book for it.

Estevao and Gabriel Gudmundsson had a coming together in the 20th minute, with the left-wing-back going down.

The Sweden international ran for the byline and found himself in a threatening position before a clear arm on his back from the Chelsea winger brought him down.

Gudmundsson was the first player to go into the book in the latter stages of the first half, but there may have been a clear case to penalise Estevao.

In the end, Oliver didn't give a foul in Leeds' favour at the edge of Chelsea's box, and VAR couldn't have intervened because contact started before the defender entered the penalty area.

Emegha scored the opening goal at Stamford Bridge, but there's an argument it shouldn't have stood.

A long ball towards Morgan Rogers was initially headed down by James Justin, and it subsequently hit the Chelsea forward's arm.

The England international then played the Dutch striker in behind and he made no mistake beating James Trafford at the near post.

Justin's header hit Rogers' arm from close range, but the Chelsea attacker gained an advantage before immediately providing the assist.

Oliver or VAR should've flagged it and ruled the goal out and Daniel Farke will certainly have questions about it.

Industry observers note that so what's actually happening? The truth is, Liverpool's system isn't setup to benefit wingers looking to cut inside. They play with a very static striker in Alexander Isak who remains in the box more often than not, and a no.10 in Florian Wirtz who roams all over the pitch.

Factor in that teams play with a low-block against the Reds more often than not and suddenly there isn't really space to be effective cutting inside. Opposition teams are manufacturer defensively, trying to cut off space there anyway. Isak and Wirtz then take away further space, forcing the wingers to stay very wide.

According to recent reports, it means the space is on the flanks and pacey wingers take advantage of that space by going on the outside. Right-footed players struggle to do that effectively on the left but on the right? They can shine. Getting beyond their full-back, driving to the byline, forcing centre-backs to step out and stretching defences.

Sources indicate that it works very well, even if it definitely wasn't the blueprint. Now Iraola has to make a massive decision about it, too. If this plan is working, should he double down on it? Go the traditional winger route and feed a striker in the box?

Or is it a sign that he needs to re-think his tactics to get the best out of these players for what they're typically known for? We're not sure there's a right answer, honestly.

In a recent development, what matters is that Iraola moves forward with a plan effectively - no middle ground. He's got to pick a direction and he's got to do it soon.

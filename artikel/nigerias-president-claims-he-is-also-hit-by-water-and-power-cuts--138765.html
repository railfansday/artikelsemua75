Nigeria's President Bola Tinubu says the presidential villa suffers from cuts in electricity and water - issues that are all too common for the country's ordinary citizens.

He said he was shaving last week when the water supply cut at the villa, known as Aso Rock, in Abuja.

He is seeking re-election next January and vowed to address these issues.

Tinubu made the disclosure on Sunday at a special prayer session organised in his honour by members of his family in Lagos.

Nigeria has long endured electricity shortages, but recent severe disruptions have been caused primarily by insufficient gas supplies and declining hydropower output, rather than the usual nationwide grid collapse.

Persistent blackouts in Lagos, Abuja and elsewhere have forced distribution companies to implement rotating power cuts, severely impacting households and businesses.

The country also suffers from chronic water shortages, the result of a structural crisis rooted in the gap between rapid urban population growth and decaying, inadequate public water infrastructure, leaving millions without reliable supplies, especially in Lagos.

Tinubu made a high-profile electricity promise during the 2023 election campaign, but the exact wording and how binding it is has become a major point of contention ahead of the election on 16 January 2027.

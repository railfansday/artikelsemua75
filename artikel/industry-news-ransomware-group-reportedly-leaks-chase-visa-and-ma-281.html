Industry News: Ransomware group reportedly leaks Chase, Visa, and Mastercard customer data, but not credit card numbers

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that after a string of tech outages and hacks, many Americans are on alert. And on Thursday, the Wall Street Journal reported that a ransomware group had leaked the data of thousands of customers at JPMorgan Chase, Mastercard, and Visa.

Crucially, all of the companies have stated that no credit card numbers or customer funds were compromised in the cyberattack. The companies' financial services are all operating normally, and JPMorgan Chase released a statement on X confirming the hack is unrelated to a Chase Bank outage earlier this week.

The leak contained some customers' names and email addresses, according to JPMorgan Chase. (Related: How we sped up transformer inference 100x for 🤗 API cus...)

For its part, a Mastercard representative told Mashable that the incident "resulted in the exposure of a limited amount of email addresses — non-payments information — from a small subset of cardholders enrolled in a specific benefit program." Customers can continue to use their cards as normal. The spokesperson added, "At this time, the exposure has been contained and our teams are working with the provider to support the impacted cardholders as needed." A Visa spokesperson told Mashable that the firm is "aware of these claims," and that "Visa’s systems were not involved and were not impacted.".

SOCRadar, a cybersecurity and threat analysis tech giant, reported that the Russian ransomware group n0n claimed responsibility for the hack. Not only that, but SOCRadar found that n0n has also claimed to have compromised data at major organizations including Airbnb, Starbucks, EssilorLuxottica, Procter & Gamble, Walmart, and AARP — all in the past three days.

However, just because a hacking group claims responsibility for an attack, that doesn't mean that an attack occurred.

In its statement, JPMorganChase stated, "Where appropriate, we will contact affected customers directly and offer support, including complimentary credit monitoring.".

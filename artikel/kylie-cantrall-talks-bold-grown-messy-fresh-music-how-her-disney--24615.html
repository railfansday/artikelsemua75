Kylie Cantrall Talks ‘Bold, Grown & Messy’ Fresh Music, How Her Disney Fans ‘Coexist’ With Growing Audience

Sources indicate that nestled into a corner of the Moxy Hotel in downtown L.A. this week, Kylie Cantrall sat down with Billboard to chat about her sold-out headlining solo tour, her songwriting process and her newly introduced era, post-Disney’s Descendants franchise.

Growing up in the nearby Valley, Cantrall starred in her breakout role as Red in Disney’s Descendants in 2024, and is now the release of her Valley Girl Problems EP on July 24 marks a upcoming chapter for the Venezuelan-American artist.

Sources indicate that with her Valley Girl Problems Tour wrapping up last month and anticipation building for her upcoming Jingle Ball LA performance in December, Cantrall surprised fans with an intimate acoustic performance at the Moxy. Reaching out directly to her fanbase through a text message, she offered a more personal, stripped-down evening and a chance to connect in a smaller setting. In a recent development, stepping out of her Descendants era, the former Disney star has found a strong connection with listeners through her solo music, which explores themes of teenage heartbreak, friendship breakups and the growing pains of finding yourself. “Valley Girl Problems was what I was going through at that time. Discovering myself and opening up about the trials and tribulations, and the messier parts,” she says. (Related: 3 Ways To Make Music More Powerful Against Pain (M)...)

Industry observers note that cantrall has described the nine-track project as a reintroduction to herself as an artist, leaning into the very Valley Girl stereotype that has become part of her identity. After landing her Disney breakout role at the age of 17, Los Angeles became an essential part of her journey into the entertainment industry. Rather than shy away from her roots — Valley Girl vocal fry and all — she’s embracing the persona as a reflection of who she is.

That sense of nostalgia also carries into the EP’s sound, with early 2000s pop influences woven throughout the project. It’s no surprise, then, that Cantrall brought in hitmaker Tricky Stewart, best known for co-writing Grammy-winning hits like Rihanna’s “Umbrella” and Beyoncé’s “Single Ladies (Put a Ring on It).”.

“Tricky made me feel like I had all the answers,” Cantrall indicates. “He told me to trust my intuition and empowered me to not second guess myself as an artist.”.

Despite feeling nervous about entering the writing room and collaborating with such an established name in the industry, Cantrall said Stewart helped bring her ideas to the surface while building her confidence as a songwriter. Reflecting on their time in the studio, she recalled how encouraging Stewart was throughout the creative process, helping her feel more comfortable opening up and finding her voice.

Among her most popular songs she collaborated with Stewart on were fan favorites “Closet,” “Carrie Bradshaw” and “Space Between.” Reflecting on “Space Between,” one of her most-streamed songs, Cantrall calls it the most vulnerable track she’s put out.

Sources indicate that fans have speculated about what inspired it and who it’s about, but she isn’t afraid to share personal experiences — she believes that honesty is what creates a connection with her listeners. She knows fans will dissect her direct lyrics, and she’s comfortable with that. “It’s the only ballad that I have, so people got to see a different side of me,” she says.

“I love writing music all the time,” Cantrall indicates. “I have so many songs. Once I’m so far into the process I then go … ‘Oh … this is now turning into a body of work.’”.

When deciding which tracks belong on her album, she takes a thoughtful approach: “If one [song] doesn’t fit for a specific body of work, I’ll save it and come back to it, maybe for a future record.”.

That may be the case with “Filthy,” an unreleased song that was partially leaked and that fans have been eager to hear. “I knew it was going to be perfect for the album that comes after Valley Girl Problems,” she mentioned. Saying that the song will eventually see the light of day, she added that she has plenty of other unreleased tracks too.

Sources indicate that fresh off the Valley Girl Problems Tour, Cantrall reflected on how much she enjoyed seeing fans of all ages come together. Some younger fans attended her tour dressed as Red, her character in Descendants, while older fans in their late teens and 20s danced and sang along to her non-Disney music. “It’s funny to see them coexist on tour,” she noted. “The littles know me as Red,” while longtime fans have followed her since her first songs on YouTube. “I’m very grateful for all of them.”.

In a recent development, that mix of audiences has made the transition from touring arenas with her Descendants castmates for Disney’s Worlds Collide Tour to performing solo in theaters especially meaningful. “It’s night and day. It’s always been my dream for fans to sing my own lyrics back to me,” she said.

Though she loved touring with the Desendants cast — including a sold-out show at Madison Square Garden — Cantrall realized she wanted more creative ownership.

Industry observers note that “It wasn’t as fulfilling for me to sing other people’s songs and not have a part in other aspects,” she says. “It’s so different playing a character and not having that creative input in all aspects.”.

As her solo career gains momentum, Cantrall hopes to play increasingly larger venues — and one day sell out Madison Square Garden on her own. Still, she sees something special in smaller highlights, especially as she focuses on her solo music. Looking ahead to her exclusive acoustic set at the Moxy DTLA, she noted, “There’s something so electric about all of us crammed in a small space.”.

In a recent development, at her fan event on Tuesday, Cantrall performed acoustic versions of songs from Valley Girl Problems to about 100 fans, with Valley Girl-themed drink names on the cocktail list. Taking photos and chatting with fans after her set, she recognized some OG fans in the audience from her smaller highlights in the past, signed vinyls and took group photos.

While she told Billboard that she’s happy to be on a break from touring and excited to relax and hang with friends in her downtime, she’s still writing fresh music. Offering a glimpse of what’s next as she leaps into her new chapter in music, she shares that each project for her is a time capsule and cannot wait to share it with fans.

“I am 21, and I feel like I’m constantly evolving,” she says. “This next album that I’m working on is fun, and I think it reflects who I am now. I’m healed, we’re back outside — she’s a little more grown.”.

Sources indicate that as she prepares to release her second album, Cantrall is still embracing the honesty that defined her last project. When asked how she decides what to share as she grows older, she laughed: “If you listen to Valley Girl Problems, you know that I have no problem airing it all out. That’s the fun thing about the music that I’m writing.”.

She describes the newly introduced project as “bold, grown and messy” and is excited to usher in this next era.

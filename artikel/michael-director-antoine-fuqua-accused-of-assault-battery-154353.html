Antoine Fuqua, the director of the 2026 box office hit Michael, has been accused of battery and assault.

In a recent development, having earned $1.028 billion worldwide, the Michael Jackson biopic is the fourth highest-grossing movie of 2026, only behind Spider-Man: Brand New Day ($2.523 billion), The Odyssey ($1.805 billion), Toy Story 5 ($1.149 billion), and ahead of The Super Mario Galaxy Movie ($1.012 billion). Michael broke numerous box office records as well, including becoming the first biopic to ever reach $1 billion globally. (Related: Grandma who accidentally invited a stranger to Thanksgi...)

In a recent development, per Variety, in a 10-page civil claim filed in the Los Angeles Superior Court on November 20, Fuqua has now been accused of assault, battery, and intentional infliction of emotional distress. He allegedly pulled a handgun on a Los Angeles bar manager and struck him in the head last February. (Related: Build A Racetrack codes (November 2026)...)

According to the civil claim, the assistant manager of the bar Good Times at Davey Wayne's, William Rosenthal, denied entry to Fuqua, his son, Brando, and his daughter, due to them being "visibly overly intoxicated." After Rosenthal was spat on by a member of the group, he told them they needed to leave the bar.

Rosenthal alleges that an hour after this occurred, Fuqua and those in his group were still standing in the line outside the bar. He was then approached by Fuqua, who accused Rosenthal of pepper-spraying his daughter. After the bar manager denied this accusation, the director allegedly "pulled out a handgun," "struck" him in the face, and uttered "a racial and anti-Semetic slur," along with "threatening to kill him.".

The complaint goes on to state that "as Plaintiff staggered back in pain, Defendant Brando Fuqua appeared and approached Plaintiff and forcefully swung a handgun at Plaintiff’s face. Defendants Antoine Fuqua and Brandon Fuqua then pointed their handguns at Plaintiff, placing him in imminent fear for his life, before fleeing down the street.".

As a result of the "serious physical and psychological injuries" that include a nose fracture, head injury, skin lacerations, post-traumatic stress disorder, along with severe emotional distress and severe scarring that have led to ongoing "great physical and mental pain and suffering," Rosenthal is seeking $50,000. The claim also states that the bar manager believes his injuries "will result in some permanent disability.".

Sources indicate that in terms of Fuqua's career, a Michael sequel has not been officially announced by Lionsgate yet, although the studio's Motion Picture Group Chair, Adam Fogelson, shared during an earnings call in August that "we are targeting a production start towards the end of this year or early next. I think that the end of calendar ’27 and the first half of calendar ’28 would be the current thought of roughly where the movie would go. We’re not ready to announce everything at the moment.".

Prior to this, the ending of Michael set up a sequel by leaving much of the pop star's life uncovered and concluding with the line "His Story Continues." Fuqua has also previously spoken about how he filmed parts of the movie that had to be cut, and that there is already footage that could be incorporated into a potential sequel.

In a recent development, when discussing sequel ideas for Michael with ScreenRant's Liam Crowley in April, around the time of the first movie's theatrical release, Jaafar Jackson, who plays the titular character, shared that he "absolutely" would be interested in playing the role again. The actor is the real-life nephew of Michael Jackson.

Recent findings suggest that outside the music biopic, Antoine Fuqua and Denzel Washington have collaborated on numerous movies, including Training Day, The Equalizer, 2016's The Magnificent Seven, The Equalizer 2, and The Equalizer 3. Among his other directing credits are Tears of the Sun, King Arthur, Shooter, Olympus Has Fallen, Southpaw, Emancipation, and an episode of Prime Video's The Terminal List.

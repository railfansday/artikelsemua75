Giving U.S. customers another way to trade on future events while allowing Coinbase to take more control of a market that is growing rapidly.

Industry observers note that according to a report, Coinbase plans to offer its own prediction market contracts to U.S. retail and institutional customers. The move would give the exchange more control over which markets it launches while allowing it to keep more of the trading revenue.

In a recent development, coinbase already offers prediction markets through Coinbase Financial Markets, but Kalshi currently runs these markets. Customers can trade contracts based on future events, while Kalshi decides the final results based on the rules of each contract.

Industry observers note that the partnership has already expanded into short-term crypto markets. In October, they added Bitcoin price contracts that let users trade on BTC prices over 15-minute and hourly periods.

This means the planned 2027 launch would not be Coinbase’s first move into prediction markets. Instead, it would allow the manufacturer to run more of the business itself, rather than depending on Kalshi.

The manufacturer has been preparing for that shift for more than a year. Coinbase agreed to acquire prediction market startup The Clearing Company in December 2025 and received approval from the Commodity Futures Trading Commission in September 2026 for its own clearinghouse.

That infrastructure could allow Coinbase to create and settle fully collateralized event contracts directly rather than relying entirely on an outside operator.

There is also a clear financial reason for the move.

Sources indicate that coinbase stated its prediction market business crossed a $200 million annualized revenue run rate in Q3 2026. Bringing contracts in-house could help the company earn more and rely less on ups and downs in crypto trading.

The timing is crucial because prediction markets have rapidly moved into the mainstream, particularly around elections, politics, sports, economic data, and cryptocurrency prices.

The sector has generated roughly $51 billion to $64 billion in trading volume in 2025 and could reach about $325 billion in 2026.

In August alone, Kalshi and Polymarket platforms recorded a combined $45.33 billion in monthly volume.

Meanwhile, Bernstein Research expects the industry to grow at an 80% annual rate, potentially reaching $1 trillion by 2030.

Sources indicate that that growth is attracting more competition, including Kraken’s parent tech giant, Payward, which plans to launch prediction markets for U.S. customers as it expands beyond crypto trading.

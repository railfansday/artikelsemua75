Update 22nd November: A WARN notice filed in Maryland, where Firaxis is headquartered, shows that 37 employees based at the studio's Hunt Valley office have been laid off.

As noted by Vertical Slice, it's not clear whether that figure accounts for everyone affected, as Bloomberg has previously reported that around 60 people had lost their jobs.

In a recent development, the cuts, which took place today, account for about a third of the studio’s staff, Bloomberg reports, citing people familiar with the matter. Studio head Heather Hazen is also leaving. (Related: All three northeast Indiana counties reject casino, lea...)

Its sources say Firaxis' unannounced XCOM game has faced development struggles and missed "a significant milestone over the summer". The project will continue, but "may face a shakeup as a result of the cuts".

A spokesperson for 2K, the Take-Two label that runs Firaxis, confirmed Hazen's departure to Bloomberg, and stated the studio is "restructuring and recentering around the studio's core strength - designing world class strategy games that have set the industry standard for the genre for the last 30 years". (Related: Industry News: “Determined” woman completes 7 marathons...)

According to recent reports, "As part of these changes, the size of the studio is being reduced to focus on design, technology and the player experience, which means saying goodbye to talented colleagues who helped build Firaxis," the spokesperson continued. "These decisions are never easy, but they’re essential to build the next chapter of Firaxis.".

The layoffs come two days before the launch of Grand Theft Auto 6, which is developed and published by Rockstar, a separate label at Take-Two.

It has been revealed that in September, around 18 months after Civ 7's widely criticised launch - and as the legendary 4X series celebrates its 35th anniversary - Firaxis unveiled a host of new updates coming to the game as it continued to improve it. These included an atomic age update, dragging Civ 7 into the present and beyond, as well as its first paid expansion, Earthrise, both arriving next year.

Take-Two boss Strauss Zelnick admitted earlier this year that the studio "got it wrong with Civ 7".

Industry observers note that "The game is a really good game. And it's certainly a profitable enterprise for us. But this is one where I think what we tried to do was a bridge too far, from the consumer's perspective," he told Game File in May.

It’s not the first round of cuts at the studio - 2K confirmed an unspecified number of layoffs at Firaxis in September 2025, and around 30 jobs went in 2023.

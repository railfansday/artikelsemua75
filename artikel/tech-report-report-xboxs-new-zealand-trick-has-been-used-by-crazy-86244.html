Ahead of the launch of GTA 6, there was a whole lot of talk online about the Xbox "New Zealand trick" and how people could get early access to the game by switching their regions to New Zealand on Xbox Series X and Xbox Series S.

We mentioned in various articles how it was a risky practice (against Xbox's ToCs), but one that had never traditionally resulted in any widespread bans, and it doesn't seem like either Xbox or Rockstar took any action at first glance. As it turns out, a lot of people decided to give it a go for the latest Grand Theft Auto.

This is according to the folks at Ampere Analysis (via The Game Business), who report that over 2.1 million people played GTA 6 in the US and the UK before it was officially released.

As you can see from the graph above, it seems the vast majority of those were based in the United States, followed by the UK, Canada, Brazil and Mexico. Only around 100,000 people tried the trick on PS5 in the U.S. (where it's harder to implement and requires a separate account), whereas 1.5 million of them ended up using it on Xbox. Ultimately, the existence of the New Zealand trick probably led to a bunch of increased sales for Xbox Series X and Xbox Series S, but it also poses the question of whether Xbox might be forced to put a stop to it on Project Helix and beyond. As much it's a beloved feature, it's also being used in a way that wasn't originally intended.

Let's see what happens in the future, but for now, it's clearly made a lot of people happy this week!

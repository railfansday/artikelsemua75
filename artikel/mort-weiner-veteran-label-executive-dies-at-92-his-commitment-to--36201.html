Veteran record label executive Mort Weiner died on Oct. 25 at age 92. Over a career spanning nearly four decades, Weiner held senior positions in sales, promotion, marketing and distribution at several major record labels, including Columbia, MGM, Metromedia, Motown, RCA and 20th Century-Fox Records.

Veteran music executive Neil Portnow, former president/CEO of the Recording Academy, tells Billboard, “Mort was a colleague from my first days in L.A. at RCA in the late ‘70’s, and then at 20th Century Fox Records where I hired him to run our marketing, sales and international efforts. I couldn’t have made a better choice because his work, energy and commitment to artists and our industry were superb. But most importantly, I gained a true friendship which has lasted through these many decades. He will be missed by all who had the privilege to work with and know him.”. In a recent development, weiner began his record manufacturer career in 1961 as the Miami branch manager for Dot Records, which had a No. 1 hit on the Billboard Hot 100 that year with Pat Boone’s “Moody River.” He went on to handle local promotion for Columbia Records in Miami, where he helped build early interest in Simon & Garfunkel. In 1966, he took responsibility for promotion across 11 Southern states for Columbia’s Date label. He joined MGM and was named national singles sales manager for Verve and Verve/Forecast in 1967.Weiner joined Metromedia Records around the time of its launch in late 1968 and rose from Eastern regional promotion to director of national sales and then to national director of marketing and merchandising. The label’s biggest star was Bobby Sherman, one of the top teen pop stars of the era. Sherman landed four top 10 Hot 100 hits for Metromedia from 1969-70, including “Easy Come, Easy Go” and “Julie, Do Ya Love Me.”Weiner joined Motown Records in its Los Angeles office, where he served as executive administrator of promotion. His tenure included the label’s Anthology franchise, which drew on Motown’s priceless catalog. The Anthology series began in 1973 with a Temptations collection that rode the Billboard 200 for six months and went platinum. While at Motown, Weiner was named a finalist for record company executive of the year at Bobby Poe’s Radio/Record Seminar and Awards Banquet.In 1975, Weiner moved to RCA as the sales and promotion manager for custom labels. He went on to direct custom label sales and, in 1977, became director of pop product merchandising, overseeing RCA’s pop product managers. His work with affiliated labels included Silver Convention’s “Fly, Robin, Fly” on Midland International (a Hot 100-topping, Grammy-winning smash from 1975), the soundtrack to The Omen on Tattoo Records, and releases by The Whispers and Carrie Lucas on Soul Train Records. Jefferson Starship thanked him in the credits to its 1978 album Earth, which made the top five on the Billboard 200 and went platinum.In 1979, Weiner joined 20th Century-Fox Records as director of sales and merchandising. He became vp of sales and merchandising in 1980. That same year, label president Portnow put him in charge of international operations. As part of the label’s push to expand sales abroad, Weiner met with executives at RCA, which handled the label’s international distribution. In an October 1980 Billboard interview, Portnow pointed to singer-songwriter Jim Photoglo’s success in Japan as an example of the label’s increased presence overseas.Weiner later served as vp of sales and distribution at Total Experience Records, where he helped introduce a program that used lower suggested retail prices and dealer incentives to support new artists. He returned to Motown in 1987 as vp of sales and distribution, a newly created position at the time. Smokey Robinson had back-to-back top 10 hits on the Hot 100 that year: “Just to See Her” (which won a Grammy) and “One Heartbeat.” Weiner’s later work took him into record and CD manufacturing, including a role as vp of sales at Nimbus Manufacturing.From his early days promoting singles in Miami to his work in national distribution and CD manufacturing, Weiner spent his career on the business side of getting music heard.

Recent findings suggest that in iOS 27, Apple confirmed iPhone Handoff, a networking feature that allows owners to switch cell service between two eSIM-enabled iPhones. Text strings in Google’s SIM Manager app suggest the firm is preparing a similar feature for compatible Android phones, according to Android Authority.

Code strings include “Use a SIM on two phones,” and note that the feature will be called Fast Switch. Little information is currently available about the feature’s limitations, including which phones or mobile carriers may not be compatible. Sources indicate that on Apple’s side, any iPhone in the US running iOS 27 with a T-Mobile or Verizon-provided eSIM can use iPhone Handoff. AT&T does not right now support iPhone Handoff. (Related: LA Noire 2 leak resurfaces after source gets GTA 6 endi...)

However, the code strings uncovered by Android Authority show that Android’s Fast Switch will work similarly to iPhone Handoff, in that customers must be signed in to both devices under the same Google account for the feature to work.

Strings in Google’s SIM Manager app also point to no sharing between two owners, so this feature isn’t a way to circumvent carrier fees by adding service to someone else’s phone for the price of one.

If Fast Switch works anything like iPhone Handoff, which it appears it will, one eSIM can be switched between two phones. So, calls, messages, location services, and other information synced to your Apple ID can remain consistent between two devices.

How the CMO of the world’s largest beer tech giant uses scale to build ‘megabrands’

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that at a firm the size of AB InBev—the behemoth behind Budweiser, Michelob Ultra, and Stella Artois—it never hurts to think big. But in his nearly five years running AB InBev’s marketing efforts, global CMO Marcel Marcondes has shown that to really spur growth, it’s necessary to think bigger—mega, even. Under his leadership, the company’s marketing strategy has coalesced around Marcondes’s “one ABI” philosophy. Marcondes harnesses the company’s scale—$59.3 billion in 2025 revenue—and identifies 50 brands from its 500-strong catalog as “megabrands” that garner a large share of marketing spend. These are typically AB InBev’s biggest global labels but also include local giants like Korea’s Cass or Brazil’s Brahma. His team then zeroes in on global “mega platforms” such as the Olympics and the World Cup. Michelob Ultra became the official beer sponsor of the World Cup, Marcondes says, because it “represents an active lifestyle, with low carbs and calories. People choose it when they want to combine being fit with having fun.” When he was promoted to his role in 2022 by CEO Michel Doukeris, Marcondes says his mission was to support Doukeris’s vision of refocusing the company from acquisition-driven growth to organic growth built around established brands. “We shifted from asking ourselves, ‘How can we outperform other brewers?’ to ‘How can we be one of the leading CPG companies in the world in how we work?’ ” he says. That’s when he developed the “one ABI” and “megabrands” approach. To put it in action this summer, AB InBev blanketed global airwaves with ads in multiple languages and brought Michelob Ultra “pitchside lounges” to the Santa Monica Pier and New York’s Standard Biergarten, where fans could purchase personalized jerseys, enjoy DJ sets, and watch matches. Fans rewarded the brand with sales. In Q2 of 2026, AB InBev noted that Michelob Ultra was its beer with the highest increase in volume share that quarter (21%), with 40% of that volume growth coming from outside the U.S. Now, as Marcondes markets megabrands from the company’s Beyond Beer portfolio, such as Cutwater canned cocktails, he’s also nurturing lower-cal and low- or nonalcoholic options that make up 11% of the company’s business. “They’re designed to be the next megabrands,” he says.

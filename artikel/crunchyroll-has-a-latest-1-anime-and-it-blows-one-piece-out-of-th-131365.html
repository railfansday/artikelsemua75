Crunchyroll Has A Latest #1 Anime, And It Blows One Piece Out Of The Water

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

One Piece has grown accustomed to being at the top of charts, but Crunchyroll has unveiled a major competitor. Frieren: Beyond Journey's End is the most popular series on the streaming service, according to Crunchyroll's list of most popular anime. One Piece is trailing behind at number six, as of the time of writing.

It has been revealed that while it's nothing unusual for anime to temporarily displace one of the greats of the Substantial Three, Frieren's dominance has been well above par. Since the premiere of Frieren: Beyond Journey's End's anime back in 2023, the series has been constantly at the forefront of best-of lists and has regularly been the most popular anime on services like Netflix—in fact, the anime has retained a steady 100% on Rotten Tomatoes since its launch. This also isn't the first time that Frieren has dethroned One Piece, with Frieren and Jujutsu Kaisen outpacing One Piece's manga sales in 2026 per ORICON's midterm 2026 report. All of these data points indicate something much larger than Frieren's individual quality; it represents Frieren's unique role within a broader cultural zeitgeist, and dethroning One Piece on Crunchyroll might just be the latest development that can prove that point. On the surface, Frieren: Beyond Journey's End and One Piece might not seem to share much in common. One Piece's story hardly needs rehashing; the long-running shōnen juggernaut is an action-adventure series based on the scruffy pirate Luffy and the Straw Hat Crew as they travel the world in search of a mythical ultimate treasure called the One Piece so that Luffy can become King of the Pirates.

Frieren, on the other hand, chronicles the adventures of the elven mage Frieren, who sets off on a journey to collect spells after her party had once defeated the Demon King. The catch is that elves age particularly slowly in Frieren, living over 1000 years; her initial 50-year journey felt like a blip for her until she reunites with her party and sees them in old age. After the death of the party's hero Himmel, Frieren sets out on another journey and slowly assembles a new gang of friends, this time aiming to understand human emotions, experience, and mortality.

There isn't obvious connective tissue between the two series; to find it, one has to dig a bit deeper. One Piece's world-building is second to none, and it's inarguable that the shōnen massively raised the bar for what stories popular series could tell. One Piece didn't develop in a vacuum—its storytelling and worldbuilding are a continuation of predecessors like Hunter x Hunter and Dragon Ball.

Frieren didn't develop in a vacuum, either; its emotional resonances and character depth reflect conventions that One Piece played a massive role in making viable cornerstones of shōnen storytelling.

Perhaps there's no better example than One Piece's iconic Fan Letter special, in which the lives, environments, and consequences of the events taking place in One Piece's main story are intimately explored. That level of depth, retrospection, and introspection is precisely the motor powering Frieren under the hood, and it's the reason the series has become so successful. It's not so much that Frieren outshines One Piece; it's more that Frieren lovingly builds on the foundation One Piece has spent years laying.

One Piece, for all intents and purposes, has a long-standing, stable, and smoothly growing fanbase; in that sense, it's almost meaningless to directly compare the success of recent anime with punchier, more contained stories to it. Taking into account how the anime industry has boomed since the pandemic largely thanks to streaming libraries, it makes sense why tight, bingeable seasonal releases like Frieren might briefly overtake long-running episodic series like One Piece, which now numbers nearly 1200 episodes.

There's also something to be said about how a series like Frieren can ring true with some of One Piece's most satisfying emotional contrasts. Like One Piece, Frieren can be light and digestible, but it can also feel epic, dark, and all too human. What Frieren does very effectively is set them into a latest context. In a fun turn of events, series like Frieren can therefore turn into a thematic gateway for casual anime fans to try a series like One Piece—whose sheer length might otherwise intimidate them.

At any rate, one thing is for certain: for an absolute anime masterpiece like Frieren: Beyond Journey's End, taking over a giant like One Piece on Crunchyroll is a massive milestone to be celebrated.

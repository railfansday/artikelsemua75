Adam Driver: ‘No One Was Paying Us’ to Develop ‘The Hunt for Ben Solo’ and ‘Star Wars’ Boss Mentioned ‘We Have a 100% Approval’ to Make It, Then Disney Killed It

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Adam Driver and Steven Soderbergh didn’t get paid for the two-and-a-half years they spent developing “The Hunt for Ben Solo,” a standalone “Star Wars” movie that would’ve centered on Driver’s fan favorite character Kylo Ren/Ben Solo. The actor said on the “Talk Easy” podcast that money was not even top of mind since they were given the upmost assurance from then-Lucasfilm president Kathleen Kennedy that the movie would happen, and thus the pay of it all could be figured out once it was greenlit. “We were gonna do a Ben Solo movie and that didn’t happen. And we worked on it for a long time,” Driver noted on the podcast. “Soderbergh did that for nothing, because we were given a lot of confidence. Like Kathy [Kennedy] said we have a 100% approval rate [from Disney]. We worked on it. No one’s paying us to work on it. The only reason we were working on it was we fucking loved the idea, how we wanted to do it, and I loved that character. And that was it. That was our guiding principle. All the money stuff came later. We were trying to figure out how to make it cheap so they could let us do what we wanted to do. So that was kind of a bummer.”. Driver revealed the existence of “The Hunt for Ben Solo” in an October 2025 interview with The Associated Press, explaining how Disney executives pulled the plug on the project because they couldn’t wrap their heads around the character coming back after he presumably died in 2019’s “Star Wars: The Rise of Skywalker.”. “I always was interested in doing another ‘Star Wars,’” Driver remarked at the time. “I had been talking about doing another one since 2021. Kathleen [Kennedy] had reached out. I always said: ‘With a great director and a great story, I’d be there in a second.’ I loved that character and loved playing him.”.

In an AP interview earlier this month, Driver said it was “criminal” for Disney to scrap “The Hunt for Ben Solo” after also saying on “The Rich Eisen Show” around the same time that the project is officially dead and he has no interest in trying to revive it (“I think the ship has sailed though,” Driver said).

Recent findings suggest that “For me it was: Steven Soderbergh wants to make a ‘Star Wars’ film. What are we debating?” Driver told AP. “It’s what it is. It’s criminal. Or it felt criminal. We’re all moving on. On to the next. And Steven already has something else that’s one of the best things he’s ever written.”.

Driver originally told the AP in 2025 that he loved “The Hunt for Ben Solo” script, calling it the “standard” of what a “Star Wars” movie “should be” and comparing it to the “handmade and character-driven” feel of “The Empire Strikes Back.” He added that Lucasfilm execs Kennedy, Dave Filoni and Cary Beck backed the project until Disney shut it down.

“We presented the script to Lucasfilm. They loved the idea,” Driver recalled. “They totally understood our angle and why we were doing it. We took it to Bob Iger and Alan Bergman, and they said no. They didn’t see how Ben Solo was alive. And that was that.”.

Kathleen Kennedy expressed her own frustration with Disney pulling the plug on the project during her Lucasfilm exit interview. Soderbergh told BK Mag soon after that he was not surprised to read about Kennedy’s annoyances.

“We were all frustrated,” Soderbergh remarked. “You know, that was two and a half years of free work for me and Adam and [writer] Rebecca Blunt. When Adam and I discussed him talking about it publicly, I said, ‘Look, do not editorialize or speculate about the why. Just say what happened, because all we know is what happened.’ The stated reason was, ‘We don’t think Ben Solo could be alive.’ And that was all we were told. And so there’s nothing to do about it, you know, except move on.”.

“I’d kind of made the movie in my head, and just felt bad that nobody else was going to get to see it,” the director continued. “I thought the conversation was strictly going to be a practical one—where they go, what is this going to cost? And I had a really good answer for that. But it never even got to that point. It’s insane. We’re all very disappointed.”.

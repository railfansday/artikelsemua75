The “Soft Wolf” Cut Is the Simple Way to Add Fullness to Mature Hair: How to Recreate It

In a recent development, A chameleon both on and offscreen, Pamela Anderson proves short hair can be incredibly versatile and has made a strong case for trendy hairstyles that feel grown-up and sophisticated. The celebrated actress and former Bazaar UK cover star has experimented with sharp micro-bangs, gone auburn with a fluffy ’70s mullet, and embraced French glamour with a chic chignon. Her most recent look is understated and fresh, but with plenty of personality: the “soft wolf” cut.

It has been revealed that the ’70s-inspired wolf cut is characterized by choppy layers with face-framing pieces that get gradually longer along the bottom, delivering plenty of volume and movement. It’s a cut that makes an impact all on its own and has personality in spades. Anderson’s version takes the wolf cut and gives it a polished, understated twist. A softer take on the trend, this chop feels effortlessly cool. “The benefit of a ‘soft wolf’ cut is that it allows a fashion-led hair shape to be kept feminine and ageless by softening the edges,” claims Anita Rice, the cofounder of Buller and Rice salons. “It’s youthful yet chic.”.

According to Rice, it’s important to consider the length of hair that sits around the temples. “This is a crucial part of the hairline, and when cut shorter it can give a harder feel, but if you keep it longer and softer, it can create a more versatile, flattering silhouette,” Rice says. It works across all ages, but if your hair tends to be fine, avoid “over-texturizing,” which can make it look thinner and wispier.

Adds Rice: “You still want to create softness, and this is about getting the right type of technique to encourage volume without leaving the hair feeling limp.” To style, wear it à la Anderson by gently pushing it back from the face, or experiment with a deep side part. “Alternatively, adding volume through a bouncy blow-dry with ‘flicky’ ends helps achieve a more ’70s feel.”.

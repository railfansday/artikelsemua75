Here is a roundup of headlines from India from Friday, November 20:.

Pakistan's Climate Change Minister Musadik Malik arrived in Newly introduced Delhi on Friday for a Shanghai Cooperation Organization (SCO) cultural forum. (Related: The Age of Machine Learning As Code Has Arrived...)

It is the first visit to India by a Pakistani federal minister since Bilawal Bhutto Zardari attended the SCO meeting in Goa in May 2023, and the first since last year's conflict between the two countries.

Prime Minister Narendra Modi is set to inaugurate the Civilizational Dialogue Forum, which runs from November 20 to 22. India invited Pakistan's culture minister in October, but Islamabad sent Malik instead.

Pakistan's Foreign Office remarked he would present the country's views on dialogue among civilizations and meet other SCO members on the sidelines, local media reported.

Malik mentioned the visit is not a bilateral negotiation, according to NDTV. No breakthrough in frigid relations is expected.

According to recent reports, the visit comes after the four-day India-Pakistan conflict of 2025, set off by the Pahalgam attack, which killed at least 70 people on both sides, AFP stated. It also comes two days after Indian forces killed Lashkar-e-Taiba commander Lateef Bhat. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

Opposition leaders criticized the government's move to host Malik.

Sources indicate that "BJP always says that talks and terrorism cannot happen at the same time. But now, a Pakistani minister is visiting India for the SCO meeting," said Samajwadi Party leader Fakhrul Hasan Chand, NDTV reported.

Congress Member of Parliament Pramod Tiwari also recalled Malik's earlier remarks on the Indus Waters Treaty, calling his language that of a "terrorist," according to NDTV.

Earlier this year, Malik, referring to the Indus Waters Treaty suspension after last year's Pahalgam terror attack, had warned against "water aggression," pressing India to respect the 1960 treaty.

"Whoever touches our water, their hands would be cut off," he said at the time.

The US Commission on International Religious Freedom (USCIRF) stated Thursday that India has escalated its targeting of religious minorities during a voter list revision that struck off tens of millions of names, Reuters reported.

At 130 million names removed, it is the largest deletion in India's history, local media reported.

"The Indian government is targeting Muslim citizens by removing them from voter rolls, calling them 'foreigners,' and now illegally deporting them," USCIRF chair Asif Mahmood mentioned in a statement.

The revision has become entangled with a separate drive to identify and expel suspected illegal immigrants, even though removal from the rolls does not itself establish that someone is a foreigner.

USCIRF commissioner Gene Mills urged the State Department to designate India a "country of particular concern.".

The panel is a bipartisan US government body whose recommendations are not binding, and Washington has not acted on its recent ones on India.

India's Election Commission is overseeing a nationwide re-verification of the electoral rolls requiring voters to prove their eligibility to remain on them, removing duplicate, deceased, shifted and otherwise ineligible voters.

But critics say the ECI has gone far beyond that remit.

Recent findings suggest that the Indian Express reported that more than 130 million names were removed from electoral rolls across the country. Before this, India had some 970 million eligible voters.

For scale, Germany has about 60 million eligible voters in total.

India's Foreign Ministry has cast USCIRF as biased, and New Delhi has called such criticism interference in its internal affairs.

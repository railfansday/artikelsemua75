The reaction to the departure of Bruno Guimaraes seems to have overshadowed that of Alexander Isak.

Fans had assumed the Brazilian’s deep connection with the United faithful was mutual, so discovering it was a bit more one-sided than anyone believed, only heightened the sense of betrayal when he left for the Emirates. (Related: Binding of Isaac fans review bomb DLC after discovering...)

Some writers on The Mag were asked for 50 words on Bruno Guimaraes ahead of this match; most accepting that he’s still a legend, but that the manner of his departure left a sour taste such that he shouldn’t have been immune to a tempestuous reception. (Related: Geren reopens Texas casino fight, but his amendment sti...)

And so it came to pass; Guimaraes was roundly booed and jeered, with both his greed and his parentage loudly called into question, which felt a touch ironic, given how the faithful had once adored his father, a man who used to sit in the away end and soak up the adulation.

As it happens, Bruno was largely anonymous and replaced by the more energetic Lewis-Skelly when his side was trailing, although he hadn’t been far from converting a Declan Rice cross in the 28th minute, something that would have amounted to a low blow for most of the 52,661 crowd had his effort found its way into the back of the net.

Recent findings suggest that that chance came just before Arsenal’s dominance saw them ahead. Kai Havertz dispossessed Lewis Hall high up the pitch and slid a sublime pass to Eberechi Eze, who whipped the ball past Lukas Hornicek.

Newcastle were struggling and it was what I’d expected, pre-match bonhomie with old friends and the lad, fueled by some decent ale, had quickly evaporated when the team was unveiled.

Right on half-time, Madueke, who had already hit the base of the post in the sixth minute, passed up a golden opportunity to double Arsenal’s lead, as some of the boos that had been reserved for Bruno were now being directed at those in the black and white.

Thank goodness Matthias Jaissle had both seen enough and managed to hold his nerve, a decisive triple substitution that introduced Tino Livramento, Jacob Ramsey, and Anthony Elanga into the fray proving transformational.

Just four minutes after the restart, Joe Willock pressed Martin Odegaard into a costly mistake on the Arsenal left. Will Osula scooped up the loose ball and teed up Harvey Barnes, who fired clinically past David Raya to register United’s first home strike in over two months.

Barnes will tear you apart echoed around the stadium, Dedic unable to join in with the bow and arrow celebrations, Livramento now doing a better job at right back, whilst Ramsey had shored up the midfield as Steur’s replacement and Elanga looked a whole lot better than the lethargic Murphy.

Energised by a crowd that was itself suitably invigorated, United turned the game on its head.

In the 62nd minute, Lewis Hall’s corner was nodded back across goal by an unmarked Dan Burn. This sparked a frenetic goalmouth scramble that ended with Malick Thiaw poking home from close range, a goal confirmed after an agonising VAR check that looked at supposed handball and offside offences, that feeling of dread never materialising as Chris Kavanagh finally signalled the goal would stand.

At this stage, United were very much on top but just couldn’t muster that elusive third, and when David Raya left the field following a collision with Osula in the 70th minute, rather than worsen the Gunners evening, the long break before Kepa Arrizabalaga replaced his compatriot allowed them to regroup and turn the tide.

I’d said to the lad that this was going to be a long twenty minutes, possibly half an hour, for us to negotiate and somewhat inevitably, Arsenal hit us on the counter in the 78th minute.

Recent findings suggest that released down the flank by Odegaard, Noni Madueke cut inside to drill a shot that squirmed through Hornicek’s grasp to level the scores. Could he have saved it? Maybe, all that good work at Craven Cottage a fortnight ago undone in an instant.

At this point, had anyone feared an Arsenal winner was coming, that might have been justified, they are a very good team and their substitutions demonstrated a squad that has strength in depth.

But the final stages, including nine minutes of added time, saw both sides pushing for a winner and it’s Newcastle that will feel more aggrieved, first Kepa denied Lewis Miley before Havertz nearly scored an own goal, the crossbar coming to his rescue as the spoils were shared.

Recent findings suggest that I wrote an article for The Mag recently highlighting that United had picked up five more points from their opening ten fixtures when making a comparison with the same fixtures from the previous campaign. You can make that six following yesterday’s gutsy performance, and you never know, Matty Jaissle might just be getting the hang of things in the St James’ hotseat.

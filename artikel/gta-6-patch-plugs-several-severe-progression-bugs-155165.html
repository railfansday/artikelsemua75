You know what’s nice about Grand Theft Auto 6 having such a monumental, far-reaching release? No, it’s not the literal billions of dollars it’s bringing in, though I’m sure nobody at Rockstar or Take-Two minds that. The best thing about having so many eyes on your game at once is that, if any bugs were missed during the game’s QA testing phases, they will eventually be caught with an almost mathematical certainty.

Considering the sheer scope and density of GTA 6’s world, it shouldn’t be especially surprising that there’s a decent amount of bugs, errors, and assorted unintentional annoyances floating around, some of which are more severe than others, causing problems with the game’s progression and critical systems or even hard crashes. Thankfully, Rockstar devs are still in the trenches even after release, hunting down bugs as they rear their ugly heads. Feel free to make your own Starship Troopers reference. (Related: Rockstar accused of tracing furry artwork for GTA 6’s c...)

In an announcement posted to the official Rockstar Support blog today, the dev revealed the latest launch updates and fixes applied to GTA 6 as of this morning that squash several severe bugs. This update applies to both the PlayStation 5 and XBOX Series X/S versions of the game, and should either already be applied or ready to be applied next time you boot up.

According to the Rockstar Support blog, this new update covers seven critical bullet points’ worth of updates. First and foremost, an unspecified number of concerns that, when triggered, caused the game to crash, have been dealt with, so it should be more stable going forward. (Related: Diffusers welcomes Stable Diffusion 3...)

Sources indicate that next, a bug that caused in-game weather to stay static for noticeably long stretches has been fixed, so you should be back to seeing regular, consistent weather patterns throughout Vice City. On a more notable note, another bug that prevented the game from autosaving properly has been patched, so progression loss should be less of a concern.

The most notable fix in the patch squashes a bug that was preventing mission-critical phone calls from triggering. Players afflicted with this bug weren’t having phone calls that progressed the story or unlocked mission content come in, which was a major progression bug. On a similarly critical level, a bug that prevented players from properly losing their current Wanted level has been dealt with as well.

Finally, in a couple of relatively smaller fixes, a bug that prevented players from using the deadlift bar in the gym, alongside a bug that prevented partners from handing over fence-able bags, have both been dealt with, so you can get your gains and sell your illicit substances.

It’s good to see Rockstar on the case, dealing with these severe bugs, but as I said, GTA 6’s world is gargantuan, and there’s probably going to be a lot more bug reports flowing in for the foreseeable future, especially if and when the game receives any substantial additional content.

Industry observers note that in addition to the bugs that have already been squashed, numerous reports have been coming in around the web of other annoyances, confusions, and assorted weirdness. For example, some players have reported NPCs walking into walls, while others report small, incidental objects floating in the air.

It has been revealed that there’s also been some reports of graphical hiccups, mostly related to unpleasant pop-in. Clothing and cosmetic items may occasionally pop-in during gameplay, large vehicles like ships and planes may pop-in at a distance, and character models may appear distorted or low-poly for a split-second when loading in cutscenes. Most of this stuff is incidental at best, with some like the unintentional first-person view bug actually being popular, but hopefully, if anything more severe crops up, Rockstar will hop on promptly.

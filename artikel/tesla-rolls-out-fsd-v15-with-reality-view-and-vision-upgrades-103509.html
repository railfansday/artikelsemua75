📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Tesla announced on November 21, 2026 that Full Self-Driving (FSD) v15 was starting to roll out, with an upgraded driving network, a live Tesla Reality View visualization and a tech giant-reported 25-fold improvement in its critical-intervention rate. Navigation, parking, driver monitoring and speed handling also change in this release.

Tesla AI lead Ashok Elluswamy said the release was going to the first wave of customers. Earlier on November 21, Elon Musk said v15 would go to wide release in December 2026. (Related: How we sped up transformer inference 100x for 🤗 API cus...)

Industry observers note that reality View gives occupants a live visualization of the drive and a way to see and control what FSD plans to do. Tesla’s full release notes describe it as a single end-to-end visualization that replaces all explicit model signals while using less total compute, linking the fresh interface to a change in how driving information is represented. (Related: Industry News: Designing Azure silicon on Azure silicon...)

Musk’s description adds technical and control details: real-time AI video generation without a gaming engine, using half of one AI4 TRIP accelerator, plus turn chevrons placed in the sky and tap-to-direct controls for sending the car to a selected point. The chevrons communicate planned turns, while tapping the visualization gives the user a way to specify where the car should go.

Beyond the display, v15 has an upgraded vision encoder shared between FSD, Robotaxi and Optimus. Tesla says it was trained on millions of internet videos for improved generalization and data efficiency, and the release notes connect that upgrade to the reported intervention improvement. The shared encoder gives the upgrade relevance beyond FSD, extending into Tesla’s robotaxi and Optimus programs.

Industry observers note that at the start and end of trips, navigation now uses an end-to-end learned model based on fleet route-preference data. Tesla plans to expand that approach to entire routes in later v15 releases. Parking location selection can draw on personalized parking history, and the map can display multiple parking options at the destination.

Tesla also lists improved detection of road signs, small animals, road debris and chains, along with potholes, road details and avoidance of deep standing water. Speed handling changes cover each driver profile, school zones, speed limits and inclement weather, and FSD can slow for speed cameras.

Recent findings suggest that for driver monitoring, Tesla reports greater availability of vision-based attention monitoring when drivers wear hats or sunglasses, with reduced strictness in higher-speed driver profiles. Autoshift now uses FSD-based gear predictions, and Bike Rack Mode allows drivers to enable FSD with bike racks or similar hitch accessories.

FSD v15 couples a latest control interface with changes to the vision and driving systems. The planned wider release will test whether Tesla’s reported intervention improvement is sustained as those changes reach more drivers.

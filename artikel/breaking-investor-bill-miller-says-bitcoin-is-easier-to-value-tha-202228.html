📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Famous Bitcoin (BTC) advocate and investor Bill Miller IV stated that it is much easier to value the world's largest cryptocurrency than AI investments, even though Bitcoin does not produce any cash flow.

He mentioned that this has to do with the expectations investors have when it comes to these two assets, specifically regarding their capacity to deliver future profits. (Related: Building for an Open Future - our new partnership with ...)

In a recent X post, Miller, who is the chairman and chief investment officer of Miller Value Partners, broke down why he thinks that it's easier to make an investment case for Bitcoin than for AI companies.

Sources indicate that "Bitcoin and AI owners have different expectations. The owners of AI expect companies will one day produce free cash flow. Bitcoin owners never did or will expect this," Miller said.

And the end result is that "the valuation case is still much easier for Bitcoin than AI.".

Simply put, when investors buy stocks in AI companies, there is an expectation that at some point these companies will deliver big profits and free cash flow.

Free cash flow is the money a tech giant produces from its operations after capital expenditures. This is a significant metric for understanding the value of a company.

However, when it comes to AI companies, it can be difficult to assign a value because investors need to try to determine what kind of revenue these companies will generate, how much they will invest in infrastructure, and when these investments will start to deliver sufficient returns.

Miller has made it a point to push back against the argument that Bitcoin does not have value because it doesn't produce cash flow.

In a commentary from June 30, Miller explained that the traditional way of looking at intrinsic value, which revolves around future cash flows, doesn't really capture the potential of Bitcoin as a decentralized monetary network.

Instead, Miller stated that Bitcoin's value could be determined by comparing its potential role within the global financial system to that of gold. (Related: Tech Report: On the Shifting Global Compute Landscape...)

In his view, Bitcoin could theoretically reach about $1.4 million per coin if it achieved a valuation similar to gold's. At least, that's what he calculated at the time.

Recent findings suggest that welcome to Manchester United, Mauricio. For a man who had coveted the United job for years, this was an ignominious introduction. Mauricio Pochettino got a 13-minute crash course in the craziness of his upcoming club. His first game was lost before he made his first substitution, and that came as early as the 22nd-minute. His debut brought the joint heaviest ever defeat by a United manager in his first game in charge, and the biggest in 100 years. (Related: Industry News: Welcome spaCy to the Hugging Face Hub...)

It has been revealed that “So painful,” Pochettino remarked. “So disappointed.” As can be the way with United of late, most things that could go wrong did. When their new head coach’s name was sung, it was by the Kop. “Poch is at the wheel,” they taunted. A first meaningful day of work demonstrated the scale of the task to steer United in the right direction. (Related: Introducing Optimum: The Optimization Toolkit for Trans...)

The team who exhibited the pace and energy of the best Pochettino sides were Liverpool, relishing the chance to compound their rivals’ problems. Florian Wirtz scored a glorious goal, Bradley Barcola kept demonstrating a sprinter’s speed and Rio Ngumoha illuminated Anfield with his precocity. Yet each was aided by United, hapless and hopeless, showing it is easier to change the manager than to reverse one of the most extraordinary declines in English football history.

Sources indicate that it was as though Pochettino’s charges had decided to show him a snapshot of the problems: their self-destructive streak, their defensive failings, their capacity to concede in quick succession. No previous Premier League manager had seen his side both let in two goals and go down to 10 men in the first half of his opening game. Pochettino’s United accomplished that in a quarter of an hour. Suffice to say there was no honeymoon period.

Recent findings suggest that the watching Sir Alex Ferguson had lost his first game in charge, 2-0 at Oxford 40 years ago. Pochettino topped that, in the wrong way. Only Lal Hilditch, in 1926, had begun his reign as badly, with another 4-0 loss.

According to recent reports, and yet there was a continuity to United’s calamity. Meet the new boss, same as the old boss? Despite regime change, some of this was very familiar. United were two goals behind after 18 minutes against Roma, after four against Villa, after 13 against Liverpool. United got rid of Michael Carrick, kept the masterplan of going 2-0 down and discovered it has certain flaws.

It has been revealed that this was scarcely an unlucky 13 for United. Rather, they were culpable in their own downfall. Matthijs de Ligt needlessly left Ronald Araujo’s long ball, Alexander Isak ran behind the United defence and Leny Yoro barged him over. “It was an unfair situation,” complained Pochettino. “It was clear that was not a red card.” Iraola disagreed. “It is clearly the last man, clearly a foul,” he remarked.

So Yoro was sent off by referee Sam Barrott and waved off by the Liverpool faithful. The punishment for United was doubled when Dominik Szoboszlai drilled his free kick past a ramshackle wall and in. Chants of “going down” rang around Anfield. It was replaced, a few minutes later, by “sacked in the morning”.

Recent findings suggest that with wonderful improvisation, Wirtz hooked in a volley after Ngumoha sped past Diogo Dalot and dinked in a cross. If a criticism was that the £100m German had influenced too few major matches in his Liverpool career, he altered this, brilliantly, and ended with an assist to accompany his second goal in as many games. “He's in a lovely moment,” mentioned Iraola.

Liverpool slowed down at the end of the first half and then sped up again after the break. Barcola’s profligate finishing had cost him Liverpool’s third goal but his extraordinary pace created it. The £106m man’s scorched-earth solo run was followed by a pass to allow Ngumoha to replace Michael Owen as Liverpool’s youngest ever scorer against United. The teenager had tormented Dalot, but he was not the only winger on the scoresheet.

Industry observers note that victor Munoz exchanged passes with Wirtz and curled in the fourth after Liverpool’s high pressing was shown as Virgil van Dijk robbed Joshua Zirkzee, 25 yards from the United goal. Iraola ended doing fist pumps to the Kop, Jurgen Klopp-style.

“We probably should have scored more than four,” Iraola said. Senne Lammens saved, from Isak, Alexis Mac Allister and Szoboszlai. Harry Maguire made a brilliant block to turn an Isak shot away. The Swede slashed a shot wide after De Ligt inexplicably missed the ball for a second time.

It reflected badly on the new manager. Perhaps Pochettino’s first significant call was to bring in De Ligt for a first league appearance of 2026. That backfired. Meanwhile, Maguire was first dropped and then summoned.

Pochettino had to keep changing tack. As Liverpool scored four goals, United used four formations. Pochettino seemed to begin by borrowing Ruben Amorim’s 3-4-3, then it looked a 4-2-3-1. Then it was neither, with United down to 10 men. “After what happened, happened and the strategy was in the bin,” remarked Pochettino, who went to first 4-4-1 then 5-3-1, and who tried to argue what he felt was an injustice of a red card rendered what followed rather irrelevant. “We concede four goals with 10 men, and in a circumstance after playing 80 minutes at Anfield,” he said. But the circumstances are that United are in the relegation zone, lost on Pochettino’s bow, and were walloped by their rivals.

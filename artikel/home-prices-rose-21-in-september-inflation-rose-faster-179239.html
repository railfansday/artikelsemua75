Home prices rose 2.1% in September, inflation rose faster

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Inflation continued to outpace home price growth in September, according to the S&P Cotality Case-Shiller Index released on Tuesday.

The data for September reveals that home prices rose 2.1% year-over-year, unchanged from a month prior, resulting in an index reading of 308.06. In contrast, inflation came in at 3.7% in September.

“Nominal home prices remain resilient, but inflation is once again widening the gap between nominal and real housing returns,” Rebecca Kaufman, the associate director of commodities at S&P Dow Jones Indices, said in a statement. “Consumer prices rose 3.7% year over year in September, accelerating from 3.4% in August, marking the 16th consecutive month in which U.S. home prices declined in real terms.”. Month-over-month the national home price index was down 0.3%.

HousingWire Data, which is more current than Case Shiller shows that home price appreciation cooled for the week ending on Nov. 20, 2026. According to the data, the median list price for a single family home was $425,000, down 1.2% from a month prior and 0.7% from a year ago. It has been revealed that case Shiller’s 10-City Composite index rose 3.5% annually to 330.38, down from a 3.6% annual increase in August. The 20-City index posted a 2.6% annual increase, the same as a month prior, coming in at a reading of 318.73. Both the 10-City and 20-City index recorded a 0.5% month-over-month decline prior to seasonal adjustment.

Of the 20 cities analyzed, Chicago was yet again the strongest market for home price growth, posting a 6.9% annual increase, followed by New York (5.8%) and Miami (4.6%).

“Miami’s rise is particularly notable: After moving into fourth place among the 20 cities in July and August, it has now climbed into the top three, a sharp reversal from its comparatively weak performance in 2025,” Kaufman remarked.

It has been revealed that at the other end of the spectrum, reporting annual price declines were Seattle (-1.88%),  Denver (-0.99%), Dallas (-0.9%), Las Vegas (-0.78%) and Tampa (-0.16%). This is the fourth consecutive month Seattle recorded the largest annual home price decline.

Sources indicate that housingWire Data reveals that the metro area with the largest annual price increase as of Nov. 20, 2026, was Monroe, Louisiana (+23.0%), followed by Grand Forks, ND-MN (+20.4%) and Cedar Rapids, Iowa (+18.2%). Santa Barbara, however, reported the largest annual price decline at -30.2%, followed by Fond du Lac, Wisconsin (-24.3%) and Dubuque, Iowa (-20.7%).

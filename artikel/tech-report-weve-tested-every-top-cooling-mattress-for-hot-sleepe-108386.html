Still not sure if either of these would be the right cooling mattress for you? The Tom’s Guide Sleep Team has spent over 24,000 hours testing over 170 mattresses, and we’re sharing our top three shopping tips for hot sleepers here.

You may be tackling your hot sleeping glitch by investing in a specialist cooling mattress, but it’s equally important to make sure the bed supports your sleep style and body weight (no back pain here, thank you.).

Industry observers note that generally, side sleepers benefit from beds on the softer end of the mattress firmness scale as more cushioning ensures their joints are cradled to avoid aches. Meanwhile, front and back sleepers need a firmer sleep surface to keep their hips aligned through the night, preventing back pain.

Sources indicate that body weight comes into it too. Firmer beds best support heavier bodies while lighter bodies would find these too harsh, needing a softer surface to sink into.

In fact, that’s another advantage when it comes to choosing to the Bear Elite Hybrid and Saatva Classic Mattresses — they both come in three firmness levels, from soft to medium and firm, to suit different bodies.

Other factors, like mobility and overnight organization, can influence your mattress buying choices, too. People with reduced mobility may need a mattress with strong, sturdy edges they can push off from to get out of bed. Moreover, if you share a bed with a fidgety partner, you’ll want it to be good at absorbing motion so you can’t feel their movements from one side of the bed to the other.

Hybrid mattress are generally cooler than memory foam mattresses, no matter how much cooling gel is infused in their foams. Hybrid mattresses, which feature pocketed coil layers alongside layers of foam and other materials, allow better air circulation as heat can escape out of the bottom and sides of the bed rather than pooling beneath you.

Recent findings suggest that on the other hand, foam layers tend to act like sponges, holding on to any heat your body gives off as you sleep. Yes, cooling infusions (like Phase Change Material, GlacioTex and copper) can help manage that heat to some extent. But, ultimately, hybrid beds will always be more breathable and reliably temperature neutral.

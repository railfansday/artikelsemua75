📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that pump.fun [PUMP] is up 4.74% over the past 24 hours as of writing, a slightly more positive gain compared to the rest of the crypto market, which rose 2.34%. While there is no verified catalyst behind the rally, PUMP’s reported cross-margin and Pro mode rollout may have sparked interest in the token again. Furthermore, the broader market’s recovery and a bid-heavy order book could be another reason for the surge.

However, the recent rally is looking cautiously optimistic, as the recovery is yet to overcome the bearish trend, leaving PUMP at considerable risk of another pullback in the near term.

According to recent reports, on the daily chart, PUMP was trading at $0.002568 at the time of writing and approaching the 23.6% Fibonacci resistance at $0.002745. However, the broader price structure remains firmly bearish, with the token continuing to make lower highs and lower lows.

Reclaiming this Fibonacci level would be an initial sign that buyers are gaining ground, but the chances of that happening in the near term appear to be slim to none.

Recent findings suggest that the indicators were also pointing to a potential near-term reversal with an RSI of 31.53 and OBV continuing its downtrend, indicating that sellers are still in control of the market. This makes it difficult for the recent price recovery to develop into a sustained reversal.

The four-hour chart offered a slightly more encouraging picture. After recovering over the past two days, PUMP reclaimed the 61.8% Fibonacci level at $0.002554 and entered the golden pocket. However, it has since started to retest the support, making this an crucial area to defend. Holding above it could open the door to $0.002654, the next Fibonacci resistance.

The MACD indicated improving momentum, with its line above the signal line, although both remained below zero. H4 OBV also maintained its broader upward trend despite a recent dip, suggesting buying interest had eased slightly.

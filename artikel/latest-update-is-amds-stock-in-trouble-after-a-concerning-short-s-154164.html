Recent findings suggest that A short-seller report can be devastating for a stock. Even if it's untrue or full of misinformation, it can strike fear into investors and send a stock reeling.

Last week, shares of Advanced Micro Devices (AMD +6.85%) fell amid a short-seller report, which called into question its ties to a Chinese firm, IEIT Systems. Ties to China are a sensitive issue in light of Nvidia's recent controversy involving CEO Jensen Huang and a possible spy. That led Huang to resign from the company, although he would end up being reinstated. (Related: How to Build a Healthcare Robot from Simulation to Depl...)

AMD is up big this year, but does this short-seller report give investors reason to dump the stock before it's too late?

Hunterbrook Media, which has a short position in AMD and thus stands to benefit from its decline in price, claims that the tech tech giant "bills over 20% of its revenue to customers in China and Hong Kong." It also states that a blacklisted Chinese company, IEIT Systems, has been gaining access to newer AMD firmware, which is needed to run AMD’s chips.

Industry observers note that AMD has dismissed the allegations of any wrongdoing, stating that it is in compliance with U.S. export laws, which impose significant restrictions on the type of chips and technology that can be sent to China. (Related: Industry News: ‘Minecraft Mommy’ goes viral on TikTok f...)

The key, however, is whether any products were sold to Chinese customers that violated U.S. export law, and as long as that hasn't been the case, and at this point there's no reason to believe it is, then there's no reason for investors to worry about references to Chinese customers.

Industry observers note that AMD's revenue totaled $34.6 billion in its most recent fiscal year, and of that tally, more than 22% was related to customers in China (including Hong Kong). Sales to China aren't on their own a reason for investors to worry about AMD's stock.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/23/26.

According to recent reports, AMD's stock has been falling in recent weeks, and it's now down more than 30% from its 52-week high of over $658. The stock, however, isn't cheap, trading at close to 100 times its trailing earnings.

At a time when market conditions may not be ideal for the tech sector, with interest rates potentially rising higher next month, there could be more bearish activity ahead for AMD and the broader tech market.

For investors who are willing to hang on for the long haul (e.g., five-plus years), AMD may be worth buying given its strong position in the chip market. In the short run, however, this could be a risky stock to own, as there may be plenty of room for it to fall lower given its high price tag today.

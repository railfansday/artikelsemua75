Industry News: Amazon faces FAA probe after delivery drone strikes Michigan fiber cable, garage

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Federal Aviation Administration mentioned Monday it will investigate a recent Amazon drone crash near Detroit.

On Friday, one of Amazon's MK30 drones "landed on a resident's garage" during a delivery in Huntington Woods, Michigan, a suburb north of Detroit, Amazon spokesperson Jack Stelzner told CNBC in a statement.

According to recent reports, the crash occurred after the drone dropped off a customer's shampoo order around 4:30 p.m. local time, and one of its propellers was damaged in the incident, according to ABC affiliate WXYZ.

A preliminary incident report posted to the FAA's website states the drone "struck a fiber cable, a residential garage, and became entangled in power lines during takeoff." The report claims the condition of the drone is unknown. (Related: Tiny Agents: an MCP-powered agent in 50 lines of code...)

Stelzner said there were no injuries, the drone has been removed and Amazon is in contact with the resident.

In a recent development, "Safety is our top priority and incidents are extremely rare," Stelzner stated. "Anytime a delivery does not go as planned we investigate what happened and use the findings to further improve the service.". (Related: Breaking: AI for Game Development: Creating a Farming G...)

An FAA spokesperson confirmed it's investigating the incident. A spokesperson for the National Transportation Safety Board, a federal agency that also investigates aviation accidents, mentioned it's aware of the incident and is continuing to gather information. The agency hasn't yet determined whether it will open a probe, the NTSB spokesperson added.

Industry observers note that amazon has been working to make widespread drone delivery a reality ever since founder Jeff Bezos laid out his vision for the program nearly 13 years ago. The rollout has been slow, as the program encountered regulatory delays and staff cuts, as well as technical challenges and pushback from some communities where it conducted early pilots.

Sources indicate that expansion has recently ramped up. The company is targeting a launch of its drone service in 500 U.S. cities and towns by the end of the year, while an Amazon executive claimed it has the "highest demand drone delivery service in the industry," CNBC previously reported.

The e-commerce giant has encountered some challenges along the way. Federal investigators opened a probe nearly one year ago after an Amazon drone downed an internet cable in Waco, Texas. One month earlier, a separate federal probe was opened into an incident near Phoenix, where two Amazon drones collided with a construction crane.

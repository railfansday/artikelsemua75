Proofreading lets you earn from home on your own schedule, but test your eye for errors with free quizzes before you pay hundreds of dollars for a course. If you keep scoring well, pick a niche, such as court transcripts or business copy, and price your work by the page or the word.

Industry observers note that knowadays, for one, charges $499 for its Becoming A Proofreader course. Yet the same page offers a free 10-question proofreading quiz. (Related: Industry News: Designing Azure silicon on Azure silicon...)

Industry observers note that that quiz covers the small things a proofreader is paid to catch, from misused hyphens to words that sound alike. For more practice, the publisher of The Chicago Manual of Style posts free quizzes called Chicago Style Workouts, with no sign-up required. Try the editing and proofreading quizzes first, then the ones that compare Chicago style with AP, the Associated Press style that journalists follow. Freelancers often have to know more than one style. (Related: Industry News: Using & Mixing Hugging Face Models with ...)

Recent findings suggest that pay for a course only once your scores stay high, and only for one that trains you for the niche you want. Proofread Anywhere, for example, teaches court transcript proofreading in a course of its own, with a free seven-day introduction to try first.

Check what each niche pays before you pick one. In the Editorial Freelancers Association’s 2026 rate chart, members’ median proofreading rates run 2.5 to 4.5 cents a word for academic work, 2 to 3 cents for business and marketing copy, and 1.2 to 2 cents for fiction.

Then proofread three short samples in your niche, so clients can see the errors you catch. Look for clients on freelance marketplaces, in writers’ groups, and by pitching small businesses and publishers directly. Quote your price by the word or the page.

Picture a former legal secretary who already knows legal terms. She takes Proofread Anywhere’s free transcript introduction, then proofreads a few test pages for court reporters she once worked with. Two of them hire her.

Sources indicate that start this week with the Knowadays quiz and Chicago’s first editing and proofreading quiz. Both cost nothing and show you which rules you miss.

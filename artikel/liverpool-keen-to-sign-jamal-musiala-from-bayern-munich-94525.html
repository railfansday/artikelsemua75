Liverpool remain interested in signing one of Europe's most exciting stars, with the latest developments at his club suggesting he could soon be unexpectedly free to leave.

The Reds spent big again in the summer with the late addition of Bradley Barcola from Paris Saint-Germain for £123 million the headline, but under latest boss Andoni Iraola form has been inconsistent. (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

Defeats to both Man City and Arsenal have damaged their hopes of mounting a challenge for the Premier League title, while they were also beaten by Fenerbahce in the Champions League earlier this month.

Further reinforcements will likely be considered in January, and newly introduced developments in Germany indicate that there may soon be a possibility of bringing a long-term Anfield target to the Premier League in the form of Jamal Musiala.

The Bayern Munich star was the subject of controversial comments from club board member Karl-Heinz Rummenigge in an interview with Sky Germany this week, which has put Liverpool and others on alert over the attacker's availability.

The German international has endured a troubled past 18 months since suffering a serious injury in a clash with then-PSG keeper Gianluigi Donnarumma at the Club World Cup in the USA two summers ago.

Musiala missed half of last season as he recovered from a broken leg and dislocated ankle, and then was hit by a series of fainting attacks ahead the current campaign.

The 23-year-old was diagnosed with a neurological condition after collapsing twice in a week during pre-season friendlies and has only made five appearances this term thanks to a muscle injury.

He returned to the Bayern side in their 1-1 draw with FC Coln on Friday night but has now had his future at the Allianz Arena thrown into doubt by Rummenigge's suggestion that he will never return to his former level of stardom.

The former Bayern president has come under fire for suggesting the club have to seriously consider the possibility of letting Musiala leave if he is unable to justify his big contract thanks to the medical disruptions to his career.

Recent findings suggest that rummenigge noted: "Unfortunately, what we know suggests that it will be difficult to reach the level that he has so often shown here in this stadium... We need to talk to the family, the player, and above all, the agency. Because then these [financial] sums are – sorry, with all due respect – no longer feasible.".

On a possible departure, he added: "I think we handled this very sensitively with the player, and we're all interested in him coming back—ideally in top form. But what happens if that's not the case? Then we'll still have to think about the finances. And that's a task that the board and the supervisory board unfortunately have to make.".

Players union FIFPRO have reacted to the comments by expressing serious concern, while Rio Ferdinand has also criticised the way the player is being treated by his club.

Only last year, Bayern manager Vincent Kompany, who himself has suffered from medical concerns and collapses this season, had been tipping Musiala to be on the way to becoming a player like Zinedine Zidane or Ronaldinho due to his technical ability, saying: "He is one of those kind of players.".

If Musiala is to leave Bayern it appears Liverpool could be at the front of the queue to take advantage of the situation, with talkSPORT reporting that the Reds hold a long-term interest in the former Chelsea youth player.

He could join another German attacking midfield star on Merseyside in the form of Florian Wirtz after his move from Bayer Leverkusen two summers ago.

But Arsenal are also stated to have an interest, while there have also been links to Manchester City and Chelsea, with the latest developments in the Bundesliga putting Premier League clubs on notice.

According to Sky Germany, Musiala has a release clause in his contract that is set to become active in the summer, while his current deal is worth around €25m per year (£21m), which equates to £400,000-a-week.

Whether the finances involved are significant enough to make Premier League clubs think twice about signing the Champions League and six-time Bundesliga champion remains to be seen.

If Bayern are prepared to move on from him it is clearly a warning sign to some degree, but opportunities to land players of his quality don't come along too often so Liverpool, or one of their rivals, may be prepared to take a risk.

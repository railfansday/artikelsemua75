In a recent development, market crashes make investors feel uncomfortable, which is certainly reasonable. But any seasoned investor knows that they're part of the investing cycle. In fact, they play an important role in bringing prices down to bargain levels so you can make the most of your investments. (Related: Tech Report: GTA 6 players report game-breaking crashes...)

As Warren Buffett mentioned during the mortgage crisis in 2008, when the S&P 500 lost 57% of its value, "When investing, pessimism is your friend, euphoria the enemy.".

Recent findings suggest that if I could only buy one exchange-traded fund (ETF) to hold through the next market crash, I'd still buy my favorite one, the Vanguard S&P 500 Growth ETF (VOOG -5.52%). Here's why.

It's important to have a diversified portfolio that provides exposure to top growth stocks and also minimizes risk. There are some excellent ETFs that do that for you, in addition to owning your own group of 50 or so stocks.

It has been revealed that it's reasonable to focus on growth in bull markets and value in bear markets, but the market isn't rational and may change before you know it. For many retail investors, it makes more sense to create a well-balanced portfolio that's always defensive under any circumstances. If you own such a portfolio of great stocks, you don't have to worry about changing things up under different conditions, because you're always prepared.

Sources indicate that the S&P 500 Growth ETF does exactly that. It's growth-focused, which means that it usually does exceptionally well in bull markets and not so well in bear markets. But since bull markets tend to last much longer than bear markets, it has outperformed the market over time.

An important component of investing success is holding through thick and thin so your investments can compound over many years.

In a recent development, I see this ETF as similar to the regular Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO -3.42%), just better. Both were created on the same day, and they're both in the top five Vanguard ETFs in terms of gains since inception. However, the Growth ETF edges it out.

VOOG Total Return Level data by YCharts.

It has been revealed that the ETF only has 153 components, and they're meant to be the top growth stocks in the S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%). But lest you think these are all risky stocks, the index it tracks contains strong value stocks like American Express and Berkshire Hathaway and even recession-proof plays like TJX Companies and Coca-Cola.

It's anchored by the tech heavyweights like Nvidia and Apple, and it has plenty of smaller artificial intelligence-leaning stocks like Bloom Energy and Sandisk. In other words, it's a well-balanced portfolio leaning toward growth.

It's an excellent way to benefit from the market's growth as long as you can hold on to it for many years.

20 High-Paying Jobs Without a Bachelor’s Degree – Many Pay You While You Train

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

You don’t need a bachelor’s degree to earn well above the U.S. median wage. Before you choose a path, check whether its training pays you or costs you. Apprenticeships in the trades pay you from your first day, while the best-paid health care jobs take two or three years of school you pay for.

Sources indicate that more than half of all elevator mechanics, power-line workers, and radiation therapists earn more than $95,000 a year, and none of these jobs requires a bachelor’s degree to start. For all U.S. workers, the median wage, the point where half earn more and half earn less, was $50,980 in May 2025, according to the Bureau of Labor Statistics. In the trades, the training is itself a paid job. An electrical apprentice, for example, works about 2,000 paid hours a year for four or five years alongside classroom instruction, then takes the licensing test most states require. The apprentice finishes with little or no student debt. That makes the trades the route for anyone who needs to earn from the start. (Related: Industry News: Distributed Training: Train BART/T5 for ...)

The health care and tech jobs start with school you pay for instead. That route makes sense if you can cover tuition and two or three years of school before you earn a full-time wage. Five of the six health care jobs pay a median above $95,000, a level only three of the eight trades and energy jobs reach.

Each of these trains you on the job, so you’re paid while you learn.

Each of these most often starts with an associate degree, which usually takes at least two years of full-time study.

The tech jobs start with an associate degree you pay for, while air traffic controllers and railroad workers are paid through their training once hired.

According to recent reports, apply for an apprenticeship the way you would for any job, since it is one. Unions, contractor associations, and employers sponsor them, and the Labor Department’s Apprenticeship Finder lists openings by occupation and location. If you need a paycheck while you apply, start with jobs that need little or no experience.

For the power plant and power-line jobs, take algebra and trigonometry in high school or at a community college, since employers look for both.

It has been revealed that for the health care jobs, choose an accredited program. In most states, a dental hygienist’s license requires graduating from one and passing written and clinical exams.

If you need to earn while you train, search the Apprenticeship Finder for elevator and power-line openings near you first, since both jobs pay a median above $95,000.

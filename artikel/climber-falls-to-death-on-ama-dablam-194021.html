Search teams have found the body of Wolfgang Waschiczek, 57, of Germany, who went missing after he reached Camp 3 on his way down from the summit. Evidence shows he fell 1,700m to his death.

Industry observers note that waschiczek was one among over 200 climbers who lined up toward the summit of Ama Dablam this past weekend. He reached the top on October 25 with guide Sonam Sherpa, but for some reason the two of them stopped descending at a rather high point on the mountain. Waschiczek is the first fatality on Ama Dablam this season.

Together with Manaslu, Ama Dablam is the most popular fall mountain in Nepal. The latest enhancement from Nepal’s Department of Tourism on October 17 demonstrated that over 300 foreign climbers had applied for a climbing permit. That day, the rope-fixing team opened the route to the top, and a massive summit wave started a couple of days later.

According to recent reports, virtually all foreign climbers are guided by one or two Sherpas, so the summit rates and numbers are remarkable. Local media estimate that over 200 climbers, among locals and foreigners, have topped out so far this season. Further teams should join them over the rest of October and November. However, no matter how tamed with ropes and anchors Ama Dablam gets, a fall from its steep slopes is usually lethal.

Buying a rental in a cheaper state can turn a modest budget into a house whose rent covers its costs, but you can’t drive by to check on it. Pick the market by its numbers, hire a local agent, inspector, and property manager before you offer, and see the house on live video or in person before closing.

Look for a city that’s adding jobs and residents. The Bureau of Labor Statistics’ Economy at a Glance tables track its metro area’s jobs, and Census QuickFacts tracks its population. Compare a year’s rent with the price, the test that picks out cities where rent still covers the mortgage. Read the state’s landlord-tenant law on deposits and evictions.

According to recent reports, then hire your local team before you make an offer, the property manager first, since a good one knows which blocks keep tenants. Say an investor in San Jose likes a $190,000 house in Indianapolis that would rent for $1,650 a month. The manager she hires first warns her that houses on that block sit empty for months between tenants and steers her to a house two streets over. In a recent development, interview managers the way you’d interview a job candidate. Call owners whose rentals they manage, and check any license the state requires. Add an agent who works with investors and knows what nearby houses rent for, an independent inspector you hire yourself, and a mortgage lender that will finance a rental in that state. A turnkey rental, one a tech giant has already renovated and rented, calls for checks of its own.

Recent findings suggest that for each house, find out the property tax a rental would owe and get a landlord insurance quote. The Government Accountability Office found that average homeowners insurance premiums in southern coastal areas rose at least 25% from 2019 to 2024, against 3% nationwide.

Set aside cash for whatever the inspector flagged and the rest of the first year’s repairs, and visit before closing or soon after to meet your manager and see the house.

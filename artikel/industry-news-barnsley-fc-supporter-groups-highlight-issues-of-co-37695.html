The meeting, chaired by Barnsley FC Supporters Trust, included representatives from Athersley Reds, East Dene Reds, Hoyland Reds, Eaden’s Bar Reds, Manx Reds, Matchday Drinkers and the Fan Advisory Board, along with supporters active on Facebook and season-ticket holders.

A wider fans forum, open to all supporters, will follow before the end of the season.

Concerns raised at the meeting included concerns about the long-term strategy and future of the football club and a perceived lack of transparency and communication from the club's leadership and absence of football experience in the day-to-day running of the club. The club's recruitment strategy was also called into question alongside a decline in match-day experience and attendances.

Questions regarding the club's search for a newly introduced chief executive officer - following the departure of Jon Flatman - were also aired alongside the skillset that the club will be looking for in terms of his permanent replacement.

A trust statement read: "It was a productive meeting, constructive in spirit but challenging in discussion. Frustrations were aired openly, yet what came through just as clearly was a shared passion for Barnsley Football Club and a commitment to seeing it prosper rather than decline.

"Much of the discussion centred on the last four years and what the future could look like. Before it began, we set out the financial headlines from that period, so that everyone in the room was working from the same information.

"The club’s four most recent sets of accounts, covering 2021/22 to 2024/25, show a combined operating loss of £28.6 million.

"We believe shareholders have invested in the region of £22 million since 2022, and the chairman, Neerav Parekh, now holds 66.36 per cent of the club’s holding tech giant and 85.2 per cent of its A shares, giving him effective control.

"Some in the room acknowledged that, given those figures, removing the current ownership without a successor able to match that level of investment would pose a greater risk to the club’s future than the present situation.".

Addressing the news regarding the meeting with Parekh, the statement continued: "Whilst we look forward to that meeting, it will only be as valuable as the answers it provides.

In a recent development, "Before then, there is a derby to play. Sheffield Wednesday come to Oakwell on Friday night, and we ask every supporter to get behind the players.

"Let’s make sure nobody leaves the ground in any doubt about what Barnsley Football Club means to this town and to its supporters.".

Second-from-bottom Barnsley are winless in nine league matches – having taken two points from the last 27 available in League One.

The Reds have not played in the fourth tier of English football since 1978-79.

According to recent reports, rep. Ilhan Omar, D-Minn., speaks during a news conference at the Capitol last summer. Few details have emerged so far about the government’s search of her home earlier this fall. (Bill Clark/CQ Roll Call).

Sources indicate that A judge mentioned he will order the release of some information about the federal investigation into Rep. Ilhan Omar, D-Minn., which included a search of her Washington house last month.

Judge James Boasberg for the U.S. District Court for the District of Columbia made the statement Tuesday during a hearing brought by news organizations and an advocacy group seeking details about the search. Boasberg ordered the government to provide him by Friday what could be disclosed in the case. He pointed to how investigators are handling the “speech or debate” clause immunity that Omar has as a sitting member of Congress. “This is purely legal questions, interesting questions, about the privilege, the extent of the privilege and how it would apply,” Boasberg remarked. (Related: Tech Report: Take-Two CEO is shutting down questions ab...)

The Constitution includes an immunity for members of Congress for the “speech or debate” they engage in as part of their jobs.

Boasberg also directed the government to propose redactions to the as-yet secret docket. While he noted next week’s expected disclosures would not include the 67-page document that investigators provided to justify the search, he left the door open to unsealing that down the road if the government closes the case or brings charges.

Justice Department attorney Alessandra Serano argued against broad disclosures, calling it “very rare that a precharging, pre-indictment unsealing would happen.”.

The high-profile nature of the investigation leaked into the courtroom Tuesday, as Boasberg cited statements by President Donald Trump and others about the investigation into Omar.

“You have had administration officials, prominent ones, talking about the purpose of the investigation,” Boasberg said.

Administration officials have accused Omar of committing immigration crimes for years, and Trump mentioned last month the DOJ was looking into Omar “strongly.” Department of Homeland Security Secretary Markwayne Mullin said on a recent podcast that investigators had served multiple search warrants and were investigating possible immigration fraud tied to when Omar entered the United States as a child.

Separately, Boasberg mentioned he may issue rulings in the coming weeks unsealing portions of other court cases tied to the investigation, including one brought by Omar herself earlier this month seeking the return of the items seized in the search.

The administration executed the search warrant in October, just a few weeks before the midterm elections. The timing of the search drew accusations of political bias from Democrats who claimed that the administration brought the case to sway the midterm elections.

For years Trump has remarked he wanted criminal charges to be brought against his political foes, including Omar and others in Congress.

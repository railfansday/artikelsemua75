‘Worst COP ever’: Climate talks collapse after draft deal fails to include fossil fuels

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Negotiations at the United Nations' climate summit collapsed on Sunday as the U.K., EU and others rejected a draft deal that failed to include fossil fuels.

Since the U.N.'s Climate Change Conference — or COP31 — began in Antalya, Turkey, on Nov. 9, almost 200 countries have been locked in talks to reach a "cover decision," with a number of drafts issued in recent days.

Antonio Guterres, the U.N.'s secretary-general, mentioned he was "deeply disappointed" that no consensus was reached.

Talks centered on electrification, funding for developing nations and ways to respond to scientific warnings about rising global temperatures, with the 1.5 degrees Celsius target likely to be overshot.

But on Sunday, after negotiations overran by almost two days, the latest cover proposal made no mention of fossil fuels, prompting outcry from governments and campaigners.

Recent findings suggest that speaking on behalf of the EU, Ireland's climate minister Darragh O'Brien said the conference was "hijacked by some oil states," when asked by Reuters about Saudi Arabia's role in the negotiations. CNBC has contacted Saudi Arabia for comment.

The EU mentioned it rejected the deal because the text "does not respond to the science" of climate change. "We will not accept a COP that greenwashes fossil fuels," it said in a statement Sunday.

In a recent development, the U.K. government "does not believe the cover decision proposed by the Presidencies meets the moment," it said on Sunday. "It moves us backwards on the science when we must accelerate implementation," it said. (Related: Introducing 🤗 Accelerate...)

Recent findings suggest that representatives of island nations — vulnerable to the effects of climate change — refused to sign the draft text. "Three years ago in Dubai, the nations of the world agreed by consensus to transition away from fossil fuels in their energy systems," Philip Davis, prime minister of the Bahamas, said in an online statement, referring to the UAE Consensus made at COP28 in 2023.

In a recent development, "Small island states produce less than 1% of global emissions, yet we carry the weight of a crisis we did not cause. We have brought looks, commitments and patience to three decades of these talks. Antalya exhausted that patience," Davis said.

Albert Salamanca, a senior research fellow at the Stockholm Environment Institute Asia, told CNBC via email that the lack of consensus on a deal is "tantamount to a death sentence for vulnerable communities still unable to respond in a timely manner to emerging risks.". Developing nations are facing "abandonment," according to Mohamed Adow, director of climate and energy think tank Power Shift Africa. He described the event as "the worst COP ever" in an online statement, adding that "rich countries blocked the creation of a just transition mechanism.".

The World Wildlife Fund, or WWF, described the lack of progress on finance for developing nations as having "real-world consequences for the lives and livelihoods of the planet's most vulnerable communities," in an online statement.

Nonsikelelo Nkomo-Mabobe, business development manager at Solidaridad, an organization that promotes fair supply chains, welcomed some of the language in COP31's proposed Antalya Pact, with "promising signs around food security, which is acknowledged as heavily affected by climate impacts." But she mentioned a finance gap remains.

"Funding must be easier for frontline communities to access, and it must be traceable once it has been disbursed," Nkomo-Mabobe remarked in an email to CNBC.

In a recent development, the WWF welcomed the engagement from participants of the COP31 Action Agenda, which comprises of businesses and other non-governmental organizations. "Momentum from communities, cities and businesses is real despite what happened in the negotiating rooms," it said.

A major goal of COP31 was to increase global electrification to 35% by 2035 — up from 23% today, with a Global Climate Action Agenda Electrification Delivery Package announced on Thursday as a "first milestone" to achieve this.

In a recent development, the U.N.'s Climate Change Executive Secretary Simon Stiell stated on Sunday that "around half" the global economy is accelerating electrification, adding that the package had made "real economy" progress. More than 90 countries signed the pledge, according to Australia's Climate Change and Energy Minister Chris Bowen, COP31's president of negotiations.

"Frustratingly," the pledge does not detail how the electricity will be generated, according to Ellen Dorsey, executive director of the Wallace Global Fund — around 57% of electricity was generated by fossil fuels in 2025, according to think tank Ember.

Sources indicate that dorsey urged states to provide electricity to those in developing countries. "Will we ensure governments provide electricity to hundreds of millions who lack it, by leapfrogging fossil fuels build-out and instead invest in small-scale renewables?" she noted in an email to CNBC.

Industry observers note that preserving sea life has "often been overlooked" in climate negotiations, according to the U.N. The language referring to oceans in the draft cover decision was "slightly weakened," according to Nicolas Entrup, director of international relations at marine conservation organization OceanCare, in an email to CNBC. But he said he was hopeful that the Antalya Ocean Current, a U.N. plan to hold future talks on oceans and climate change, would be carried forward.

More than 95% of island and coastal nations now have ocean preservation in their climate plans, but around 60% of those commitments rely on external funding, according to research by ocean conservancy groups. Scientists also called for ongoing investment to monitor oceans beyond short-term research.

It has been revealed that "I share the deep disappointment of many: COP31 has been unable to agree on problems related to both Presidency's led decisions," Stiell said. Turkey and Australia shared the presidency at this year's conference.

Sources indicate that if you're enrolled in Medicare, there's an vital deadline approaching that could affect how much you pay for healthcare next year. Medicare's fall open enrollment period ends on Dec. 7. That means you only have a limited amount of time to review your coverage and make changes for 2027.

In a recent development, even if you're satisfied with your current Medicare proposal, assuming everything will remain the same and there's no better option out there could be a costly mistake. So make sure to do these three things before the Dec. 7 deadline arrives.

Open enrollment gives you an opportunity to make changes to your Medicare coverage. But before you can decide whether a new plan is best, you have to understand what's happening with your current one.

According to recent reports, to that end, if you haven't reviewed your current strategy's annual notice of change, now's the time to read through it thoroughly. That notice should explain any upcoming changes to things like premiums, deductibles, copays, covered services, and other benefits.

Don't just skim that document, though. Even a relatively small monthly premium increase could add up over the course of a year. And if your Medicare Advantage plan is dropping benefits, that could have a huge impact on you, too.

According to recent reports, before comparing Medicare prepares, take the time to make a list of every prescription medication you currently take. Include the medication's name, dosage, how frequently you take it, and the pharmacy you typically use for refills.

This step is notable because Medicare Part D plans can change their drug formularies, which are lists of covered prescription medications, from one year to the next. A medication covered by your current proposal might be moved to a different cost-sharing tier in 2027, making it more expensive. Having a comprehensive list should make it easier to compare plans and determine which one offers the best coverage for your specific medications. It has been revealed that if you've been diagnosed with any new conditions over the past year, it also pays to see if there's a Medicare Advantage blueprint offering supplemental benefits that could help you manage it better. Medicare Advantage plans commonly offer added benefits beyond what original Medicare covers.

According to recent reports, it might seem like you have plenty of time to take action on your Medicare coverage before Dec. 7 rolls around. But that deadline will be here before you know it. So if you haven't yet reviewed changes to your current Medicare strategy, your prescription list, and upcoming plan options, the time to get moving is now.

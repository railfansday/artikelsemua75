📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

President Trump praised Nvidia’s decision on Monday to reinstate founder Jensen Huang as the company’s CEO and president, saying on his social network Truth Social that “the United States Government has no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to NVIDIA poses any risk to National Security.”.

The Nvidia board’s statement had also referred to consultations with “senior levels of the United States government” as informing its decision. It stated that, based on that input, Mr. Huang’s reinstatement was deemed not a national security risk. (Related: Transformer-based Encoder-Decoder Models...)

Recent findings suggest that in his comments on Monday, Trump claimed that U.S. Attorney General [Todd Blanche] had found no evidence that Mr. Huang had engaged in unlawful conduct. It’s an unusual statement for a U.S. president to make, as the attorney general typically conducts those investigations independently. Notably, Attorney General Blanche has not yet made a formal statement on the case.

Industry observers note that there’s also indication that President Trump may have been aware of the decision before the announcement by the Nvidia board. Speaking to a PBS journalist over the weekend, he remarked of Huang “he’s back where he should be and we see he did absolutely nothing wrong.” Asked to clarify about whether he meant Huang would be reinstated, Trump said, “I hope so, I hope so.”. (Related: Industry News: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

Huang was accused of having multiple extramarital relationships, including one with a direct report at Nvidia and another unnamed employee. However, the affair of most concern was the one with the alleged Chinese spy, Alice Huang (no relation). Ms. Huang was an executive at a Singapore-based organization called Megaspeed, which had purchased billions in Nvidia chips over the past couple of years. This relationship had raised national security concerns about whether the CEO ties to Ms. Huang could have compromised sensitive information or facilitated China’s access to advanced U.S. AI chips, which are subject to export restrictions.

Industry observers note that trump remarked last week that he believed this relationship didn’t threaten the U.S.’s national security, and instead said that no one had proven she’s a spy. “She may not be a spy,” the president said, adding that “China plays games with us, and we play games with China.”.

Trump’s full statement on Huang’s return, released on Monday, is below.

The NVIDIA Board of Directors has made the correct and proper decision to return Jensen Huang to the position of CEO. The Country needs Jensen’s leadership at NVIDIA in order to ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Company, but the American Economy. The United States Government has no reason to believe that Jensen’s return to NVIDIA poses any risk to National Security. The Attorney General of the United States has confirmed that the U.S. Government does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct. On a personal note, I have gotten to know Jensen, and he is a Great Patriot and National Treasure. Separately, NVIDIA should continue its thorough internal investigation of the Company’s compliance with U.S. export controls in order to ensure that it is fulfilling its legal obligations and Patriotic Duty to protect U.S. National Security. Congratulations to Jensen, the NVIDIA Board, to the People of the United States — and, indeed, the World! Thank you for your attention to this matter. President DONALD J. TRUMP.

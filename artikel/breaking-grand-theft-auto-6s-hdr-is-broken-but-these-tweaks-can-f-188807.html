📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Grand Theft Auto VI is a visual and audial treat, and is easily one of the most technically impressive games on the current generation of consoles. But it isn't without concerns, especially if you're trying to experience the sights and sounds of Vice City with all the moody contrast and bright highlights that HDR offers. That's due to an HDR calibration tool that isn't exactly working in the way that you expect it. Like many other games, GTA VI doesn't use the system-level HDR settings on either the PlayStation 5 or Xbox Series X|S. Instead it features its own calibration tool, which lets you adjust the maximum brightness, black levels, and paper white values that work best on your display. While the tool makes calibration seem straightforward, there's an issue with it that makes it very unreliable. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=d3UaVoP6dPg Specifically, the maximum brightness and paper white point values seem to be linked in a way that is both unclear and incorrect. Normally you would set these values individually, with maximum brightness set according to the value your display supports and the paper white value that looks best to you. GTA VI instead changes the maximum peak brightness value it is storing as you change the paper white point. For example, if you set a maximum peak brightness at 1000 nits (a typical value) but then reduce the paper white point (given how how its default is), the peak brightness value reduces as well, with no indication to you that it's happening. HDR enthusiasts and handy calibration assistors HDTVTest and GamingTech have both confirmed the issue, and provided some rough workarounds until Rockstar (hopefully) releases a fix. The idea is setting the maximum brightness value higher than your display supports, but in line with what the game is expecting based on the paper white point value to choose. It's confusing, but GamingTech has drawn up a table that might help depending on your display's specifications. HDTVTest's approach is similar but with different recommended values for both the ideal paper white point and black level values, so you'll need to do some experimenting on your end to find what suits your setup best. Rockstar has released the first patch for GTA VI, but it mostly addresses some widespread bugs that players have been encountering since launch. HDR issues were also common at launch with Red Dead Redemption 2 but eventually were fixed sometime after, but if you're like 45 million other people and planning to explore Leonida today, then hopefully these tweaks let you get it looking its finest.

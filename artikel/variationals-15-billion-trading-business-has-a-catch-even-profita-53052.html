📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Variational raised $110 million and intends to use it to lift trading limits on Omni, its derivatives platform.

Recent findings suggest that the tech giant is recruiting more dealers as it expands into more stock, index, commodity, and currency markets. Omni traders will still rely on a single counterparty to pay their gains.

Bain Capital Crypto led the Series B at a $1.5 billion valuation, Variational announced on Nov. 18. CEO Lucas Schuermann noted in the tech giant’s release that stocks, indexes and commodities already accounted for more than half of Omni’s daily volume.

Recent findings suggest that variational remarked that an unnamed tier-one market maker already supplies liquidity for swaps. It is seeking institutions that can quote equities, commodities and currencies around the clock.

The Omni Liquidity Provider (OLP) is Variational’s in-house market maker, which quotes a price and takes the other side when a customer trades. It can then hedge that exposure with a traditional dealer or on another venue.

More dealers can help OLP quote more markets and take on more exposure, but the customer still settles with OLP.

At 20:10 UTC on Nov. 20, Omni’s public market table showed $94.6 million in open interest on the Nasdaq-100 swap and $57.1 million on the gold swap.

It has been revealed that variational had set the initial caps at about $10 million per market when the two swaps launched in September. It noted then it planned to lift each cap to $50 million or more. The organization put potential capacity under its initial dealer agreements at more than $1 billion across the arrangements.

Sources indicate that under the standard schedule, both swaps trade from midnight to 5 p.m. and from 6 p.m. through 11:59 p.m. Eastern time on weekdays. They close on weekends, and holidays can change trading hours.

During a closure, orders and liquidations wait until the market reopens, while around-the-clock swap trading remains a goal.

Industry observers note that variational reported over $1.3 billion in Omni open interest with the round. Entropy Advisors’ on-chain dashboard shows a series that includes OLP alongside one that counts only traders’ positions. On its traditional-markets chart, OLP’s side equals the traders’ side. Omni’s public API counts both sides in the platform total and counts only traders in its per-market long and short figures. The Series B release leaves its counting basis unspecified, so lining it up with the dashboard would require matching the series and the observation time.

Each Omni trader and OLP post collateral to a separate on-chain settlement pool for that pair, and a vault holds USDC to supply OLP’s margin.

OLP uses its own capital when hedging outside the platform, leaving customers’ collateral in their pools. (Related: Breaking: How we sped up transformer inference 100x for...)

Traders settle with the Omni Liquidity Provider. OLP can hedge separately with dealers and other external venues.

In extreme volatility or a severe liquidity squeeze, OLP’s margin problem can force a trader’s position to close.

If OLP becomes insolvent, gains that accrue from then on can go unpaid, even if the trader has realized them.

It has been revealed that the trader and OLP both post collateral to their shared on-chain pool. OLP owes the trader any gains.

Sources indicate that if OLP cannot safely maintain margin in an extremely volatile market or needs to free up margin during a severe liquidity squeeze, its counterparty liquidation rules allow a forced close. The position closes for both sides at the settlement price even if the trader has kept up their own margin.

Sources indicate that variational indicates customers’ posted collateral stays in the protocol if an outside venue loses OLP’s funds. If OLP itself becomes insolvent, gains that accrue from then on can go unpaid, including gains a trader has realized on Omni.

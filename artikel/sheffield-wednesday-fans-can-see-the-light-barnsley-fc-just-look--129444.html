Three words from the 5,700 Sheffield Wednesday supporters at Oakwell carried so much meaning – especially with Barnsley wandering around looking lost.

The football fans who turn up on freezing cold nights like Friday's and pay to watch a match they could just as easily see from their sofa or a warm pub indisputably love their clubs, but do not always feeling in tune with them.

Barnsley's appreciated the efforts of their players in a frustrating 2-1 loss and understand the handicaps their coach, Daniel Stendel, is fighting against. But their dissatisfaction with the direction those above are taking the club in was vented outside the ground before kick-off. (Related: Umamusume horse girls are taking over a Denver Broncos ...)

They cannot see how their club can get back to where they want it to be without a massive change of direction.

In a recent development, the same was true of Wednesday's fans a year ago as they emerged from the wreckage wrought by Dejphon Chansiri. Although David Storch's takeover brought optimism they were not convinced manager Henrik Pedersen was going in quite the right direction. (Related: Breaking: AI for Game Development: Creating a Farming G...)

His successor, Dave Challinor, is miles from his destination but even if it might have been premature after two league games, "Wednesday are back" was a vote of confidence he can guide them to the right path.

The Owls did not play at anywhere near the level of the team they aspire to be. Jamal Lowe's goals were their only shots on target.

Much has been made of the entertainment levels this season, but a night so cold you could see every breath in a derby mismatched enough that you felt the Owls had much more to lose than gain was not about putting on a show, just coming back into the warm three points better off.

Barry Bannan ran around like a man ten years younger, Liam Cooper marshalled his defence, Lowe played in a wide position that demands a lot of dirty work without forgetting his primary job.

Barnsley refused to accept their fate when Lowe scored his second goal after 14 minutes.

According to recent reports, the trouble is, like Wednesday's players last season, they are just not good enough for the level. Between the boxes they are, but not the bits that decide matches.

Their defending for Lowe's goals – leaving Cooper unmarked at a Bannan corner to head across to Lowe, then standing off the striker as he finished a move which took them from the halfway line to the back of the net in two passes and a shot – was relegation standard.

With the exception of Vimal Yoganathan arriving onto Reyes Cleary's low ball in to halve the deficit after 40 minutes, their final balls and finishes were not much better.

That they are trying their best only highlights their best is not good enough – not by much in most games, but by enough in almost all of them. Their winless run in League One now stretches to double figures – unlike their points tally.

Bromley's win at Reading on Saturday sent them bottom of the table.

It is hard to see them escaping relegation without a really good January transfer window, and very hard to imagine even a quite good one.

"When you play games like Sheffield or Stockport or Oxford. Luton, you do not have the feeling the gap is too so massive," noted Stendel after 90 minutes which backed him up.

"Between both boxes, we are surely a team who shouldn't be in the bottom of the league, but games decided not between the boxes.

"You would think, 'What you do in training?' but (on Thursday) we prepared the team especially for a situation like (the second goal).".

Whilst adamant his team can pull things around, Patrick Kelly – standing in for the suspended Cameron McGeehan as captain – was not pulling any punches.

"You can't start games like that," he noted. "We just had too many times this season where we just gave ourselves too much to do.

"We've conceded far too many from free-kicks and stuff.

"After that we were good, probably the better team for the rest of the game, but didn't create enough clear-cut chances.".

Barnsley's doom and gloom was in contrast to the joy of away fans who made up about a third of the crowd condensed into one end.

It felt crucial they shared it with the players at full-time.

In a recent development, "They have to enjoy it and look forward to the next one," noted Challinor. "It's probably a little bit outside my comfort zone (celebrating).

"It's brilliant to be able to celebrate, but more brilliant to be able to send them home happy. They've been through the mill over the past couple of years, and maybe there's a little bit of a feeling they've got the they've got the football club back.

"You build that camaraderie between the team and supporters by winning and we've done that on the last couple of couple of occasions.

"We need to improve individually, collectively. There's so much more to come.

Sources indicate that "But most importantly for the supporters, they want to see effort. They want to see the team put the bodies on the line. They did that in the second half.".

After too much darkness, they think they can see the light. Barnsley fans are squinting to make it out.

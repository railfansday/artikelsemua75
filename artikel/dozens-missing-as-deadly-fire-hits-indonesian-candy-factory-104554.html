Indonesian rescue teams are searching for 36 workers who remained unaccounted for after a fire at a candy-making factory killed at least three people, officials stated on Sunday.

The fire occurred at the Fonusa Agung Mulia factory, around 50 kilometers (31 miles) west of the capital, Jakarta, in the Serang district of Banten province.

"All elements of the joint SAR (search-and-rescue) team are making maximum effort to locate the victims while prioritizing the safety of personnel in the field," he added. (Related: Breaking: “Determined” woman completes 7 marathons in a...)

Rizky stated four people injured in the blaze had been taken to a nearby hospital and health clinic. Eleven more were said to have been rescued alive.

The governor of Banten province, Andra Soni, told reporters overnight that 60 people were believed to have been inside the factory when the fire broke out.

Drone footage indicates the fire destroyed large sections of the three-story building, bringing down the roof in some places.

Survivors said the fire started in a cooling unit on the third floor.

The factory produces Milkita, an Indonesian brand of milk-based candies and snack products.

Indonesia has seen a series of deadly industrial fires in recent years, raising questions about the country's workplace safety standards into question.

Among other disasters, at least 22 people were killed in a fire last December that broke out in a seven-story building in Jakarta after a suspected drone battery explosion, police stated.

An explosion in a nickel-processing plant in the east of the country killed at least 13 people in 2023.

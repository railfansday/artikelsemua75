The latest 13F filings with the Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC) are out, and a growing list of Wall Street financial institutions and billionaire hedge fund managers appear to be looking for ways to ramp up their exposure to Bitcoin (BTC +4.19%).

One of the biggest names on that list is billionaire investor Paul Tudor Jones. From June 30 to Sept. 30, Tudor Investment doubled its stake in the iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT +0.92%). It now holds 1.36 million shares of the exchange-traded fund (ETF) valued at $64.6 million as of Sept. 30.

So if billionaire hedge fund managers such as Paul Tudor Jones are buying Bitcoin, should you too?

Bitcoin is right now trading 53% below its all-time high of $126,000. That's the bad news. The good news is that it has likely already bottomed at about $60,000.

Sources indicate that tudor Investment was ramping up its exposure to Bitcoin at the same time the cryptocurrency was recording one of its best Augusts in recent memory. Bitcoin soared 25% during the month, eventually hitting $80,000.

Recent findings suggest that but there's more to Bitcoin than just its upside potential. In the past, Jones has talked up the value of Bitcoin as a hedge against macroeconomic uncertainty and global instability. (Related: Tech Report: On the Shifting Global Compute Landscape...)

The billionaire hedge fund manager has even referred to Bitcoin as "unequivocally the best inflation hedge there is." And Jones has argued that Bitcoin should account for at least a tiny portion of any portfolio, given its unique hedging properties.

It's notable to understand the broader context. First, the current Bitcoin ETF holdings of Jones (1.36 million shares) are nowhere near what they were in 2024. At that time, Tudor Investment held 8 million shares of the iShares Bitcoin Trust ETF.

Moreover, Bitcoin still accounts for just a tiny portion of the overall portfolio. According to the HedgeFollow database, Tudor Investment has total assets under management (AUM) of $23 billion. So its allocation to Bitcoin is well under 1% right now. Bitcoin doesn't even rank among the top 10 holdings.

Although 13F filings can provide a broad overview of where a fund manager is putting money to work, they offer only a snapshot in time. In other words, a lot has happened since Sept. 30, and portfolio managers are constantly rebalancing their funds.

As a result, we don't know if Jones moved out of Bitcoin before or after the midterm elections, or if he trimmed his Bitcoin position in response to the recent crypto sell-off.

It has been revealed that still, the fact that Jones remains a supporter of Bitcoin is important. The world's most popular cryptocurrency has a unique combination of upside potential and downside protection and could be worth a closer look as a unique portfolio diversifier.

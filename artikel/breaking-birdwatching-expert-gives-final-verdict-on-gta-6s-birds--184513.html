A bird expert who has found every feathery friend in GTA 6 has given his take on the game’s depiction of Leonida’s avian wildlife, praising Rockstar for the detail while also sharing a few notable inaccuracies.

When you think of GTA 6, taking relaxing cross-country trips in search of local flora and fauna probably isn’t the first thing that comes to mind. Instead, you’re probably keeping an eye out for the best cars to steal, or beefing out Jason and Lucia’s arsenal with deadly weapons for your next massive heist. While adrenaline-fueled shootouts and speedy getaways are all part of the game’s fictional Florida, GTA 6’s sunny shores are also home to beautiful wildlife (excluding the terrifying Bigfoot Easter egg). (Related: How to Start a Side Business While Working Full-Time...)

One birdwatching enthusiast has ditched the handguns for a pair of binoculars, using his expertise to reveal what Rockstar got right and wrong about GTA 6’s birds.

Writing in a column for Audubon, birdwatching expert Nicholas Lund shared his experience travelling across Leonida. Lund was particularly taken aback by how much care Rockstar had taken, especially when it comes to the boundaries each bird species has in the game.“The map is true to the ecological boundaries of South Florida, with herons, gulls, and other waterbirds in the south while birds like Red-bellied Woodpecker, Wood Stork, and American Robin are found farther north. Birders know that certain exotic species have made their home in tropical South Florida, including Spot-breasted Oriole and Red Junglefowl, both in the game.”.

Recent findings suggest that so, this means you’ll need to be prepared to grab a car (preferably a fast one), pop on your favorite radio station tunes, and put in the hours driving around Leonida in pursuit of all 60 bird types. It’s not all sweet birdsong, though, as Lund did find a few complications during his quest to spot them all.

Industry observers note that “I found a number of avian inaccuracies in just my first few hours of playtime. Certain species like Whooping Crane and Red-cockaded Woodpecker are common throughout the map, despite being endangered and threatened, respectively, in real life.

“Golden Eagles are extremely rare in Florida, but I found one soaring over the Mt. Kalaga area (mountains, one may point out, aren’t found in Florida either),” explained Lund.

According to recent reports, “Some species have confusing naming quirks. The chickadee is Black-capped instead of Carolina, and there’s a ‘Newly introduced World Oriole’ instead of a Baltimore Oriole, but those changes are likely due to the fact that Carolina and Baltimore don’t exist in the GTA universe.”.

Rarity and name changes aside, GTA 6’s resident bird watcher was keen to point out that the hunting for birds to document in Rockstar’s open world feels like an accurate representation of Florida-based birdwatching.

It has been revealed that “There’s garbage almost everywhere, lapping the shorelines and blowing along the streets. There seems to be no inch of landscape in GTA 6 that hasn’t been lessened by the touch of humans. It is, I have to admit, a realistic depiction of birding in South Florida,” he added.

easily my favorite thing to do so far is just screech to a halt in the middle of a busy intersection and start birding pic.twitter.com/QsDOLmNfYq.

Lund was also keen to mention that “GTA 6 provides what feels like the most accurate version of modern birding ever put to screen,” while also stating that “obsessing over the realism misses the point of having fun.”.

As a fan of Rockstar’s recreation, he also urged fellow enthusiasts to visit the real South Florida and see its wildlife for themselves, but noted that GTA 6 has one bonus over the IRL birdwatching experience.

“Birders can, and should, visit the real South Florida to find the actual birds depicted in the game (and many more), but they can’t do it while also jumping over broken highway bridges on a stolen motorcycle. Birding is a bonus in GTA 6; mayhem is the draw.”.

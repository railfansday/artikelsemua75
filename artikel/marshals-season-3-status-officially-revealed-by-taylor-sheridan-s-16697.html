Between movies and TV demonstrates, Taylor Sheridan has written dozens of projects in the last 10 years, but he's best known for his work creating Yellowstone, the gritty Western crime thriller starring Kevin Costner. Yellowstone aired its final season at the end of 2024, and it didn't take Sheridan long to produce a new hit like he did with Landman, the Billy Bob Thornton-led oil drama that's right now shooting Season 3, which is expected to air on Paramount Plus sometime next year. Still, despite his investment in new shows like Landman and Frisco King (the Tulsa King spin-off starring Samuel L. Jackson), Sheridan continues to expand the Yellowstone universe with new offshoots such as Dutton Ranch (starring Cole Hauser) and Marshals (starring Luke Grimes).

The first seasons of both Marshals and Dutton Ranch aired earlier this year, and both were swiftly renewed for Season 2 after millions of Yellowstone fans tuned in to see the next chapter in the stories of Kayce and Rip. It's still unclear when Dutton Ranch is going to come back for Season 2, but Marshals has already brought back Kayce Dutton for the second season, which is premiering new episodes every week. This is largely due to the nature of it being a network TV show, the first in Yellowstone history, making quicker turnaround times more of a requirement than a luxury. Still, despite another round of strong viewership in Season 2, the fate of Marshals for Season 3 has not yet been decided. When asked during a recent interview with Screen Rant if he had anything new to share about Marshals Season 3, leading star Luke Grimes shared an enhancement on what he thinks is going to happen:. The show that claimed the most of your answers is the world you were built for. If two tied, both are shown — you're complicated enough to straddle two Sheridan universes.

You are a Dutton — or you might as well be. You understand that some things are worth protecting at any cost, and that the modern world's indifference to history, to land, to legacy, is not something you're willing to accept quietly. You lead from the front, you carry your family's weight without complaint, and when someone threatens what's yours, you don't escalate — you finish it. You're not cruel. But you are absolute. In Yellowstone's world, that combination of ferocity and loyalty doesn't make you a villain. It makes you the only thing standing between everything that matters and everyone who wants to take it. Industry observers note that you thrive in the chaos of high-stakes negotiation, where the money is enormous, the margins are thin, and the wrong word in the wrong room can cost everyone everything. You're a fixer — the person called when a situation is already on fire and needs someone with the nerve to walk into it. West Texas oil country rewards exactly what you are: sharp, adaptable, unsentimental, and absolutely clear-eyed about what people want and what they'll do to get it. You're not naive enough to think this world is fair. You're smart enough to be the one deciding who it's fair to.

It has been revealed that you are a Dwight Manfredi — someone who has served their time, paid their dues, and arrived somewhere unexpected with nothing but their reputation and their wits. You adapt without losing yourself. You build loyalty through respect rather than fear, though you're not above reminding people that the two aren't mutually exclusive. Tulsa King is for people who are still standing when everyone assumed they'd be finished — who find, in an unfamiliar place, that they're more capable than the world gave them credit for. You don't need a throne. You build one, wherever you happen to land.

Sources indicate that you carry the weight of a system that is broken by design, and you do it anyway — because someone has to, and because you're the only one positioned to do it without the whole thing collapsing. Mike McLusky's world is for people who are comfortable operating where there are no good options, only less catastrophic ones. You speak every language: law enforcement, criminal, political, human. That fluency makes you invaluable and it makes you a target. You've made your peace with both. Mayor of Kingstown belongs to people who understand that keeping the peace is not the same as being at peace — and who do the job regardless.

"We haven't heard anything yet. This past season, the pickup was so fast, so I don't know what to expect. I think like one of them aired, and they were like, 'We're doing it again!' And we're like, 'Now?' I feel like we just did press for Season 1, and now we're already doing it again. So yeah, we'll see, but I'm sure you guys will all find out when we find out.".

In a recent development, new episodes of Marshals air every Sunday night at 8 pm EST, according to the show's official listing on the Paramount website. They are also available to stream the day after they air on CBS on Paramount Plus, the home of all Taylor Sheridan content, at least until he leaves the studio for NBCUniversal at the end of 2028. Marshals is at the moment the #5 most-watched show in the world on Paramount Plus, along with other Taylor Sheridan titles like Tulsa King and Lioness.

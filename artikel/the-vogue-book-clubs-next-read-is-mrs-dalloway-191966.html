What a lark! What a plunge! Next up on the Vogue Book Club’s reading list is none other than Mrs. Dalloway, the classic 1925 novel by Virginia Woolf.

Our selection comes near the end of a year crowded with Woolf adaptations: Virginia Woolf’s Night and Day, based on her 1919 novel, directed by Tina Gharavi and written by Justine Waddell, and two retellings of Mrs. Dalloway—Rachel Rose’s The Last Day, starring Alicia Vikander, Victoria Pedretti, and Wagner Moura, and Clarissa from Arie Esiri and Chuko Esiri, starring Sophie Okonedo, David Oyelowo, and Ayo Edebiri. In the September 15, 1925, problem of Vogue, our reviewer, Berenice Claire Skidelsky, compared the prose style employed in Mrs. Dalloway—fleet and fluid, drifting in and out of the minds and memories of its characters—to James Joyce’s in Ulysses, published three years earlier. “Both books have as their substance the odyssey of a group of persons through a day—not only their outward circumstances, but the intricate twistings and windings of their thoughts and the interplay of their lives, one with another,” Skidelsky wrote. “Both recognize the importance of the nuance in human significances; and both get arresting effects in an extremely novel manner.”.

Industry observers note that on the subject of Mrs. Dalloway’s actual plot—Mrs. Dalloway prepares for a party while a veteran of World War I suffers from post-traumatic stress—Skidelsky noted, dryly: “No summary of the story can be given, for the simple reason that there isn’t any story.” Yet that isn’t, strictly speaking, a criticism. “A series of incidents move with sequence and with charm into the formation of a unity that has novelty and strength to recommend it,” she continued. “A faintly melancholy consciousness of the evanescence of youth, and, indeed, of life itself, that rings like an echo ever and again throughout the book…adds poignancy of appeal for readers to whom the essence of life reflected in literature is of more moment than a story.”. Befitting, in a way, the book’s unusual structure, the Vogue Book Club is varying its format for Mrs. Dalloway. Instead of publishing a succession of stories on a weekly basis, we’ve declared December 14–18 Woolf Week here at Vogue. (Clarissa is in limited theaters December 18.) Every day that week will yield upcoming stories about Mrs. Dalloway and the life and work of Woolf more broadly.

Eager to follow along? Those who don’t already have a dog-eared copy of Mrs. Dalloway at home can pick up a (discounted!) paperback here. Then let the schedule below guide your reading—and when you’re done, meet us here (and on the Vogue app!) for Woolf Week. See you soon!

Fascia Release Is the Key to Regulating Your Nervous System, Getting Better Sleep, and Improving Skin

It has been revealed that I’m curled up on a treatment bed in the Corinthia spa, feeling a knot of tension dissipate slowly from my neck and shoulders. Within minutes, tears prick my eyes. I’m familiar with the sudden catharsis bodywork can bring, but this is different. Known as “myofascial release”—a targeted therapy that works with fascia, the continuous matrix of connective tissue that wraps every muscle, nerve, and organ—it aims to reduce tightness and release restricted tissue, restoring its natural glide.

“Fascia is deeply connected to the entire sensory network,” explains Nadia Alibhai, a London-based osteopath who was unfazed by my heightened emotions. “When you move or hold stress, forces are transmitted through this interconnected system. It senses everything and dictates how the whole body responds. This treatment directly influences how we perceive and react to tension.”. (Related: Transformer-based Encoder-Decoder Models...)

In a recent development, once dismissed as mere biological wrapping paper, fascia—made up of water, collagen, elastin, and hyaluronic acid—is now understood as a living, three-dimensional web. “It’s part of a complex system involving movement, circulation, lymphatics, posture, and tissue health,” Alibhai explains. When it works well, it keeps everything moving and prevents systems from becoming sluggish, but when it becomes restricted, often from scrolling, sedentary habits, or chronic stress, the fluid between the layers can thicken. This affects physical mobility and emotional well-being, leading to complications ranging from pain and stiffness to a dysregulated nervous system. Recent findings suggest that fascia and neurology are intrinsically linked, explains skin-health expert Annee de Mamiel. “Fascia changes according to how we move, breathe, and repeatedly load the body,” she states. “Cortisol and adrenaline change our physiology; the nervous system alters breathing, muscular tone, and stress response. Over time, these repeated patterns affect the tissues.” It’s a catch-22: greater stress can tighten fascia, which in turn can send your body into fight-or-flight mode, prolonging the anxiety that caused the damage in the first place.

This mind-body connection is just one reason why fascia has piqued the wellness industry’s interest. Google searches for “fascia release” have soared in the past year, while facials targeting the system are becoming more popular. Robert Schleip, the director of the Fascia Research Group at Ulm University, attributes this to its holistic possibilities. “Fascia is now studied in relation to the autonomic nervous system, and therefore emotional regulation, as well as the immune system and aging,” he states. The latest findings show that supporting your fascia goes far beyond easing a tight neck; it's linked to reduced arterial stiffness and improved cardiovascular health.

According to recent reports, fascia release has also entered the skin-health conversation. Tight fascia can be an overlooked culprit behind loss of luminosity, puffiness, and premature aging, states de Mamiel, because it’s heavily involved in fluid drainage and lymphatic pathways. “The diaphragm plays a role in pressure changes and fluid movement through the body, and the fascial system connects these areas,” she explains. “When I work on movement and tension through the neck, chest, and rib cage, I often see a change in how congested or puffy the face appears.” However, releasing fascia effectively doesn't require aggressive force. “If I use so much pressure that your body braces, I’ve produced the opposite response—it’s about creating conditions where the nervous system and tissue can both settle.”.

Facialist Nichola Joss achieves similarly impressive results with her signature Inner Facial, which fuses lymphatic drainage, fascia release, buccal massage, and sculpting techniques. “Fascia surrounds and supports our muscles; when restricted, it affects how freely muscles move,” Joss explains. This affects lymphatic flow and your skin’s natural glow. “By releasing muscular and fascial tension, you lift, tone, and sculpt contours while boosting circulation and softening fine lines.”.

Recent findings suggest that beyond professional treatments, supporting fascial health depends on consistent daily habits. First, fascia thrives on varied movement: try walking, twisting your body, reaching overhead, and squatting to support overall health. Unfortunately, there are no true quick fixes: Spending the day glued to an office chair and then going straight into an intense HIIT session can actually be counterproductive. Instead, interrupt patterns throughout the day: Stand during phone calls instead of sitting, swing your arms gently during screen breaks, and make sure you’re not holding your breath during stressful periods at work. Counteract hours spent hunched over a device with this simple doorway stretch: Place your forearms against the frame, step forward gently, and hold for 20 to 30 seconds while breathing deeply.

It has been revealed that schleip recommends aligning exercise with your circadian rhythm. “Incorporate dynamic, elastic, or bouncing movements in the morning, ideally outdoors, and slow stretches in the evening,” he suggests, adding that sustained yoga positions, such as a child’s pose held for at least 30 seconds, can be particularly helpful. “Regular stretching improves joint mobility and may benefit cardiovascular markers such as arterial stiffness,” he adds. Finally, make nutrition and hydration a priority: fascia is 70 percent water and needs hydration to glide freely.

As Alibhai reflects at the end of my session: “The goal isn’t to constantly release fascia; it’s to keep your body moving, adaptable, and well nourished.” I leave feeling lighter and more grounded—and I instinctively skip the Tube and walk home.

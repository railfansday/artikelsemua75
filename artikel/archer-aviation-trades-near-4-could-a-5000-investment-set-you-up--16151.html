According to recent reports, at the time of this writing (around noon on Nov. 16), Archer Aviation (ACHR -8.75%) stock is trading below $4 a share. That's a seemingly remarkable price for a company with ambitions to revolutionize urban transportation, especially considering its stock was flying high in the double digits just last year. But is today's share price really a bargain? And if so, could a $5,000 investment today be enough to set you up for life?

NYSE · As of 2:14 PM ET 11/20/26.

Archer is developing an electric air taxi, which it calls Midnight. It doesn't have regulatory approval to fly its eVTOL (electric vertical takeoff and landing) commercially (no one has that certificate in the U.S.), but it is widely considered one of the frontrunners in this nascent industry. (Related: Industry News: Judge blocks construction of Trump’s arc...)

What the heck is an air taxi? Basically, a cross between a helicopter, an airplane, and a drone. This feat of engineering can take off vertically like a helicopter, then switch to forward flight like an airplane, cruising at speeds of up to 150 mph. If it gets all the necessary approvals, it could radically change how people travel, turning hour-long traffic jams into urban hops that last minutes.

Industry observers note that how close is Archer to getting its approval ducks in order? Closer than ever, but not quite ready for takeoff. Indeed, the company has entered the fourth and final phase of the FAA's type certification process. "Final" sounds encouraging, but this phase is not for the faint of heart. It involves rigorous testing to prove that Midnight can carry passengers safely, with regulators scrutinizing everything from the flight's performance to its safety systems. The process could take years. There's no guarantee Midnight will make it through.

If Midnight does make it through, the long-term opportunity could be enormous. Morgan Stanley (MS -4.04%) has estimated that the global eVTOL market could reach $255 billion by 2035 and $1 trillion by 2040. Archer doesn't need to capture the entire market to grow substantially; even a small slice could translate into billions in annual revenue.

Let's play with some numbers. At $4 a share, a $5,000 investment would buy you 1,250 shares. If the stock were to climb tenfold, that investment would be worth $50,000. A 20-fold increase would make your shares worth $100,000, and a 50-fold increase would be worth $250,000.

Industry observers note that so, yes, on paper, the potential returns are enormous. On the other hand, all of them would require Archer to develop into a major player in the aviation industry, with a thriving air taxi business, substantial revenue, and, eventually, consistent profits.

According to recent reports, can Archer do that? Maybe, maybe not. And it's precisely the uncertainty behind that "maybe" that helps explain why Archer trades at $4 a share, not, say, $14. Risk-tolerant, intrepid investors might be interested in Archer at this price, but those averse to risk might want to avoid the eVTOL industry for now.

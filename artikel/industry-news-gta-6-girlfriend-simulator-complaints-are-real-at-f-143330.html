One former Rockstar Games lead has a revolutionary take about GTA 6 – "it is good." But even though that may be the case, he reckons that "the complaints are real" in Chapter 1 from those suggesting that it "doesn't feel like a sandbox" and instead feels like a "girlfriend simulator.".

Sources indicate that obbe Vermeij worked at Rockstar Games between 1995 and 2009, serving as technical director on GTA 3, 4, Vice City, and San Andreas. Obviously, he'd left the studio well before GTA 6 started development (it might have been a long time coming, but it wasn't that long), so he's been playing through it alongside the rest of us.

Recent findings suggest that "It is good. Very good," he begins in a post on Twitter. However, there's a "but" coming.

It has been revealed that "In chapter 1 (The first 8 hours or so) the game doesn't give you much choice," he continues. "There are many tutorials, cut scenes, mandatory missions and the missions are basic. The complaints are real (realism over gameplay, girlfriend simulator, doesn't feel like a sandbox).".

Opinions here do vary – I've definitely seen more than a few social media posts complaining about the optional tasks of grocery shopping for Lucia and buying her flowers, for example. They probably weren't high up the list of activities fans who planned to enter Leonida and immediately commit heinous crimes had in mind, I suppose – though it's worth keeping in mind that, again, those parts are optional.

Equally, though, I've seen plenty of people enjoying the almost life-sim-esque vibe of GTA 6's opening hours, and that's probably reflected in how much time people spend in that first chapter. As someone who spent 18 hours in Chapter 1, you can probably guess what camp I fall into. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

Regardless, though, Vermeij reckons things really pick up by the time you reach the second chapter. "In chapter 2, the game backs off and gives you a bunch of missions to choose from," he says. "This is when the GTA magic kicks in.".

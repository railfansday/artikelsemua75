The past 24 hours have delivered a reality check to investors.

Sources indicate that following weeks of selling due to the Jensen Huang situation, around $8 trillion was wiped off the U.S. stock market. However, on the 23rd of November, following the re-appointment of the CEO, almost $3 trillion flowed back almost immediately. NVDA soared by more than 31% and gained back two weeks’ worth of value in one day, showing how promptly sentiment can shift when uncertainty fades. (Related: What Is Considered Upper Middle Class? However, the same recovery did not spill over into crypto. Ethereum initially rose above $1,800 only for it to then trend back down to around the $1,700 mark by the close. The key takeaway? Capital is returning to stocks, but crypto is struggling to attract the same demand. This growing divergence has put “ETH vs. NVDA” at the center of a newly introduced debate over where investors see better opportunities.

Interestingly, investors are using five-year returns to make their case. (Related: Restaurant owners left in tears after discovering their...)

One post pointed out that investing $10,000 in ETH 5 years ago would now be worth approximately $4,200, marking a 58% loss. This pales in comparison to the over $12,500 that the same investment in NVDA would now be worth, delivering a 25% gain. That’s an $8,300 gap in return on the same investment, adding to the debate over whether investors now see better opportunities in stocks than in crypto.

In this context, the past 24 hours have delivered a reality check for investors. This raises a key question: Is the ongoing ETH distribution  indicative of this change in long-term ownership as money flows once again into stocks? And could this put further pressure on Ethereum [ETH] and ETH-heavy treasury firms?

The past 24 hours have brought some much-needed relief for digital asset treasuries.

It appears that the current NVDA outperformance is boosting sentiment in the U.S. stock market generally, with almost $3 trillion re-entering. In crypto too, several treasury stocks are benefiting. BitMine, for example, has surged by over 11% and recorded its best weekly inflow in more than 3 months. This suggests that improving sentiment could also be supporting demand for ETH-focused companies.

It has been revealed that however, Ethereum’s spot flows tell a different story. As shown below, a whale sent a further 85,500 ETH (around $146.98 million) to Binance. Over the past seven days, the same whale has deposited 176,000 ETH (worth approximately $309.21 million) into the exchange. Furthermore, institutional sentiment continues to be negative. BlackRock has seen net withdrawals of 1 million+ ETH, valued at $1.75 billion since October.

This creates a clear divergence: ETH-focused stocks are recovering, while spot market flows continue to signal selling pressure.

That shifts the focus back to the Ethereum vs. Nvidia debate. While treasury stocks are recovering, whales continue to sell, and institutions are also exiting. This suggests that there is still weak demand for spot crypto. Investors seem to be moving away from crypto and into equities.

According to recent reports, if this trend continues, it could suggest a broader change in the long-term holding strategy of investors. Whales may not just be selling ETH at random but could be actively moving funds out of the asset and into equities such as NVDA.

Recent findings suggest that lockheed Martin (LMT +2.15%) and L3Harris Technologies (LHX +1.48%) are top defense stocks. Each stock has its advantages. Lockheed is a more stable stock with lower price volatility and a higher-yielding dividend. L3Harris, on the other hand, is showing more growth and benefits from higher margins. Lockheed Martin's shares have soared this year, up more than 18% as I write this, while L3Harris' have slumped by more than 13%.

Here's a quick comparison of these two defense stocks.

F-35 Stealth fighter jet. Image source: Lockheed Martin.

Lockheed Martin is a much larger company, with nearly three times L3Harris' market cap and employee count. The company's third-quarter backlog of $292 billion provides around 3.6 years of revenue visibility, substantially deeper than L3Harris' 2.25 years for its $53.2 billion backlog.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

Lockheed management is predicting high single-digit growth across all four of its business segments in 2027, led by F-35 sustainment/production and a $52.5 billion multiyear award for PAC-3 MSE interceptor missiles. That scale allows the firm to thrive even if one segment falters. (Related: Introducing Optimum: The Optimization Toolkit for Trans...)

Recent findings suggest that as a specialist in defense electronics, resilient communications, and propulsion, L3Harris generated a 15.9% segment operating margin in the third quarter, roughly 500 basis points higher than Lockheed Martin. L3Harris reported third-quarter earnings per share (EPS) of $3.28, up 33% year over year, prompting management to raise full-year EPS guidance to a range of $12.25 to $12.35, an increase of 44% at the midpoint.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

Lockheed, on the other hand, saw its third-quarter EPS slip by 11% to $6.44. It reaffirmed full-year EPS of between $29.35 and $30.25, a 38% gain at the midpoint.

Both companies have above-average dividend yields. At their current share prices, Lockheed's dividend yield is 2.42% while L3Harris' is 1.94%. Lockheed has increased its dividend for 24 consecutive years, while L3Harris has raised its dividend for 25 consecutive years. L3Harris has been more aggressive in increasing its dividend over the past decade, raising it by more than 135%, compared to just more than 89% for Lockheed, which could be an advantage in a stock-to-stock matchup.

Despite its share gain this year, Lockheed's stock is trading at a better price point. Its forward price-to-earnings ratio is slightly more than 18 times earnings estimates compared to more than 20 for L3Harris.

Lockheed's biggest problem is that it is the lead on massive hardware programs, such as the F-35, F-16, C-13, and Sikorsky helicopters, and, as such, has lower operating margins, around 10.8% as of the third quarter. It is also affected more by fixed-price development or production charges. It stands to lose more, too, if a Democratic-controlled Congress tightens defense spending.

Sources indicate that L3Harris carries more debt, with a debt-to-earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) ratio of 3.1, compared to 1.9 for Lockheed, and its debt payments could easily weigh on its earnings growth. L3Harris carries a heavier debt load relative to its size, with $9 billion in net long-term debt. Much of that is due to the manufacturer's $4.7 billion acquisition of Aerojet Rocketdyne in 2023.

However, L3Harris plans to sell a majority stake in its space propulsion and power Systems business to AE Industrial for $845 million, while retaining a 40% ownership stake in the newly created Rocketdyne venture, which includes manufacturing and selling RS-25 rocket engines.

Deciding which stock is best really comes down to your investing needs. Lockheed certainly offers more stability, though with less growth potential.

It has been revealed that L3Harris is expected to see more revenue and earnings growth, though its higher valuation highlights some of that expected growth is already priced into the stock. If I had to choose, Lockheed is the safer pick because of its scale, better dividend, and lower valuation.

Industry observers note that nvidia (NVDA -14.01%), Coherent (COHR -12.90%) and Dell Technologies (DELL -11.04%) all supply the same artificial intelligence (AI) build-out. Nvidia makes the chips, Coherent makes optical parts that carry data between them, and Dell builds the servers they go into. (Related: Industry News: Hyperparameter Search with Transformers ...)

All three stocks have also tumbled. Nvidia closed at a record $239.24 on Oct. 6 and trades around $153 as I write this, about 36% lower. Coherent, near $223, is off about 48% from its June 2 record close. And Dell, roughly $322, is about 45% under the $588.40 record close it set on Sept. 17.

But their businesses haven't followed the stocks down. Each firm's latest report shows data center demand still growing, and I think each stock now leaves room for gains through 2030.

In a recent development, on Nov. 11, Nvidia's board accepted Jensen Huang's resignation as CEO and made Colette Kress, the chief financial officer, interim CEO while it looks for a permanent one.

Industry observers note that the business, though, kept speeding up. In the fiscal third quarter of 2027, which ended Oct. 25, 2026, Nvidia's data center revenue hit $107.6 billion. It was 21% above the previous quarter, and sequential growth accelerated from 18% in fiscal Q2. Management expects around $130 billion in total revenue for the fiscal fourth quarter, but that period has an extra week. (Related: Industry News: How we sped up transformer inference 100...)

Shares still dropped about 3% the day after the Nov. 17 report. Using analysts' average forecast for fiscal 2028 (the year ending January 2028), the stock costs under 10 times earnings, while the forward price-to-earnings ratio of the S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) is around 18.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

The CEO search might keep some investors wary for a while. But for a business whose data center sales more than doubled year over year, I think the stock is cheap.

Coherent's lasers and optical transceivers move data between chips inside a data center, and between data centers.

Recent findings suggest that showing how much AI now powers the company, Coherent's datacenter and communications segment took in $1.95 billion in its fiscal first quarter of 2027 (which ended Sept. 30, 2026), or 82% of total revenue. Segment sales rose 79% year over year and 21% sequentially, up from $1.09 billion a year before and $1.62 billion in fiscal Q4 2026.

For a 2030 holder, the longer deals might matter more. Coherent signed one for its fresh PhotonLink optics platform worth around $20 billion over five years, with revenue starting to ramp in early 2028. Also, on Nov. 18 the board approved a share buyback program of up to $2 billion, around 5% of Coherent's market value at the current stock price.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

Granted, operating cash flow was negative $93 million last quarter, and Coherent spent another $776 million on fresh equipment and facilities to expand capacity. And at around 22 times earnings using analysts' fiscal 2027 forecasts (that year ends in June 2027), it's the priciest of the three. Still, with management expecting pro forma revenue growth (which leaves out businesses it sold) over 60% this fiscal year, I'd call that valuation reasonable.

It has been revealed that dell's AI server orders are arriving faster than Dell is shipping them. In its fiscal second quarter of 2027 (which ended July 31, 2026), the organization booked a record $60.9 billion in AI server orders, almost four times the $16.4 billion in AI server revenue it recorded. It closed the period with a record $95 billion AI server backlog.

Total revenue rose 58% year over year to $47.0 billion. And with management guiding to non-GAAP (adjusted) earnings per share of $25.50 this fiscal year, the stock trades at around 13 times those earnings.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

But profits have outpaced cash. Dell's operating cash flow dropped 13% year over year to $2.2 billion in fiscal Q2, even as net income more than tripled to $4.1 billion. A significant reason is financing receivables (money customers owe Dell under financing deals), which cut operating cash flow by around $6.7 billion in the quarter. The fiscal third-quarter report on Tuesday, Nov. 24, might show whether more of that cash is starting to come in.

All three stocks might continue to slide together if AI spending cools. But each tech giant is growing fast, and I think each trades at a valuation that leaves room for that growth to reach shareholders by 2030.

I'd start with Nvidia, the cheapest of the three relative to its expected earnings and the one whose data center growth accelerated last quarter. Coherent would be next, with its multiyear PhotonLink deal. Dell is worth a look too, but I'd keep the position smaller until its cash flow catches up to its profits.

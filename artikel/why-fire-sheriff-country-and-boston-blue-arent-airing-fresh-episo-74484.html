Friday night looks a little different on CBS with Fire Country, Sheriff Country and Boston Blue not airing new episodes after a schedule change.

Instead of audiences getting latest episodes of the scripted shows, CBS will be airing college basketball on Friday, November 20. Kentucky is going up against Indiana and the game at Lucas Oil Stadium will take over the usual time slots for Sheriff Country and Fire Country.

Industry observers note that boston Blue is also not releasing newly introduced content but a rerun of a Sheriff Country episode will air during the Blue Bloods spinoff’s usual time. New episodes aren’t scheduled until December 11 — following Thanksgiving — before a proper hiatus over the holidays. (Related: Industry News: Distributed Training: Train BART/T5 for ...)

Sources indicate that before going on break, Boston Blue went through a second surprise recasting when news broke that Amaury Nolasco has been cast as Arturo Muñoz in an upcoming episode that will reintroduce the former cartel leader — who is also the biological father of Baez’s (Marisa Ramirez) adopted daughter, Elena. He was previously portrayed by Juan Francisco Villa in two episodes of Blue Bloods before the Boston Blue shakeup.

Boson Blue has recast in the past when Danny’s (Donnie Wahlberg) son Sean went from being played by Andrew Terraciano to Mika Amonsen.

“It was a difficult decision — certainly for me — and it’s one of the decisions that made me uncertain about doing the show. It’s like, ‘How do we do this with a different [actor]?'” Wahlberg, 57, admitted to Us in February. “I have to say, Mika has the same level of grace and class that Andrew indicated. Mika has great respect for Andrew. He stepped into the role very mindfully and carefully and he took his time. He was very thoughtful about how he went about playing this character.”. Terraciano, 23, also broke his silence on the onscreen swap.

Recent findings suggest that “It’s beautiful because you’ll always remember every single line and every day that you had the opportunity to be that character. It doesn’t mean for even a second that somebody else can’t go and do something beautiful with it,” he shared with Us in December 2025. “I got the joy of playing Sean, and I got to send him off into the world. Now somebody else gets that joy. We’re both getting to show what we do and then send Sean off into the world.”.

Amonsen, 24, also spoke with Us about how the transition has since gotten easier.

Industry observers note that “That initial pressure from fans and making sure that I represented Sean in a way that Andrew would appreciate, fans would appreciate and have them understand that I wasn’t just coming into this creating whatever I wanted [was hard],” Amonsen exclusively told Us in March. “It’s been reciprocated well and I’m happy about that. But I think that that was the biggest challenge. At this point, I’ve slipped into comfortability.”.

The thriller is one of the most timeless and popular genres in cinema. Who doesn't love to be shocked and challenged by tales of danger, betrayal, deceit, and murder? Indeed, many of the all-time greatest movies belong to this riveting genre. A great thriller can keep you on the edge of your seat, second-guessing every move, every line, until the very end, when a cathartic reveal makes everything worthwhile.

However, on the opposite end of the spectrum, the most unwatchable thrillers are lame, uninteresting, and ultimately unworthy of your time. Whether because of their silly stories, misguided performances, bad direction, or a combination of multiple factors, these thrillers fail to impress and instead settle for mediocrity. The entries on this list are the most unworthy thrillers released this century, and while some might not rank among cinema's all-time worst, they are certainly not worth watching either. Industry observers note that basic Instinct 2 is a sequel to, you guessed it, Basic Instinct, and sees Sharon Stone reprising her role as the murderous temptress Catherine Trammell. This time, she's in London and comes under Scotland Yard's suspicion when her latest boyfriend dies in a car crash. Court-appointed psychiatrist Michael Glass (David Morrisey) treats her for "risk addiction" (lol) and becomes trapped in her web of seduction and danger.

Industry observers note that the original Basic Instinct isn't exactly great cinema, but it has value, not only as a solidly crafted erotic thriller, but also as a sordid noir powered by a terrific Stone. This baffling sequel has no redeeming quality, and saying so is no exaggeration. The plot is weak to the point of ridicule, and the acting isn't much better. Morrisey and Stone are usually great actors, but here, they're unconvincing at best and genuinely bad at worst. It's not like the script is giving them anything to work with, but they are also doing next to nothing to sell, much less elevate, what's there, delivering each line with all the subtlety of a chainsaw. Industry observers note that heather Graham and Joseph Fiennes star in Killing Me Softly, one of the worst erotic thrillers this century. The film follows Alice (Graham), an American woman who leaves her boyfriend for Adam (Fiennes), a mysterious mountain climber. After a whirlwind romance that ends in a quick marriage, Alice begins suspecting Adam, questioning his elusive past and the fates of his previous lovers.

Killing Me Softly is director Chen Kaige's only English-language film, and it's no wonder why. Based on the eponymous 1999 book, this cheap effort is the erotic thriller at its laziest and most salacious. Even worse, it's not even entertaining in a so-bad-it's-good kind of way — in fact, it's boring, and so nonsensical that one can't help but roll one's eyes once the "mysteries" start taking shape. Hollywood has never known exactly what to do with Graham and Fiennes, but the truth is, they are simply too talented to be stuck in such mediocre thrillers.

John Travolta steps into the shoes of gangster John Gotti in a biopic that might just be as infamous as its real-life subject. Appropriately titled Gotti, the film chronicles decades in the mobster's life, including his rise as the head of the Gambino crime family, his many legal troubles, and his contentious relationships with his wife, Victoria (Kelly Preston), and son, John Gotti Jr. (Spencer Lofranco).

In a recent development, gotti has been ridiculed enough, and it's not hard to see why. It's the typical movie that sees itself as "serious" and "heavy"; alas, the only thing that's "heavy" here is the mounting prosthetics on Travolta's face. Director Kevin Connolly is hilariously out of his depth here, struggling to maintain the barest sense of momentum and drowning the film in a dull pacing only livened by moments of unintentional ridicule. For some reason, Travolta and the script try hard to glorify Gotti, and the result is a movie that is outright problematic. Chances are you won't make it past the first thirty minutes, so this puzzling biopic truly amounts to the cinematic equivalent of a whisper into the void.

It has been revealed that directed by Daniel Farrands and starring Hilary Duff (who should really know better), The Haunting of Sharon Tate is as offensive and terrible as the offensively terrible title implies. The plot follows Tate (Duff) in the days leading up to her murder as she has visions of the attack she'll suffer at the hands of the Manson Family. The film also stars the once-Aaron Samuels, Jonathan Bennett.

This borderline insulting movie is what happens when people sacrifice their decency for a check. Indeed, The Haunting of Sharon Tate is outright offensive, an insult to one of Hollywood's biggest victims that exploits the very real, very tragic circumstances of Tate's death in service of a cheap movie that shouldn't even exist. The acting is bad, the screenplay ridiculous, the directing clumsy, and the "twist" ending is the stuff of cinematic nightmares. Sloppily made and in offensively poor taste, few movies are as morally bankrupt and utterly contemptible as this one.

Let's end with a true cinematic dud, shall we? The Last Days of American Crime stars an ensemble cast led by Édgar Ramírez and is set in a near-future United States, where the government will soon launch a "synaptic blocker" that will prevent people from willingly committing crimes. A group of thieves races against the clock to pull off one last, ambitious heist before the signal activates. The film is based on the eponymous 2009 comic.

It has been revealed that the premise is intriguing, but sadly, the execution is truly terrible. The Last Days of American Crime is among the worst movies of the 21st century; there really is no other way to put it. At a whopping 149 minutes, the movie is so dull it's unbearable, offering violence for violence's sake and ultimately amounting to a hollow, cheap, and shameful experience. It was also a victim of a woefully bad release date, coming out at the height of the 2020 police protests, a fact that made it seem all the more out of touch and tone-deaf.

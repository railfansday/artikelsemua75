Sources indicate that it’s spanned the lifecycle of countless gaming consoles, survived multiple scandals, and even withstood a Congressional investigation. The Grand Theft Auto franchise is seemingly unkillable. Not even a more-than-a-decade wait for the latest installment, Grand Theft Auto VI, could dull the rabid hunger the global gaming community has for the series of open world crime games.

Yet, the massive franchise was almost over before it began. The original Grand Theft Auto, it turns out, was on the proverbial chopping block in the middle of its development. It was only through a few fateful glitches that developers saw what they really had on their hands. (Related: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Faster Language M...)

“It was awful,” former creative director Gary Penn recalled in an interview quoted by Game Informer. “It never really felt like it was going anywhere.” At the time, in 1994, the game wasn’t even called Grand Theft Auto,—it went by the much tamer moniker Race’n’Chase. Its developers, now known as Rockstar North, were then a small Scottish studio called DMA Design Ltd. They were best known at the time for a cute, family-friendly (if mildly macabre) PC game called Lemmings.

Sources indicate that “The aim of Race’n’Chase,” read a March 1995 design document, “...is to produce a fun, addictive and fast multi-player car racing and crashing game which uses a novel graphics method.” The problem was, the game they were working on was neither fun nor addictive. The car crashes, for one, were a nightmare. “They just didn’t work,” programmer Mike Dailly told The Guardian. “The code was like spaghetti junction.”.

It has been revealed that then there was the matter of the game’s central premise. Originally, Race’n’Chase was meant to be a top-down game where you could play as either the criminals or the police in pursuit, but the developers found that the latter element needed to be excised “’cause nobody wanted to play the cops.”.

Industry observers note that even worse, as the developers tried to deal these design challenges, they were missing key deadlines, and their counterparts in America were getting impatient. “Every week they wanted to kill this game,” recalled level designer Paul Farley in a video for The Guardian. “We’d have to kind of argue to keep it going.”. (Related: Industry News: Designing Azure silicon on Azure silicon...)

Recent findings suggest that then something broke. And it was the best thing that ever happened to them.

The game had refocused around the criminal element, with the police now pursuing the player in a manner inspired by, of all things, the ghosts in Pac-Man. “Then one day,” Penn recounted, “I think it was a bug, the police suddenly became mental and aggressive. Everybody suddenly went, ‘Hey this is actually pretty cool. There’s something in this, this is working.’”.

In a recent development, that malfunction switched things from Pac-Man ghosts to maniac cops—and recontextualized the entire game in the developers’ minds. “It was less about the mission stuff, which we always thought was another mess, and more about just general play—just being able to p**s around.”.

In a recent development, the developers leaned into the freedom of the game’s sprawling digital city, focusing less on what players could accomplish and more on what they could get away with. In that moment, Race’n’Chase became Grand Theft Auto in all but name. Team members went home at night to watch American action fare like Speed and The A-Team, then came back the next day to pitch scenes from them as missions players could pick up at Liberty City’s trademark phone booths. Most importantly, they started seeing bugs not as roadblocks, but as launching pads.

Later in development, another glitch caused groups of NPC pedestrians to “start following one person around,” Farley recalled, much like the marching creatures in DMA’s previous hit, Lemmings. “We thought: ‘Can we use this? What sort of people walk around in a long line like that?’ And we thought of Hare Krishnas.” Rather than do some drastic rewrite of the code, they just tweaked the look of the pedestrians’ sprites, and got the game out to the public.

Recent findings suggest that the game was famously met with controversy after its release because of its violent content, but that uproar actually played into the game’s marketing, and it became a massive financial success for the small Scottish studio. Grand Theft Auto has gone on to become one of the best-selling video game franchises in history, a title sure to be cemented with the release of the notably “sick day inducing” Grand Theft Auto VI.

And none of it would have happened without a single, fateful glitch.

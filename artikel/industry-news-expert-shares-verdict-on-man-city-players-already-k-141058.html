Manchester City's recent struggles are highly unlikely to have come as a result of the noise surrounding their financial charges.

It has been suggested that the players "already know" they will face a points deduction or potential relegation, leading to a downturn in their recent performances.

This comes as their appeal is ongoing against the Premier League's decision that they are guilty of 114 financial charges, with a punishment then to be decided.

However, former Man United coach Mick Brown told Football Insider that Man City's players are unlikely to be considering the charges and cannot use them as an excuse.

Instead, they have been urged to look inwardly, with Enzo Maresca's tactics and the players' complacency to blame for their recent performances on the pitch.

Man City have now failed to win any of their last four Premier League games, and a 1-1 draw against Fulham have sparked concerns about the players and the manager.

Brown told Football Insider on 2 November that Man City's players could be distracted by the ongoing speculation about their punishment, with Maresca growing concerned.

However, that is unlikely to be the biggest cause for their recent form, with the players unable to control the outcome of the club's appeal despite the noise surrounding it.

Erling Haaland and his teammates have insisted publicly that they are just getting on with their jobs, and a recent dip in form is unlikely to be due to the club's problems.

"It's a bit ridiculous to blame the charges for why Man City have struggled in recent games," Brown told Football Insider.

"I don't put any stock in this idea that the players 'already know' the outcome and have downed tools because of that, it's a really poor excuse to try and use.

"There are a lot of issues in this squad, the players look complacent and a bit like 'you can't beat us', when the results are proving otherwise because their form has been shocking.

"The manager has got things wrong, I'm not convinced by the way he sets up his team, and a lot of their play is passive and slow which doesn't exactly inspire players.

"But any suggestions that it is down to the charges is just lazy, if they want somebody to blame, they're better off looking inwardly at the way the players have been performing.".

Since Man City were officially found guilty of 114 financial charges, speculation has been rife about their potential punishment and when it will be delivered.

It has been suggested that they could face immediate relegation, hefty fines, a transfer embargo, or any manner of punishments, despite their appeal still being underway.

Football Insider revealed last month that Man City are increasingly likely to get a single-season points deduction of 40-50 points should their appeal against the ruling fail.

Sources say the Premier League are very keen to get the appeal wrapped up months before the 2026-27 campaign ends, but are facing concerns in that respect.

Should City's guilty verdict on most of the charges remain, then a separate hearing would determine sanctions and work is ongoing to determine what those would be.

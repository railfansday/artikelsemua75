According to recent reports, there’s a point when a smoky eye goes from elegant with a slight edge to worn in and a bit reckless...in a good way. As the antithesis of the clean girl aesthetic and all its minimalist, nude, no-color chicness, the indie sleaze, grungy makeup look of the ’90s and early 2000s has returned, but with a few updates. Makeup artist and founder of KJH.brand, Katie Jane Hughes, describes how this 2.0 take differs from its first iterations, why it’s making a comeback, and exactly what you need to create your version of the gritty, slightly imperfect look.

Clean, barely there beauty has dominated runways and social feeds in the past few years, but as we reach peak maxxing in other areas, it looks like makeup is finally following suit. “I think people are craving fun again. And they’ve begun seeing it on the likes of Gabbriette, in recent Gucci fashion reveals, or in makeup done by Sam Visser and other makeup artists like Yasmin Istanbouli,” Hughes tells ELLE when asked why the look is seeing a revival right now. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

Recent findings suggest that grunge, in both beauty and fashion, has always leaned a little dark and dirty, but Hughes says the look is now paired with more elegant and chic details. “[It’s] more purposeful and less disheveled outfits paired with it,” she notes. The fully loaded emo energy is being replaced by bursts of color like the hot pink outline seen on Alex Consani during Gucci’s SS27 The Store Show—under the guidance of Sam Visser.

The slept-in grittiness of grunge makeup looks more involved to create intentionally, but you actually only need a few products. Hughes suggests starting with a good black eye crayon like the KJH.brand Precision Sculpture Stylo in Forge. “It’s helpful to have an eyeliner that’s not too pointed and precise,” she advises. Jet-black eyeshadow: “I really love the Plume eyeshadows from VOILETTE_FR in black shade. It really lets you stretch color substantial and wide and offers the blendability of a cream with the buildability of a powder,” she adds. She may also top the lids in her version with something greasy like the KJH.brand Hyper Shine Serum.

According to recent reports, if you’re trying the look for the first time, Hughes says to keep it simple. “Start with one product, like the Stylo. Smudge around the lash line, blend, and you’re done,” Hughes instructs. As for dos and don’ts, Hughes says, do put some concealer on a small brush, prime it in, and clean up any mistakes. Don’t pair indie sleaze with a bold lip. “Keeping the rest of the makeup nondescript or barely there makes it look cooler,” Hughes notes.

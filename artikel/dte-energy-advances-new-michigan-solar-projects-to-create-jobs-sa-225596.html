DTE Energy has confirmed aims to add up to 150 megawatts of new solar energy in Michigan, creating construction jobs, supporting local communities, and saving customers money on fuel costs. (Related: AI for Game Development: Creating a Farming Game in 5 D...)

Because solar generation does not require fuel, the newly introduced projects will help reduce the manufacturer’s exposure to changing fuel prices over their operating lives. The projects will also support DTE’s work to provide customers with energy that is reliable, increasingly clean, and affordable. (Related: 3D Asset Generation: AI for Game Development #3...)

In a recent development, “These projects are an investment in Michigan and the communities we’re proud to serve. By producing more Michigan-made energy that doesn’t require us to purchase fuel, we can help lower energy costs for our customers and reduce our reliance on fuel from outside our state. At the same time, these projects will create good jobs and bring new investment and tax revenue into local communities, providing more resources for schools, first responders and the services Michigan families count on every day.”.

Industry observers note that DTE is seeking competitive proposals for up to 150 megawatts of upcoming Michigan-based solar generation. The procurement supports the requirements of the settlement agreement in Michigan Public Service Commission Case No. U-21662, approved May 15, 2025.

It has been revealed that projects must achieve commercial operation by June 30, 2030, be located in Michigan and connect to either the MISO transmission system or DTE’s distribution system. Interested bidders can register and obtain additional information through PowerAdvocate. Proposals are due Jan. 19, 2027, and DTE expects to execute contracts in the second or third quarter of 2027.

Sources indicate that DTE is Michigan’s leading producer of and investor in renewable energy, generating enough clean energy from wind and solar to power nearly 1 million homes. As the company works toward net zero carbon emissions, it remains committed to growing its renewable energy portfolio while maintaining a balanced mix of energy resources that supports reliability and affordability for customers. DTE’s 2026 Integrated Resource Plan builds on that commitment, calling for an additional 15 gigawatts of renewable energy by 2042.

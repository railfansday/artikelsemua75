I assume that the future expense numbers are in TODAY'S dollars and could increase in nominal terms based on inflation.With $200K in expenses per year + a $100K pension with a 2% COLA, that means that your portfolio needs to generate around $100K in today's dollars.You could always create a TIPS ladder (https://www.bogleheads.org/wiki/Treasur ... d_Security) that will generate the required income for you for the next 12 years. According to TIPSladder.com, you would need approximately $1.5M $1M* to create such a ladder. Then the remaining money could be allocated to growth, and can be tapped into as a buffer if needed.* - EDITED to correct the math. When I put the numbers into TIPSladder.com, I used the default 20 year ladder. You only need a 12 year ladder. Sorry.There are a couple of potential landmines to be aware of:1. Taxes: You don't say whether your expenses include taxes. However, you may need to account for that somehow in your planning so you don't fall short.2. Your personal inflation outpacing your pension and SS COLA. While this is a bigger risk for your pension, it is still a risk for your Social Security payments. Yes, SS is adjusted based on inflation, but the inflation rate of an older adult is usually higher, and this is usually driven by health care costs. Also, you are in a unique position where you will be paying college tuition for a child in retirement, and college tuition inflation might also outpace your pension COLA.3. Child's health care. Once you go on Medicare, you might need to factor in health care for your child. Not sure if you included this in your expense calculation.4. Long term care. What's your initiative if one or both of you need to go into a nursing home?Finally, one potential tip for you.It looks like your child will be under 18 when you are eligible to claim SS. If you were to claim while your child is under 18, your child would eligible for a child benefit based on the record of the claimant, increasing your overall SS amount. Likewise, if you claim while your child is under 16, the non-claiming spouse would be eligible for a child-in-care spousal benefit. A child-in-case spousal benefit can be very powerful as you can claim it without having to file for your own benefit. This is an exception to the deemed filing rule where you are required to file for your own benefit when filing for a spousal benefit. Thus, the non-claiming spouse can get this benefit while allowing their own benefit to grow.If you haven't done so already, I would strongly suggest that take this into account when considering when to file, as it could tip the scales towards filing earlier for one of you. You can run the numbers by using the free tool at opensocialsecurity.com, as it allows you to model these scenarios.Taxes are indeed not included either on withdrawals from tIRAs or spending or brokerage.We've structured things to have a low personal inflation rate and college will be a contribution number instead of we pay whatever it costs (at least that's what I'm saying to the DW - we'll see).Child's healthcare is include in spending both before and after medicareLTC is really a self-insuring combination approach: 1) Appreciated portfolio, 2) Offset in-home care with offering extra room(s) in house, 3) Sell appreciated rental, 4) Sell appreciated primary. Assuming LTC doesn't start until 80 (which is a good bet in our families), we should have all of these strategies available and several years of lower spending prior including two mortgages dropping off.Statistics: Posted by realistdreamer — Sat Nov 21, 2026 3:36 pm.

Roy Keane was left furious following Manchester United's heavy defeat against Liverpool and made his anger very clear.

The former Red Devils midfielder, and no-nonsense pundit, savaged the current crop of players as well as his fellow pundit Gary Neville in the wake of the 4-0 defeat.

Mauricio Pochettino's side fell 2-0 down just 13 minutes into the game, after Dominik Szoboszlai and Florian Wirtz found the net following an early red card for Leny Yoro.

Rio Ngumoha and Victor Munoz doubled the scoreline in the second half, and Liverpool could have had more, having created countless chances to put their rivals away.

There were echoes of the 7-0 win Liverpool recorded against Man United in 2023, and yet, the fans applauded the players off the pitch and pundits have begun making excuses.

Keane, however, was not in a mood to tolerate excuses and he demanded the players take responsibility and the pundits, including Gary Neville, assess their priorities.

Yoro's red card is largely being pinned as the reason for Man United's defeat, having ripped up any plans they had heading into what was always set to be a tough game.

However, Keane has insisted that cannot be the only reason for the defeat, as he questioned how teams have coped in the past after going down to ten men early in the game.

While the 4-0 scoreline did flatter Man United, that should be treated as a major concern rather than a positive to take from the game, and Keane has made his feelings clear.

Speaking live on Sky Sports, he ranted: "If I hear that one more time from the lads on commentary and in the studio, '4-0 is not too bad, we were down to ten men', has a team never got a result before with ten men?

"I thought they were so bad. Neville, who's played for Man United hundreds of times, saying he's okay with 4-0? Are you telling me they couldn't have done better?

"At least show a bit more fight, a bit more quality. I can't believe what I'm hearing from people who've played for the club. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

In a recent development, "The fans are applauding the players. Applauding what? Applauding rubbish.

"This team, two years ago, finished 15th and the last year or two we're blaming the board, blaming ownership, blaming managers, when do the players take responsibility?

"Liverpool probably should've scored eight or nine, and everyone's coming off the pitch saying 'four's not too bad', I feel like swearing.".

Despite the result and Keane's rant, there can be no doubt that the biggest talking point will centre around Yoro's red card and the impact it had on the game.

While it should not be used as an excuse for their dismal performance, it certainly weakened the side, and Liverpool took full advantage in the minutes that followed.

However, former PGMOL chief Keith Hackett told Football Insider that Man United may have reason to complain after analysing footage of Yoro's dismissal.

While referee Sam Barrott made a quick decision to brandish a red card for denying a goalscoring opportunity, replays suggested it may not have met all of the criteria.

Questions have been asked about whether Alexander Isak would have got to the ball, and as a result, the subjective decision is bound to cause debates.

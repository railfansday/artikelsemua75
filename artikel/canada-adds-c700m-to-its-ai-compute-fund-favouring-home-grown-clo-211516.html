Canada adds C$700M to its AI compute fund, favouring home-grown clouds

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, canada is adding C$700M to its AI Compute Access Fund, taking it to C$1B, with bigger subsidies for companies that use Canadian-owned computing. A first new call targets health and life sciences, and 45 more firms are getting C$52M from the fund’s first phase.

It has been revealed that canada is adding C$700M to a fund that helps smaller companies pay for the computing power behind AI, the government said on Thursday. That takes the AI Compute Access Fund to C$1B, up from C$300M. (Related: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Faster Language M...)

The fund helps pay for the cloud computing firms need to build and run AI products. In its latest phase, it pays more when they use computing that is owned and run in Canada. The aim is to create demand for local providers. (Related: My Journey to a serverless transformers pipeline on Goo...)

“Canadian ideas need Canadian computing power,” noted Evan Solomon, Canada’s minister of artificial intelligence and digital innovation.

The first new funding round is open to health and life sciences projects. Solomon also unveiled more than C$52M for 45 companies from the fund’s first phase, bringing its total to over C$118M for 89 firms. Last month, Ottawa also put C$2.5M into helping 25 Prairie firms adopt AI.

Recent findings suggest that the money comes from Canada’s national AI strategy, unveiled in June. Ottawa has also pledged to bring 500 megawatts of its own AI computing online.

Sources indicate that in October, it backed Cohere’s C$7bn funding round. Foreign firms are interested too: OpenAI has said Canada has the energy and land for data centres.

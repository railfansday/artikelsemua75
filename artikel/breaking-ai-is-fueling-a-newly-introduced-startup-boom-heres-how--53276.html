Just as the Industrial Revolution transformed business through machines, mass production and long-distance transportation, AI may be powering a upcoming entrepreneurial boom.

Industry observers note that we’ve seen this before: Fresh technology dramatically lowers the cost of starting and running a business. But where the steam engine and electricity offered only a first step, still requiring substantial capital for machinery, factories and workers, AI offers modern entrepreneurs a full staircase. Today, an aspiring founder with a good idea can access powerful capabilities with little more than a computer and a Wi-Fi connection. Business creation has surged in 2026, with nearly every month setting a record for latest registrations compared with the same month in previous years. Since January, 5.9 million new businesses have been formed nationwide — up 16% from the same period in 2025 and 24% from 2024.

Recent findings suggest that what’s driving that growth? It’s not a single engine but a combination of forces, including the continued popularity of remote work and the appeal of being your own boss and having more financial freedom, both now more within reach because of AI. Sources indicate that if you previously dropped a business idea because it seemed too complicated or expensive, now may be the time to reconsider. AI has reached a level of capability that didn’t exist just a few years ago. It’s steadily eroding traditional startup barriers, helping founders turn even ambitious ideas into real businesses with less money, fewer employees and fewer outside resources.

According to recent reports, until recently, launching a company often meant spending money before making money. Office space, equipment, expensive software and employees with specialized skills were requirements in most sectors, not to mention the accountants and consultants needed just to get started.

Recent findings suggest that now, cloud infrastructure has replaced costly servers. No-code platforms make it possible to build websites and apps without extensive programming knowledge. E-commerce platforms give small companies access to national and global markets. Generative AI accelerates this trend by helping entrepreneurs research markets, analyze competitors, draft business plans, develop marketing campaigns, create content and automate tedious administrative work.

Start by asking yourself, “What do I need to reach customers at this stage, and how will it maximize my return on investment?” Use inexpensive tools to create a product prototype, a landing page or a basic marketing campaign. Talk to potential customers. Test your pricing and measure the response. Save major investments for ideas that show real demand.

AI gives founders a chance to rethink hiring. Entrepreneurs once had to hire employees or contractors before they were generating enough revenue to support them. Today, a solo founder with the right AI tools can handle many of those functions alone.

In a recent development, that doesn’t mean putting complete trust in a chatbot. AI-assisted work still requires human oversight, judgment, verification and refinement. And when you’re building a trustworthy brand from scratch, you have to consider accuracy, privacy and legal concerns.

Use AI to draft routine marketing emails, but hire an experienced marketer when strategy matters. Organize basic finances with bookkeeping software, but consult an accountant for notable tax decisions. Automate answers to repetitive customer service questions, but keep a human in the loop for complicated interactions or when customers ask for one. The goal isn’t to eliminate employees. It’s to delay fixed expenses until your business can support them.

Sources indicate that historically, founders moved to places like San Francisco or Austin because those cities offered concentrated pools of money and talent. Those hubs remain vibrant, but AI and remote technology are reducing the importance of location.

A tech founder in a small town can use the same AI platforms and cloud services as someone in Silicon Valley. Remote collaboration provides access to talent anywhere, and e-commerce can reach customers far beyond a organization’s hometown.

Sources indicate that with AI tools, you can explore many ideas from almost anywhere. Instead of asking, “Where does my business need to be based?” you can ask, “Where would I like to live while I grow my business?” That kind of freedom can make a real difference in your quality of life.

Forming a business and building a successful one are different things. Many of the companies registered this year will never become fully operational. Others will struggle to find customers or stay profitable. AI can’t fix a weak business model, create demand where none exists or eliminate the financial risks of entrepreneurship.

What AI can do is make the process easier and cheaper, and therein lies the lesson. Instead of investing heavily up front in one untested concept, make a series of small bets. Develop a minimum viable product. Test marketing messages. Experiment with prices. Ask AI to identify the assumptions in your business initiative, then test them with real customers and real-world data.

With more than 5 million businesses formed since the start of 2026, the U.S. could finish the year with close to 7 million new registrations if the current pace continues. There’s evidence that AI may be contributing to that momentum. But like the steam engine, electricity, the internet and smartphones before it, AI is a tool. The real power lies with entrepreneurs who are willing to start lean, test early, automate selectively and spend strategically. With that approach, we may see a boom not only in startups with potential but, more importantly, in businesses that endure.

Sources indicate that A Bundesliga battle between two teams desperate to snap poor runs of form ended in favor of the Bavarian Swabian hosts. Two matchdays into the current Bundesliga campaign, Manuel Baum’s Augsburg were celebrating their status as the top team in the table. Unfortunately, Baum’s Fuggerstädter then proceeded to go seven league fixtures without a win. That streak is now history following a 3-1 home win over Mainz.

Industry observers note that the hosts entered Sunday’s encounter hovering just above the relegation zone, three places below Urs Fischer’s Mainz. Fischer’s team nevertheless only possessed one point more and counted as one of the biggest disappointments of the German top flight. The Rheinhessen traveled to Bavaria on a four-match losing streak, including a loss to 2. Bundesliga outfit Bochum that dumped them out of the Pokal in the second round. Baum, already dealing with a banged-up defensive corps, had to contend with more bad news this week in the form of injuries to central defenders Chrislain Matsima and Jeffrey Gouweleeuw. Matsima’s absence forced the FCA trainer to hand 18-year-old prospect Tim Schnitzer his Bundesliga starting XI debut. Captain Gouweleeuw, meanwhile, could luckily enough be replaced by Manchester City loanee Juma Bah, who had returned from suspension. Schnitzer and Bah were among five changes to the starting XI Baum employed in the recent 5-2 away defeat to RB Leipzig. Marius Wolf, Michael Gregoritsch, and Alexis Claude-Maurice started over Han-Noah Massengo, Arijon Ibrahimovic, and Rodrigo Ribeiro.

Sources indicate that fischer too needed to pull the trigger on several changes. Though the Mainz trainer declared himself largely satisfied with the performance of his team in a narrow loss against Bayern Munich just prior to the international break, four latest outfielders took to the pitch for the Rheinhessen. Fischer, having several of his preferred starters back, sent Phillip Tietz, Phillipp Mwene, Paul Nebel, and Stefan Posch onto the field in place of Ransford Königsdörffer, Silvan Widmer, Jae-Sung Lee, and Fabio Gruber. Posch returned from suspension after getting sent off in a disastrous encounter against Elversberg that saw Fischer’s charges dispatched 4-1.

Recent findings suggest that baum’s changes certainly produced attractive football in the opening stages. Wolf’s placement as the right wingback enabled the reliably versatile Robin Fellhauer to slide inside and work in the packed midfield alongside Fabian Rieder, Claude-Maurice, and Anton Kade. This particular FCA foursome strode forward with reckless abandon, mostly keeping Mainz pinned back in their own half throughout the entire first quarter-of-an-hour. Sharp combos involving the midfield and the wingbacks produced plenty of incursions into the FSV penalty area. Kade unfortunately couldn’t find the right angle on the best of the opportunities to score, shooting well wide from only six meters out in the 12th minute.

Industry observers note that the opener finally came on Augsburg’s second corner of the afternoon. A quality free-kick from Claude-Maurice was actually shepherded out into touch deftly by former FCA striker Phillip Tietz in the 19th minute. Newly minted FCA record holder Gregoritsch was able to convert the subsequent set-piece for the 1-0. Makeshift Augsburg defender Yannik Keitel did an excellent job beating Mainz’s Kaishu Sano in an aerial duel after Rieder swung in the corner from the right. Keitel’s header was initially saved by Robin Zentner, who parried the ball toward the far post. The rebound fell perfectly for the onrushing Gregoritsch, who easily slid in his sixth Bundesliga goal of the campaign. As Baum and his crew knew full well, however, an early lead meant little. It has been revealed that in their ongoing seven-game winless run, Augsburg had actually squandered the lead in six of them. On five occasions, Baum’s team scored the opening goal of the match. Some five minutes after Gregoritsch opened the scoring, it appeared as if another lead was almost certain to evaporate. Somehow, one of Mainz’s most dependable scorers spurned a chance to equalize. Tietz found himself free in front of goal in the 24th off a blitz counter, but blasted the ball well over the bar in front of his former FanKurve. Apart from that chance, the Bavarian Swabian hosts did still remain the more active side.

According to recent reports, the 2-0 finally came off yet another corner from the right in the 36th minute. This time, fellow fresh starter Bah would bundle home another Rieder corner when Mainz couldn’t get the service from the right clear. Amid all the jostling prior to Bah bundling the ball home, the second goal withstood VAR scrutiny. Regrettably, the rest of the half would fall prey to officiating controversies. A sequence of events shortly after the second goal rapidly led to match official Martin Petersen losing control of the match.

Recent findings suggest that augsburg thought they had scored the 3-0 in the 41st minute when Keitel slotted home off a quick throw-in. Petersen nevertheless ruled that FCA defender Bah had fouled Mainz counterpart Kacper Potulski in the lead-up. Petersen showed restraint by not booking the crowd of Augsburg players who stood before him to protest his decision, but offered head coach Baum no such quarter when he kept Nadiem Amiri from executing a throw-in in the 43rd. Baum kept the ball himself whilst protesting to Petersen. The FCA trainer was promptly ejected from proceedings on a straight red.

Recent findings suggest that petersen found himself at the center of more protests from players before it was time to head into the tunnel. The hosts were not pleased when the referee declined to whistle for a shove by Posch on Claude-Maurice just outside the box during first-half injury time. Prior to that, Petersen was compelled by VAR to take another look at a Fellhauer foul on Sano in the 44th. Petersen initially only awarded a yellow card despite the fact that Fellhauer caught Sano on the knee with his swinging boot. Petersen stood by his decision and was surely pleased to retreat to the tunnel after a heated, scrappy, and contentious end to the half.

To the relief of most observers, the focus returned to football after the change of ends. There would be no controversy attached to either of the goals scored in the opening five minutes of the second half. The hosts added their third goal shortly after play resumed. A free-kick delivery from the right was initially cleared before Behrens headed the ball back into the penalty area. Wolf helped it onward, and Posch inadvertently glanced the ball into Gregoritsch’s path in the 48th minute. The Austrian striker stroked home the 3-0. Fischer’s team responded by pulling a goal back promptly, with halftime sub Königsdörffer polishing off a nice distance effort on a quick counter three minutes later.

In a recent development, königsdörffer’s 3-1 worked wonders for the carnival club’s confidence, and Fischer’s men undeniably looked more assured heading forward for the duration of proceedings. Mainz tightened up the possession statistics and looked more inspired when forging ahead. The more optically pleasing football from Mainz nevertheless didn’t end up producing another goal or even another memorable attempt on target. Instead, it was Augsburg who looked more dangerous on the counter. FCA sub Uchenna Ogundu unluckily missed out on the 4-1 with a strike off the inside of the far post in the 81st.

According to recent reports, the Austrian international proved the difference-maker with an exceptionally efficient performance in front of goal. Gregoritsch scored twice from just three shots, putting both of his efforts on target past Robin Zentner and outperforming his 1.36 xG. The 31-year-old wasn’t particularly involved in Augsburg’s build-up play, registering just 26 touches and completing 56 percent of his passes during his 72 minutes on the pitch. Gregoritsch nevertheless demonstrated precisely why Baum continues to place his faith in him. The veteran striker’s opportunistic finishing on both goals helped Augsburg snap their seven-match winless streak and lifted his Bundesliga tally to six goals for the season.

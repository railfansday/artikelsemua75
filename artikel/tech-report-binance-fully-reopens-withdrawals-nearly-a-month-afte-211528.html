Tech Report: Binance fully reopens withdrawals nearly a month after its $6.3B hack

According to recent reports, binance has lifted the last of the withdrawal limits it put in place after its record $6.3 billion hack in October. The exchange noted on Thursday that its withdrawal recovery work is complete. (Related: The Reformer - Pushing the limits of language modeling...)

“Our withdrawal recovery work is completed and the withdrawal suspension from the October security incident has been fully lifted,” Binance said.

In a recent development, A small number of networks remain paused for maintenance or planned upgrades. Binance said those pauses cover a very small share of normal traffic and are separate from the recovery. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

Industry observers note that binance halted withdrawals after the breach on 29 October and began restoring them on 1 November, starting with Bitcoin. Deposits stayed open throughout, crypto.news reported.

The first day brought a rush. Customers took out $4.65 billion and put in $1.01 billion in the first nine and a half hours on 1 November, the exchange mentioned. That left a net outflow of $3.64 billion. Binance reopened 87 more networks in the week to 14 November.

In a recent development, the exchange has said it covered the stolen assets with more than $6.34 billion of its own money. It drew on its corporate treasury, part of its user protection fund and part of its futures insurance fund. It has pledged to rebuild the user fund to about $1 billion, and published a reserve snapshot covering 198 tokens.

According to recent reports, wallets linked to the hack moved another $35.52 million in Ether on Thursday, according to blockchain tracker Onchain Lens. It put the hackers’ remaining unfrozen holdings at about $1.9 billion.

US authorities and private companies have frozen more than $2.7 billion of the stolen crypto. The Justice Department also won court approval to seize more than $1.2 billion of it, The Block reported on 12 November.

The FBI has blamed North Korean hackers for the theft. Binance said a fake job interview gave the attackers their way in. Days after the hack, the exchange insisted it was in ‘very strong’ health.

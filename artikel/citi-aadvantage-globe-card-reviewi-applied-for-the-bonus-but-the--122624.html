Industry observers note that I applied for the Citi® / AAdvantage® Globe™ Mastercard® [See rates and fees] when it introduced because it was a newly introduced card I wanted to try up close and because it carried an initial bonus I hadn’t had yet. I wound up with two Globe cards, though: the one I applied for, and a second when my Barclays AAdvantage Aviator Silver card became a Globe. (Related: Industry News: Hyperparameter Search with Transformers ...)

According to recent reports, the reason I like having a Globe beyond the initial bonus is its Flight Streak benefit. You can earn up to 15,000 additional Loyalty Points each year for American Airlines flights, without putting spending on the card. I already have the Citi® / AAdvantage BusinessTM World Elite Mastercard® [See rates and fees] and Citi® / AAdvantage® Executive World Legend Mastercard® [See rates and fees] cards for different reasons. Business is the best one for spending towards status, while Executive gives me lounge access and more bonus Loyalty Points. But Globe adds something neither of those provides. (Related: How to generate text: using different decoding methods ...)

It has been revealed that at $350 a year, Citi AAdvantage Globe Card is worth considering both as your main American Airlines benefits card and as an addition to the cards you already have. But you need to value the benefits and having two Globes doesn’t double the Flight Streak bonus.

According to recent reports, the Citi AAdvantage Globe Card awards 5,000 Loyalty Points for every four qualifying American Airlines flight segments, up to three times per status qualification year. Qualifying Segments Flown Total Flight Streak Loyalty Points 4 5,000 8 10,000 12 15,000 These are segments, so a roundtrip with a connection each way is four flights. Three of those trips can generate the full bonus. Six nonstop roundtrips would do it, too. You don’t have to buy the tickets with your Globe card. You can use another card for its rewards or protections, or fly on a ticket your employer purchases, and still earn the bonus on your qualifying travel. The qualification year runs March 1 through the last day of February. Flights must be taken after you become a primary Globe cardmember. Qualifying flights are marketed or operated by American Airlines or American Eagle and ordinarily must earn AAdvantage miles. The terms expressly include American’s basic economy segments. Award flights don’t count. The benefit belongs to the primary cardmember. Adding authorized consumers doesn’t give each person their own Flight Streak bonus. Citi allows 8–10 weeks after qualifying flight segments post for the additional Loyalty Points to appear. (In practice it happens swiftly.) These are points toward status, not another 15,000 miles you can spend on awards. For someone already flying American regularly, this can reduce how much additional spending or other earning they need to do to reach their next status level. There’s no requirement to divert $20,000 or $50,000 of purchases from a more rewarding card. I view the card as an inexpensive way to purchase Loyalty Points which are valuable both for earning status and for additional milestone rewards. Globe And Executive Work Together—Two Globes Don’t Double The Bonus When I analyzed Globe at launch, my criticism was that Citi wasn’t giving customers much reason to keep spending on it. From the customer’s perspective, though, that’s potentially a good thing. I earn the flight bonus while putting spend wherever it makes the most sense. The Executive card by contrast includes 10,000 bonus Loyalty Points at each of 50,000, 90,000, 165,000 and 240,000 Loyalty Points earned in a qualification year. Globe’s Flight Streak bonus can stack with that. Someone meeting all the requirements can earn up to 55,000 bonus Loyalty Points from the two products combined. The final Executive bonus requires reaching 240,000 Loyalty Points, so that maximum isn’t a shortcut to earning Executive Platinum from scratch. My second Citi AAdvantage Globe Card came from the April 2026 Barclays conversion. I initially wondered whether two accounts would generate two sets of flight bonuses. American subsequently confirmed to me that they wouldn’t. The Initial Bonus And Annual Fee The current offer for the Citi AAdvantage Globe Card is 60,000 AAdvantage miles after $4,000 in purchases within the first three months. The annual fee is $350, isn’t waived the first year, and there’s no additional charge for authorized users. There are no foreign transaction fees. The bonus restriction is specific to the Citi AAdvantage Globe Card: you can’t have received a fresh account bonus on Globe within the past 48 months, and there’s also a restriction involving another Citi account with a recent bonus that you converted into Globe. Simply having the Executive or Business card doesn’t disqualify you. What You Earn When You Spend The card’s earning categories are: Eligible Purchases AAdvantage Miles Per Dollar Loyalty Points Per Dollar AAdvantage Hotels bookings 6 1 American Airlines purchases 3 1 Restaurants, including takeout and delivery 2 1 Eligible transit, including taxis, rideshares and public transit 2 1 Other purchases 1 1 Bonus categories increase redeemable miles, not Loyalty Points. A $500 eligible American Airlines purchase earns 1,500 miles and 500 Loyalty Points from the card, in addition to whatever the passenger earns for flying. The 6 miles per dollar on eligible AAdvantage Hotels bookings are separate from the miles and Loyalty Points awarded for the hotel reservation itself. (Reserve Now, Pay Later bookings don’t qualify for the card’s 6-mile earning rate.) For my own spending, Executive already offers 4 miles per dollar on eligible American purchases (and at 4 points per dollar, I actually prefer my Chase Sapphire Reserve® [See rates and fees]) and 12 on eligible AAdvantage Hotels bookings. Globe’s Splurge Credit can give me a reason to charge a particular hotel purchase to it, but the regular earning rate doesn’t. For everyday purchases, one American mile and one Loyalty Point per dollar can make sense when you’re trying to reach a status threshold. Otherwise, there are better returns available. Holding Globe doesn’t obligate you to make it your everyday spending card. It’s a good card to have, not a daily spend driver outside of the quest for status. Four Admirals Club Passes Each Calendar Year Globe comes with four digital Admirals Club passes each calendar year. Each is valid for 24 hours after activation, including visits to multiple Admirals Clubs during that period. The access rules make these especially useful for an occasional traveler with children: Each adult needs a pass. Each adult using a pass can bring up to three children under 18. The primary cardmember must be present, including when using passes for adult companions. You need a same-day eligible American Airlines or oneworld boarding pass. Unused passes expire at the end of the calendar year. A couple traveling with two young children would use two passes for a travel day, rather than four. But you can’t give the passes to relatives traveling without you, and these aren’t Flagship Lounge passes or a full membership with partner lounge privileges. In my experience, the usual capacity restrictions on purchased one-day passes don’t apply to Globe passes. And just because you see a sign saying day passes aren’t being accepted doesn’t mean they aren’t being accepted – sometimes they just don’t take away the sign. American sells ordinary day passes for $79. That doesn’t make four Globe passes worth $316 to everyone. I already have Admirals Club membership through Executive, so I don’t assign these passes additional value for my own access. The Companion Certificate Doesn’t Require Annual Spending After renewing the card, starting in your second cardmembership year, you receive a certificate for one companion to travel on an eligible domestic roundtrip for $99 plus taxes and fees when you buy a qualifying Main Cabin roundtrip. However, this isn’t a certificate for any flight you want. It has fare inventory restrictions and restricted dates. You book through American Airlines Meeting Services, and travel generally must be within the contiguous 48 states on American or American Eagle. Residents of Alaska, Hawaii, Puerto Rico and the U.S. Virgin Islands have exceptions allowing qualifying trips from home to the lower 48. It doesn’t cover basic economy or partner flights. The primary cardmember must travel. The account must remain open for a full billing cycle after the anniversary month, and Citi allows 8–10 weeks for the certificate to become available. The certificate is valid for 12 months from issuance, with booking and travel completed by its expiration. I’ve gotten good savings from Barclays companion certificates, so I don’t dismiss these. If the eligible companion ticket would otherwise cost $500, replacing that with $99 plus taxes can cover much of the annual fee. The Credits Are Worth What They Save You Citi advertises more than $750 in value. I wouldn’t start with that number, but several benefits overlap with what I already have, and others require purchases I wouldn’t otherwise make. Up to $100 annual Splurge Credit. Choose up to two eligible merchants at a time from AAdvantage Hotels, 1stDibs, Future Personal Training and Live Nation. Activate your selection at Citi’s Splurge Credit page before purchasing. This is $100 total (not $100 per merchant). Eligible AAdvantage Hotels bookings are likely the most practical use for many readers. Up to $100 in annual inflight credits. Use the card for eligible purchases on domestic American Airlines or American Eagle flights. Food, drinks and eligible Wi-Fi purchases can qualify. This is less valuable when drinks are already included in your seat or status benefits, or when your flight has free Wi-Fi. Up to $240 annually with Turo. This is up to $30 per completed trip, so it takes eight trips to get the full amount. Link the card at Turo’s Globe page and pay with it directly; digital wallet payments don’t qualify. I wouldn’t assign this much value unless I were already using Turo regularly. Up to $120 for Global Entry or TSA PreCheck every four years. Useful if you need it, but I have plenty of other cards offering this reimbursement. My original reaction to the Splurge Credit was that it wasn’t worth much to me. I already have plenty of hotel credits. And an AAdvantage Hotels booking generally means giving up the hotel points, elite night credit and status benefits you’d receive by booking directly with a chain. I like the inflight credit myself now that American offers more food for purchase on more flights – I get one item free as an Executive Platinum member but I can buy sandwiches and snacks for my family. The calendar-year benefits also improve the first year. Open a card in November, and you can use available credits and passes before year-end and receive fresh allotments in January, before the next annual fee. Flight Streak resets in March. The companion certificate starts after renewal. Bags, Boarding And Other Benefits Of The Citi AAdvantage Globe Card The card provides a first checked bag free on qualifying domestic American Airlines itineraries for the primary cardmember and up to eight companions on the same reservation. Group 5 boarding also covers up to eight companions. You don’t have to pay for the tickets with Globe. Those can be valuable for someone without status, particularly a family traveling together. They add little for me because my existing status already provides better benefits. Your AAdvantage miles also don’t expire while you’re the primary holder of an eligible AAdvantage credit card. There’s also a 10% discount on eligible non-flight components of American Airlines Vacations packages booked through the designated Citi booking page. The travel and purchase protections include trip cancellation and interruption, trip delay, lost or damaged luggage, rental car coverage, extended warranty and purchase protection. Rental coverage is secondary in your country of residence. Citi Entertainment and Mastercard’s World Legend benefits add access to selected ticketing, dining and other experiences. I’ll Pay $350 To Keep The Citi AAdvantage Globe Card If you only want a checked bag and earlier boarding, Citi® / AAdvantage® Platinum Select® World Elite Mastercard® [See rates and fees] is cheaper. If you want regular Admirals Club access, Executive provides a membership for its $695 annual fee. Globe earns its place with the combination of flight bonuses, occasional lounge access and a companion certificate without an annual spending requirement. There’s a case for holding Globe alongside Executive for the additional Loyalty Points. Here’s how I’d look at renewal. If you get a real $100 of savings from the Splurge Credit, there’s $250 of annual fee left to justify through the flight bonus, companion certificate and other benefits. Earning all 15,000 Flight Streak Loyalty Points at that cost works out to about 1.67 cents per point before assigning any value to the remaining benefits. An extra 15,000 that gets you to the next status level is much more useful than points that leave you between thresholds. For someone flying at least 12 qualifying segments and pursuing American status, I think Globe is a strong addition even with another AAdvantage card.

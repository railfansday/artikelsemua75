How Is a Hurricane Deductible Calculated?

The HelloNation article explains that a hurricane deductible often differs from a standard deductible. Instead of a fixed amount, it is usually a percentage of the home's dwelling coverage.

For example, a home with $300,000 in dwelling coverage and a 2% hurricane deductible would have a $6,000 deductible. At 5%, that amount would increase to $15,000.

The article notes that these amounts depend on the insured value, not the repair bill or market price. (Related: Breaking: Future Value Calculator: How to Project Your ...)

How Do Hurricane Deductibles Affect Insurance Claims?

The article explains that homeowners must pay their deductible before insurance covers the remaining eligible damage.

If a hurricane causes $10,000 in covered damage and the deductible is $6,000, the insurer may pay $4,000, subject to policy terms.

The article also notes that Florida has rules governing when hurricane deductibles apply and how covered hurricane losses are handled within one calendar year.

What Should Homeowners Review Before Hurricane Season?

The HelloNation article recommends checking the insurance declarations page for dwelling coverage and deductible details.

Industry observers note that homeowners should also understand that standard homeowners insurance policies generally exclude flooding. Separate flood insurance may be needed for storm surge or rising water.

According to the article, reviewing deductible options can help homeowners compare yearly premiums with possible costs after a storm.

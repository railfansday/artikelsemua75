The film enters a crowded and dynamic period for the indie box office with a weekend of diverse debuts including a standout by Janus Films and Criterion’s exclusive 35mm tour of Paul Thomas Anderson’s Cameron Winter At Carnegie Hall. The concert film grossed $162k at just three locations for a $54k per-screen average, one of the year’s best. Shows were sold out at all theaters weeks in advance, and the 35mm-only meant additional showings couldn’t be added.

The tour continues through January, bringing the film to sixty of the largest historic theaters across the country for limited engagements, dozens of which have been sold out since October when the film premiered at the Upcoming York Film Festival. Theaters are selling exclusive merch at the demonstrates. Up next is the Music Box in Chicago, The Castro in San Francisco, the AFI Silver Theatre outside Washington, D.C., and the TIFF Bell Lightbox.

Recent findings suggest that god With Us, an event release from Variance Films in partnership with Blue Harbor Entertainment, saw a sizable $885k on 432 screens. The gross from writer-director Jimmy Jenkins’ modern day parable came largely without planned Saturday or Sunday evening showtimes, although theaters were adding them throughout the weekend. Originally programmed as a one weekend, three showtime event engagement, it will now remain in theaters throughout the holiday season. Recent findings suggest that meanwhile, Club Kid from A24 grossed $1.4 million on 229 screens in limited release for a cume of $3.39 million. Jordan Firstman’s feature debut, with strong exits and word of mouth, continues to build ahead of a moderate nationwide expansion over Thanksgiving.

The distributor’s You Can See Everything remains in the top 10 (no. 6) after six weeks of release with a $2.5 million gross and a domestic cume of $93.4 million. The blockbuster doc by Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim has crossed $100 million globally.

Limited: Neon’s Paper Tiger from James Gray grossed $336k in a week 2 expansion to 28 screens for a domestic cume of $622k. Adam Driver, Scarlett Johansson and Miles Teller star.

It has been revealed that mubi’s release of Andrey Zvyagintsev’s Minotaur, winner of the Cannes Grand Prix, opened to $97k at 3 theaters in Latest York and Los Angeles to a $32.4k PTA — the highest for two-time Oscar-nominated director. The Hitchcockian thriller saw several sold out shows across the weekend at all three opening theaters in a crowded specialty marketplace heading into the Thanksgiving holiday.

Josephine from Sumerian Pictures, winner of the Grand Jury and Audience Awards in U.S. Dramatic Competition at Sundance, debuted to $84.2k on 4 screens in Newly introduced York and Los Angeles. Will hold in limited release through the Thanksgiving holiday weekend before expanding nationwide Dec. 4. “We couldn’t be prouder of the initial response to Beth de Araújo’s visionary work, or more excited to share it nationally with moviegoers on the crest of such positive word of mouth,” mentioned Sumerian President Rob Williams.

Pedro Almodovar’s Bitter Christmas from Sony Pictures Classics is eyeing $44k in week 2 at 11 locations for a cume of $99k.

Ash and Crayons from Briarcliff Entertainment opened one NYC screen, Angelika’s Village East, to $11.5k. Bohdan Yermokhin, one of the film’s subjects, traveled from Ukraine for the Friday premiere. Expand to additional screens and markets in the coming weeks.

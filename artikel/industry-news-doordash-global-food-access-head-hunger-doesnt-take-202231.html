Industry News: DoorDash global food access head: Hunger doesn’t take a holiday. Putting food on the table will take all of us

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Hunger gets harder to bear at the holidays, when everyone else seems to be gathering around a full table. With nearly 48 million Americans experiencing food insecurity in recent years, including 14 million children, millions of Americans will be facing a Thanksgiving table that has less on it.

No single organization can close this gap. It will take food banks, businesses, neighbors and public leaders all showing up.

Recent findings suggest that at a time when everything seems to cost more, putting food on the table has become harder than ever. Hunger exists in every county, and increasingly often among those who never expected to need food assistance before. More than eight in ten people surveyed by Feeding America identified rising costs as the primary driver of food insecurity.

The pressure is building for those in need. Recent changes to the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program (SNAP), the nation’s largest federal nutrition assistance program, have contributed to a significant decline in participation. According to the Food Research Action Center, approximately 5.5 million people have lost access to SNAP benefits since last year. According to recent reports, food banks and pantries have always been the country’s backstop. But now, like everyone else in this economy, they are being asked to do more with less. Organizations are bracing demand rivalling or surpassing experienced during last fall’s government shutdown that created unprecedented disruption to SNAP. Some food banks are experiencing demand levels exceeding the COVID-19 pandemic.

Industry observers note that getting food to people can be just as challenging as securing it. Many of those served by food banks — including seniors, people with disabilities, or those without reliable transportation — cannot easily get to a distribution site on their own. Sources indicate that tackling this challenge requires everyone contributing what they do best. For DoorDash, that’s logistics, and closing the gap between food and the people who need it.

Industry observers note that through Project DASH, DoorDash transports goods from food banks and pantries to doorsteps. Since 2018, 9 million deliveries of an estimated 150 million meals across the U.S. and Canada have arrived via DoorDash.

This holiday season, we’re waiving fees for more than 300 Project DASH partners between Thanksgiving and Christmas, supporting delivery of an estimated 2 million meals. By removing that expense, food bank partners can plan larger holiday distributions with confidence and reach more people.

In a recent development, many of our grocery partners are also doing everything they can to make the holidays more affordable for Americans who are struggling to get by, such as offering discounts for SNAP customers to stretch their budgets further. The data is clear: Making it easier to use SNAP benefits can also have a significant impact in the fight against hunger. Since introducing SNAP payments on our platform in 2023, more than 4.5 million consumers have added their SNAP cards to DoorDash, and nearly a third of those we surveyed remarked it has helped make them less likely to run out of food.

According to recent reports, this is only one incremental step in a larger national conversation about hunger. No single manufacturer, program, or nonprofit can solve it alone. Food banks understand their neighbors and the needs of their communities, and businesses can bring their resources, technology, and expertise to bear.

Industry observers note that hunger is a neighbor-helping-neighbor issue. Americans of every background can agree that no one should go without a meal, especially at the holidays.

According to recent reports, there is enough food for everyone in America. What’s missing is a way to get it to every table. With everyone at the table, we can build toward that together.

In a recent development, anthropic (ANT +8.67%) raised a record amount of capital with its initial public offering (IPO). It sold 980 million shares to the public at $83 each, raising about $81 billion. (Related: Tech Report: Over $60k raised for famous station cat af...)

The offering’s underwriters exercised their greenshoe option, buying an extra 147 million shares for just over $12 billion. The AI lab also raised $26 billion in concurrent private placement deals with several big tech companies alongside its IPO. All in all, that brought the company $119.5 billion before underwriting expenses. (Related: Latest Update: F1 star Max Verstappen played 40 hours o...)

According to recent reports, but Anthropic's public offering accounted for only about 6% of its fully diluted share count, which includes stock options, restricted stock units, and convertible notes. The rest of the shares — those held by insiders and pre-IPO stakeholders — remain locked up. But the expiration of the first phase of that lock-up comes in just a few weeks on Dec. 15.

There are some important details investors should know before the first lock-up expiration.

According to recent reports, anthropic's valuation at its IPO was about $1.5 trillion. Given that massive market cap, staggering its lock-up expiration helps prevent a flood of shares from weighing down the stock price. Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +3.63%) is taking a similar approach with its IPO lock-up.

According to recent reports, anthropic outlined its lock-up expiration calendar in its prospectus. Of note, Anthropic has two share classes: Class A shares are ordinary shares of capital stock; Class N shares are those that were issued to strategic investors, such as tech companies investing in its private placement offerings. Class N shares convert to Class A shares upon sale to unaffiliated parties.

A huge number of shares are set to be added to the stock’s float — the pool of tradable shares — over the next year. The current float is set to double with the first four lock-up expirations between December and February, and it'll increase by another 70% from there after Anthropic's first earnings report as a publicly traded manufacturer.

Those early lock-up expirations will be a major test of just how much appetite there is for Anthropic shares. SpaceX faced similar dynamics. While the first expiration went well, with the market fully absorbing the new shares without much impact on the price, its more recent lock-up expirations haven't gone as smoothly for shareholders.

Another important detail about Anthropic's share lockup that you might notice is that Class N share lockups are backloaded. That's because shareholders with more than a 0.5% ownership stake are subject to the full 365-day lockup period, while smaller shareholders will be able to sell all of their stock after 180 days.

The 0.5%-plus ownership group includes names like Alphabet and Amazon. They'll likely seek to reduce their market influence on the stock price until they can sell all their shares.

In a recent development, the big fear for investors is that a flood of newly tradable shares will increase selling pressure on the stock, sending the price lower as Anthropic’s early investors and employees look to cash out. Indeed, that's the whole reason why Anthropic scheduled a much more spaced-out lock-up schedule than the typical IPO.

Recent findings suggest that while the increase in float could push the stock price down, investors know these selling events are coming. That could lead them to discount shares ahead of key lock-up expiration dates, making it easier for the market to absorb sellers.

It has been revealed that that's exactly what we saw with SpaceX. Shares actually climbed 6% on the date of its first lock-up expiration.

According to recent reports, over time, Anthropic's share price moves should more closely reflect changes in the business's value. Short-term selling pressure from lock-up expirations could provide discounts that long-term investors can take advantage of to build their positions.

But investors shouldn't take an unlock-related sell-off in Anthropic shares as a good reason to buy the stock. There are considerable risks for the AI stock that need to be factored into even the rosiest outlooks for the company. If you don't buy it with a wide enough margin of safety, you could face some significant losses if your forecast proves too optimistic. Determining the price you're willing to pay for the stock before a potential selling event can help you capitalize on the potential opportunity it creates.

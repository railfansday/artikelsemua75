Applied Systems unveiled on November 24, 2026 that it had acquired Tailwind, an insurance document and data retrieval specialist, to move carrier documents automatically into its management systems and expand AI automation across agencies’ operational, financial and trading workflows.

Recent findings suggest that the acquisition addresses the gap between documents held by carriers and the data agencies need inside their management systems. Applied CEO Graham Blackwell said: “Critical insurance workflows depend on risk and policy data, yet that data is locked in documents behind carrier portals and across fragmented systems.” Tailwind adds retrieval and normalization technology for moving that information into Applied’s software. According to recent reports, applied expects the combination to reduce back-office costs and support growth by extending automation across the risk lifecycle. The practical significance is a shared intake path for information that would otherwise require agency staff to search portals, monitor email and move documents between systems. According to recent reports, tailwind’s software collects insurance documents, standardizes their data and produces client reports used when pursuing new accounts and renewing existing ones. Tailwind reports in its product documentation that it retrieves loss runs—carrier records of claims histories—from more than 1,000 carriers, wholesalers and managing general agents through portals, email and direct connections.

Sources indicate that agencies can schedule retrieval before a renewal, while Tailwind manages carrier contact, follow-up, authorization paperwork and broker-of-record letters. Request statuses show which documents have arrived and which still need attention.

In a recent development, tailwind’s AI reads claims from the varied formats used for loss runs and standardizes paid, reserved and incurred values so policy years and carriers can be compared without re-keying the data. A separate mailroom capability consolidates documents arriving by carrier email, portal download or upload. It classifies items such as policies, quotes, endorsements and loss runs, then associates each with the correct client, policy and term in the agency management system.

Recent findings suggest that once standardized, the claims data can be turned into agency-branded summaries and analyses of frequency, severity, loss causes, large claims and open reserves. For a brokerage, one intake process can therefore feed both a fresh-business submission and the claims discussion at renewal, with the original loss runs available alongside a multi-year claims history for underwriters.

Recent findings suggest that applied prepares to use Tailwind across the three domains of Epic Conductor, the AI platform built into Applied Epic that it launched on September 29, 2026. Those domains are operational automation, financial automation and connected trading. Epic Conductor already includes AI extraction from carrier documents, automated reconciliation with human review and submission workflows linking agencies and carriers; Tailwind is intended to extend the documents and data available to those processes.

For financial automation, Applied intends to retrieve employee-benefits commission statements and property-and-casualty statements that are not delivered by download. The initiative is to remove the monthly step in which agencies locate and upload those statements before automated reconciliation, connecting document retrieval directly to the accounting workflow.

It has been revealed that applied also wants information from non-downloaded policies and client messages to fill the appropriate agency-management fields automatically for work such as starting a submission or setting up a plan record. For connected trading, loss-run data retrieved from carriers would populate renewal and new-business submissions. All three remain planned Epic Conductor integrations.

Applied’s acquisition FAQ notes the majority of Tailwind’s customers already use Applied Epic, and that Tailwind customers and partners will see no change to their primary services or support contacts following the acquisition.

That overlap makes deeper Epic integration immediately relevant to much of Tailwind’s installed base. The continuity commitment is also consequential because Tailwind’s software handles documents used in time-sensitive renewal and submission work.

It has been revealed that for Applied, the deal is intended to turn document access into a shared input for agency operations, commission reconciliation and carrier submissions. The central execution question is whether the planned Epic Conductor connections can remove portal searches, monthly statement uploads and claims re-keying across whole workflows, rather than merely speeding individual retrieval tasks.

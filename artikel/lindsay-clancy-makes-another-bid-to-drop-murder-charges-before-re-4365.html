UPDATE — 11/19/2026, 6:17 p.m. ET: Massachusetts’ highest court will hear Lindsay Clancy’s petition to dismiss the murder charges against her before her upcoming retrial in March, according to reports.

It has been revealed that the state’s Supreme Judicial Court confirmed on Thursday, November 19, that it will listen to oral arguments after Clancy’s defense team filed a petition calling on the court to intervene on Monday, November 16, according to the Latest York Times. The court is set to hear arguments in January.

The original story continues below.

Lindsay Clancy’s defense team has called on Massachusetts’ highest court to intervene ahead of her upcoming retrial in her latest bid to have her murder charges dropped.

Recent findings suggest that in a petition filed Monday, November 16, with the state’s Supreme Judicial Court, Clancy’s lawyers asked to reverse Plymouth County Superior Court Judge William Sullivan’s recent denial of Clancy’s motion to dismiss the charges, per Boston.com. Sullivan’s decision cleared the way for a retrial.

The day of the petition’s filing, Sullivan scheduled Clancy’s second murder trial to take place on March 29, 2027.

Clancy, of Duxbury, sought to have the charges she’s facing in the killings of her three children dismissed on double jeopardy grounds after Sullivan declared a mistrial in her case on September 4.

Clancy’s attorneys argue in their petition that she was “aggrieved” by Sullivan turning down her motion to dismiss on November 9.

“Respectfully, contrary to the trial court’s stated belief at the time, it did have viable choices other than declaring a mistrial,” her attorneys wrote in the filing.

They contend she “should not be ‘forced to run the gantlet’ again” after a deadlocked 11-1 jury couldn’t unanimously agree on whether she was criminally liable for the January 2023 deaths of her children, Cora, 5, Dawson, 3, and Callan, 8 months.

Sources indicate that clancy’s defense team argues Sullivan should’ve investigated the lone holdout juror further after the jury foreperson alleged the man, Michael P. Desronvil, didn’t follow instructions relating to reasonable doubt.

Industry observers note that “There is a substantial possibility that the trial court, after inquiry, would have discharged the non-compliant juror,” her attorneys wrote in the petition. “Such possibility, therefore, constitutes a viable alternative to the mistrial, precluding a finding of manifest necessity.”.

Davis Keillor-Dunn's sale – or rather the way the Reds responded to it – is symptomatic of why home fans are so grumpy with their club, with protests against the management planned before and during the televised derby game.

In the short-term, the bigger worry is now he is in blue-and-white stripes, the Owls are belatedly starting to work out how to best use him. (Related: British Airways Avios — Short Nonstop Domestic Flights...)

The Wearsider is a versatile forward, which is not always as helpful as it sounds.

At Barnsley he scored 16 goals as a "false nine" last season before joining Wrexham on February’s deadline day. Swallowed up in their squad, he failed to score for the Dragons, and even though David McGoldrick stepped up to find the net in his absence, the failure to spend the fee on a replacement summed up why some home fans initiative to chant "Enough is enough" four minutes into Friday’s derby.

After bringing the 29-year-old back to South Yorkshire on loan, Henrik Pedersen used Keillor-Dunn from the right, the left and in the hole before asking him to lead the line, the job his successor Dave Challinor has earmarked for now.

Three goals in as many games, six in his last nine, suggests the Owls are starting to find the right formula.

Recent findings suggest that "With Ali (Al-Hamadi) being out, he's probably the closest," claims Challinor when asked if his squad lacks a genuine 'No 9'. "Harry (Gray) I think will develop into a nine, but as an 18-year-old playing in League One, the physical part of that is always going to be tough for him.

"Davo's probably a nine-and-a-half. He can play as a nine but also likes to link the play.".

Industry observers note that for those not au fait with football's trendy numbers game, a No 9 is a traditional centre-forward who leads the line, either as a targetman or playing off the shoulder of the last defender. A 'ten' plays in the hole behind, and a "false nine" or "nine-and-a-half" flits between the two, as Harry Kane does for England.

And although Challinor spoke after Saturday's 3-0 win over Plymouth Argyle about the need for Keillor-Dunn to get into positions to score proper striker's goals, as he did that day, it does not mean shackling him at the top of the pitch.

Industry observers note that "I'm okay with him dropping off," he insists. "The important thing is that when the ball is in certain areas, he gets in the right areas. If the ball is in a wide area, he's a box finisher.

Sources indicate that "I've shown him all his goals over the last three years and 90 per cent, 95 per cent, are within the width of the posts in the 18-yard box. If the ball is in a wide area, we want him in the box. That's where he'll finish.

"There's nothing to say that outside of that we don't want him working and running channels.

"As long as the majority of his stuff is in central areas and we're getting the ball to him, if he wants to impact the game in different areas, then great, but only on the odd occasion.

In a recent development, "I'll go back to the Oldham game (Challinor's first as manager, in the FA Cup first round) in the first half. He spent far too much time almost as a left wing-back, which is not playing to his strengths.

"(The message was) go and get close to the goal, he gets a goal, he gets the same on Saturday, another couple of opportunities within that.

"We will give them freedom, but freedom within the structure.".

In a recent development, quite what that structure will be is a challenge for Daniel Stendel's Barnsley players. It is hard for the coach to tell them exactly how the Owls will play when, three games in, Challinor is still working it out.

"We prepare the team for the next opponent every time," claims Stendel. "But I think two different things (about Wednesday).

It has been revealed that "A upcoming manager has come in, but the quality is the same as before. Maybe some things on the tactical side have changed. We can see some things in the video analysis.

"The other thing is the situation. It's the derby, it's a night game, it's live on TV. It's a lot more than Sheffield against Barnsley and I think that makes the game so different.".

Stendel sees it as a no-lose situation for a club with no wins in their last nine League one games and one from bottom.

"The feeling against Leicester and Oxford (two draws), especially when you concede a red card (as they did early in both games), what you can expect against a top team in the league goes down from everybody and you say, 'Okay, we can only win.' The game has this feeling a little bit.

Sources indicate that "But there's also a responsibility for Barnsley Football Club to know it is a Yorkshire derby. It's maybe the most key derby for this club.

According to recent reports, "It can maybe help to bring energy. We can fight against them more and we can only win in this game.".

As for special looks for DKD, "not a lot," he replies, "but it's clear he's a really good player in this league.

"A lot of players know his quality in front of the goal, especially in the box. We have to look after him.

"Wednesday has a lot of quality players but he's surely a player that we have to mark.".

Leeds United have had a mixed start to their season in the Premier League, and Daniel Farke has made some strange decisions in the eyes of the fans.

The Whites claimed a 1-0 win over Tottenham Hotspur just before the international break to move the team up to ninth in the table, as they snapped a run of just one win in their previous nine fixtures against the north London club.

Sources indicate that despite their healthy position in the Premier League table, Leeds have won just three of their ten games so far this season in the top-flight, and have scored just 10 goals. But the manager has continued to persist with his safety-first style of football, leaving a number of attacking talent out of the side to ensure there is more defensive stability.

Dominic Calvert-Lewin continues to be the focal point of the attack, and has rewarded the manager with a healthy return of four goals in those 10 games, while his strike partner has often been Noah Okafor, who has scored twice himself. In a recent development, but aside from those two players, everyone else is more defensive minded. A midfield three usually consisting of Ethan Ampadu, Ao Tanaka, and Anton Stach has produced just one goal and one assist combined this season, while the back five is made up of traditional defenders.

Recent findings suggest that there has also been frustration at a lack of opportunities for summer signing Jean-Matteo Bahoya, who has featured for just 11 minutes this season, despite arriving as a highly-rated and skilful attacker. But while there has been speculation that he could find his spell at Elland Road being cut short, despite an option to make the move permanent next summer, he isn't alone in being ostracised from the side. (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

Fellow winger Daniel James had been a regular under Farke for much of his Leeds career, but this season he has been very much a bit-part player.

It has been revealed that the Wales international has featured for just 117 minutes across four appearances this season, making just one start in the Premier League. As expected, that means he is yet to contribute to a goal or assist, and there is believed to be growing frustration at his situation, despite having signed a latest contract last year.

Speaking to Football Insider, expert Mick Brown revealed that the former Man Utd winger could push for a move away in January as a result of a lack of opportunities.

"Daniel James is in a bit of a strange position at Leeds, it's hard to know where he stands," he noted.

"He is a valuable asset to have coming off the bench, because he's quick, he's direct, he can put defenders under pressure, and he's good to have when you're chasing a game.

"But despite all of those things I've said, notice there's nothing there about his quality as a player, and that's where the doubts are.

"Physically, he's great, he offers a lot in that department, but his actual skill level has always been in doubt and I'm still not convinced he's a Premier League player in that respect.

"He's done well at Leeds, he's played an notable role for them, but now he's not starting games and it looks like they're moving to a level where he won't be needed.

"From his point of view, he is not happy with his role, he wants to be starting games at this stage of his career, and he doesn't just want to be sitting on the bench and coming on every now and then.

"So there is every chance that, if things don't change soon, he could push for a move away because that's what it might take for him to move on.".

While James was a key figure in the team's promotion to the top flight, his performances in the Premier League have been far from the level needed or expected.

It has been revealed that his pace is a clear weapon to have in the squad, but he isn't a wing-back and he doesn't have the technical quality or physical attributes to be able to play centrally. He at the moment finds himself behind Harry Wilson, Brenden Aaronson, Lukas Nmecha, Okafor and Calvert-Lewin in those roles too, which makes keeping him around a bit pointless.

The player is clearly unhappy with his situation and wants to go, and while Farke doesn't want his squad to become weaker, the club should be looking to cash in, make some money from him, and reinvest that into the squad for a player who can actually help them.

A new striker is likely to be a priority, but if the club want to bring in a more attack-minded wing-back option that could help too. Either way, it's obvious that James hasn't got a long-term future at Elland Road, and January seems to be a perfect chance for both parties to go their separate ways.

A hacker has pleaded guilty to a number of online crimes, including the 2022 breach of Rockstar Games which resulted in a bunch of mid-development clips of GTA 6 in action leaking onto the net.

The BBC report that, on November 16th, Arion Kurtaj pleaded guilty at Woolwich Crown Court to the Rockstar Games hack and a number of others he carried out with the hacking gang Lapsus$ while aged 16 and 17. Kurtaj, now aged 21, was previously sentenced to stay in a secure hospital indefinitely due to the severity of his autism, but was subsequently deemed fit to stand trial. He'll be sentenced at a later date. (Related: How to Start a Side Business While Working Full-Time...)

Industry observers note that kurtaj carried out the 2022 Rockstar hack while on bail for other hacking offences and being housed at a Travelodge hotel under police protection, the latter on account of rival hackers threatening to attack him over stolen Bitcoin stashes. The Rockstar hack was reportedly done using an Amazon Fire Stick plugged into the hotel room's TV, plus a Bluetooth keyboard and mouse. (Related: Industry News: Average Retirement Savings by Age: Are Y...)

90 clips of unfinished GTA 6 were flung online by that hack, with Rockstar having refused to cooperate with Kurtaj's demand via an internal Slack channel that the tech giant contact him on massaging app Telegram within 24 hours. Rockstar claimed the breach cost them $5m million to recover from, in addition to staff having to waste thousands of hours.

Sources indicate that earlier this year, GTA 6 suffered yet another leak, with a hacker or hackers going by "Cyberleek" posting various videos of the game in action. Rockstar and parent manufacturer Take-Two rapidly sprung into action, taking down videos, filing subpoenas, and issuing a statement that called the leak "heartbreaking".

Hopefully with the game's console release later this week, those will be the only two GTA 6 leaks which have to be added to the annals of gaming history. As much as marketing can leave a bad taste in the mouth, especially when it barely shows anything of the actual product being advertised, neither of the GTA 6 leaks served much of a purpose beyond forcing people to waste some time and energy talking about the perpetrators.

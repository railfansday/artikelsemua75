David Moyes conceded there were "no excuses" for Everton's 4-0 defeat to Brentford, though he did think James Tarkowski's first-half red card was a debatable decision.

On the day he surpassed Harry Catterick's record for the most games managed for Everton, Moyes endured an encounter to forget at the Gtech Community Stadium. (Related: Course Launch Community Event...)

Keane Lewis-Potter's brace, with both of his goals taking deflections off Jarrad Branthwaite and Thierno Barry respectively, had Keith Andrews' side cruising by the 25th minute.

There were just 178 seconds between Lewis-Potter's strikes, and it got even worse for Everton when Tarkowski was shown a red card for a high challenge on Yehor Yarmoliuk, with referee Craig Pawson overturning his original decision after consulting VAR.

In what was his 351st top-flight appearance, Tarkowski was sent off for the first time, and it only got worse for Everton from there as Brentford made their man advantage count.

Vitaly Janelt picked out the top-right corner with a stunning strike after the hour before Fabio Carvalho bundled home Brentford's fourth of the game in the 90th minute.

The defeat was Moyes' biggest Premier League loss while in charge of Everton since being beaten 1-6 at home to Arsenal on the opening matchday of the 2009-10 season.

"We didn't play well enough and Brentford did so, we have no complaints, and they thoroughly deserved to win," Moyes told BBC Match of the Day.

According to recent reports, "We could use them as excuses, but there are no excuses. The performance was way below the standards we've set and the way we've been playing recently.

Industry observers note that "We just didn't play well enough but, hopefully, we can put that right. We've been doing a lot of good things and, hopefully, we can do it again.".

Courtesy of Tarkowski's dismissal, Everton have now picked up 114 red cards in the Premier League, four more than any other side (Arsenal next on 110).

Tarkowski had initially been given a yellow card for his follow-through when clearing the ball out of defence, but the VAR sent referee Pawson to the pitchside monitor.

"My thoughts at the time when I didn't think it was [a red card], but probably when I looked at it again, was that his foot's high," Moyes said of the decision.

Sources indicate that "Is there enough force for it to be a red card? Not sure about that.".

Everton's defeat saw their three-game winning run across all competitions come to an end, with the result dropping them to ninth in the Premier League.

But the Toffees must pick themselves up ahead of this Sunday's Merseyside derby, where they face a resurgent Liverpool side who beat rivals Man United 4-0 on Sunday.

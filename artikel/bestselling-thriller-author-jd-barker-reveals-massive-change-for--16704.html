It has been revealed that dani Kessel Odom (they/them) is an autistic lead writer and TV critic who covers sci-fi shows like Doctor Who and Pluribus, fantasy shows like The Magicians and Percy Jackson, and horror of all kinds. They also cover Marvel and DC TV shows and movies, with a robust knowledge of comic books. Their TV reviews can be found on Rotten Tomatoes.

Dani is also part of ScreenRant's books team, using their fiction writing and literature studies as the backbone for their book analyses.

Recent findings suggest that they have covered events, such as the Denver Fan Expo. Professionals in the field, such as Damien Leone and Lucy Hale, have shared their articles. Their review of Ponies was quoted in the show's TV trailer.

In university, they majored in English Writing with a minor in psychology. They have always had a passion for analyzing TV and movies, even taking filmography and scriptwriting classes in university. They also studied and participated in onstage and onscreen acting extensively from the ages of 7 to 18.

Sources indicate that since his self-published debut novel, Forsaken, boomed in popularity in 2014, bestselling author J.D. Barker has become one of the most popular thriller writers, and his body of work continues to grow. As of the time of writing, Barker has 24 titles listed on his official website. He writes plenty of books solo, but he’s also not afraid to team up with other brilliant writers like Dacre Stoker and Christine Daigle. He’s also a frequent co-author of James Patterson. Together, they’ve published five books with a sixth already on the way. (Related: Industry News: Trump calls Putin and Xi friends. They h...)

Recent findings suggest that with such a massive portfolio, it's no surprise that Barker has had many substantial successes in his career. He gained a Bram Stoker Award nomination with his debut book, reaching the finalist stage. The 2016 book The Fourth Monkey brought him a number of significant firsts. It was his first traditionally published book and his first seven-figure deal. The next year, it earned him his first Goodreads Choice nomination. The thriller writer finally hit the Newly introduced York Times bestseller list in 2020, when he published The Coast-to-Coast Murders with James Patterson. Just this year, he had another big achievement, announcing a TV adaptation of his crime thrillers developed by Sylvester Stallone.

J.D. Barker’s next book will be another big first for the author, according to a November 20, 2026, GlobeNewswire press release from Hampton Creek Press. Dedicated readers should prepare to dive into a latest genre. Rather than a traditional thriller, J.D. Barker is publishing his first-ever sci-fi thriller, The Faces of Marcus Blake, which he co-authored with I.O. Adler. Hampton Creek Press will release the book on February 16, 2027, in partnership with Simon & Schuster. Barker took on the challenge of shifting genres in an effort to introduce a new audience to his signature pacing and dread. If he succeeds, it could widen his already incredible reach.

Luckily, the description of The Faces of Marcus Blake sounds mind-bending and thrilling. A volunteer chaplain, Thomas Reyes, grants a dying inmate’s request to deliver a letter, unaware that the man is actually Marcus Blake, a person capable of changing faces. By morning, an orderly is dead, and Blake walks out of the prison as someone else, eager to find the scientist who experimented on him. Unfortunately, Reyes’ small act of kindness marks him, and he’ll have to find the man with many faces to stop him.

So far, 16 individuals have reviewed Barker and Adler’s upcoming book on Goodreads using ARCs, and it sits at a 3.71-star rating. Obviously, that’s too small a number to predict how the wider audience will react to the genre-bending book when it comes out. It’s a good sign, though, that readers have described the book as “dark,” “gripping,” and “twisty.” One even shared that they stayed up all night to finish it.

Goodreads Stats (as of 11.20.2026).

Given the successful track record of both J.D. Barker and I.O. Adler, The Faces of Marcus Blake has serious potential to become a bestseller with a large readership. That being said, their ability to blend their voices and capture Barker’s signature style in a sci-fi will make or break the book for existing readers. Readers might be willing to cross over to another genre, but they still undoubtedly want the elements that made Barker's writing so magnetic in the first place.

Recent findings suggest that “I’ve always had so much respect for small business owners because I know firsthand what it takes to turn an idea into something real,” Longoria told Entrepreneur. “Being an entrepreneur can be incredibly rewarding, but it can also feel overwhelming when you’re making significant decisions and trying to figure things out on your own. “No two small businesses face the same challenges, and the best support starts with listening,” Emily Ketchen, SVP and CMO of Intelligent Devices Group & International Markets at Lenovo, told Entrepreneur.

At the heart of the initiative is Twinning, a mentorship program pairing small business owners across industries and borders. A new docuseries narrated by Longoria follows Twinning entrepreneurs as they tackle real business challenges together.

Sources indicate that and then there’s Call Eva, which gave entrepreneurs a direct line to Longoria, Lenovo’s global ambassador. Founders left voice notes with their biggest business questions, and select callers get to speak with her directly.

“It’s been rewarding to be part of something that helps small business owners feel supported as they grow,” Longoria added.

Industry observers note that the stakes are big. The U.S. has 33.2 million small businesses that employ 61.6 million Americans and represent 43.5% of America’s GDP, according to the U.S. Chamber of Commerce.

An analysis of 88 Call Eva submissions from eight countries revealed four overlapping themes. Here are the most common concerns, and Longoria’s advice for overcoming them.

1. How to be seen. More than half asked about marketing and reaching fresh customers. Longoria’s advice: Lead with the story only your business can tell.

“The tool that you have to use is storytelling,” she stated. “You’ve got to tell the story of the artisans and tell the story of the heritage behind these materials. I always think that cuts through the noise.”.

Sources indicate that 2. How to scale. Half asked about expanding without losing what makes them distinctive. Longoria urged starting focused and building on what works.

“I would think about one SKU, one target customer, one initial region, one retailer… Let’s see how that performs,” she stated.

In a recent development, 3. How to lead and partner. One in five asked about juggling leadership and competing priorities. Longoria stressed finding collaborators whose audiences and values align.

Industry observers note that “Who are they trying to reach? What’s their demographic? How do you make this a win for that brand?” she stated.

Recent findings suggest that 4. How to fund the future. About one in eight asked about raising capital. Her advice: Find investors who bring expertise and relationships, not just cash.

Recent findings suggest that “You’re looking for a partner that adds value and not just in money,” she said. “I think one of the biggest mistakes you can make is partnering with the wrong investor.”.

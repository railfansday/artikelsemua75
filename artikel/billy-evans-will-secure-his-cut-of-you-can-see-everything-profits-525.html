Sources indicate that it looks like Billy Evans will get paid for the success of You Can See Everything after all. He just won’t keep the money.Evans and his representatives have negotiated for the backend share he gave up to be paid out, according to two sources not affiliated with him or his partner Elizabeth Holmes. The money will go to an unnamed children’s charity instead of to Evans, the sources mentioned.While the exact percentage is unknown, the total payout will be a lot bigger now than it would have been even a few weeks ago. The film has grossed $89 million domestically to date, a jaw-dropping sum for a documentary.Subject payment is a key plot point in Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim’s hit about the convicted Theranos founder and her life partner Evans. The film demonstrates Evans back in 2023 seeking money from A24 without Holmes’ knowledge, casting him in a greedy light; the filmmakers reluctantly then agree to pay him in order to secure access and make a deal. But a title card at the end says that “Billy Evans ultimately relinquished his negotiated share of the film’s profits and would not receive backend compensation.” Related Stories Movies IDA Documentary Awards Nominations: 'Concrete Land,' 'To Hold a Mountain,' 'You Can See Everything' Among Top Nominees Movies Billy Evans Fires Back at Amanda Seyfried and Nathan Fielder: "It's My Life and I Love It".

It is unclear if Evans at the time of that relinquishing secured a payout to charity with the title card omitting that point, or if Evans had fully given up on the possibility at the time and had a change of heart after the film was released and started raking in cash. Supporting the latter timing, Page Six reported on Oct. 23, a week after the movie opened, that Evans had contacted lawyers to try to restore his deal, with no word in that story that he had even succeeded in retaining counsel let alone striking a deal.

Sources indicate that A representative for Evans did not provide comment.A spokesperson for A24 did not immediately reply to a request for comment.Documentary subjects these days often ask to be paid, and distributors sometimes agree, usually without giving up control of a film’s final cut. That is what happened here until Evans decided to relinquish what he’d negotiated.

Speaking to The Hollywood Reporter this week, Evans said he made that decision “maybe five months ago” because the filmmakers “said it would help the project”.

Recent findings suggest that “I am totally stubborn, but if there’s anything that I can do to help Liz regardless of the cost, I will do it,” he said.(He explained his earlier push for money this way: “I’m a single dad with two kids whose best friend’s about to be sent to prison. And at that time I didn’t have a job. I’m trying to provide for my family and I’m really scared about what’s to come.” Evans is a member of the family that owns Evans Hotels, which operates three high-end Southern California properties including The Lodge at Torrey Pines, the Bahia Resort Hotel and the Catamaran Resort Hotel.)The new deal means A24 will be cutting a profit-participation check for Evans’ role, just not to the entrepreneur himself. The move could still possibly help Evans’ image. The entrepreneur has become a pop-culture villain thanks to moments like a domineering scene in which he indicates Holmes how to whisk eggs, along with diatribes at filmmakers over questions he doesn’t like.

Amanda Seyfried, who plays Holmes in the film’s reconstructions, challenges Evans on camera and tells Fielder she wants to break up the couple. Fan boards, meanwhile, routinely debate his level of villainy.

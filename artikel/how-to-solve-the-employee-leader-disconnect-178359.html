In all my decades of conducting surveys, this was the first and only time I’d seen employees rate their organization more positively than leaders did. (Related: Restaurant owners left in tears after discovering their...)

On every other survey I’d led, leaders had always rated their satisfaction with the organization higher than employees did. There was always a disconnect.

In fact, research from the past 25 years documents this perceptual disconnect between leaders and employees across multiple industries (Dunger, 2025; Johnson, 2000; Patterson et al., 2004; Silver Wolf et al., 2014).

Might the disconnect be due to… leaders having more autonomy to make decisions than employees, who are expected to follow their directives? Leaders not carrying out the tasks resulting from their decisions and thus not experiencing the difficulties firsthand? Leaders not knowing about problems due to subordinates’ reluctance to bear bad news or appear unable to handle challenges? Leaders’ reluctance to acknowledge organizational shortcomings due to being more responsible for them? Leaders feeling more satisfied with the organization due to their significantly higher status and compensation? Leaders being above the rules that apply to everyone else? (Ahem, Jensen Huang.).

Research proposes that many of the above may be contributing factors (Dunger, 2025).

Why is the disconnect bad for business? What’s wrong with leaders thinking most things in the organization are just peachy when the employees think otherwise?

If leaders don’t have a clear picture of how the organization is functioning and what employees face, employees feel unheard, unseen, and unsupported. And not only does team morale suffer, but performance does, too (Bashshur et al., 2011).

Sources indicate that if leaders don’t have a clear picture, they can’t lead as effectively. It’s almost as if they are driving the bus with a hand over one eye. If they don’t know the true problems, the major sources of dissatisfaction, and their extent, they can’t address them.

So how do we bridge that gap and ensure leaders have a good sense of the employee experience?

From my academic and consulting experience, here are a few recommendations for companies.

To reduce the disconnect between leaders’ and employees’ perceptions of the organization, and for leaders to lead effectively, they must gather and listen closely to bottom-up communication nearly as often as they distribute top-down communication.

Companies that collect this feedback are better for it. It builds trust, and leaders with a more accurate understanding of their organization are better able to lead it. So if you’re in a leadership position, test out some of these recommendations.

And if you’re not a leader but would like to see some of these ideas implemented, here’s an easy script to share with your boss or your boss’s boss.

“I’d love to see us create more opportunities for employees to share their experiences with leadership. Could we try a small listening session where employees discuss what’s getting in their way and suggest solutions? We could start with one problem, then follow up on what we heard and what we’ll do next.”.

I hope one day to be surprised not by the direction of the employee - leader disconnect... but by the lack of it!

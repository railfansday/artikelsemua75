A quick Google search for the yearly cost of attending the University of Pennsylvania returns a flat figure: $99,082. But that remarkably high number doesn’t tell the full story. Nearly half of Penn students receive financial aid, with 68% of those students having all of their tuition and fees covered by need-based assistance. But prospective students and their families often don’t know whether they’ll fall into that group, instead daunted by the university’s projected $100,000 price tag. The uncertainty around the cost of college contributes to Gen Z’s declining interest in four-year degree programs. As of 2026, just 37% of college graduates say they believe their degree was worth the cost, while less than half of high school students in the U.S. (44%) say they expect to earn a bachelor’s degree. The latter stat, from a 2022 survey, marked a 20-year low for college expectations. That’s why Penn has joined a growing cohort of universities offering cost calculators to students, using information like family size and income to project a student’s cost of attendance long before they’re ever admitted. Calculator culture To use Penn’s cost calculator, prospective students and their parents input data from their tax forms (or the forms themselves) as well as information like their marital status and the number of college students in their family currently enrolled. The calculator then returns a four-year estimate of the cost for that student to attend Penn, taking into account their projected need-based aid. While the calculator takes time to return a result, it also offers a selection of sample families to compare with. Are you closer to Hana, whose family makes $37,000 and has 86% of her tuition covered by Penn, or Nathan, whose family makes $425,000 and is paying the full tuition cost themselves? Another college offering a cost calculator is Brandeis University, which launched a tool called FAYE (an acronym for “financial aid you can expect”) in May. Brandeis also offers academic merit-based aid, so FAYE takes students’ transcripts into account along with financial and family data. Other colleges offer less-sophisticated estimates of students’ annual costs, not with calculators, but with graphs. Take Yale’s instant net price estimator, which shows a low, high, and best estimate of cost based on a family’s annual income. More than 60 other schools offer similar tools in collaboration with the College Board.

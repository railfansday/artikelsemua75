Crawley extended their unbeaten run in the league under Blackwell to four games with a 0-0 draw at home to title contenders Barnet in midweek – and Blackwell now believes that his side are “competitive” – not only on the eye but also according to the data.

In a recent development, the former Sheffield United manager mentioned: “The data shows that we're very competitive now with sprints, decelerations, accelerations, high-speed runs. We're now coming at the top half of the table with that so it just shows that we've been improving all over.”. (Related: AI for Game Development: Creating a Farming Game in 5 D...)

From a defensive point of view the past two games have shown a rejuvenated back-line – helped by the recent additions of Ben Heneghan and Josh Flint, which have made the Reds look a lot more resolute and solid. (Related: Among Us player & his mom reveal how they helped save k...)

According to recent reports, exeter are currently one spot below the play-off places sitting in 8th place on 23 points. The Grecians have the best defensive record in the league having only conceded 10 goals but Blackwell is not fazed by that.

He has communicated as much to his players: “It doesn't matter whether they're the top or the bottom, we feel now that we can compete with people, and that's all I wanted to do, make us competitive. Then hopefully Lady Luck smiles on you on the day and you can get some good results.”.

Blackwell was pleased that more players have stepped up to the mark to give him more confidence in choices.

He said: “We talk about how any team wants to be competitive from 1-22 really, and know that if you lose a player, that someone can seamlessly step in. What was pleasing was that we made some changes, shifted it around a little bit, and then whoever we brought in did a good job, so that gives me more power.”.

With the Reds involved in a lunchtime kick-off, Blackwell was asked whether that makes a difference to the way they approach the game.

He replied: “I think it's something that I've been used to now for the last 15 years, you know, being in a Championship or the Premier League and abroad in Poland is exactly the same.

“You don't know whether you're playing a Friday, a Saturday, a Sunday or a Monday, they're the four days that you normally play.

It has been revealed that “You don't know whether you're going to be at 12 o'clock, 2pm, 4pm, 8pm – so it's now it's just normal for players; it certainly has been for me. So nothing different, whatever the time is, we'll prepare as if it were a 3pm kick-off or a 1pm kick-off. Preparation is the same.”.

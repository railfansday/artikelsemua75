Square Enix’s reasoning is quite simple; peak player times vary across regions so when the servers are slammed in North America, players can choose a European data center to potentially side-step thousand-player-deep log-in queues and vice versa. And not only is this a great way to get ahead of the impending server traffic jam, it’s a neat social feature that eases some of the siloing that happens between servers. Sources indicate that right now, if you have a cross-continental friendship you’d have to make an alternate character on the appropriate data center in order to play together. Cross-region travel will let you port your main character over letting you quest together and, critically, run endgame content like raids without the need to start from scratch on a fresh server. That’s huge and players are suitably hyped for it, hoping that the option to data-center hop remains available permanently.

Recent findings suggest that square Enix warns that visiting other data-centers might increase latency but encourages players to test it out before Evercold launches in February 2027 to get a feel for it. Also, sorry to Oceianin data center players, you still don’t get to go anywhere for now.

According to recent reports, after waiting three years for Relic to expand Firm of Heroes 3's singleplayer, watching DLC after DLC focused on expanding the roster of units available in multiplayer matches, Operation Torch looks like a welcome addition. It's a new 15-mission campaign set in North Africa. following two US officers as they try to break the German Afrika Korps' hold on the region.

In a recent development, having played through the whole thing, I really want to say it's been worth the wait. Unfortunately, it's not so straightforward as that. (Related: Latest Update: F1 star Max Verstappen played 40 hours o...)

It has been revealed that about halfway through the campaign, I'm put in charge of managing a retreat under a German advance. While I must use my limited forces to hold back enemy troops at the head of the map, I'm also to drive a supply truck to different structures on the map so its crew can evacuate the buildings. It's a fraught start to the mission, forcing me to balance my attentions on the embattled front while also keeping a watchful eye on the vulnerable supply truck. German forces are seeping past the line and it would only take one well-placed anti-tank gun to spell an end to the evacuation. (Related: Industry News: Uber Eats driver saves elderly woman aft...)

According to recent reports, the tension that comes in those moments, where I'm hopping about the battlefield barely holding things together, is Manufacturer of Heroes at its best. Operation Torch has several moments that reach that height of trying to command chaos, but they're squandered just as swiftly as they come.

When the three structures are broken down, I'm ordered to pull back to a hill outside of town. The mission isn't over, though: German officers are sifting through the abandoned buildings, looking for intel that was missed in the evacuation, and I'm to take two squads of snipers into enemy territory to kill them before they find anything. While it sounds exciting on paper, the mechanics of the mission's scripting make the reality underwhelming. My weakened army is sitting on a hill outside of town but no Germans come their way. In town, German units either stand still or travel in small looping patrols. Navigating the camouflaged sniper squads between them is easy work, but slow going. And even when they come out of stealth to shoot the officers and are spotted by a unit of German soldiers, they walk away from the fight and the Germans don't give chase. This may all be because I'm playing on standard difficulty, but the AI is oddly passive throughout Operation Torch.

Recent findings suggest that across multiple missions, I repeatedly used the same cheesing tactic. I would send a unit with the stealth passive to hide invisibly behind cover near the enemy, with strict orders not to open fire. With my infiltrator acting as a forward spotter, I'd then pummel the enemy with mortars from a safe distance. This tactic worked again and again. I knew, really, that it was no fun to do – turning every battle into a slow grinding down of the enemy – but it was also too effective to ignore when the enemy would never fight back.

It's a shame so many of Operation Torch's missions hit a bum, overly-scripted note because I really enjoyed their unfurling narrative. Admittedly, my military history is poor, and all my knowledge of the North Africa campaign is drawn from the bits in SAS Rogue Heroes before they go to Europe, but my general understanding is that it was a campaign that saw rapid advances turn into just-as-quick retreats. This plays into Operation Torch's missions where success often isn't pushing the front forwards, but holding the line while covering routed allied forces, or defending a pass long enough for air support to arrive and cause enough damage to force the Germans to slow down. And, because you're battling backwards and forwards over the same territory, Relic employ one of my favourite RTS tricks: throwing you back into the same map but from a different perspective. In one mission you may be on the assault, prizing free the Afrika Korps' grip on a town street by street, but then a few missions later you find yourself defending the same territory from a retaliatory attack. It's unfortunate that the same push and pull isn't as evident on the battlefield.

According to recent reports, relic are experimenters, often reinventing a game's systems between entries in a series or trying out a newly introduced mechanic in their expansions. In Operation Torch, the new toy is a choice of unlocks at the end of each mission. After a tank-focused assignment, for instance, you're offered a choice between a passive perk that makes Shermans 25% harder to hit while moving, or another which unlocks a different Sherman variant in your motor pool. Complete the map's optional objective and you also get to choose between a 10% reduction in tank costs and an upgrade that sees all your tanks produced at level 3 veterancy. These perks then apply throughout your campaign. By the final mission, the right side of your screen is a fat stack of passive and active perks. It's a neat idea but, in practice, it didn't seem to make a dramatic difference to how I played. I do like the idea of gradually developing your forces capabilities over a campaign but perhaps the difference needs to be more dramatic. Making your tanks slightly cheaper or harder to hit doesn't make that much of a tangible difference when you're right in there amidst the tracks and trenches.

In a recent development, thus, it really does fall back to the strength of Operation Torch's missions, which are sometimes great but too often a case of rolling over the enemy very, very slowly. Perhaps this is a game best played with the difficulty whacked up high.

While I was hoping for more from Operation Torch, mind, I do hope it's not the last time we see Relic flesh out Organization of Heroes 3's singleplayer. There's still plenty of scope there, and plenty of room for further experiments.

Sources indicate that in an action movie, there’s nothing worse than an underwhelming final battle. While Shang-Chi and the Legend of the Ten Rings was one of the best post-Avengers: Endgame movies of the Marvel Cinematic Universe, its final battle became a disconnected CGI fest that was hard to follow. For Teenage Mutant Ninja Turtles: The Secret of the Ooze, the sequel’s fun energy was undermined when a mutated Shredder died after collapsing a dock onto himself without a fight ever even starting.

Industry observers note that it can be hard to stick the landing with that final boss fight, especially after a movie has been good up until that point, and a lame final battle can undermine everything that came before it. Fortunately, James Cameron’s 1986 action thriller Aliens — leaving Netflix on Dec. 1 — does not suffer from this issue, even after setting an impossibly high bar for itself. After Ellen Ripley (Sigourney Weaver) defeated one Xenomorph alien aboard a spaceship in Ridley Scott’s 1979 horror thriller Alien, Cameron blew up the franchise by having Ripley visit a planet full of those same creatures and fend off dozens of them with the help of a platoon of Colonial Marines.

Spoilers ahead for the end of Aliens.

Towards the end of Aliens, after Ripley rescues the young girl named Newt (Carrie Henn) from an alien nest filled with eggs, Ripley encounters the Xenomorph queen, a massive creature far larger and more frightening than any of the aliens she’d encountered thus far. After a momentary hesitation, Ripley proceeds to turn her blowtorch on the eggs and blow up the egg sack attached to the queen, while the creature writhes in pain. After Ripley escapes with Newt, the queen tears herself free from the egg sac​​​​​​​ and pursues the duo, who narrowly escape.

Sources indicate that what follows is one of the great fake-outs in cinema history. Bishop (Lance Henriksen), the loyal android, retrieves Ripley and Newt in a dropship, and they escape as the alien-infested planet below is nuked.​​​​​​​ They return to the main ship and all seems well until​​​​​​​ the queen, who had stowed away on the scout ship, tears Bishop in half and comes after Ripley and Newt. Newt hides under the floor panels and the queen nearly gets her​​​​​​​ until Ripley reappears with an exosuit cargo loader and the two have an epic brawl for the ages.

Discussing the queen in a 1987 interview, Cameron explained,​​​​​​​ “The queen was an opportunity to take a design stance that was beyond the first film — create a new organism, basically.”.

She was a creature that Cameron designed himself, building upon the work of artist HR Giger, who designed the Xenomorph alien for the first film.

According to recent reports, “I had an image of what I wanted this thing to look like, which was, obviously, inspired by Giger's approach,” remarked Cameron. “I wanted to continue with that design philosophy, but I wanted to give it certain other characteristics in terms of size and speed and grace and certain feminine characteristics that the alien warrior didn't have. So I just sat down and drew a few different versions of it. And, actually, the first full sketch that I did of it is pretty remarkably close to what we finally wound up with.”. Special effects wizard Stan Winston​​​​​​​ then led the team of effects artists that created the most complex animatronic monster made up to that point​​​​​​​. It took up to 16 puppeteers to control the queen, with some parts moved by hydraulics and others by rods and cables​​​​​​​. And, because this was 1986, none of that operating equipment could end up in the shot to be removed later digitally. What we see in the movie is precisely what was filmed during production.

Perhaps the most impressive thing about the Xenomorph queen, though, was just how perfectly it built upon everything else in the film. When compared to the single alien in Ridley Scott’s original film, Aliens was already full of surprises just by the sheer volume of creatures, but the queen managed to perfectly pay off the entire film with a terrifying design and an incredibly tense battle with Ripley. Four decades later​​​​​​​, it’s still one of the best final fights in cinema history, and certainly worth revisiting before the film leaves Netflix.

According to recent reports, aliens is available to stream on Netflix until Dec. 1. It's also on Hulu and Disney Plus.

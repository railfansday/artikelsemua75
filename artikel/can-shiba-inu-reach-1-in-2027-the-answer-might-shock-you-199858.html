The Shiba Inu (SHIB +5.74%) cryptocurrency was created in 2020 by an anonymous developer named Ryoshi, who saw the success of Dogecoin and wanted to build a more useful alternative. Shiba Inu runs on the Ethereum (ETH +2.68%) network, so it inherits all of the functionality, security, and infrastructure of the world's biggest platform for developing decentralized software applications. (Related: Hugging Face and VirusTotal collaborate to strengthen A...)

Sources indicate that shiba Inu soared by a whopping 45,278,000% in 2021, a return so strong that it could have turned a perfectly-timed investment of $3 into over $1 million. It's one of the largest single-year gains in the history of the financial markets, so it's no surprise many investors still watch this token.

However, speculation was the primary driver of the 2021 rally, so it simply wasn't sustainable. Shiba Inu has since lost 96% of its peak value, and trades at just $0.0000036 per token as I write this on Nov. 24. But there is one way Shiba Inu could stage another historic bull run, and potentially even reach $1 per token. Can it happen in 2027? The answer might shock you.

Having a legitimate source of demand is the key to sustainable upside for any asset. People buy real estate because houses have utility, and they buy stocks because the underlying companies generate revenue and earnings. Many cryptocurrencies also have real use-cases that attract demand, although whether it will last over the long term is up for debate. (Related: Breaking: 3D Asset Generation: AI for Game Development ...)

XRP (XRP +4.84%), for example, is the native cryptocurrency in Ripple's payment network. It helps banks send money around the world instantly, and with minimal fees. Then there is Ether, the native coin in the Ethereum network, which is used to pay fees that cover the computing costs when people use decentralized software applications.

Sources indicate that since Shiba Inu is built on Ethereum, users can program smart contracts to establish autonomous rules when using the token on decentralized platforms like lending marketplaces and crypto exchanges. Developers created a Layer-2 blockchain solution called Shibarium to improve transaction speeds and reduce costs, but it hasn't necessarily made the Shiba Inu ecosystem any more popular.

Industry observers note that unlike Ether, Shiba Inu isn't required to build decentralized apps or cover the associated fees. Plus, while it can technically be used as a payment mechanism, just 1,254 businesses around the world are willing to accept it in exchange for goods and services (according to Cryptwerk).

Sources indicate that in short, Shiba Inu continues to lack a true use case, and the subsequent lack of organic demand is the main reason it has lost 96% of its peak value. However, there's an even bigger glitch standing in the way of a meaningful recovery.

Industry observers note that shiba Inu has a circulating supply of 589.2 trillion tokens, so at the current price of $0.0000036 per token, it has a market capitalization of $2.15 billion. Therefore, basic math suggests that a price of $1 per token would result in a market cap of $589.2 trillion, which would make Shiba Inu almost nine times as valuable as all 500 companies in the S&P 500 (^GSPC -0.25%) combined.

In a recent development, such a high valuation is impossible to justify, so there is no way Shiba Inu will reach $1 as things presently stand. However, the community is trying to resolve the supply problem by "burning" tokens, which typically involves sending them to a dead wallet where they can never be retrieved. In theory, the price per token should increase in proportion to the number of tokens taken out of circulation, creating a real potential pathway to the $1 milestone.

Based on Shiba Inu's market cap of $2.15 billion, burning 99.99998% of the 589.2 trillion tokens presently in supply could result in a price of $1 per token.

However, only 349 million tokens were burned in October, which equates to an annualized rate of 4.2 billion. It would take an eye-popping 140,285 years to burn enough supply to justify a price of $1 per token at this pace, so it definitely won't happen in 2027.

In a recent development, but there is even more bad news. Burning tokens doesn't create any real value, so nobody will make any money. Every investor would simply have 99.99998% fewer tokens, so even though each token would be worth $1, their net financial position won't have changed.

Well, actually, 140,000 years' worth of inflation would make the descendents of each investor significantly worse off.

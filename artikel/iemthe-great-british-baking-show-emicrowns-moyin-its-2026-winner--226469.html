According to recent reports, moyin Odeniran is officially the winner of season 17 of The Great British Baking Show, and after everything the 22-year-old went through in the tent, he’s got plenty to celebrate. The political economy student from Essex beat fellow finalists Mo Mahir and Tom Whittaker after 10 weeks of elaborate desserts, demanding technical challenges, and a finale that put their baking skills to the ultimate test.

Even Paul Hollywood seemed a little swept up in the emotion of it all.

According to recent reports, “The reaction from his family, I have never seen anything like it. It was pure magic!” Hollywood stated in the official winner announcement. “He has done an amazing job because out of all the bakers, he struggled at the beginning, and he actually got better and better. That cake in the Signature was one of the best cakes I have had for a long, long time.”. (Related: 103-year-old grandmother competing in Tekken 8 tourname...)

Nigella Lawson agreed, while making sure the runners-up got their flowers, too. “He is such a worthy winner, but having stated that, Tom and Mo are sensational. They have created cakes that I will never forget.”.

Somewhere in the middle of all that celebrating was a 22-year-old still trying to wrap his head around what he'd just accomplished.

Industry observers note that “I don't know how that happened, I don't know how I got here. I can’t believe it, I am in shock,” Moyin said. “I have made friends for life, and I have just loved every single second of it, and I don't think I could have asked for anything more.”. (Related: Industry News: Uber Eats driver saves elderly woman aft...)

It has been revealed that it’s quite the achievement for someone who started baking alongside his mother at just five years old and had mastered macarons by 14. His Nigerian heritage has influenced many of his creations, but even with years of experience under his belt, Moyin quickly discovered how much he still had to learn.

“Before you get there you think you know how to bake, and by Week 2 you question whether you know anything about baking,” he recalled.

In a recent development, by Week 8, however, he’d found his stride. During Caramel Week, Moyin made a sticky-toffee treacle tart, finished second in the millionaire's shortbread technical, and constructed an elaborate Omo Oba nougatine sculpture for his showstopper. Those incredible efforts earned him his first (and only) Star Baker title of the season.

According to recent reports, the episode also delivered some serious emotional whiplash and a goodbye Moyin—and likely most viewers—probably would’ve preferred to skip. Just as he was celebrating his biggest achievement of the competition, Elizabeth “Connie” Lewis, one of his closest friends in the tent, was eliminated the same week. Their bond had also become a favorite among viewers, making the timing of her departure that much harder to take.

“I think the worst moment for me was when Connie left, and I cried for about an hour,” he admitted. “That surprised me as I am not really a crier, but I found it very emotional as I knew I was going to miss her.”.

According to recent reports, for fans who’d watched their friendship blossom week after week, that goodbye was a particularly heartbreaking moment to watch. Despite their 41-year age gap, Moyin and Connie clearly adored each other, and Baking Show fans would probably be more than happy to watch an entire episode of the two of them baking up a storm together.

In a recent development, when it came time for the finale, the bakers faced citrus celebration cakes, crème brûlée doughnuts, and ambitious showstoppers inspired by British landscapes. Moyin admitted his final creation became “a bit messy... well, a lot messy” toward the end, although he still managed to enjoy his last two days in the tent.

"I didn't want to put too much pressure on myself, but I knew I had put my best foot forward and I couldn't have done anything better than I did and I had a great time doing it,” he stated.

Moyin also credited the show with helping him open up to people in ways he hadn’t before, something that made his experience all the more meaningful.

“What was crucial to me was that you were appreciated as the person you are and allowed to be free,” he said. “I will never forget my time there and the friendships made, which will always continue.”.

For someone who started the season doubting his own abilities, he isn’t exactly dreaming small anymore.

“My baking dream ideally would be to be one of the best bakers in the world, but in the meantime I would like to continue baking, look forward to newly introduced openings, and just see what happens for me out there," he noted.

After this season, that doesn’t sound like such a far-fetched goal at all.

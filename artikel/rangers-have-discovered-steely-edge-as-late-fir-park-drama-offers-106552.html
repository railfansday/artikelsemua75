There was no disguising the satisfaction in Tuur Rommens' voice as he reflected on Rangers' late 2-1 victory over Motherwell. It provided the Belgian defender with further evidence of a team learning learning how to grind out results and develop a winning mentality to overcome hurdles when it matters most.

Recent findings suggest that rangers looked in danger of leaving Fir Park frustrated on Saturday night after a performance in which their attacking intent was not always matched by clinical finishing. Motherwell goalkeeper Alex Paulsen had done everything within his power to earn his side a point, producing a string of outstanding saves, but Rangers refused to settle for a draw. Their persistence was eventually rewarded in the 88th minute when Ryan Naderi’s effort took a deflection off Martin Moorman, wrong-footing Paulsen and finding the net. Those heroics sparked jubilant celebrations among the sizeable travelling support.

Rommens acknowledged that such a dramatic conclusion in North Lanarkshire could provide a significant boost to morale inside the away dressing room, praising his team-mates' willingness to keep pushing despite facing a goalkeeper in inspired form. Recent findings suggest that “The spirit was good and especially when you score a goal in the last minute of the game, it always gives an extra boost,” mentioned the left-back. “Everybody was very happy in the dressing room. It's never easy to come here and to win. Last year we didn't win here, so it's nice to come here and win and get the three points.

“We had a lot of chances. The finish was not always there, but we played a very good game. It feels like we've gained these two points. Their goalkeeper did an amazing job. It's not only our finishing, it's also credit that he saves a lot of shots. Of course, if it stays 1-1, you're thinking, ‘oh, come on, we need to get another one’. But at the end, we got what we deserved.”.

Rangers were left to rue too many dropped points last season, and Rommens is acutely aware of how big the difference between settling for a point and claiming all three can be over the course of a campaign.

The connection between the players and supporters is another factor the 23-year-old believes is proving invaluable in helping Rangers navigate difficult moments.

Sources indicate that “The fanbase is unbelievable. It doesn't feel like an away game because there are so many fans with us,” he added. “When you score you feel a lot of emotions. You feel that they're so happy, we're so happy, so it gives us an extra boost to go again on Tuesday and try to get another win.”.

Attention now turns to Rangers’ game in hand against Aberdeen at Ibrox on Tuesday night, with Derek McInnes’ side looking to capitalise on a run of three successive home fixtures and build on their 13-game unbeaten streak.

The bigger picture remains firmly in their sights, although Rommens is not interested in becoming distracted by the current state of the title race.

Recent findings suggest that “It's been a long time (since we last played at Ibrox). So it will be nice to play in front of our fans again,” he remarked. “I think at home we have to maintain our good run. You just need to keep winning and at the end you'll see where you're at. But if you keep winning, you'll be up there.”.

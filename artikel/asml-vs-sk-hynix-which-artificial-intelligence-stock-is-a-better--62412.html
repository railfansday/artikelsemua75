It has been revealed that the global AI expansion has created a massive demand for advanced hardware, but should you invest in the machines that print the chips or the memory that powers them? That's the choice between ASML (ASML -6.63%) and SK Hynix (SKHY -13.11%). (Related: The one thing Putin, Trump and Xi agreed on — fewer lim...)

Recent findings suggest that ASML provides the essential lithography systems needed to manufacture high-end logic and memory chips. SK Hynix produces the memory hardware itself, focusing on specialized high-bandwidth memory required for generative AI. Both are critical players in the global chip ecosystem, though they occupy very different niches. (Related: Tech Report: GTA 6 players report game-breaking crashes...)

Industry observers note that ASML occupies a unique position among tech stocks because it is the sole provider of extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography systems. These massive machines use light to print complex circuits onto silicon wafers, making them indispensable for foundries producing the latest generation of processors. The firm serves the world's largest chipmakers, though it does not disclose individual customer names in its recent SEC filings.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached $43.4 billion, representing growth of 22.5% compared to the prior year. The firm reported net income of $14.8 billion during this period. This performance was supported by a strong net margin of 34.1%, which reflects its dominance in the high-end equipment market.

In a recent development, as of its December 2025 balance sheet, the company carries a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.2x. This ratio indicates that total debt is quite low relative to shareholder equity, indicating a conservative capital structure. Its current ratio of 1.3x measures its ability to cover short-term debts with liquid assets. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, free cash flow was $12.2 billion.

Industry observers note that SK Hynix manufactures memory semiconductors including DRAM and NAND Flash, which are essential for data storage in everything from smartphones to data centers. The company has recently focused on high-bandwidth memory, a specialized technology that allows data to move rapidly enough for modern AI applications. While it does not disclose its largest customers in SEC filings, its products are integrated into major global electronics and server platforms.

In the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached $201.2 billion, fueled by a massive growth rate of 219.8%. Net income during this same timeframe reached $141.4 billion. This resulted in an exceptionally high net margin of 70.3%, as demand for premium AI memory products outpaced supply across the industry.

It has been revealed that as of its December 2025 balance sheet, SK Hynix reported a debt-to-equity ratio of 0.2x. The current ratio stands at 1.9x, indicating a healthy buffer of assets available to meet upcoming financial obligations. In the fiscal year ended Dec. 31, 2025, the company generated free cash flow of $18.5 billion, providing significant capital for further research and expansion.

Sources indicate that ASML faces risks from geopolitical trade restrictions that limit its ability to sell advanced lithography machines to certain international markets. Because its systems are incredibly expensive and complex, the business is sensitive to the capital spending cycles of just a few massive chipmaking customers. Any significant downturn in global semiconductor demand or delays in new chip factory construction could swiftly impact its order backlog.

SK Hynix operates in the notoriously volatile memory market, where prices for DRAM and NAND can fluctuate wildly based on global supply and demand. It faces intense competition from large rivals like Samsung and Micron, both of whom are investing heavily in competing memory technologies. Additionally, the company is exposed to regional geopolitical tensions and supply chain disruptions that could impact its major manufacturing hubs.

According to recent reports, as major players in the artificial intelligence boom, ASML and SK Hynix are both attractive stocks to own. But if you had to choose one, I recommend SK Hynix. While ASML is a compelling company to invest in because it has a monopoly on EUV lithography technology, SK Hynix's share price valuation is incredibly low, while its sales are soaring.

According to recent reports, the reason for SK Hynix's low valuation is that Wall Street is concerned the current shortage in memory chips will ease as the firm rushes to expand manufacturing capacity. Once that happens, prices and margins are likely to fall. This is typical for the cyclical nature of the computer memory industry, where boom and bust cycles are common.

However, the AI market is expected to expand well into the 2030s. Forecasts estimate the AI industry will grow from $539.5 billion in 2026 to a whopping $9.5 trillion in 2035. This suggests SK Hynix has a multi-year tailwind to power its business. As a result, now is the time to grab shares before Wall Street realizes SK Hynix is trading at a great price.

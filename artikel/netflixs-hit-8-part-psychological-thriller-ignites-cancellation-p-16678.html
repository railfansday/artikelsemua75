Netflix's hit latest series has ignited a petition with over 135,000 signatures calling for its cancellation.

Recent findings suggest that this is far from the first instance of a controversial Netflix series. In 2019, the Brazilian comedy, The First Temptation of Christ (2019), sparked protests and petitions claiming blasphemy. In 2020, the French drama Cuties sparked backlash and petitions claiming it sexualized young girls. In 2021, Netflix's own employees protested outside the tech giant's headquarters, claiming that Dave Chappelle's comedy special, The Closer, was transphobic. In a recent development, now, over 135,000 people have signed an online petition calling for the cancellation of Netflix's fresh show, The Body, calling it "blasphemous" and "anti-Christian." The show follows a group of Catholic schoolgirls who perform a mock Satanic ritual, jokingly offering one of their classmates as a virgin sacrifice. After she falls into a coma, they begin having prophetic visions of her, triggering mass hysteria. (Related: Industry News: Anthropic gave $40M to an AI safety grou...)

Sources indicate that the petition, on CitizenGO, calls the show "a blasphemous anti-Christian series featuring satanic ritual," and warns, "Parents cannot screen every show teens press play on. Netflix is counting on that." The petition was launched ahead of the show's debut on November 11, urging Netflix to scrap its release. Now, at the time of writing, the petition has surpassed 135,000 signatures.

Talking to Decider, Marcel Ruiz, who appears in a recurring role as Tyler Flores, reacted to the viral petition, saying the backlash may actually be a sign the show is striking a nerve and tackling complications people care about. Whether audiences are watching because of outrage or curiosity, it's helping the show climb the charts. Despite calls for cancellation, the cast is hoping for The Body season 2. Read Ruiz's full comments below:.

Sources indicate that I wonder if all of those people that have signed the petition have seen the show? That’s like, something I wonder. And also, in a way, it doesn’t surprise me, not because the show is disrespectful, or because the show deserves to have a petition like that, but that’s sometimes art. I did hear a funny story; my dad told me that this friend of his told him that in his daughter’s private Catholic school, they were telling the kids not to watch it. And I thought, "That’s such a flex." I loved hearing that.

Sometimes, things that are moving the ball forward are also going to have some pushback in some areas. You can’t please everyone at the end of the day, I think. It’s good enough that the show is number one on Netflix. Can’t ask for much else.

We’re waiting by the phone. What was so cool is when we were shooting Season 1, you can’t help yourself but think of a future for these characters and ideas. And I remember the day that I wrapped, I went home and I was so emotional that it was over that I just went on my phone and started writing ideas for Tyler for Season 2. Just like as a way to cope and like have some closure. Definitely, I’m excited to, when the time comes, be able to pick that back up.

Ruiz is right in the sense that The Body debuted at No. 2 on Netflix's Top 10 indicates in the United States and No. 3 globally, receiving 5.1 million views and 37.4 million hours viewed worldwide for the week of November 9–15. As of today, November 20, The Body is the No. 1 show on Netflix, both worldwide and in the United States.

Despite the backlash, The Body reviews have been mostly positive from both critics and audiences, resulting in 87% and 96% Rotten Tomatoes scores from those groups, respectively. Critics claim The Body blends religious guilt, teenage angst, striking visuals, and vibrant storytelling into an unapologetically bold and queer coming-of-age tale that constantly defies expectations.

Despite public backlash, streaming services seldom cave to demands to pull titles from their platforms, so it's unlikely to happen to The Body, especially given its success.

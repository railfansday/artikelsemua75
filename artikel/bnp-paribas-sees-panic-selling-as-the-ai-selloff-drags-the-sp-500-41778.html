The S&P 500 fell into a correction Friday, and the Nasdaq Composite gave back everything it had gained in 2026.

The S&P 500 closed more than 10% below its Oct. 6 record close, and the Dow also ended in correction territory. The S&P fell 3.59% to 6,918.45, its seventh straight losing session and its longest losing streak since February 2020. (Related: Industry News: One Year Since the “DeepSeek Moment”...)

Investors were “panic selling” as they tried to judge whether enthusiasm for AI had peaked, Benedicte Lowe, an equity derivatives strategist at BNP Paribas, told CNBC.

Industry observers note that anthropic led the AI slide, as Fortune reported Friday morning. The Claude maker raised $81.34 billion in an IPO that valued it at $1.414 trillion, and its shares fell about 9% Friday, their second day of trading, giving back much of Thursday’s 17% pop. Reuters cited worries about slower data-center expansion, higher borrowing costs and Jensen Huang’s departure as Nvidia CEO. Huang resigned on Nov. 12, and CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO. The people with the most money in stocks already felt it. Those with the largest stockholdings reported a decline in personal finances in the University of Michigan’s November survey, which ran through Nov. 16, “consistent with continued AI-related agitation in stock markets,” survey director Joanne Hsu mentioned.

Industry observers note that chipmakers took the worst of it. Micron, hotlink]Broadcom[/hotlink], Nvidia, and AMD were down between roughly 12% and 14%. The VIX, Wall Street’s fear gauge, topped 30 for the first time since March, CNBC reported.

According to recent reports, the selling wasn’t only in the U.S. Taiwan’s stock index fell 5.65% Friday, capping its steepest weekly drop since October 1997, according to LSEG data cited by CNBC. Japan’s Nikkei and South Korea’s Kospi each fell about 5%.

According to recent reports, washington added to it. President Donald Trump threatened a “tremendous tax” on U.S. companies that build data centers abroad, CNBC reported.

At midday, some analysts still saw a rotation.

“Money is leaving tech, not the market,” independent tech analyst Luke Lango, editor of the Innovation Investor newsletter, wrote in a note at 12:59 p.m. He said the Dow and the equal-weight S&P were higher at the time. His advice: Avoid AI stocks “while they’re being combative teenagers.” He also pointed to credit: SpaceX had to raise the yield on its $32 billion debt sale to 8.3%, which he read as the “capital spigot” closing for weaker AI borrowers such as CoreWeave and Nebius.

Most of the Street remarked the drop would pass.

“Given the solid economic and fundamental backdrop, the move appears to be highly sentiment driven,” Chris Larkin, head of trading at E*Trade from Morgan Stanley, said in commentary sent to Fortune. He added that “only five of the past 15 corrections from all-time highs since 1983” turned into bear markets.

Deutsche Bank’s Binky Chadha called the market “very oversold” and noted growth and earnings remained strong, according to CNBC. JPMorgan Asset Management chief global strategist David Kelly told Seeking Alpha markets were overdue for a tech correction. Chuck Carlson, CEO of Horizon Investment Services, told Reuters the market may be in a correction within a bull market.

Recent findings suggest that E*Trade’s own research is less soothing. A March 2025 E*TRADE study of the 14 times since 1983 that the S&P 500 first closed 10% or more below a record found five became bear markets. But the index fell another 10% or more after closing in correction six of those 14 times, and in 1990 and 1998 it came within a point of bear territory. The median total drop from the record was 19.9%.

In a recent development, the backdrop Larkin calls solid includes a rate hike on the way. Philadelphia Fed President Anna Paulson noted Thursday the central bank will need to raise rates again because inflation is too high, Reuters reported. Futures put the odds of a December hike at about 95%, per CME FedWatch, with the 10-year Treasury yield near 5%.

The Fed meets Dec. 9. Investors will find out then whether Friday was panic or just early.

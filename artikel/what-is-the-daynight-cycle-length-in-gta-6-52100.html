Sources indicate that every day in Grand Theft Auto 6 is relatively long, but it's not as long as real life. A day has to be long enough to do some of the many things in the game, but it also has to reflect the size of the map. If you drove from one end of the map to the other, it wouldn't be realistic if it took you multiple days just to drive across the city.

Recent findings suggest that the same goes for days that are too long, though, since it wouldn't be realistic for it to take only an hour or so to drive all the way around the entire state. Luckily, we did the math to determine just how long a day is in Leonida.

According to recent reports, every minute in Grand Theft Auto 6 lasts for five real seconds. That means every hour lasts 300 seconds, or five real-life minutes. With the same conversion for a 24-hour day, multiply that five minutes by 24, and you find that a full 24-hour cycle in the game lasts for two real-life hours.

This is significantly longer than a day in Grand Theft Auto 5, which lasted 48 real-life minutes. The scaling makes sense, though, because the map size matches each day's length. It has been revealed that while you can't directly speed up the clocks, you can skip chunks of time if you don't want to be out at a certain time. If you go to bed, you can choose to sleep, and setting your alarm will determine what time of day it is when you get up.

You can set the alarm for morning, noon, evening, or night. It doesn't matter what time you go to sleep; you can sleep until any time of day. If it's earlier than it at the moment is, it will move on to the next in-game day. That way, if you have tasks that depend on a certain time of day or require you to wait for the next day, you can skip the wait.

It has been revealed that luckily, there's plenty to do day or night in this game, and certain events only happen at certain times. It's good to know you can skip time if you need to, but there are lots of reasons to enjoy the day/night cycle to the fullest.

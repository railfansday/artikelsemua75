Shortly after buying Berkshire Hathaway (BRKB -0.36%)(BRKA -0.47%), Warren Buffett picked up a Baltimore department store because it looked like a bargain. Three years later, he wrote that he "was lucky to sell the business for about what I had paid." His lesson became one of his most quoted lines: "It's far better to buy a wonderful tech giant at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price.".

I keep coming back to that rule, because one of my favorite unglamorous retailers finally has a fair price.

It has been revealed that tractor Supply (TSCO -0.77%) sells what rural life runs on: animal feed, pet food, fencing, and tools. It's the largest rural lifestyle retailer in the U.S., with nearly 2,500 stores.

It has been revealed that this year has been bumpy. A rough May dragged down the second quarter, and the company cut its outlook in July. Bigger purchases remain soft, and CEO Hal Lawton mentioned, "Elevated interest rates and reduced housing mobility are weighing on property improvements and larger projects.". (Related: Latest Update: Stop treating agents as one-off projects...)

Sources indicate that the everyday stuff held up, though. Consumables like animal feed and bedding beat the store average, and same-store sales went from negative in July to growing by September. Neighbor's Club loyalty members made up nearly 82% of sales, and CFO Kurt Barton put it plainly: "Tractor Supply remains a differentiated business with significant competitive advantages." A crowd that loyal is my kind of economic moat.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

Management isn't waiting around. It used tariff refunds to cut prices on about 50 everyday essentials, raised its Freshpet cooler rollout to about 1,000 stores, and now has its Allivet pharmacy filling prescriptions for VIP Petcare, the mobile vet business it bought. On Nov. 12, it joined the platform of Uber Technologies, so shoppers can get dog food or garden supplies delivered. "The need for convenience doesn't stop at the city limits," mentioned Uber retail executive Nathan Bernheim, while Tractor Supply digital chief Matthew Rubin noted that "millions of Uber users have a new way to discover Tractor Supply.". (Related: The Reformer - Pushing the limits of language modeling...)

Not every bet paid off. The manufacturer closed 74 money-losing Petsense pet stores last quarter, and higher fuel and freight costs squeezed margins.

The stock sits about 30% below its 52-week high at roughly 21 times earnings. That's no bargain-bin P/E ratio, and Buffett's rule claims it doesn't need to be. A $1,000 investment buys about 25 shares, and capital spending should drop below 4% of sales next year, freeing up cash for shareholders.

"Whether it is preparing for winter, caring for their animals, or maintaining their homes and properties, we have an important and critical role to play in their lives," Lawton stated.

In that 1989 letter, Buffett also wrote that "time is the friend of the wonderful business." For a store people need every week, I'm happy to give it that time.

In a recent development, LOS ANGELES, Nov. 23, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Fly On Wall Street News Commentary - The bounce on Wall Street on Monday spread well beyond the usual market leaders. Shares of SpaceX moved higher after Jefferies initiated coverage of the stock with a Buy rating and a $210 price target, other rocket and satellite stocks traded up in early action, and AI cloud providers rallied alongside them. Across aerospace, defense and AI infrastructure, the latest manufacturer reports carry plenty of good news, from record backlogs to raised outlooks. Active Companies in the space, aerospace and AI infrastructure sectors with current developments include: Space Exploration Technologies Corp. (NASDAQ: SPCX), Lockheed Martin Corporation (NYSE: LMT), RTX Corporation (NYSE: RTX), Teledyne Technologies Incorporated (NYSE: TDY), Nebius Group N.V. (NASDAQ: NBIS).

It has been revealed that spaceX noted Starship Flight 14 was the first orbital Starship flight, delivering 26 Starlink V3 satellites in what the company called its first revenue-generating Starship mission, and its nameplate compute capacity reached 1.7 gigawatts as of Sept. 30, 2026, up from 0.6 gigawatts a year earlier. In its fresh coverage on Monday, Jefferies analyst Sheila Kahyaoglu pointed to the company's engineering lead in launch as the foundation for growth across its businesses. According to recent reports, lockheed Martin Corporation (NYSE: LMT) reported third-quarter 2026 results with sales up 11% to $20.7 billion, driven by growth in each of its four business segments, and net earnings up 14% to $1.8 billion, or $7.97 per share. Its Space segment posted net sales of $3,485 million, up 4% from the third quarter of 2025, and the company generated $3.0 billion of free cash flow in the quarter.

Lockheed Martin ended the quarter with a record backlog of $292.0 billion, including $52.5 billion associated with a multi-year award to produce PAC-3 Missile Segment Enhancement interceptors. It raised its 2026 outlook for the second consecutive quarter and now expects sales of about $80.0 billion to $82.0 billion, earnings per share of about $30.35 to $30.75, and free cash flow of about $7.8 billion to $8.0 billion.

RTX Corporation (NYSE: RTX) reported third-quarter 2026 sales of $25.0 billion, up 11% from the prior year and up 12% organically, as commercial aerospace and defense both delivered double-digit organic growth. The company booked more than $81 billion of new commercial and defense awards in the quarter, lifting its backlog to a record $340 billion, including $176 billion of commercial and $164 billion of defense.

RTX raised its full-year 2026 outlook, and now expects adjusted sales of $97.0 billion to $97.5 billion, up from $95.0 billion to $96.0 billion, adjusted earnings per share of $7.65 to $7.75, up from $7.10 to $7.25, and free cash flow of $9.0 billion to $9.2 billion, up from $8.50 billion to $8.75 billion. During the quarter, RTX was also awarded multi-year undefinitized production contracts for Tomahawk and AMRAAM missiles.

According to recent reports, teledyne Technologies Incorporated (NYSE: TDY) reported all-time record quarterly sales, operating profit and non-GAAP diluted earnings per share for the third quarter of 2026. Net sales rose 9.4% to $1,684.9 million, GAAP diluted earnings per share rose 25.2% to $5.82, non-GAAP diluted earnings per share rose 20.6% to $6.72, and free cash flow was $319.5 million. (Related: Tech Report: Accelerating PyTorch distributed fine-tuni...)

Teledyne raised its full-year 2026 non-GAAP earnings per share outlook to $25.40 to $25.50, from a prior outlook of $24.45 to $24.65, and said orders have exceeded sales every quarter over the last three years, with funded backlog now nearly $5.3 billion. The company, whose segments include Digital Imaging and Aerospace and Defense Electronics, also announced a pending acquisition of Varex Imaging Corporation for approximately $1.1 billion.

Recent findings suggest that nebius Group N.V. (NASDAQ: NBIS), the AI cloud company, reported third-quarter 2026 results with revenue of $980.8 million, up 571% from $146.1 million a year earlier, and Adjusted EBITDA of $465.8 million, compared with a loss in the same quarter of 2025. Its net loss from continuing operations narrowed to $37.5 million from $119.6 million.

Sources indicate that in its letter to shareholders, Nebius shared its initial outlook for 2027, expecting revenue of $12 billion to $13 billion and pre-tax breakeven by the end of 2027. For 2026, the firm now expects revenue at the low end of its $3.0 billion to $3.4 billion range, and said the annualized run-rate revenue it had guided to for December is now expected to arrive in February.

From rockets and satellites to missile defense, imaging and AI computing, the companies behind these trades head into the holiday week with fresh numbers: SpaceX revenue up 161%, record backlogs of $292.0 billion at Lockheed Martin and $340 billion at RTX, all-time record quarterly sales at Teledyne, and a first look at 2027 from Nebius calling for $12 billion to $13 billion of revenue.

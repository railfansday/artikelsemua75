Industry observers note that lookAtMyQuant is a quantization compiler. It measures how much each stored piece of a model hurts the output when compressed. It then picks a code for each piece so the whole model fits a size budget with the least damage.

On Gemma 4 E2B, its GGUF has 12× lower KL than Q4_K_M at the same file size. The method and every result are on the site.

According to recent reports, lookAtMyQuant 2 takes Qwen3.5-4B to 1.23 GB, 2.3 bits per weight. The file solves 330 of 540 exact-answer math problems. Prism ML's Bonsai 2 Ternary 4B solves 328 at 1.20 GB, and the original solves 359. Its KL from the original on held-out text is 0.45 nats, against 0.59 for Bonsai. (Related: Second Circuit denies Kalshi and Coinbase interim relie...)

Sources indicate that the recipe is post-training quantization with trellis codes, calibrated on the model's own worked math solutions with digit tokens weighted 10×, and with bits allocated per row by Fisher information. 4,000 steps of distillation follow, in which every layer is re-quantized once from the original weights. The page has the findings, the full recipe and its cost, about $34 of GPU time.

The recipe's code is here, and the next section runs it. The 1.23 GB file itself is the lm2-qwen35-4b-1.2gb release, packed for the Apple Silicon runtime.

It has been revealed that the same recipe takes Qwen3.8-27B from 53.7 GB to 7.2 GB, 2.1 bits per weight, calibrated and distilled on English prose and chat, code and math. The file solves 401 of the 540 math problems, as many as the original, and Unsloth's UD-IQ2_XXS GGUF of the same size solves 348. It also scores above that GGUF on HumanEval (73.8% against 67.1%), MMLU (78.8% against 75.0%) and GSM8K (94.5% against 92.7%).

Beside it sits a 4-bit DSpine drafter (1.02 GB, converted from tencent/Qwen3.8-27B-DSpine-b16 with runtime_mlx/convert_dspine.py and packed with pack_drafter.py), which roughly doubles decode speed on math and code on Apple silicon.

Extended to a 26.4B physical / 5.4B active 3×-looped MoE (L6–17 looped 3× = 48 logical visits, 128 experts top-8), the compiler and full-MoE proximal fine-tuning (SQYFR / SQYFR v2) compress thinkingmachines/Inkling-2-Mini from 18.89 GB of text weights into six Apple Silicon MLX builds spanning 8 GB iPhones up to lossless teacher parity: 3.85 GB (1.18 bpp), 4.72 GB (1.45 bpp), 5.38 GB (1.65 bpp), 8.87 GB (2.72 bpp), 11.18 GB (3.43 bpp), and 12.76 GB (3.92 bpp) — combining 4 KB SRAM hashed block-vector trellis codes (ht2/ht3), row trellis codes (rtr1/rtr4), dedicated attention projection floors, a tr2 floor on the tail lm_head bucket (head.7), and exact native NVFP4 passthrough (69/192 and 109/192 highest-sensitivity expert buckets kept at 0.0 error in the 11.2 GB and 12.8 GB builds).

Evaluated untruncated (max_new_tokens = 1,536, greedy, reasoning_effort="none") on the same BF16 teacher, openai/gsm8k (test[:200]), all 164 executable openai/openai_humaneval problems (strict <|content_text|> execution across every build), 4 Vision VQA tests (with the untouched 637 MB BF16 vision tower), and 16+13-language TextMix windows:.

At 12.76 GB, Inkling-2-Mini-LookAtMyQuant-12.8GB matches 100.0% of the untouched BF16 teacher on every benchmark (349 / 349 pooled) in 32.5% less text weight memory. Both 11.18 GB and 8.87 GB retain 99.2–99.3% 3-task mean (345 / 349 = 98.9% pooled), with 8.87 GB cutting KL 2.34× on English/chat/code (0.0208 vs 0.0486) and 29.6% across all 13 languages (0.0571 vs 0.0811) below Unsloth's 9.14 GB UD-Q2_K_XL GGUF. Below 2 bpp, SQYFR v2 (jointly training all 24.46B MoE experts and 130–144 attention trellis projections on position-0-aligned BF16 teacher <|content_thinking|> -> <|content_text|> rollouts with 6.0× control-boundary weighting, full-rank live Hessian projections, and 250 exact-P continuous scale + steering polish steps) lifts 5.38 GB (1.65 bpp) to 90.0% top-1 agreement (free_KL = 0.0864), 87.0% GSM8K, and 71.3% strict HumanEval (117 / 164), lifts 4.72 GB (1.45 bpp, sized to fit under the 12 GB iPhone 5.99 GiB Jetsam ceiling alongside the 179 MB drafter at 4.35 GiB active / 5.35 GiB decode peak) to 82.5% GSM8K and 67.1% strict HumanEval (110 / 164), and rescues 3.85 GB (1.18 bpp, 3.54 GiB active / 4.57 GiB decode peak for 8 GB devices) from static PTQ collapse (1.0% GSM8K, 1.8% HumanEval) to 78.5% GSM8K (157 / 200), 48.2% strict HumanEval (79 / 164), and 4 / 4 Vision.

All six run through lamq and runtime_mlx via unified single-launch Metal kernels (UnifiedMoESide mixing unrotated NVFP4 and Hadamard-rotated trellis experts across decode, speculative verify, and M5 MetalPerformancePrimitives prefill + FlashAttention-2 SDPA + exact unbiased temp > 0 speculative sampling), optionally paired with the 178.9 MB 4-bit DSpark drafters: on Apple M5 Max, 12.8 GB reaches 2,016 tok/s T=512 prefill, 60.5 tok/s serial, and 112.7 tok/s median lossless drafted decode (119.2 tok/s math, 106.2 tok/s code; 12.72 GB active RAM); 11.2 GB reaches 2,032 tok/s prefill, 62.4 tok/s serial, and 103.7 tok/s median (111.7 tok/s math; 11.13 GB active RAM); 8.9 GB decodes at 63.5 tok/s serial and 106.0 tok/s median (125.8 tok/s reasoning, 123.8 tok/s math; 9.41 GB active RAM); 5.4 GB reaches 1,807 tok/s prefill, 75.3 tok/s serial, and 124.4 tok/s median (142.9 tok/s math, 126.5 tok/s reasoning; 5.33 GB active RAM); 4.7 GB decodes up to 122.6 tok/s on math (98.7 tok/s median, 3.53 tok/check; 4.68 GB active RAM); and 3.9 GB decodes at 75.5 tok/s serial and 103.1 tok/s median (117.9 tok/s math, 128.3 tok/s short math; 3.81 GB active RAM).

runtime_mlx/fast_q35.py decodes the packed 1.2 GB Qwen3.5-4B file one token at a time, straight from its trellis codes. runtime_mlx/drafter_q35.py adds speculative decoding. A small drafter proposes the seven tokens after the model's latest one in a single pass. The model then checks all eight positions in one pass over its coded weights (runtime_mlx/block_q35.py) and keeps the proposals that match its own choice, so the text is exactly its own greedy text.

On an M5 Max, over 30 held-out prompts of up to 256 tokens, generation with the drafter was 2.0 times as fast: 176 tokens a second against 88, timed in alternation in one process on 2026-11-11. A check kept 4.3 tokens on average. Over eight everyday prompts it was 1.5 times as fast. The gain follows how predictable the text is: 2.5 times on algebra, 2.0 on code, 1.2 on chat and 1.05 on a story, and one of the 30 held-out prompts ran slower with the drafter. The text matched plain greedy decoding on every prompt. These are speeds after the first token; reading the prompt takes about 0.1 s either way.

Linear mode also enables self-calibrating adaptive fallback by default (adaptive=True; pass --no-adaptive to disable): when rolling acceptance drops below the measured break-even ratio of drafted-check latency to serial-step latency (accounting for cache flush and drafter context replay), it pauses drafting for short exact serial bursts and resumes once acceptance recovers, preserving bit-exact greedy parity while avoiding slowdowns on low-acceptance prose.

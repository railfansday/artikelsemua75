Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang on Tuesday struck a conciliatory and remorseful tone while leading his first employee meeting since being reinstated, and said how sorry he was for what he had done.

Huang told employees that he let them down and promised to do a better job the second time around, according to people inside the room, who asked not to be named in order to share details. "I hurt all of you," Huang remarked, according to one of the people. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

The CEO also addressed succession planning during the event, the people said. He told staff that Nvidia has built a team that doesn't require succession planning the way a normal organization does, because the tech giant operates a unique business model where there are multiple capable leaders at Nvidia that are leading different parts of the organization. A succession plan would create unnecessary hierarchy, Huang said, according to one of the people present.

Sources indicate that investors and analysts were left to speculate about the manufacturer's future leadership in the wake of Huang's resignation on Nov. 12.

Industry observers note that on Monday, Huang was reinstated as CEO of the chipmaker by Nvidia's board of directors. Eleven days before, Huang had resigned from Nvidia after multiple reported affairs, including one with a Chinese businesswoman who is alleged to be a spy for Beijing.

Harvey Jones, Nvidia's newly appointed board chair, said last week that Huang resigned after "disclosure of personal behavior inconsistent with the tech giant's code of conduct, standards and cultural norms.".

Huang started the meeting by thanking CFO Colette Kress for her leadership as interim CEO.

According to recent reports, huang told staff that Tuesday's company meeting was the second hardest meeting of his life, the first one being with his wife. He remarked he is in therapy, according to one of the people present.

In a press release announcing Huang's return on Monday, the company stated the board "received input" from senior officials in the federal government about any potential national security risk around the CEO. President Donald Trump told CNBC that he gave the board assurances and "made the deal" to restore Huang to his post.

After the company had its worst week in the stock market since 2008, the stock surged more than 30% the day his return was unveiled. Shares fell on Tuesday, however.

An Nvidia representative didn't immediately respond to requests for comment.

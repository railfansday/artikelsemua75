The resignation of Jensen Huang sent shockwaves through the investment world, with the longtime CEO of Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) stepping down following allegations of multiple affairs, including with subordinates and a potential Chinese spy. With its co-founder no longer at its helm, some things will certainly change for the manufacturer. (Related: Three 'e-waste' Nvidia V100s hit 100 tokens per second ...)

Let's look at three things that could go right for the semiconductor stock without Huang, and three things that can go wrong.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

Recent findings suggest that the biggest thing that could go right with Huang gone is that Nvidia's moat remains intact. The tech giant's moat has long been its CUDA operating system, on which most original artificial intelligence (AI) code was written and optimized for its graphics processing units (GPUs). However, with AI models now showing they can help run models on other chips in just a few days, this moat is promptly shrinking.

In a recent development, CUDA is not Nvidia's only edge, though, and right now it has a large supply chain moat. It has locked down a lot of future supply-constrained high-bandwidth memory (HBM) through a $500 billion deal with SK Hynix (SKHY -13.11%), and it also has the most access to fab capacity at Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing (TSM -9.48%). In a supply-constrained market, availability matters, and right now this is one of Nvidia's biggest advantages.

NVDA PE Ratio (Forward 1y) data by YCharts.

Nvidia is growing quickly, and that is expected to continue, with the company recently forecasting that its revenue would climb 75% in fiscal 2028. This growth is being led not just by surging GPU demand, but also by the company getting an ever-increasing piece of the data center revenue pie.

It has been revealed that with its Hopper architecture, Nvidia was capturing about $18 billion in revenue per gigawatt of capacity. Through networking, the addition of more chips like central processing units (CPUs) and language processing units (LPUs), and a more robust software stack, Nvidia is now getting $40 billion per gigawatt for its Vera Rubin System. Keep expanding its system to capture more revenue opportunities, and Nvidia's strong growth will continue.

It has been revealed that trading at a forward P/E of only 9 times fiscal 2028 (ending January 2028) analyst estimates, Nvidia's stock is cheap and has the potential for some substantial multiple expansion. The manufacturer will just have to show that it can continue to grow under newly introduced leadership.

Under Huang, Nvidia developed a close relationship with Elon Musk and SpaceX (SPCX -7.69%), which decided to exclusively build out its data centers on Vera Rubin. SpaceX is set to be one of the most aggressive companies in the space, and if the loss of Huang changes this relationship, it could be a blow to Nvidia's future growth.

Musk has already stated he wants Huang to join his tech giant, and if he does and the company pursues its own chips, it would be a blow to Nvidia.

According to recent reports, even before Huang's departure, there was a push to slow the data center build-out in the U.S., with a large contingent of anti-data-center candidates winning key seats in the midterm elections. This is most likely to impact neoclouds, a major source of revenue for Nvidia.

In a recent development, any delays not only reduce potential sales, but could trigger financing issues for these customers. Neoclouds and enterprise made up 44% of Nvidia's data center revenue in fiscal Q3, so this is no small matter.

Perhaps the biggest risk Nvidia faces with Huang gone is the potential for lost market share and margin compression. Custom AI accelerators and Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -13.25%) have been gaining share, and Nvidia's CUDA moat now appears to be dwindling.

In a recent development, if this causes the organization to slash prices to stay competitive, it would significantly reduce its gross margins, potentially putting pressure on earnings even if sales were to continue to grow. This would likely cause the stock to tumble.

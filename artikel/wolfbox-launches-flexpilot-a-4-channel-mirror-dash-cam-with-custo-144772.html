Recent findings suggest that now on Kickstarter, the FlexPilot dash cam combines flexible camera placement with a 12-inch, 1,500-nit display. Four-camera early-bird packages start at $569.

In a recent development, DALLAS, Nov. 23, 2026 /PRNewswire/ -- Wolfbox, a leader in dash cams and automotive electronics, has rolled out the Wolfbox FlexPilotâ¢ dash cam on Kickstarter. FlexPilot is the world's first off-road mirror dash cam with four customizable camera mounting positions, giving off-roaders more control over what they can see from the driver's seat. The FlexPilot's QuadFlexâ¢ system lets drivers install up to four cameras almost anywhere on a vehicle or trailer, with all four channels streaming live to a 12-inch rearview mirror touchscreen display. Standard dash cams only record the view through the front and rear windows. This might be fine for some city drivers, but off-roaders driving down a rocky trail will rapidly find that the view below the front bumper is what they actually need. Similarly, travelers often wish they could better monitor the lengthy blind spots on larger RVs, or keep an eye on a trailer hitch and the rear of a towed camper.

FlexPilot was designed to solve these problems. Instead of working around fixed camera positions, FlexPilot lets drivers choose what types of cameras are used and where they're mounted, customizing their very own camera system so that it is suited to their vehicle and the way they use it.

Custom views. Uncover the unseen.

The QuadFlex system supports up to four cameras, and there are five types of cameras to choose from: a 4K front camera, a 4K Flex camera and 1080p exterior, side and cabin cameras. The front, rear and Flex cameras sport Sony IMX678 STARVIS 2 sensors for excellent low-light visibility. A setup can combine up to two 4K and two 1080p cameras.

According to recent reports, when navigating a rock crawl or V-ditch, drivers care most about underbody clearance and the positions of individual tires. To keep an eye on these views, FlexPilot cameras can be mounted on the front bumper, on a fender or even under the chassis. With driveshaft repairs running from $1,500 to $4,000, it pays to see the trail ahead before committing to a line, especially in remote areas. Sources indicate that towing calls for a different set of views. A camera near the hitch helps the driver line up more easily when connecting a trailer. A camera installed on the back of a trailer gives a view of a campsite when backing in. On an RV, a side-mounted camera can give a different blind spot view that regular side mirrors cannot match. The Flex camera can be connected to the driver's choice of 21.3-, 32.8- and 49.2-foot (6.5-, 10- and 15-meter) cables to accommodate different RV and trailer installations.

The real-time mirror display is similarly flexible. Drivers can use the display as a regular rearview mirror when needed and switch to any single camera feed when approaching an obstacle. They can even display multiple views, including all four channels at once.

For drivers filming their trips, an optional cabin camera can record passengers and commentary alongside exterior footage. The cabin camera is sold separately.

Similar factory camera packages can cost more than $2,000. Wolfbox offers a lower cost aftermarket option for drivers who want extra views for trail driving or towing, with four-camera FlexPilot early-bird packages priced at $569.

Clear views at all times of day.

It has been revealed that having all those cameras makes little difference if the driver can't see the display. An exposed stretch of trail can cast glaring sunlight onto the screen, only to transition to a wooded section within a few yards, plunging the cabin into darkness. Wolfbox designed the FlexPilot with a 12-inch Adaptive Displayâ¢ that adjusts to these changes, reaching up to 1,500 nits in daylight and dimming in the dark.

FlexPilot has the brightest display of all Wolfbox dash cams, with peak brightness comparable to some flagship smartphone and television screens. The display limits reflections and ghosting in direct light and reduces glare at night, giving drivers a clear view in most lighting conditions without an overly bright or too-dim screen hampering their vision.

FlexPilot also includes an advanced driver assistance system (ADAS) with lane departure and forward collision warnings, a lead-vehicle start reminder and blind-spot detection, making the system also an ideal companion for everyday driving. Drivers have access to voice control, GPS and dual-band Wi-Fi 6, with support for memory cards up to 1 TB.

Fitting FlexPilot to the vehicle.

FlexPilot is designed for most SUV, pickup, trailer and RV setups, including popular off-road vehicle models from Jeep, Ford, Toyota, and other brands.

Depending on the installation, drivers can use an OEM-style bracket, straps or a center-console base. A universal OEM bracket is sold separately for $29.

The Flex, rear, exterior and side cameras carry IP67 dust and water protection for installation on the outside of the vehicle.

Kickstarter packages and availability.

According to recent reports, the FlexPilot campaign opened on November 17, 2026, and runs through December 31, 2026. Kickstarter has selected it as a "Project We Love.".

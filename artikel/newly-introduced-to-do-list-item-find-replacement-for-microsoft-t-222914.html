According to recent reports, if you, like me, are the type of person who makes a lot of to-do lists and can’t breathe a sigh of relief until you check items off them, I have some bad news. One of the best free options for organizing your work and home life is reaching the end of its life. Microsoft looks to retire its To Do app at the end of June 2027.

According to recent reports, we’ve seen no announcement or acknowledgment in the app itself that To Do, which debuted in 2017, would be retired. But several Microsoft-watching websites this week spotted a blog post from the company recommending people switch to its Planner app.

Sources indicate that microsoft To Do is retiring. For work or school accounts, Microsoft Planner will be the destination for managing personal work tasks alongside assigned work. The updated My tasks experience begins rolling out in December 2026.

Microsoft To Do apps will retire on June 30, 2027. This includes To Do on the web, Windows, Mac, iOS, and Android, for both work or school accounts and personal Microsoft accounts.

The underlying To Do service, APIs, and related synchronization support will retire on separate, later timelines. We’ll communicate those dates and any required preparation separately.

But there’s a big catch for Microsoft customers who are on 365 Personal or 365 Family plans: Planner will not be available to them. That means Microsoft is telling To Do users on those accounts to find a comparable app elsewhere if they’re not on a business account that includes Planner. The change was also mentioned in a separate Microsoft post about unified tasks and plans for Copilot and Microsoft 365 from Microsoft product manager Ashna Patel.

In a statement provided to CNET, Howard Crow, director of product, agents and AI at Microsoft, said the change consolidates task management across Microsoft 365. “We recognize this change will affect customers differently, particularly those using personal Microsoft accounts, and we’re committed to helping them prepare,” Crow said. (Related: Show, Don't Certify: How to Prepare for Your First Data...)

Crow mentioned the company will share more information about the transition in the coming months.

While the end of June 2027 may sound like a very long time from now, the path Microsoft has offered for To Do consumers is not a great one if you want to take action now. There’s no built-in export feature in the app, though in the blog post, Microsoft promises, “Microsoft will provide a way to export your To Do data before the apps retire. We’ll update this article with instructions, supported data, requirements, and limitations when those details are finalized.”.

Already, there are some pretty severe limitations even for those on Microsoft plans that support Planner: Existing To Do list items won’t work after that June deadline. “After that date, you will no longer be able to view or manage them in Planner,” the post mentioned.

In the meantime, you can shop around for alternate apps, some free, including Google Keep, ToDoist and Notion.

To Do consumers, myself included, are not happy. As Windows Latest reported, comments in response to Microsoft’s decision included, “Do you actually hate your users?”.

In a recent development, if this were simply a case of Microsoft creating software people didn’t like and then abandoning it, that would be one thing. But the To Do retirement is especially painful for those of us who were forced to move to Microsoft’s app a few years after it purchased Wunderlist.

It has been revealed that wunderlist was a German app that launched in 2011 and swiftly became popular for its slick interface, easy-to-enhancement task lists and its availability across web, desktop and mobile platforms. Microsoft absorbed some of Wunderlist into To Do and killed the original app in 2020 without ever offering all of the features that made Wunderlist great.

Industry observers note that as someone who used Wunderlist for years and has been using To Do, I can tell you that while To Do has been essential in my work and personal life, it never became as intuitive to use as Wunderlist. It never handled predictive task creation as well as Wunderlist did (such as understanding as you typed the date of a task or if it was repeating). It lacked features for sharing lists or collaborating on them, and simply didn’t look as stylish. As far as aesthetics, the best thing Microsoft did with To Do was add a Dark Mode.

Wunderlist was a joy to use. To Do always felt like the knockoff diet version of it and never quite as good.

After several years, I simply gave up on the idea that Microsoft would improve the app or add any fresh features, but kept using it out of necessity given the lack of a built-in export option.

The death announcement is not surprising. Just sad.

RIP To Do, but really RIP to the last remnants of Wunderlist.

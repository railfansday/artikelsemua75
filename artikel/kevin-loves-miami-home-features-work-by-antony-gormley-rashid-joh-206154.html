Ahead of Art Basel Miami Beach, which runs from December 4 to 6, NBA star Kevin Love reflects on his journey into art collecting, which has made him the proud owner of works by Antony Gormley, Rashid Johnson, Ed Ruscha and George Condo.

In an interview with Art Basel’s Miami Beach Magazine, the five-time NBA All-Star and 2016 NBA champion and his wife, Kate Bock, opened their Miami home to reporters, displaying an impressive art collection of paintings and sculptures. He reveals that he has Ed Ruscha’s The End (2021) hanging over his bed, a painting to which he feels a personal connection. “I hate to be one of those people who watches a lot of TV in bed, but we do, and The End has a connection to Ruscha and to film. It’s also very literal: It’s the end of the day, you’re in bed,” Love noted. “And it’s a reminder: I’m thinking about the end of a career. Whether it’s in two months or two years, it’s there; and with that, latest beginnings as well. I’ve always loved his work. He’s a great man, he’s empathetic and supportive. My wedding gift to my wife, Kate, was a Ruscha.” Love is also the owner of three other Ruscha artworks: Woman on Fire (2024), He Enjoys the Co. of Women (2025) and The Girl Always Did Have Good Taste (2021). Industry observers note that even though Love can’t pinpoint when his enthusiasm for art collecting started, he said that around the 2012 Olympics he learned his peers, including Carmelo Anthony and LeBron James, were interested in art. “That made the whole world feel a little less intimidating. I asked a lot of questions. ‘Where do you start?’ ‘What should I be looking at?’” Love said, noting that art adviser Jane Suitor has always encouraged him to stay “curious.”. In the magazine photo shoot, four works by Johnson are displayed: Untitled Anxious Red (2020), Run (2018), Falling Man (2015), and another painting from the Anxious Drawing series. Other pieces in his home range from Antony Gormley’s sculpture SMALL LOOK V (2015), a George Condo line drawing Untitled (2017), Richard Prince’s Untitled (Palomino) (1982–86), Hank Willis Thomas’ O! thus be it ever (2021), and John Baldessari’s Aerial View/People (1988).

Industry observers note that over the years, Love has built a friendship with artists Jonas Wood and Rashid Johnson. “Jonas Wood is a crazy, avid basketball fan, and my second favorite sport is tennis, so personal stories are initially where the connection resides,” noted the basketball player. “I’m very lucky to have Rashid Johnson in my life. I first connected [with the work] for emotional reasons without knowing much about him. I’m seven minutes down the road from his studio in the Hamptons.”.

It has been revealed that in the interview, Love recalls buying his wife a neon Tracey Emin heart with the words “An Insane Desire for You” written inside. He later followed up by buying her a 10-foot Dan Flavin in all his favorite colors, which they haven’t installed yet. “Turns out my wife doesn’t like neon,” Love mentioned.

Industry observers note that at Art Basel Miami Beach, Love will be joining the likes of Brooklyn Nets and Newly introduced York Liberty co-owner Clara Wu Tsai, soccer player Crystal Dunn, and NFL chief marketing officer Tim Ellis in the fair’s Art & Sport day, a series of panel talks between the art and sports worlds. Love and Keith Rivers, a former professional football player, will be in conversation with each other on December 3 to discuss “when athletes become collectors.”.

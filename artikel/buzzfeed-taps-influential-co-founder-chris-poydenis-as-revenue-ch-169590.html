BuzzFeed Taps Influential Co-founder Chris Poydenis As Revenue Chief

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The digital media publisher BuzzFeed has hired Chris Poydenis, the co-founder and longtime chief revenue officer of the influencer marketing manufacturer Influential, as its new chief revenue officer, the company announced Monday.

Poydenis spent more than 10 years building Influential, which he and his partners sold to Publicis in 2024 for $500 million, according to the tech giant. (Related: Industry News: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

Sources indicate that poydenis is also bringing along Jesse Batres, most recently the executive vice president and head of national sales and partnerships at Influential, to BuzzFeed, where he will serve as its new president of sales and partnerships. Both executives start Monday.

“I had breakfast with [CEO Byron Allen], and it didn’t take more than 30 minutes for me to be completely aligned and bought in,” Poydenis stated. “BuzzFeed already has such beloved brands—with Tasty and HuffPost. There’s an incredible base to build from.”.

The hires come at a precarious moment for BuzzFeed. Six months after Allen took a controlling stake in the tech giant, the publisher has cut more than a third of its staff, and its stock has cratered, all while it tries to reinvent itself as a video-first, free-streaming platform.

Whether that pivot works will depend largely on whether advertisers follow BuzzFeed into the living room, a job that will now fall to Poydenis and Batres.

Under its newly introduced ownership, BuzzFeed aims to pair content from its existing brands with premium content from Allen Media Group and distribute it across a connected television app, the website, and the mobile app, according to Poydenis. The publisher also intends to expand into user-generated and creator content, according to The Newly introduced York Times.

“If you look at what other streaming platforms are doing, they are all clamoring to figure out a way to infuse the creator economy into their product,” Poydenis remarked. “With [Batres] and I coming over, we’re in lockstep with [Allen] to make that a reality.”.

FOR Scoops, Sharp Analysis, and Insider Context.

The goal is to recruit more creators and build a platform where they can upload their libraries and monetize them with brands. In doing so, the creators would be adding to BuzzFeed’s 75 million monthly active owners and 2 billion monthly content views, according to Poydenis.

Still, the odds facing the firm are long.

Sources indicate that allen took over as chairman and CEO in May, after his holding manufacturer, Allen Family Digital, agreed to pay $120 million for a 52% stake: $20 million at closing and the remaining $100 million over five years. In tandem with the deal, cofounder Jonah Peretti stepped down as CEO to become its new president of AI.

Before the deal, BuzzFeed had warned investors that it was at risk of not continuing as a business, citing $58.4 million in debt and millions in annual losses, according to The Newly introduced York Times.

In July, the organization laid off about 180 employees across BuzzFeed, HuffPost, and Tasty, roughly 35% of its workforce, according to an SEC filing. Earlier this month, its stock fell 38% in a single morning after a disappointing third-quarter earnings report, sinking to $0.57 per share, according to Futurism.

For Poydenis, the turnaround is part of the appeal.

In a recent development, “At my heart, I’m a competitor, and I’m a builder,” Poydenis said. “After the acquisition with Publicis, I feel like for me the job was done. [Allen] is offering me an opportunity to go in somewhere with a phenomenal foundation and to build once again.”.

Mark Stenberg is ADWEEK's senior media reporter.

Senegal have been confirmed as champions of AFCON 2025 after the Court of Arbitration for Sport (CAS) upheld their appeal, bringing an end to an ugly months-long saga.

Morocco and Senegal met in the AFCON 2025 final, but the match was beset by unprecedented controversy.

Late in regular time, Morocco were awarded a penalty, which Senegal protested by leaving the pitch.

According to recent reports, players and staff, led by head coach Pape Thiaw, alike returned to the changing rooms, and the match was paused for 14 minutes. Some Senegal players remained on the pitch and returned to the pitch only at the behest of captain Sadio Mane.

Crucially, the referee never abandoned the match, and play resumed once the entire Senegal squad had returned to the pitch.

Brahim Diaz then missed the vital penalty and Senegal went on to win in extra time, with Villarreal’s Pape Gueye scoring the decider.

According to recent reports, in March 2026, two months after the final, CAF unveiled the shock decision to reverse the result, saying Senegal had forfeited the match by leaving the pitch. The result was thus changed to a 3-0 win for Morocco.

Senegal then appealed the decision at CAS, and have now been reinstated as champions after it was confirmed their appeal was upheld.

“Consequently, the result obtained at the end of the AFCON 2025 final is confirmed (1-0 for Senegal).”.

A decision shared in a press release on Tuesday explained: “By blowing his whistle to restart play, the referee considered that the conditions for resuming the match had been met – both teams were on the pitch again, and both expressed their willingness to resume.

“In the panel’s view, there is no doubt that the effects of the referee’s decision were limited to the pitch.”.

The panel decided that Articles 82 and 84 of the tournament rules, which formed the basis of Morocco’s appeal, were only applicable when a referee was forced to permanently call off a match. Given he allowed the match to restart and finish, those articles were no longer considered applicable.

“In conclusion, the panel considers that in resuming the match, the referee did not err in law,” the court press release continued.

“The panel acknowledged that the referee had to make a decision in an atmosphere of extreme tension. However, these circumstances do not justify describing the decision as ‘arbitrary’. On the contrary, the referee’s judgment did not appear to have been affected by factors external to the game.”.

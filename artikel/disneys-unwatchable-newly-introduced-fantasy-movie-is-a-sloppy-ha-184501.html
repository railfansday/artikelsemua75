Disney’s Unwatchable Newly introduced Fantasy Movie is a Sloppy Harry Potter-The Incredibles Mash-Up

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Hexed directors Fawn Veerasunthorn and Jason Hand really want to remind viewers of Disney's most magical movies. The fantastical realm of Hexe is filled with enchanted objects straight out of Beauty and the Beast. A parody of The Little Mermaid’s sea witch Ursula voiced by Jodi Benson, the star of the 1989 classic, hawks spells in the town square. The three-eyed cat Beef Roger Crummchuk (Walton Goggins) collects figurines, including the one sporting the hat Mickey Mouse wore in Fantasia. But rather than feeling like a love letter to Disney’s legacy, these Easter eggs come across as classic reference slop covering up a jumbled mess of a plot that buries some surprisingly dark ideas in cheerful chaos.

Hexed kicks off with a standard exposition dump explaining that Hexe was formed as a sanctuary for persecuted witches and wizards. Their realm is protected by a magical spell meant to be empowered through a ritual that the extra-special “chosen witch” will complete during an upcoming lunar event. Billie Doe (Hailee Steinfeld) opened a portal to Hexe when she was a baby, and her overprotective mom Alice (Rashida Jones) has been suppressing her daughter’s powers ever since. But when Billie accidentally regains her abilities and travels to Hexe, she runs afoul of its villainous ruler Queen Celeste (Jodie Foster). (Related: Course Launch Community Event...)

In a recent development, the story plays out like a chaotic mashup of classic Disney princess tropes, Harry Potter, and The Incredibles. There are lots of cute talking animals and pretty outfits perfect for dress-up – Billie makes a crack about needing a costume change before the final confrontation, then magics up a more stylish cloak. The first scene in Hexed is extremely reminiscent of a visit to Harry Potter’s Diagon Alley, packed with whimsical food, swooping owls, and people wandering around in robes and pointed hats. Like Mr. Incredible, Alice’s attempts to keep her family safe have trapped her in a comically dull life selling boxes in suburban Ohio. She and her daughter come to understand each other better while on an adventure to stop a villain who, just like The Incredible's Syndrome, has decided they should control how special people are. According to recent reports, hexed is a very pretty movie, using a vibrant neon-colored palette that’s particularly striking in creepier moments like when Celeste’s shapeshifting part-crow minions descend on Billie, or when the teen has to run from a centipede made of eyeballs. But screenwriters Jared Bush and Dana Ledoux Miller don’t seem to have a coherent reason to drive Billie to each new location for the next chaotic fight. At one point, Billie hits a dead end in her search for answers about the moon ritual and a passing kid just shouts at her that pirates have captured an owl who has the information she needs. How does he know this? How does the owl (voiced by Henry Winkler) know this? Why did the pirates want an owl? None of this is answered.

In yet another cinematic reference, Billie reveals she just needs to find her own version of the Yellow Brick Road to round up allies to fight the wicked witch. But unlike Dorothy’s friends, Billie’s followers don’t really want anything or have much personality beyond being voiced by notable actors. No one has to make a really difficult decision, and one moment that seems like a dramatic sacrifice is immediately undone for laughs.

Recent findings suggest that the incoherent plot might have been forgivable if Hexed did a better job at realizing its emotional core. A pair of scenes where Billie and her mom get inside each other’s heads are squandered by mostly repeating moments that already happened in the film, rather than actually showing each other or the audience anything fresh. The reason Alice kept Billie away from Hexe turns out to be a genuinely horrifying dark secret, but rather than really dealing with the fallout from that revelation, the film just switches Alice into cool mom mode. The story immediately moves past the trauma that shaped her parenting style and effectively declares Billie was right about everything.

The Super Mario Galaxy Movie was also manic, shiny, reference slop, and it made $1 billion. Hexed is unlikely to be that successful, but it leaves open plenty of possibilities for future adventures for kids who want pretty traditional stories of evil queens, good chosen ones, and grateful peasants. Hexed hints at a deeper, darker film that had something more to say about how mothers treat their daughters, but instead it just winds up being a sparkly way of repackaging concepts from more magical movies that came before.

According to recent reports, hexed premieres in theaters on Nov. 25.

However, tucked away in the small print in the organization’s support document are some exclusions. One of them is no surprise: EU countries.

Siri AI isn’t available at all in the EU due to Apple’s perception of potential antitrust problems. Essentially, Apple believes that if it launches Siri AI there, it will be required to give competing AI services the same level of access to its devices. But three other countries are specifically excluded:.

Apple gives no explanation, simply saying:.

Increased access to Siri AI with iCloud+ is subject to Siri AI availability and is not available in Brazil, the EU, UK, or South Korea.

However, I have a strong suspicion about the reason.

What all three countries have in common is that they’ve taken a strong antitrust stance with Apple on a number of occasions over a range of issues.

Sources indicate that it may be that Apple has genuine concerns about how regulators might respond if they effectively throw in free access to increased Siri AI usage. The firm may fear that this would be viewed as anti-competitive by implicitly disadvantaging other AI services.

A less charitable take would be that Apple is firing a warning shot across the bows of these countries, seeking to communicate that there are consequences for what it views as attacks on its ecosystem. If customers are angered by missing out, Apple might be hoping that these feelings would be directed at regulators rather than the iPhone maker.

It was both a surprise for guests, and a proper highlight, its lyrics resonating just as they did on release more than a quarter century ago.

Sources indicate that hayes wasn’t done there. He returned to the ARIAs stage to present Tame Impala spearhead Kevin Parker with the ARIA Global Impact Award, in recognition of his extraordinary international success and cultural impact in the past year.

For Hayes and his ex-Savage Garden bandmate Daniel Jones, the ARIA Awards is a warm and fuzzy place. At the 1997 edition, the pop group dominated with a then-record 10 wins, including album of the year and group of the year.

Savage Garden would collect a total of 14 career ARIAs, including the best-selling album award in 2000 for Affirmation, before disbanding in 2001.

The Brisbane group flew the flag high during their active years, and became one of a select handful of Australian groups to enjoy a No. 1 on the Billboard Hot 100. Indeed, they did it twice: with 1998’s “Truly Madly Deeply,” and again in 2000 with “I Knew I Loved You,” while their 1997 track “I Want You” also entered the top 5 (peaking at No. 4). Both albums (Savage Garden at No. 3, Affirmation at No. 6) cracked the top 10 of the Billboard 200. Career album sales top 35 million units worldwide.

Earlier this year, Hayes was feted with the Excellence in Community Award at the 17th annual Music In The House charity fundraiser, in recognition both of his work with Savage Garden and his ongoing solo career, and for his community leadership and advocacy for the LGBTQIA+ community.

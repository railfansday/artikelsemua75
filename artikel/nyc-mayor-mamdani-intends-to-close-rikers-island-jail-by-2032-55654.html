Recent findings suggest that the New York City Council voted in 2019, under former Mayor Bill de Blasio, to close Rikers by Aug. 31, 2027. But meeting that deadline is widely considered impossible, particularly after delays under former Mayor Eric Adams, who opposed the borough-based jails being built to replace Rikers.

Mamdani aims to reduce the daily jail population from 6,600 to 4,100 through alternatives to detention, programs to reduce recidivism and efforts to reduce court delays.

It has been revealed that “It is an extensive, rigorous proposal,” said Mamdani, who appeared at on crutches at Friday’s announcement, explaining that he had torn his Achilles tendon during a friendly soccer match against members of the press on Thursday night. The first borough-based jail, under construction in Brooklyn, is set to open in 2029, followed by facilities in Queens and the Bronx in 2031 and Manhattan in 2032. Upcoming York City Council will have to approve the deadlines.

“These jails will look, feel and most importantly operate very differently than Rikers,” Mamdani remarked.

Rikers has become notorious for filthy conditions, deteriorating facilities and understaffing that contribute to widespread violence, injuries and illness. Five people have died in its jails in 2026 alone.

Sources indicate that better access to public transit would ease family visits, while jail reform advocates say keeping detainees connected to their communities and courts is essential to fair proceedings and successful reentry. Most people held at Rikers are awaiting trial.

“Any family member of someone held at Rikers will tell you how isolating that jail is. How it can take hours to visit a loved one. How far away you feel from the rest of the city,” Mamdani said.

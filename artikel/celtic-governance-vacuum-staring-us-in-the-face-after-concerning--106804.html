Celtic are facing an obvious "governance vacuum" after the release of their latest round of club accounts.

That is according to former Aberdeen chief executive Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who believes that the Bhoys' latest figures make for hugely concerning reading. (Related: Supermicro sued by shareholders over claims Nvidia exec...)

Celtic's accounts for the financial year ending in June 2026 showed a £6.6million loss overall, compared to a £45m profit from the previous year. (Related: LA Noire 2 leak resurfaces after source gets GTA 6 endi...)

Wyness believes that Celtic's accounts show the value of a strong European run, but also outlined that the executive team deserve to be questioned for the accounts.

The club hierarchy have faced continual protests from the fans throughout the last 12 months, but a potential sale is seemingly not on the cards right now.

Wyness believes that the financial issues at Celtic are clear to see for all concerned, but doubts whether the board are picking up on them right now.

Aberdeen's former chief Wyness - who served as CEO at Pittodrie between 2000 and 2004 and now runs a consultancy business advising elite clubs - believes that Celtic's challenges are "staring us in the face" at the moment.

The Glasgow giants' accounts revealed a drop in revenue of around 23 per cent, and worrying numbers across the board.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider's Inside Track podcast, Wyness explained how the financial problems are stemming from the leadership at the top of the club.

Recent findings suggest that "Look at these, read those numbers again. Yeah, I'd be concerned, definitely. I think costs went up 43 per cent and revenue was down 23 per cent. I mean, these are not good numbers and not good trends," he noted.

Recent findings suggest that "To me, those numbers show the governance vacuum again, and it's a management problem. We've been critical all the way through, and I think I haven't wasted any words on that.

Sources indicate that "I've been critical about the Celtic executive team, and these numbers just justify what we've been saying. So look, not good results at all. That's the victim of getting knocked out of Europe and losing those sorts of income streams.

Industry observers note that "We've explained why and how they could have fixed those. And if it's staring us in the face, I don't know why action isn't being taken appropriately.".

Football Insider revealed ealier this week that Celtic are pushing for a deal to sign Le Havre midfielder Simon Ebonog in January.

The 22-year-old has emerged as a clear target for the Bhoys this winter, as they aim to bolster their midfield ranks for Martin O'Neill.

Sources say that Celtic believe Ebonog can help to add more energy and dynamism to their midfield ranks.

He is understood to be available for a fee of less than £5m this winter, a deal which Celtic are sure to be able to afford.

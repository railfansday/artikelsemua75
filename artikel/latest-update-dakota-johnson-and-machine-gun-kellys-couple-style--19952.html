According to recent reports, dakota Johnson and Machine Gun Kelly have much more in common than their fondness for leather jackets and a little rock-and-roll romance. Very good handbags, for one.

On Thursday evening, the pair were photographed leaving a restaurant in rainy Los Angeles, bundled up against the chill and threats of floods. Johnson wore a long chocolate-brown leather coat over charcoal gray tailored trousers and glossy black loafers, with a voluminous emerald-green scarf wrapped around her neck. Kelly, meanwhile, opted for an oversized black leather jacket layered over a hoodie, wide-leg black pants, extremely chunky shoes, and a black-and-white hand-print knitted beanie. Industry observers note that johnson carried a generously proportioned, vintage leopard-print Prada bag with black leather straps—a familiar favorite that’s accompanied her on previous outings with Kelly. Kelly, meanwhile, appeared to be sporting a capacious black-and-white Louis Vuitton tote from the house’s 2021 collaboration with Swiss artist Urs Fischer. The collection saw Fischer playfully reinterpret the house’s famous Monogram, recreating its motifs from memory in a hand-drawn pattern, with a velvet-like tufted finish that makes for a tactile bag. It’s an interesting accessories pairing of equally loud and collectible pieces. And both suggest an appreciation for a look where the bag is the outfit. According to recent reports, dakota Johnson, of course, has long been a serious handbag enthusiast, with a wardrobe that runs from classic Gucci Jackies to studded Valentino shoulder bags. MGK has also been spotted on more than one occasion with a trusty oversized denim Hermès Birkin. Clearly, neither is particularly interested in keeping their accessories understated.

As their couple style continues to develop, the two seem increasingly in sync without dressing identically. There’s a heck of a lot of leather, increasingly oversized proportions, and affections for an aesthetic that’s equal parts offbeat and glam-rock. And who notes an It-bag has to be hers alone?

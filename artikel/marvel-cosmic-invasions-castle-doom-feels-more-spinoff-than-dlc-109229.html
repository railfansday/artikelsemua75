Marvel Cosmic Invasion’s Castle Doom Feels More Spinoff Than DLC

According to recent reports, adding a roguelike or roguelite mode to a game post-launch to keep people playing has become a popular and pretty effective trend. The Last of Us Part 2 and God of War Ragnarok are just two examples of games that have been praised for their roguelike DLC, but despite the trend’s success and my love for almost all things roguelike, I was skeptical of whether it was the right move for Marvel’s Cosmic Invasion.

Although I really should have had faith in Tribute Games to do right by roguelite expansions after what they did with Shredder's Revenge. (Related: LA Noire 2 leak resurfaces after source gets GTA 6 endi...)

I’m pleased to report that my skepticism was misplaced. Not only does it turn out that the building blocks set in place by Cosmic Invasion’s base game were the perfect foundation on which to build an unexpectedly entertaining roguelite mode, but the upcoming blocks placed on top of them have made its Siege of Castle Doom expansion feel more like a spinoff than a DLC. (Related: Build A Racetrack codes (November 2026)...)

I liked Cosmic Invasion a lot. So much so that ever since I returned to lay siege to Castle Doom, I’ve been racking my brains trying to figure out why I fell off before I made it to the end of the base game. I love everything Marvel (maybe a little too much), and started playing video games in the ‘90s, so naturally a 2D beat ‘em up designed to look like it could have been released during that golden era for the genre ticks just about all my boxes.

It’s also the best this revived genre has had to offer in an era where we have been spoiled by classic beat ‘em ups tied to IP.

Recent findings suggest that there’s no risk of me falling off the Cosmic Invasion bandwagon again, at least not until Doctor Doom is dealt with, both in the game and on the substantial screen. The Castle Doom DLC adds what is effectively a roguelite mode. Choose your heroes and lay siege to the castle, progressing up through its various levels, the enemies you face getting more powerful, and the man himself, the formidable Doctor Doom, waiting for you at the end.

According to recent reports, like a roguelite, you progress by clearing rooms or areas so that you can move onto the next one. You’re presented with options as you move between rooms; the symbols on the door representing what you’ll get or face in the next, very similar to the decisions you’re presented with when moving between rooms in Hades.

In a recent development, the green symbol, for example, means you’ll enter a room where you can refill your health. The blue symbol, however, will present you with more battery charges. These are key, as you need to charge your batteries to make it deeper into the castle. You have to do this before Doom’s gauge - the green one in the opposite corner of the screen - is full. Enemies get faster and more aggressive as it fills, and once it’s full, your run ends.

Once a run ends, some of those battery charges come with you. They can then be exchanged in Reed’s Lab to unlock items and events for your subsequent runs, potentially making them less challenging. Again, leaning into those roguelite elements. You can also select temporary buffs that expire at the end of each run after every three full charges, having to choose between improvements that will help you attack, defend, or boost your tactical prowess.

Recent findings suggest that I’m really only scratching the surface of what you can do in this newly introduced mode, which is why, again, it feels more like its own game than an extension of Cosmic Invasion. I’ve not even touched on the two new characters it adds to the roster yet either - Invisible Woman and Doctor Doom.

Yes, you can be Doom to fight Doom, and he might be the best character in the game.

The two fresh additions continue a trend of something so difficult to nail in the beat ‘em up genre, and that’s making sure every character feels unique. Cosmic Invasion’s roster is already bigger than most beat ‘em ups’, so another two characters feeling this different again is quite the feat.

Doom is, naturally, all about feeling powerful. Not only is his moveset easy to learn and master, but everything he does feels big and impactful. So much so that using him is almost like lowering the difficulty. I mean, he’s battling through a castle built by his variant, so that kind of makes sense.

Invisible Woman is the exact opposite. Her move set is so different from anyone else in Cosmic Invasion that it took a few runs for me to really get a feel for how she works. She shields herself rather than blocks or evades, just like in the comics and the movies, and to really get the most out of her, you need to be attuned to what she can do and what she’s capable of. That includes turning invisible and phasing through enemies when you sprint, and throwing up what is effectively a shield that also deals damage when you hold square (or the relevant button on your platform of choice).

In a recent development, even when using characters who have been in the game since launch, it feels entirely different. No, the DLC technically hasn’t altered them in any way, but the upcoming mode poses a new challenge. You’re not just fighting to the end of a level anymore. This is an onslaught that’s going to take time and tenacity. You need to be more methodical in your approach. You can’t just wade in with Wolverine and take damage while you slash away. Do that, and you’ll be done before your first full charge.

Cosmic Invasion getting Doom DLC two months before Avengers: Doomsday is, of course, no accident. We are, unfortunately, well beyond the era of movies being able to have tie-in games that release simultaneously. Instead, studios have to come up with more inventive ways to create brand synergy, and this is a fantastic way to do it.

As I may have already mentioned, I’m a huge Marvel fan. Doomsday is the most excited I’ve been about a movie since Avengers: Endgame, and anything that gives me more Doom between now and then is incredibly welcome. It helps that it’s as well done as the Castle Doom DLC, and it will be an expansion I keep playing after Doomsday has rolled through and the terrifying latest MCU villain has torn through all of the heroes I’ve formed emotional attachments to over the last two decades, thoroughly ruining my Christmas.

He’s going to kill everyone, isn’t he?

Recent findings suggest that as much as I like Cosmic Invasion, it never struck me as a game that would have a long shelf life, because beat ‘em ups rarely do. Tribute Games has gone above and beyond to keep the game in the zeitgeist and put it back on the radar in a major way just in time for what may well end up being the biggest theatrical release ever.

If you fell off of Cosmic Invasion for whatever reason, like me, or you finished the game, you now have the perfect reason to go back. The Siege of Castle Doom forces you to reinvent your approach, feels like an entirely fresh experience without detracting from the fantastic game Cosmic Invasion already was, and best of all, it only costs $7.99. Even if you defy the odds and take Doom out on your first run, it’s worth $8 for the euphoric feeling you’ll have if you somehow manage to pull off such a feat.

You definitely will not pull off such a feat.

Recent findings suggest that the #SaveMassEffect petition has rallied over 300,000 signatures, urging EA to resume development on Mass Effect: Universe with "sufficient funding, staffing, and time"—failing that, it called on the publisher to sell or license the IP to another studio. EA has not responded to the petition or the calls on social media for the series to continue, so, aside from the upcoming Prime Video series, the future of Mass Effect is in limbo. However, Commander Shepard herself has an idea on how to get the Normandy and its crew back on course.

In an interview with GamesRadar+, Jennifer Hale mentioned she was actually "excited and hopeful" when BioWare shut down, because she has "this vision of all these fans coming together, making a glorious Kickstarter, just like the folks at Critical Role did.".

Her idea, plainly, is that instead of just petitioning to Save Mass effect, fans should pool together the funds to buy the IP from EA outright. "One of those kind of Kickstarters," she mentioned. "Games are expensive, so maybe one or two well-heeled individuals who enjoy investing in creative ventures come along and fortify that. Then an indie game company, or any game company with openness and vision that's story-driven and community-conscious, comes into the picture to make it so.". Hale states that the group who take over the series should be known as the "Mass Effect collective," and assert "governing rules around the IP.". (Related: Breaking: Elon Musk states he regrets midterm spending...)

It's hard to imagine EA being willing to sell one of its franchises to fans, especially through a Kickstarter that would need some hefty investment. A self-governing body of fans is also admittedly a messy way to handle an IP — rather than it being bought back directly by veteran talent or other dev teams who could spearhead latest games under a more structured vision. With that being said, Hale remains optimistic about her idea, and suggests that a small portion of whatever the community makes could even go into "a fund that helps displaced devs.".

In a recent development, "EA can't buy that kind of publicity," she stated. "You cannot buy that kind of goodwill. The love that people and appreciation people would have for you for a long time, and the hats off to you would be invaluable. I have a business degree, and there is such a thing on the books called goodwill, and it is invaluable.".

Mass Effect trilogy director Casey Hudson, however, thinks that the impact from fans will look a little different. He recently said that he remains "optimistic" about the future of the series, and believes that the outpouring of support from the community will pave the way for its "triumphant return", pushing EA to do something with it in the future.

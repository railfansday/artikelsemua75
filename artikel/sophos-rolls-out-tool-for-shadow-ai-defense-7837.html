According to recent reports, unauthorized artificial intelligence tools and agents are creating security risks for organizations as AI adoption increases, according to cybersecurity organization Sophos. In a recent product announcement, the company highlighted the growing use of shadow AI, referring to AI tools and agents operating without formal organizational approval or oversight. The security firm cited research indicating that 69% of organizations suspect or have evidence of unauthorized AI use.

Separate research from Gartner and Cloud Security Alliance found that 82% of organizations discovered shadow AI agents in their environments during the previous year. (Related: My Journey to a serverless transformers pipeline on Goo...)

According to the security firm, organizations have struggled to identify which AI tools employees use, what information those tools access, and whether their use complies with security policies.

"Organizations are adopting AI faster than they can secure it, and most security teams can't answer three basic questions: which AI tools are running, what data is going into them, and whether that activity lines up with policy," stated Kyle Falkenhagen, senior vice president of product management at Sophos. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

The organization also cited an IDC projection that the number of AI agents could reach 1.3 billion by 2028.

Sophos researchers reported observing attackers use AI agents within a customer's network to build and test nearly 80 malware modules.

The cybersecurity firm revealed the general availability of Sophos AI Defense, a security offering designed to identify AI tools, monitor their activity, and enforce organizational security policies.

The product operates through Sophos Fusion, the firm's cybersecurity platform, and uses information from devices, networks, identity systems, and browsers.

According to the manufacturer, AI Defense can inspect prompts, uploads, and other AI interactions to identify potentially risky activity and help prevent sensitive data exposure.

For more information, go to the Sophos site.

A new study from consulting firm KPMG found that while AI investment continues to grow, relatively few organizations consistently measure whether the technology's benefits justify its costs.

Network-provider trust concerns are impacting enterprise AI looks, with AI and data-driven projects the most affected initiatives among respondents reporting disruption, according to a report from telecommunications provider Arelion.

SMART Technologies has introduced SoundFlow, a plug-and-play broadcasting system that allows classroom audio content to flow directly from a SMART Board display to students' compatible hearing aids, earbuds, headphones, personal listening devices, or speakers.

CrowdStrike's latest Threat Hunting Report traces a multiyear shift from conventional intrusions toward attacks that exploit trusted identities, cloud services, AI systems, and software dependencies.

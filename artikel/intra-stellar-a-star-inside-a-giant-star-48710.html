📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that towards the end of their lives, stars experience a drastic change in their structure as they swell and enter what is known as their giant phase. Because most stars are thought to host orbiting companions (such as planets, other stars or black holes), interesting interactions can happen as the giant host expands. The most striking of these occur when a companion star is engulfed by a giant: astronomers refer to this as the common-envelope phase1. Despite theoretical expectations that common envelopes should be widespread in stellar evolution, they have been extraordinarily difficult to observe. Writing in Nature, however, Karambelkar et al.2 report the first direct detection of a star inside a giant star.

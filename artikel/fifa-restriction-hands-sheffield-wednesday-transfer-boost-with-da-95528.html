A new era is underway at Sheffield Wednesday following the appointment of Dave Challinor as the club's new manager.

Wednesday parted company with Henrik Pedersen last month after an inconsistent start to life back in League One, and they did not take long to appoint former Stockport County boss Challinor as his replacement. (Related: Stop treating agents as one-off projects and start runn...)

Challinor had been out of work since leaving Stockport in June following a near five-year spell at Edgeley Park, during which he led the Hatters from the National League to League One, and he oversaw two consecutive play-off finishes in the third tier in the last two seasons.

Indeed, Challinor, who has also had stints with the likes of AFC Fylde and Hartlepool United, boasts the impressive record of finishing in the play-off places or higher in each of his 15 full seasons as a manager, winning seven promotions during that time.

Challinor had long been linked with Wednesday due to his relationship with Owls sporting director Simon Wilson, who he worked with previously at Stockport, and the pair may already be looking ahead to the January transfer window.

Wednesday brought in 18 new signings in the summer as part of a rebuild of the squad following David Storch's takeover, but while the need for further reinforcements has been clear in the opening months of the season, Challinor's main January wish may involve one of his existing players. Forward Davis Keillor-Dunn became Wednesday's final signing of the summer when he joined on loan from Championship side Wrexham, with the deal running until 2nd January.

Short-term loans are relatively uncommon in the EFL these days, but with the Owls still under financial restrictions following their exit from administration, Wilson explained that the length of the deal was due to "the capacity we had in our budget", claiming that the club "emptied the piggy bank" to bring him to Hillsborough.

Wilson also revealed that Wrexham had received "higher offers" for Keillor-Dunn, and the 29-year-old had "other options" on the table, but he was "really adamant he only wanted to come and play for Sheffield Wednesday".

Given that Keillor-Dunn scored 35 goals and provided six assists in 78 appearances during a prolific spell at Barnsley in League One prior to making the move to Wrexham in January for a fee of around £2 million, it is clear to see why the Owls were so keen to land his signature, and they will certainly be pleased they went the extra mile to get the deal done.

Keillor-Dunn took a little time to find his feet in South Yorkshire as he failed to score in his first three appearances for Wednesday, but he has established himself as his side's main attacking threat in recent weeks as he begins to rediscover the goalscoring touch he displayed on a regular basis at Oakwell.

The January transfer window may still be more than a month away, but talk among Wednesday supporters has already started about whether the club will be able to keep hold of Keillor-Dunn.

Given Keillor-Dunn's recent form, there will surely be plenty of other League One clubs wishing they could sign him once his loan deal at Hillsborough expires, but FIFA regulations mean that a fresh move elsewhere in January is highly unlikely.

Prior to making the switch to Wednesday, Keillor-Dunn made two appearances for Wrexham in the early stages of the season, featuring as a substitute in their EFL Cup defeat at Middlesbrough and opening Championship game at Cardiff City, and the rules state that he is unable to play for a third club within a single campaign.

That does not necessarily stop Keillor-Dunn signing for another club, as players are allowed to be registered with three clubs in a season, but they are only to play official matches for two of them, so there would be little benefit to him making an alternative move in the winter window.

Wilson referenced the regulation after Keillor-Dunn's arrival at Hillsborough in September, and when discussing a possible extension beyond January, he admitted that "the odds are somewhat in our favour in the sense that he can't go to another League One team".

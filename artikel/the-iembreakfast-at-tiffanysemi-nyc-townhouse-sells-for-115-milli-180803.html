It has been revealed that holly Golightly might have had questionable taste in men, but she had a keen eye for real estate. Memorably portrayed by Audrey Hepburn in Breakfast at Tiffany’s, Golightly lived in a handsome Upper East Side brownstone that had been subdivided into apartments. Though the 1961 classic film shot its interior scenes on a Los Angeles soundstage, the exterior of the New York City townhouse served as a backdrop throughout the movie.

In May 2025, the five-story residence hit the market for $15 million, following a total overhaul that included a slew of luxe upgrades, such as a new roof terrace and an elevator. Although StreetEasy listed the property as “in contract” last December, its official sale date is November 11, 2026. In the end, the two-family house traded hands for $11.5 million, roughly 23 percent off the original price, per Realtor.com. (Related: House Democrats will start next Congress with largest m...)

Set on a tree-lined block between Lexington and Third Avenues, the East 73rd Street home is distinguished by its Tiffany Blue front door and matching window casings. Built in 1866 by architect John Sexton, the Italianate-style landmark encompasses nearly 4,500 square feet and has four bedrooms in total, three in the main residence—which spans the top four floors—and one in the garden-level apartment, which has its own separate entrance. The seller was retired beverage executive Joseph Harkins, who acquired the property in 2015 for $7.4 million and spearheaded a complete transformation that modernized the interiors while still preserving the house’s 19th-century charm. The project even included excavating the basement level, which now has nearly eight-foot ceilings, a powder room, a second laundry room, and a wine cellar.

In a recent development, outside, the 470-square-foot rear garden is fully enclosed by Walpole lattice fencing and sports stonework paving. It will no doubt be the scene of plenty of “Breakfasts” for the home’s newest steward.

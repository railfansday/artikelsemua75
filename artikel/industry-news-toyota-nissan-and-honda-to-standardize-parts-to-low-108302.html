Japan’s three largest automakers have agreed to begin standardizing vehicle parts in an effort to reduce costs, according to Automotive News. The outlet reports that Japan Automobile Manufacturers Association chairman Koji Sato, who is also the vice chairman of Toyota, confirmed the decision at a news conference in Tokyo this week.

In the short term, the standardization efforts will begin with unified quality criteria for steel, consistent protocols for plastics and resins, and a universal color-coding scheme for wiring harnesses. In the long term, the Japan Automobile Manufacturers Association has looks to move to more components that cover the entire car. The three areas were chosen as the first to be standardized because they are parts that are generally not noticed by consumers, and they shouldn’t have an impact on any brand’s individual identity. All three are also areas that account for significant production costs for the automakers, meaning they can have an immediate impact. The push to simplify and lower costs comes as Japan’s seven automakers, including Toyota, Nissan, Honda, Mazda, Subaru, Mitsubishi, and Suzuki, work to fend off the increasingly competitive list of Chinese automakers. The automakers are mentioned to hope that they can channel the money saved by homogenizing into fresh innovations.

According to recent reports, “We all share this strong sense of crisis, creating a momentum for change,” Sato said. “Based upon our sense of a crisis, we decided to choose these three areas.”.

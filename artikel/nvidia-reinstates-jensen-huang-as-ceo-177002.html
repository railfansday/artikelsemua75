Nvidia Reinstates Jensen Huang as CEO

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Nvidia on Monday announced Jensen Huang has been reinstated as CEO, effective immediately, a dramatic twist in a scandal that hammered the chipmaker's stock price and rocked the tech and artificial intelligence industries.

Industry observers note that the board of directors said it unanimously voted to reinstate Huang following "a series of discussions and meetings" with him. The board added that it "received input from senior levels of the United States government" and that it is confident Huang's reinstatement "poses no national security risk.". President Donald Trump told CNBC on Monday that he had been speaking to Nvidia's board "directly and indirectly," and that Huang "did nothing wrong." Trump noted he talked to Huang on Sunday and asked him to promise to "work hard.". (Related: Industry News: I Asked About Dirty Dishes. You Delivere...)

Sources indicate that huang resigned from his role as chief executive earlier this month after a series of shocking reports about multiple extramarital affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese spy. The Fresh York Times reported on Sunday that the woman, Alice Huang, was in contact with an officer of China's Ministry of State Security during at least part of the time that she was engaging in a relationship with Huang.

Shares of Nvidia closed up 31% following Huang's reinstatement. The stock had shed nearly 33% from the time Huang admitted to an extramarital affair on Nov. 8. The stock closed at $186.64 on Nov.6.

"I am honored to return to NVIDIA and lead one of the most consequential technology companies in the world at the beginning of a latest industrial revolution," Huang noted in a statement. "I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment. I want to thank the NVIDIA Board for its confidence and deliberation through this process.".

Senior government officials briefed Nvidia's board late last week and communicated that no national security laws were violated, according to sources familiar with the discussion who asked to remain anonymous because they were not authorized to speak publicly. That update prompted the board to reach out to Huang, the people noted.

Huang and the board held their first conversations since his ouster on Saturday, and the group determined he would return as CEO during a second meeting on Sunday, the people mentioned. Nvidia's cratering stock price played a role in accelerating those conversations.

Trump celebrated Huang's return to Nvidia on Monday, writing in a post on his social media site that the board made "the correct and proper decision" to reinstate him. Trump said the U.S. needs Huang's leadership to "ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Firm, but the American Economy.".

He reiterated that the U.S. government has "no reason to believe" Huang's return poses a risk to national security, and that it "does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct.".

"On a personal note, I have gotten to know Jensen, and he is a Great Patriot and National Treasure," Trump wrote.

It has been revealed that several tech industry leaders, including Elon Musk, CEO of SpaceX, Gavin Baker, managing partner and chief investment officer at Atreides Management, and Brad Gerstner, Altimeter Capital CEO, had been actively lobbying for Huang's return. Musk wrote in a post on X on Monday that the board's "mistake has been corrected.".

Nvidia did not immediately respond to CNBC's request for comment.

In a recent development, for weeks following Huang's ouster, the company's board maintained that it was not considering Huang's reinstatement. It appointed Colette Kress, Nvidia's chief financial officer, as the chipmaker's interim CEO, who reiterated that position in an interview with CNBC following Huang's exit. Kress will continue to serve as Nvidia's CFO, according to a filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission.

Nvidia's board formed an independent committee and hired the law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to conduct an investigation into Huang and whether firm secrets or national security were compromised. The board said Monday that "the committee's investigation will proceed to conclusion" and that Huang is "committed to cooperating with it.".

"The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers," Nvidia's board chair, Harvey Jones, said in a statement. "We have every confidence in Jensen and look forward to working with him as he resumes his leadership of the Company.".

Even with Huang back at the helm, Nvidia is facing increased scrutiny over its chip sales in Southeast Asia and China.

Lawmakers have been preparing for investigations into how Huang's affair with the alleged spy may have damaged U.S. AI dominance, influenced his positions on export controls, and impacted his close relationships with Trump and his Cabinet. Huang has been one of the loudest voices opposing strict export controls on advanced chips.

"The reckless decision to bring Jensen Huang back as CEO does not change the fact that Mr. Huang, NVIDIA executives, and the company's board still owe Congress answers about the company's compliance with U.S. export controls," Sen. Elizabeth Warren, D-Mass., remarked in a statement Monday.

It has been revealed that huang is due for a closed-door transcribed interview with the House Foreign Affairs panel and two other committees on Dec. 1. Nvidia's general counsel, Tim Teter, is scheduled to appear before the panels on Dec. 2. Those dates were set before Huang's return, and John Fisher, who represents Huang, did not comment on whether there are any changes in his looks to testify.

It has been revealed that senate Banking Committee Chairman Tim Scott, R-S.C., noted Monday that Huang will publicly testify on Dec. 3 as the sole witness during a hearing titled, "Protecting America's AI Advantage: Oversight of U.S. Export Controls.".

Industry observers note that "Americans deserve answers about what NVIDIA and the Trump administration knew, when they knew it, and whether sufficient steps were taken to prevent sensitive technology from falling into the wrong hands," Sen. Andy Kim, D-N.J., a member of the Banking Committee, wrote in a post on X on Monday.

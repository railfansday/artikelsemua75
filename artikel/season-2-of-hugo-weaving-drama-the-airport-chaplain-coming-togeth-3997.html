Deadline can reveal Sarinah Masukor, co-writer of Netflix miniseries My Brilliant Career, and Keir Wilkins (Heartbreak High) have been brought on as writers for Season 2, which is currently in development at SBS under lead writer and co-creator Elise McCredie.

What makes this particularly notable is the holdback window for a network original series like The Airport Chaplain in Australia is usually three years, but this will hit streaming in just five months. Wooden Horse co-founder Richard Finlayson confirmed the show is in the works, telling Deadline: “We’re developing a second series with SBS, and the writers are back in the room under Elise McCready’s guidance.”.

Jude Troy, who launched the Sydney-based Wooden Horse with Finlayson, co-created the show with McCredie. Weaving stars alongside The Pitt breakout Shabana Azeez, Claudia Karvan (Bump), Thomas Weatherall (Heartbreak High) and Erroll Shand (All Her Fault).

The Airport Chaplain has been a breakout hit for Australian multicultural public service broadcaster SBS, achieving its biggest launch of 2026 with 1.7 million tuning in for episode one and streaming records broken. The series broke further records for SBS, reaching 2.4 million viewers on a national total TV basis to date.

Netflix took worldwide rights, as we revealed, and its safe to say it has been one of the most notable new series out of Australia this year.

In a recent development, the eight-part show stars Weaving as as unconventional airport terminal chaplain Tobias Wallace and The Pitt breakout Shabana Azeez as his put-upon boss. The show was inspired by Troy’s chance encounter with a Melbourne Airport chaplain, who helped her recover a lost handbag.

Troy pointed to the show’s “rounded realism,” and noted it “takes some major swings in the stories it deals with, but it does so with a light touch and real humanity,” noting that there have been “great responses” from viewers in the U.S., UK and her home country, Ireland, among other territories.

“It speaks to humanity and connects with people,” said Troy. “Audiences today are looking for something like that. It is a world and a precinct we all recognise, that we have all travelled through, and it depicts very real people doing very real jobs.

“It is about the lower echelons of the airport and the clash with the upper echelons, and highlights a working-class perspective that you don’t often see on Australian television.”.

The news came in an exclusive interview during which Wooden Horse’s bosses also revealed a host of hires, and projects such as the first Australian adaptation of an R.L. Stein book as a TV series, along with co-production developments with the likes of I May Destroy You and Two Weeks in August maker Various Artists Limited and Wild Cards producer. “We have a great team and a lot of different international relationships and collaborations underway,” said Finlayson.

It has been revealed that the former ABC television chief and ex-eOne TV production boss Troy co-founded Wooden Horse with Troy in 2020, as we revealed at the time, and have over the following six years established the company among the leaders in Australia. In a tough market where even the likes of NBCUniversal-backed Matchbox Pictures closed down this year, that’s no mean feat.

Sources indicate that “We are six years in, and I think we have developed a brand that is known for interesting approaches and stories, strong relationships with the best creators and being a trusted producer,” said Finlayson. “Now it is about having the capacity to keep delivering, because we have a lot in development and a number going into production.”.

Credits include the Logie-winning ABC scripted shows The Clearing and Mother and Son, unscripted series Stuff the British Stole, which is being developed as a TV drama, and Deep North true crime doc series The Cape, and there’s about to be a flood of upcoming work hitting the market.

According to recent reports, upcoming is a first children’s TV show, live-action drama Emma’s Dance Club, which is also noteworthy for its combined TV and movie theater rollout. “The first season has 20 episodes, and we have created five packages, each effectively an hour long,” stated Finlayson. “Hoyts will release them over the course of the year as family experiences. Hoyts has a large family loyalty club, so it is an interesting way to bring together different release strategies.”.

Recent findings suggest that we can reveal Wooden Horse has teamed with U.S.-based producer Global Production Studios (GPS) on a screen adaptation of spooky YA stories author R.L. Stine’s Dangerous Girls, with Thomas Wilson-White, who wrote episodes of Netflix’s Heartbreak High, helming the project.

According to recent reports, “This is the first time R.L. Stine has ever been adapted in Australia,” said Troy.

In a recent development, she also noted there Wooden Horse has an unnamed co-production with Canada’s Blink49 Studios underway, while the heist caper adaptation of Australian unscripted series Stuff the British Stole is now known as Plunder and has UK producer Various Artists Limited aboard, along with Warner Bros. International Television Production Australia, as we revealed in June 2024.

In a recent development, anchuli Felicia King, who wrote on HBO’s The Sympathizer and The Baby and Deadloch for Prime Video, is the writer and creator. A streamer is circling, we hear.

According to recent reports, elsewhere, an adaptation of Ashley Collins’ Dark Mode with Dan March’s Dynamic Television, confirmed in January 2025, is being set up with an unnamed commissioner, with Safe Home writer Anna Barnes attached. There’s also work ongoing to finance The Other Guy star and creator Matt Okine’s debut feature film in Queensland. We first flagged development on Being Black n’ Chicken n’ Chips when Wooden Horse debuted six years ago.

Wooden Horse has backed up a busy 2026 with series of hires, which we can officially reveal here. Felicity Harrison has joined as the producer’s first Head of Commercial and Business Affairs.

She was business affairs chief at Matchbox, which shuttered in February after 18 years in business. At Wooden Horse, she will focus on areas such as legal, business affairs and growing revenues. “We’ve benefitted from Matchbox’s misfortune, as they had such a great team of people,” stated Finlayson.

Bridget Harvey has become Senior Development Executive, joining from Mr Inbetween and Sunny Nights maker Jungle, and Alexis Talbot Smith (The Familiars) joined as a development producer in charge after spells at WildBear and Aquarius Films, with her first job being an associate producer on Emma’s Dance Club, on which she led the day-to-day work.

Production and development assistant and coordinator Will Lane has been promoted and Nell O’Grady has been hired from Stranger Than Fiction Films in a similar role. The Other Guy star Okine remains within the firm as an exec producer.

Staffing up has been a key part of forward planning for what’s gearing up to be a big 2027 for tech giant, which is operating in an Australian creative market that has appeared on the up for several years now.

Finlayson gave a measured assessment of the production sector, noting the strength of local commissioners, who include such as the ABC, SBS, Stan, Binge and Network Seven, and investment from global streamer, who have continued to order originals under the quota system introduced in late 2025.

“We are well supported by our broadcasters and by local and global streamers, the public broadcasters are relatively well funded, and the Labour government has been supportive. The quotas make a difference, so the local market is in a pretty good place.

Recent findings suggest that “The challenges for Australian producers are much the same as those facing British producers. The U.S. market has changed structurally and is still in a state of flux. We are not seeing it return to pre-COVID levels, so raising the gap is hard for everyone. You have to be well networked internationally and agile when it comes to working with budgets.”.

Sources indicate that troy addressed how to remain independent in a global production landscape where Banijay Entertainment is now the largest studio outside of the Hollywood big guns, and M&A appears only to apply to those seeking mega-scale. Wooden Horse is owned by its founders, and has investors and board members including Damian Keogh, CEO of theater chain Hoyts, and Peter Allen, former CEO of Scentre Group and current Chairman of Coles.

“There are always conversations, and the rumour mill is always active,” she said. “Fully independent Australian companies have shown how agile they can be. That flexibility matters. Not being tethered to one mothership is probably how we have continued to get projects made.”.

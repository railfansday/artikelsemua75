When a project developer in Arizona needs a utility-scale solar plant built, they don't look for the cheapest panel on a container ship from Asia. They look for First Solar (FSLR -0.80%). The company builds cadmium telluride thin-film modules using a proprietary, highly automated manufacturing process that doesn't rely on the standard crystalline silicon supply chains dominating the rest of the industry. This strategy has turned a once-volatile manufacturer into a strategic, policy-backed national champion trading at $146.53 as of Nov. 23, 2026, though the stock is down 44% in 2026 amid industrywide pricing headwinds and regulatory uncertainty. First Solar has sustained decades of competitive and policy pressures and continues to be the leading photovoltaic producer in the U.S. Not only does the manufacturer survive, but it also thrives: 2027 net income is projected by Wall Street analysts to be $2.47 billion, which would be a more than 42% profit margin on First Solar's expected $5.79 billion fiscal 2027 sales.

The company also has the flexibility to supply the U.S. market and international markets, thanks to its long-established manufacturing plant in Malaysia and more recent entry into India. India, itself a fast-growing solar market, can also supply partially finished product to First Solar's U.S. plants for finishing, capturing some of the federal tax credits, or the company can capture all the tax credits by providing customers with 100% U.S. built PV panels-- whichever combination provides the best margins. Management expects high demand to give it some pricing power in the years after 2027 as more backlog comes due to be delivered. To mitigate large revenue swings in solar panel pricing or raise fresh capital, management can also sell its tax credits.

The former Plymouth, Bristol Rovers and Wycombe Wanderers forward is in trouble with the law, and this is far from the first time that this has happened.

The former EFL forward Chris Zebroski is back in trouble with the law again. Appearing at Swindon Magistrates' Court on the 7th October, Zebroski pleaded guilty to assault causing actual bodily harm following an incident which was described as an "alcohol-fuelled attack" in which "the 39-year-old headbutted the older man and knocked out one of his teeth, leaving him with a significant purple bruise under his eye.".

Zebroski had been appointed as a player-coach at Southern League side Gosport Borough during the summer.

Chris Zebroski started his career at Swindon Town, but moved to Plymouth Argyle after failing to be offered a senior contract at The County Ground, following a spell with non-league Cirencester City. But he first hit the headlines for all the wrong reasons in August 2007 after he was sacked by the club for gross misconduct following an altercation in the bar during their pre-season tour of Austria the previous month, which left the club captain Paul Wotton with cuts to his head.

Zebroski moved on to Millwall and then to Wycombe Wanderers, but his legal problems continued to pile up. After going on loan to Torquay United, where he was a success in his first season with 19 goals for them in the National League throughout the 2007-08 season, he was given a 12-month community order and a fine for kicking and punching a soldier in a street fight in Torquay in August 2010.

He left Wycombe in 2011 to return to Torquay before moving on to Bristol Rovers, After one unsuccessful season there, he joined Cheltenham Town, but midway through his first season at Whaddon Road he had his contract cancelled after repeatedly pulling out of the first-team squad at short notice.

His next move was into the non-league game with Eastleigh, but at the end of the 2012-13 season he was picked up by Newport County. Again, he was a success on the pitch, with 12 goals in 35 League Two appearances as the Exiles finished 14th in League Two, but again his extracurricular activities would come to catch up with him. Sources indicate that the law would come to catch up with Zebroski again in the summer of 2015, and this time the matter at hand was far more serious. His second season at Rodney Parade had been less successful than his first, with seven goals scored in 36 appearances, but he found himself back in court in July 2015, pleading guilty to four charges of robbery, attempted robbery and assault in relation to two incidents.

The first of these incidents had come in December 2014, when Zebroski crashed his BMW into a taxi and then attacked a witness - another taxi driver - who filmed him when he got out of the car, trying to take his mobile phone from him.

Industry observers note that the BBC reported at the time that: "Zebroski hit Mr Madhu [one of the taxi drivers] in the side of the head and tried to wrestle his mobile phone from him before taking and smashing it. Mr Vladimirej [the other taxi driver] ran off so Zebroski chased him, pushed him to the ground and punched him at least 10 times, saying "give me your phone."" He was also found to have assaulted and attempted to rob two men the following February. (Related: Umamusume horse girls are taking over a Denver Broncos ...)

Sources indicate that in mitigation, Zebroski's counsel pleaded that his client had an alcohol problem, but neither the court nor his employers were impressed by this explanation. He was sentenced to four years and four months in prison, and had his contract terminated by Newport County.

It has been revealed that he was released after serving two years of his prison sentence, and later claimed that he had come close to re-signing for Newport County after leaving prison. At the time, Zebroski said that, "To get sent to jail had a big impact, a massive wake-up call," and that, "Playing in jail, it definitely built my character.".

This time there were no further offers from EFL clubs, and Zebroski instead ended up as a non-league journeyman, playing for numerous clubs before arriving at Gosport in the summer as a player/coach, and on the pitch, this hasn't been a bad season for Gosport, who are mid-table in the Southern League Premier Division South.

Industry observers note that but it's been a different matter behind the scenes. Manager Simon Lane was sacked just two games into the season, but his replacement, Jermaine McGlashan, left the club for personal reasons on the 7th October, meaning that they're already onto their third manager of the season. Whether it's a coincidence or not that McGlashan left Gosport on the same day that Zebroski pleaded guilty to the latest set of charges brought against him is unknown.

What is clear about Chris Zebroski's story is that he has been given plenty of chances. After the incidents at Plymouth, Torquay, Cheltenham and Newport, he managed to find someone who would take him on again, but whether this will be the case should he be sentenced to time in prison again is unknown. Zebroski passed forty years of age at the end of October 2026, and his contract position with Gosport will remain unclear until the cost of his latest loss of control is confirmed.

Sources indicate that fans of the clubs for whom he played may well be left wondering what he could have been capable of had he been able to stay on the right side of the law throughout his playing career. And any chances of launching a successful coaching career may already have been blighted by his latest legal problems.

Given that there have been almost two decades since getting sacked by Plymouth and his current predicament during which he might have successfully tackled the underlying issues which lead to these outbreaks of violence, it doesn't seem especially likely that he'll be given many more opportunities within the game.

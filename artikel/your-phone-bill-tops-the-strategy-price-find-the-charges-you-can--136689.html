Industry observers note that your phone bill runs above your plan’s advertised price because taxes, the carrier’s own fees, device payments, and add-ons are billed on top of it. Taxes and the carrier’s fees stay with the plan, so read your latest bill line by line and cancel the lines and add-ons you no longer need.

According to recent reports, verizon’s plan terms say taxes, other government charges, and the company’s own surcharges can add 16% to 58% to standard monthly charges. Only part of that is set by the government. (Related: The Open Evaluation Standard: Benchmarking NVIDIA Nemot...)

According to recent reports, taxes and government fees, such as the 911 fees some states and localities require, come with any plan. The carrier’s own fees only sound like taxes. AT&T’s Administrative & Regulatory Cost Recovery Fee is $4.99 a month per line, and T-Mobile’s Regulatory Programs & Telco Recovery Fee is $5.49 a month per phone line, or $21.96 across four lines. Verizon’s Administrative and Telco Recovery Charge rose from $3.78 to $4.69 a month per phone line on Nov. 15, 2026. (Related: Tiny Agents: an MCP-powered agent in 50 lines of code...)

Recent findings suggest that some plans include taxes and fees in the advertised price instead. US Mobile, for one, lists its unlimited plans with taxes and fees included.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Like a family reunion you’d do anything to avoid, the Fockers franchise has returned with a fourth installment 16 years after the last one. The series had already reached a point of diminishing returns, with Meet the Fockers and Little Fockers trending ever downward in critical appreciation after 2000’s original Meet the Parents. Focker In-Law, which brings Ariana Grande into the mix, feels like the nadir in a series that recycles the same tired jokes in increasingly desperate manner.

It has been revealed that it’s downright painful watching Robert De Niro, one of our greatest actors, and Ben Stiller, who’s capable of so much more, reprising the same formulaic shtick in this edition helmed by John Hamburg, who co-wrote the previous three films. The comic set pieces, of which there are many, nearly all revolve around humiliation, with Stiller painfully going through the motions as his character does one moronic thing after another. Sources indicate that the chief conceit of this redundant exercise is that Stiller’s character, Greg Focker, is now actually older than his forbidding father-in-law Jack (De Niro) was in the original. And he’s assumed the same persona, deeply suspicious of his son Henry’s (Skyler Gisondo, Primetime) upcoming girlfriend Olivia (Grande), who’s accompanied him to a family reunion celebrating Jack’s 80th birthday.

Industry observers note that prior to their meeting formally, Greg and Olivia had an encounter on an airplane when Greg attempted to use a bathroom not in his assigned section and somehow wound up stealing Olivia’s birth-control pills (don’t ask). It’s but the first of many labored, would-be comic sequences that are so devoid of reality that it’s amazing they made it into the finished product. Another running gag is that Jack is now on a health kick and only eats vegan, with De Niro, clad in tight exercise wear, revealing that he’s in excellent shape at age 83. When Jack turns down Greg’s offer of a steak dinner, Olivia states she can cook him a vegan one, resulting in a kitchen face-off with slapstick complications including Jack seriously burning his face (oh, the hilarity).

Industry observers note that then there’s Jack’s German Shepherd, Lorenzo, who naturally acts aggressively only toward Greg and to whom Jack speaks exclusively in German. If Lorenzo were a poodle, Jack explains, he would speak to him in French. (Related: Industry News: Faster TensorFlow models in Hugging Face...)

In a recent development, the storyline’s main element is Greg’s desperate attempts to sabotage Henry’s wedding proposal to Olivia, whom everyone else in the family instantly adores, to the point of stealing the vintage engagement ring that Jack has given his grandson. This leads to another wildly unfunny segment in which Greg is rabidly pursued through the house by Lorenzo, who’s picked up the scent of the ring, culminating in the dog viciously tearing apart the taxidermied body of Jack’s beloved cat Mr. Jinx from the previous films.

Sources indicate that I could go on, relating one forced moment after another (did I mention the scene in which Greg, attempting to cover his duplicitous tracks, sings John Legend’s deeply romantic song “All of Me” to an increasingly uncomfortable Jack?), but you get the idea. It’s a relief when Owen Wilson indicates up to reprise his laid-back Kevin Rawley character and quietly steals the film, getting laughs just with his use of Jewish idioms.

Much of the supporting cast is wasted, including Blythe Danner, hardly given any screen time; Teri Polo, reduced to looking on with exasperation at Stiller’s antics; and Beanie Feldstein, barely given a chance to exploit her comic talents as Jack’s daughter Samantha. Series newcomer Eduardo Franco (Stranger Things) proves amusing as Samantha’s boyfriend, who desperately yearns to become a member of Jack’s “Circle of Trust.” And De Niro does his De Niro thing, slyly underplaying Jack’s outrageousness.

Sources indicate that grande, whose character is supposed to be a former FBI hostage negotiator, is the best thing in the film, going toe-to-toe with her more cinematically experienced co-stars and displaying both formidable steeliness and deadpan comic skills. If, as seems sadly inevitable, there’s yet another installment in the series, her presence should be required.

On September 30, 2026, we saw an unbelievably horrifying attempted terror plot, whereby a flydubai Boeing 737 first officer attempted to hijack the plane he was second in command of, and crash it into Tel Aviv’s Ben Gurion Airport.

He attempted to kill the captain of the flight using the axe in the cockpit, but thank goodness the captain managed to open the door after being struck, so that passengers could restrain the first officer. The details that have come out ever since have been downright chilling, including how the Omani pilot had specifically sought out a job at flydubai because the airline flew to Israel, and he wanted to crash into the airport there.

Sources indicate that ever since that incident, flydubai has been banned from flying to Israel. For that matter, air service between Israel and the United Arab Emirates is just a tiny fraction of what it previously was. However, there’s now an patch, and it looks like this service could resume very soon. (Related: Hawaiian Interisland Awards — Hop Between Islands...)

The Times of Israel reports that Israeli Transportation Minister Miri Regev has approved the resumption of flydubai flights to Israel, pending approval from Israel’s security authorities. (Related: Is Social Security Taxable? How to Calculate What You O...)

This approval comes after a delegation of Israeli security and transportation officials traveled to Dubai to meet with their counterparts, to discuss latest security measures to allow airlines to resume flights between Tel Aviv and Dubai.

Emirati officials reportedly presented the Israeli officials with newly introduced steps that have been taken to ensure security in light of the incident, but details beyond that haven’t been made public. Per a statement from the Israeli Transportation Ministry:.

It has been revealed that “The delegation today presented Regev with the steps the manufacturer has taken in the wake of the incident. As a result, Regev approved the return of flydubai, subject to clearance from security authorities. The move is a significant step toward restoring air connectivity between Israel and the UAE and expanding flight options for passengers.”.

In a recent development, tel Aviv was flydubai’s single biggest destination, as the airline operated in the market with up to 10 daily flights. Previously, Israeli authorities had banned flydubai from returning to the country through at least December 15, 2026.

Israel is typically known for its very tight security and good intelligence, but obviously there are lapses sometimes.

One of the odd things about the flydubai attempted terror attack is that the Israeli government reportedly had a policy whereby pilots of foreign airlines couldn’t hold citizenship with countries that don’t have diplomatic relations with Israel. Oman and Israel don’t have diplomatic relations, so based on that, the first officer shouldn’t have been able to fly on the plane.

However, it sure seems that there wasn’t actually enforcement of this rule, given that the flydubai first officer had flown to Tel Aviv several times before. I’d imagine that Israeli forces will now much more closely screen the pilots on these flights.

I am curious if there were some latest policies put in place beyond that, or if the extent of the enforcement here is that it’s about the potential nationality of pilots.

Israeli authorities have met with their counterparts in the UAE, and the proposal is for flydubai to be able to resume flights to Israel in the near future. This comes nearly two months after the awful terror plot, whereby a flydubai pilot intended to kill the captain, and then fly a 737 full of passengers into the terminal at Ben Gurion Airport.

According to recent reports, some new security policies have reportedly been put into place, though the details of that aren’t being made public. I think one thing is for sure — Israeli authorities will carefully be scrutinizing manifests and keeping an eye on the pilots at the controls of these flights.

This resumption is a massive deal for flydubai, given that Tel Aviv was the carrier’s biggest market.

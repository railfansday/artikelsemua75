According to recent reports, anya Taylor-Joy is a fan favorite for playing a variety of roles, be it horror movies like The Gorge, the psychological thriller The Menu, playing Princess Peach in The Super Mario Bros. Movie, or taking over the iconic role of Furiosa in George Miller’s Furiosa: A Mad Max Saga. Later this year, she will appear as Alia Atreides in Denis Villeneuve's franchise capper, Dune: Part 3, starring alongside Timothee Chalamet, Zendaya, and Robert Pattinson.

In her growing filmography, she has already worked with some major directors like George Miller, Doctor Strange’s Scott Derrickson, The Sixth Sense’s M. Night Shyamalan, and more. But before them, Taylor-Joy made her debut with a director who is now well renowned as a modern horror auteur. The filmmaker in question is Robert Eggers, who worked with Taylor-Joy in his directorial debut, The Witch. (Related: Director of Machine Learning Insights...)

According to recent reports, set in 1630s Fresh England, we follow a devout Puritan family banished from their settlement. Now forced to live on a remote farm beside a dark forest, things take a turn when their newborn vanishes while in the care of their eldest daughter, Thomasin. The distraught family descends into paranoia as crop failures and strange occurrences follow. They suspect witchcraft and blame Thomasin for being under the influence of Satan. The movie's simple premise will sit with you only for the first 15 minutes; then the ritualistic isolation will set in. (Related: Grandma who accidentally invited a stranger to Thanksgi...)

In a world of suffocating morality and religious paranoia, The Witch examines how fear and dogma can turn a family against itself. With dark forest imagery and oppressive silence, Eggers evokes the emotion of an ancient nightmare. Thomasin’s final transformation leaves you wondering whether she was liberated or damned. But beneath the folklore lie the themes of the fear of female autonomy, forbidden desires, and religious control. The film deliberately leaves room for both; the evil is real and so is religious fanaticism.

Taylor-Joy gives an intense performance as Thomasin, bringing brilliant nuances in her debut role. Ralph Ineson portrays Thomasin’s father, William, with a patriarchal bent of mind and as a man full of fears. Equally impressive is Kate Dickie’s performance as the matriarch of the family, Katherine. The 91% Rotten Tomatoes-rated feature is loved for its gothic atmosphere and physically and emotionally suffocating environment as Eggers delivers a masterclass in slow-building horror. For fans who'd love to check out the film, or discover it, time is right as The Witch is coming to Hulu on December 2, as per ComicBook.

Teacher Loan Forgiveness Cancels Up to $17,500 – but Costs 5 Years of PSLF Credit

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that teach full time for five school years in a row at a low-income school, and Teacher Loan Forgiveness can cancel up to $17,500 of your subsidized and unsubsidized federal loans, depending on your subject. But those years can’t also count toward Public Service Loan Forgiveness, which has no cap, so compare the two before you apply.

In a recent development, the comparison starts with your subject, which sets the cap on Teacher Loan Forgiveness (TLF). Secondary math and science teachers and elementary or secondary special education teachers can get up to $17,500, and other teachers up to $5,000.

Industry observers note that the catch is what TLF does to Public Service Loan Forgiveness (PSLF). PSLF cancels whatever federal Direct Loan balance remains after 120 qualifying monthly payments, made over at least 10 years at a public school or another qualifying employer. Take TLF, and your payments from those five years stop counting toward PSLF, so you’d need 120 more. (Related: School sends out minibus to catch absent pupils playing...)

For a small balance, TLF beats waiting for PSLF. A high school math teacher who borrowed $16,000 can have what’s left canceled after year five, since it’s under her cap. A colleague who also teaches math aims to stay in public schools and repay on an income-driven proposal, which sets payments by income. With $60,000 in loans, she’d get at most $17,500 from TLF and likely far more from PSLF at year 10, so she skips TLF. (Related: Obsessed fan breaks into YouTuber’s home demanding marr...)

Before you count on TLF, confirm three things:.

With a balance under your cap, you can ask your loan servicer each year for a TLF forbearance, which pauses payments so you don’t pay down debt TLF would cancel. Interest still builds and can push your balance past the cap.

After year five, have your principal, superintendent, or HR office certify the Teacher Loan Forgiveness Application, then send it to your servicer, one form per servicer.

Before you choose, use StudentAid.gov’s Repayment Calculator to compare what you’d pay in total under each program, since TLF can’t be undone after it’s granted.

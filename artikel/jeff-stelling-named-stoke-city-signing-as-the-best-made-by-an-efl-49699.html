Stoke City's start to the Championship season under Mark Robins has caught plenty of attention, with the Potters, for once, making headlines for the right reasons.

It has been well-documented that the Potters are yet to finish inside the second tier's top-half following relegation from the Premier League in 2018.

This time last season, Robins' side found themselves in a similar position to their current standing of third, as only goal difference separates them from league leaders, Birmingham City.

However, there is a strong feeling that City could last the course of the 46-game season in the promotion mix if their key men continue living up to the bill. (Related: Tech Report: Course Launch Community Event...)

Stoke have parted with plenty of cash since their top-flight relegation, and much of it has gone to waste given their league finishes.

Eight-figure fees for the likes of Benik Afobe and Tom Ince are enough to make supporters cringe, with neither making the desired impact, but this summer, they were joined by Ethan Galbraith on this particular front, with it taking £10m to prize the Northern Ireland international away from divisional rivals, Swansea City. (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

Galbraith, 25, previously excelled in both central midfield and right-back roles for Leyton Orient in League One, and took such form with him to South Wales under Alan Sheehan and Vitor Matos.

With the Swans only parting with £1.5m to secure his services, it is a testament to how promptly Galbraith adapted to the Championship's rigours as an all-action midfielder, with his former employers making at least £8.5m worth of profit, but Stoke have certainly been the early winners of said deal.

Given Swansea finished six places and nine points clear of Stoke in 2025/26, plenty of eyebrows were raised over Galbraith's ambition when opting to return north.

Despite only registering five goal contributions last term for the Welsh side, Galbraith still earned plenty of praise from Championship onlookers, as well as former Stoke and current Northern Ireland boss, Michael O'Neill.

They were also joined by television personality and talkSPORT host, Jeff Stelling, who pinpointed the deal as the standout acquisition of the summer, even ahead of West Ham United's EFL record buy for Celtic's Arne Engels.

"Best signing for me, and this will please Stoke fans, not much to shout about in recent seasons, they signed Ethan Galbraith, paid around £10million for him from Swansea," noted Stellling.

"I've always been impressed with him; I saw him for Swansea and Northern Ireland as well, a former Manchester United kid, he's 25 now.

"I thought he had a sniff of a move to the Premier League, to be honest, so for Stoke, I think that's a real coup.".

Galbraith's career path means there has been plenty of interest in his performances from a young age, having come through the Red Devils academy between 2017 and 2019, before making a solitary first-team appearance against Astana in the UEFA Europa League.

The following pre-season, Galbraith found himself in Ole Gunnar Solksjaer's first-team plans, with it previously being claimed by a club insider: "He's the best young player outside the core first-team group at the club.".

During an encounter with Aston Villa, Solksjaer publicly revealed that Galbraith needed to aclimatise to the pace of play against top-level performers compared to at youth level, and Stoke are currently the beneficiaries of such a vast improvement.

Galbraith may have been one of several eye-catching transfer moves made by Stoke this summer, especially due to the fee in question, but it is one which could land the club in excess of £200m in the coming months.

No Potters player has registered more goal contributions than the 21-time international's seven, which outperforms his expected combined number of just 3.2, further highlighting his talismanic influence.

Galbraith has also created 25 chances for his teammates, whilst also averaging 8.1 line-breaking passes and 5 recovery tackles per 90 minutes.

Given his versatile nature, if he remains injury-free and staves off further suspensions, having missed the recent away success at Blackburn Rovers following a fifth yellow card, Robins and Stoke will be laughing, with Galbraith's valuation likely to continue increasing in the near future, such is his importance.

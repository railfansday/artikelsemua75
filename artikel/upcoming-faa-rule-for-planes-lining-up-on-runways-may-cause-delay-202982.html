The Federal Aviation Administration (FAA) has just added some upcoming restrictions to how air traffic controllers can clear aircraft to “line up and wait” on the runway, in anticipation of takeoff clearance. This is intended to add an extra safety buffer, but to quote an airline pilot on an ATC frequency, “that’s probably going to cause some delays, that’s for sure.”.

As of today (November 24, 2026), the FAA has introduced a new rule regarding how aircraft can be cleared onto runways at airports. Air traffic controllers want to utilize runways as efficiently as possible for both takeoffs and landings, and that often involves having planes line up on the runway and wait, in anticipation of takeoff clearance.

Sources indicate that to give an example, sometimes a plane will be cleared for takeoff, and then a bit of separation is required until the next plane can depart. In those situations, it’s common for the air traffic controller to clear that next plane to “line up and wait.”.

Recent findings suggest that the idea is that the plane then has clearance to enter the runway, and then await further takeoff clearance. After all, it might take 30 seconds or so for a plane to taxi from the hold short point of the runway, to getting into position on the runway.

Under the latest FAA rules, air traffic controllers will only be allowed to clear pilots to “line up and wait” in situations where there’s a dedicated supervisor or controller-in-charge who can’t perform other duties while these operations are underway. In the past this was a recommendation, but now this is a rule.

Industry observers note that the core purpose of this change is to elevate the safety standard for Line Up and Wait (LUAW) operations by removing ambiguity in staffing requirements. By upgrading the guidance from “should” (a recommendation) to “must” (a mandatory requirement), the FAA ensures that the personnel providing watch supervision (OS/CIC) are fully dedicated to overseeing the safety of the operation without the distraction of simultaneously managing other operational tasks.

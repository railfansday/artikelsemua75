Recent findings suggest that the making of “Project Hail Mary” was a miracle on par with the outer space rescue mission that Ryan Gosling is tasked with pulling off. Phil Lord and Chris Miller’s adaptation of Andy Weir’s bestselling novel manages to be epic without ever losing its intimacy, as Gosling traverses the galaxy and saves the world with only a very lovable rock acting alongside him. The final product was a complicated blend of practical effects, on-set puppetry, and a healthy dose of VFX magic.

Sources indicate that incorporating so many production elements creates more work for all involved. The puppetry team lead by James Ortiz had to fit into very small spaces, which frequently meant they highlighted up in the frames. Visual Effects Production Supervisor Mag Sarnowska and her team were tasked with removing all of the puppeteers. But as she explained during the VFX panel at IndieWire’s Craft Roundtables, it was a small price to pay for the way it enhanced Gosling’s acting performance. In a recent development, “It was so good to have James and his puppeteers, which we called Rocketeers,” Sarnowska remarked. “It was great to have them there, but it did not work out sometimes. Our sets were really, really tiny. Having five people there to puppet Rocky was hard sometimes. But to everyone’s point, having something there in the frame was so helpful, especially for Ryan to act to. The eyeline? That’s a real thing. Even though our Rocky didn’t have eyes, a person’s brain makes it easier to figure out where the eyes would be just by having that constant relation between Rocky and Ryan.”. Sarnowska — who will share the Innovation Award at the IndieWire Honors with Ortiz, Neal Scanlan, and Paul Lambert — explained that having a real reference point for VFX sequences is what ultimately makes the most effects-heavy sequences feel real.

“In Ryan’s space, he has to be in the xenonite glass, so having reflections, adding the glass, it was a lot of work,” she said. “But having that real thing, having that reference, even if we had to replace it, that was so notable. Because we knew what Rocky would look like.”.

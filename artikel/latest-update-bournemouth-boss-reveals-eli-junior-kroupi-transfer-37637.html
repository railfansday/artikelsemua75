Bournemouth manager Marco Rose has stated the club do not blueprint to sell Arsenal and Tottenham target Eli Junior Kroupi in January.

Both north London clubs have been heavily linked with a move for the French striker, who scored his first senior goal against Turkey last weekend, during the summer before he suffered a foot injury in pre-season which required surgery.

Kroupi has recently returned to fitness after more than four months out, stoking speculation that goal-shy Spurs could reignite their interest in the 20-year-old, who scored more goals than any other teenager in their debut Premier League season last term. In an interview with The Times, though, Rose has revealed that Bournemouth do not proposal on selling any of their star talent in January.

That also includes midfielder Alex Scott, who was the subject of two rejected bids from Chelsea over the summer and is also stated to be attracting interest from Liverpool and Premier League champions Arsenal.

“It’s not the plan to sell someone in January,” Rose noted, though he later admitted selling players was “part of the process” for Bournemouth as they look to comply with UEFA’s squad cost rules.

It has been revealed that “It’s not our strategy and so far there are just rumours and noises. We are quite calm, like we were in the summer and I think everything we stated was clear and the things happened the way we communicated. For us, it’s not a plan to sell someone in January.”.

After failing to win any of their first six league games, the Cherries have hit form under Rose and have won each of their last eight games in all competitions.

There were question marks early on about whether Rose could handle the step up to the Premier League.

But the highly-rated German has now firmly dismissed those suggestions, with the Dorset club sitting sit joint-second in the form table across Europe’s five major leagues, behind only Paris Saint-Germain and level with Real Madrid.

Bournemouth appear to have made a shrewd appointment in naming Rose as Andoni Iraola’s successor, but he admits it all could have been very different, with Spurs sounding him out to take over as interim manager before landing on the ill-fated appointment of Igor Tudor.

No concrete offer was made by the north Londoners, but Rose states there was interest from the capital and a number of other Premier League teams.

“No, they didn’t make me an offer,” Rose added.

Industry observers note that “I was in touch with some Premier League clubs, that’s right, but it was never to speak about a real offer. I did it the same way as with Bournemouth, I tried to get to know the environment, the club, the situation and then I picked the club I felt could be the right next step for me. The other club decided to take another manager, so it’s not always just about me.”.

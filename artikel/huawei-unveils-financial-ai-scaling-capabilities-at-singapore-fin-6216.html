To Move from AI Adoption to AI at Scale, Financial Institutions Must Build Their Own AI Capabilities.

It has been revealed that the competitive focus in financial AI is shifting from model performance to enterprise-grade system capabilities. Financial institutions must be able to independently select, securely operate, replicate at scale, and continuously evolve their AI capabilities.

In a recent development, jason Cao shared lessons from China's AI journey: an open ecosystem makes AI affordable; extreme engineering makes it effective; and commercialization at scale makes AI deeply integrated and reliable in business. He identified four characteristics among China's financial institutions: ongoing application modernization (Zero Legacy); hybrid infrastructure combining on-premises deployment and public cloud; enterprise-grade AI architecture for centralized development and management; and AI in core business workflows such as mobile banking, credit, and risk control, reshaping business processes to enhance competitiveness.

Industry observers note that as AI evolves to understand intent, blueprint, and act proactively, customers and employees will rely on their own AI agents. Banks must ensure their financial services can be understood, connected, and invoked by these agents, while building an AI flywheel linking data, execution, learning, and feedback toward Customer-AI-Friendly Finance.

Huawei is exploring a "Two Super Agents, Multiple Specialized Agents" architecture. The Customer Super Agent and Employee Super Agent understand user intent, deliver personalized services, and flexibly invoke specialized banking capabilities. Multiple Specialized Agents support domains such as credit, marketing, risk management, and wealth management through human-AI and multi-agent collaboration. Banks can turn expertise in compliance rules, credit judgment, marketing strategies, and risk management into invokable, reusable, continuously evolving AI capabilities. Through the Two Super Agents and Multiple Specialized Agents, Huawei helps banks connect customers and employees, coordinate business tasks, and continuously capture and refine expert intelligence to build self-evolving, enterprise-grade systems.

Cao said scaling AI for business growth requires hybrid infrastructure combining on-premises deployment and public cloud for flexible options, open architecture for technical agility, standardized engineering to build and replicate AI efficiently, and proven knowledge, skills, and agent capabilities as shared assets for reuse across teams and scenarios. Three Intelligent Factories on Open Hybrid AI Infrastructure Accelerate Production-Grade Deployment of Financial Agents.

On the Frontier Stage, Alvin Feng delivered a keynote titled "Owning Your AI, Advancing Agentic Finance" and unveiled Huawei's Fintelligent AI Solution. He outlined an enterprise-grade framework to address security, sustainability, and scalability: open architecture for flexibility and continuous technological evolution; standardized engineering for mass production of AI agents; and shared assets for reuse across teams and scenarios.

Built on open hybrid AI infrastructure, the solution brings these capabilities together through three intelligent factories: the Agent Factory for agent development and governance; the Token Factory for full-lifecycle token operations; and the Data&Knowledge Factory for continuous, high-quality data and knowledge supply.

At the Huawei Finance Exhibition Area, Huawei showcased its capabilities and partner practices for scaling AI worldwide. With 16 years in finance, Huawei has served more than 7,100 financial customers, including 54 of the world's top 100 banks.

Mark Robins (Stoke City), Cesar Peixoto (Wolves), Chris Davies (Birmingham City) and James Morrison (West Bromwich Albion) all have cause to feel content.

It is beginning to look a lot like Christmas for followers of Midlands football.

Granted, it is actually only November, but with more than a third of the 2026-27 Championship season gone, already the table is taking on an increasingly familiar weekly look in terms of the primary promotion contenders.

There has been many a year when there have been early-season stirrings in the Midlands, only for the momentum to fade.

Industry observers note that but Coventry City proved it can be done after 25 years away from the Premier League. And now Stoke City, Wolves, Birmingham City and West Bromwich Albion are all in contention to join both the Sky Blues and Aston Villa in the top flight. (Related: Industry News: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

Stoke in second, Wolves third, Blues fourth and Albion fifth makes for both a heartening, yet surprising sight, with only Middlesbrough in front of them.

It is only six months since Wolves were relegated and, for all the summer discontent at Molineux and doubts among disillusioned fans about their worthiness as serious candidates, the fact is they are very much in the mix. (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

But, while most people at Molineux can now at least contemplate a quick return, for their local rivals the parachute payments have long since expired.

Blues have been away the longest, their 15-year absence from the top flight having even included a one-season return to the third tier and back.

But, given their extensive American backing, the club have ambitions to be serious contenders - and the only surprise was that it took so long to find their feet.

For Stoke and Albion to be up there was not necessarily forecast, but it has made for all the more happy motoring for the BBC correspondents who cover the quartet of clubs on a regular basis.

BBC Sport asked reporters from BBC Stoke & Staffordshire and BBC West Midlands Sport for their analysis of the season so far and what lies in store for each club...

Mark Elliott, BBC Radio Stoke commentator.

Mark Robins has spoken a lot about evolution and incremental gains in his time as Stoke City manager, but it's hard to escape the feeling that there's been a full-blown revolution over the past three months.

Stoke began the season with three defeats, a manager under pressure and the impending loss of their most productive player in Sorba Thomas - but a change in formation, an excellent summer transfer window and an unbelievable run of form has quietened all dissenting voices.

Thirty two out of a possible 39 points from 13 games since the window closed is a brilliant record - but Stoke are maybe still short of truly turning the hopefully optimistic among their fans into true believers.

Most of that is a hangover from last season's promising start eventually falling apart, but there are substantial differences.

The squad has more quality and depth, a more ambitious way of playing and the less quantifiable qualities of tenacity and resilience and a mental strength that, coupled with defensive solidity, has allowed them to find multiple ways to win close games.

Luke Graham, Ethan Galbraith, Jack McGlynn and Ben Johnson have been their most eye-catching summer acquisitions but several who struggled last season, Eric Bocat first among them, have found success too.

The next step is to create more chances and score more goals, whilst maintaining anything close to their current return of points per game.

Even the most hardened pessimists will then maybe see a play-off place as a realistic aim for 2027.

Stoke reached a different level - Robins.

By Mike Taylor, BBC Radio WM commentator.

Only a point behind, and with the feeling they have not played to their full ability very often yet - not very often for 90 consecutive minutes, certainly - Wolves are not badly placed.

Their supporters can be hard taskmasters at the moment, and can hardly be blamed for that in view of the mess of recent seasons and the substantial promises made of going full-tilt to get straight back up.

For all the understandable misgivings, though, there is a clear path towards achieving it.

Reaching the end of that path may require some judicious spending in January to tidy up a squad that still looks imbalanced, with a surfeit of players in some areas and a shortage in others.

Personal surges in form, from Fer Lopez early in the season to Adam Armstrong more recently, have covered up the gaps and reminded everyone that this squad is not short of class at this level.

Cesar Peixoto, newly introduced to England, has handled a delicate situation in a tumultuous league with quiet confidence.

He will have to complete the job in May before he can claim credit more widely, but appears to realise the power he can harness if, like a certain Portuguese predecessor, he can unify all the forces around the club to point in the same direction.

Wolves' Mane of the moment has to now kick on.

By Richard Wilford, BBC Radio WM.

For all of the outside noise about the future of Chris Davies, the Birmingham City board have steadfastly backed their man. Now that decision is paying dividends.

Thanks to that rarely tried footballing trait of continuity, they spent the international break sitting at the top of a very congested Championship table.

But Saturday's 1-0 loss against 10-man Stoke was a reminder of just how tight the division is.

The reboot of the squad started in earnest in January, when the future for Davies was at its wobbliest.

The Â£18m-plus combined investment in Colombian midfielder Jhon Solis, Danish striker August Priske and Spanish winger Carlos Vicente gave them a chance to bed in to English football over the second half of this season, before kicking on this time around.

Recent findings suggest that priske, at 22, has a high ceiling. His six Championship goals this season has come with improved physicality and increased confidence.

The industry and physique of Solis have stabilised Blues midfield, while Vicente is one of four options on the wing following the loan addition of Liam Millar.

According to recent reports, davies himself has learned from his first Championship season. His team are now versatile, more able to move the ball forward rapidly, no longer wedded to possession at all costs.

Defensively too there is a greater resilience and a visible hunger to succeed.

Whether they are serious contenders for automatic promotion remains to be seen, but they have the ingredients to retain a top six spot over the full 46-game course.".

James Morrison's mind was at ease over this international break following Albion's 3-0 win over Millwall.

He is refusing to reveal any pre-season expectations, but looking at the Championship table makes for satisfying reading.

Certainly externally, nobody expected the Baggies to be fifth - just one point off the automatic promotion places - a third into this campaign.

Morrison has now racked up 51 points from 30 league games in charge. That includes his three interim spells, although the second was a single game as head coach in the FA Cup in January against their next opponents, Swansea City - in between the dismal reigns of Ryan Mason and Eric Ramsay.

It is wild to think of the difference Morrison and his excellent coaching staff have made in 2026.

Albion were heading in the direction of League One when the key were handed to the club legend, who'd been in the back seat the entire time.

A 10-game unbeaten run steered them to safety, and he even had to navigate a points deduction from the EFL for a breach of financial rules.

The club is in a much better place on and off the field, with Mozza now firmly at the wheel.

The upcoming January transfer window could play a massive part in determining whether they continue on this positive path, but Baggies fans are at least enjoying the ride again.

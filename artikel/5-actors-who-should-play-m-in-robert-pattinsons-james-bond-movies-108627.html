Recent findings suggest that the next era of the "James Bond" film franchise is finally taking shape. After years of wondering who will be suiting up as our new 007, Robert Pattinson has officially been cast as the next James Bond, taking over for Daniel Craig following his final entry with "No Time to Die," which came out way back in 2021. A lot has happened since then, including Amazon buying the rights to the property. Now, with a new lead actor in place, it's time to get the rest of the pieces of the puzzle locked in. That includes finding Bond's boss.

It has been revealed that historically, this has been the one they call M. Originally portrayed by Bernard Lee in the earliest Bond films, the codename given to the person behind the big desk at MI6 is an notable one, as they head up the 00 program. Thus, M is the one who gives Bond his assignments and, at times, even helps him out in the field. It's an important role, one that has been played by the likes of Dame Judi Dench and Oscar-nominee Ralph Fiennes.

Sources indicate that there's much to be done. Director Denis Villeneuve also has to find an actor to play the villain in Pattinson's "James Bond" movie, but M is a longer commitment, a multi-picture deal. It's a substantial responsibility. So, who should play the role alongside Pattinson? Who should be the one to help guide 007 through his next several missions for her majesty's secret service? We're going to go over a handful of options, ranging from very obvious to unlikely yet intriguing. Let's get into it.

In a recent development, "The amount of time I have wanted to be M. I'm not sure who I need to call...".

Those are the words of Olivia Colman speaking to British Vogue in 2023. She wants the role. She's very well qualified for the role. If everyone wants to make this easy, why not just offer her the role? Sometimes, the easiest answer is also a very good answer. Industry observers note that colman is responsible for one of the most shocking Oscars upsets of all time when she took home Best Actress for "The Favourite." But it was more shocking because the Academy recognized Colman's impressive work over the rest of the pack. Ever since, she's been on a tear, turning in outstanding performances in everything from "The Lost Daughter" to "Paddington in Peru." Heck, she even ate up some screen time in Marvel's otherwise very disappointing series "Secret Invasion.".

But Colman has been doing it at a very high level for years, long before she won an Oscar. "Hot Fuzz," "The Iron Lady," "Broadchurch." You name it, Colman is one of the best parts of everything she's in. Beyond that, she could bring both a seriousness to the role of M, which is required, while at the same time allowing for a playful dynamic with Bond. Colman feels like a slam dunk. It just so happens that the person campaigning for the role is very much right for the role. That's not always the case.

If Denis Villeneuve or Amazon want to go with a distinguished, male British actor who could be older and wiser than Robert Pattinson's Bond, one capable of being both convincingly stern and charming, Colin Firth is the man. An Oscar-winning actor who is almost too easy to picture sitting at a big desk behind a padded burgundy door, this is another "the obvious choice can also be the right choice" pick.

It has been revealed that we have an actor here capable of delivering exposition, as M often has to, in a way that would be entertaining, as Firth could read the phone book and it would be engaging. But let's not forget that Firth politely destroyed fools in "Kingsman: The Secret Service," too, and that movie owes a lot to the "James Bond" franchise. What's more, it proved that Firth looks absolutely fantastic in an expensive suit, an important quality of a good M.

His resume speaks for itself. "A Single Man," "The King's Speech," and even "Mamma Mia!" prove that Firth is one of the finest actors of the last 30 years. There's zero question as to whether or not he's qualified to be M. So, it begs the question: Is he right for M? If we're looking to address that question in the traditional sense, then the answer is a resounding yes. The shoe fits and it fits well. Simple as that.

Sources indicate that if it weren't for Judi Dench playing M so damn well for so damn long, we might have to have the utterly irritating "let's gender-swap M" conversation. Fortunately, that's not the world we live in. Bearing that in mind, it's going to be damn hard for anyone to argue against Tilda Swinton bossing around Robert Pattinson while dressed to the nines. What's not to like about that?

Swinton won a Best Supporting Actress Oscar for "Michael Clayton" so, much like many of the other performers on that list, she's Oscar-caliber. That almost feels like a must for M. Setting that aside, Swinton brings a domineering, powerful quality to so many of her best roles. Even so, she's a bit of a chameleon, which is partially what would make her an interesting M. It's not hard to imagine her playing an MI6 agent who used to work in the field before taking a desk job, yet she still has that darkness behind her eyes from yesteryear.

Sources indicate that oddly, it's Swinton's transformation into the Ancient One" in "Doctor Strange" that might make the best case here. She can do powerful yet quiet without having to be at the center of the action, and she can absolutely handle being a part of a giant, globally popular franchise. Really, there's nothing this role requires that she couldn't do, seemingly with ease.

Some might argue Swinton would be better as a "Bond" villain. That certainly wouldn't upset me, but it might be subverting expectations a bit to have her as Bond's buttoned-up boss. She could also help bring something fresh to the role for this fresh era.

Ralph Fiennes made his entry into the "James Bond" franchise in 2012's "Skyfall" before fully taking over as M in "Spectre" following the death of Judi Dench's M. (May she rest in peace.) So, why replace a perfectly great actor in the role if we don't have to?

Industry observers note that let's not forget that the "James Bond" franchise is notorious for bringing actors back in roles even after reboots. Dench as M for both Pierce Brosnan and Daniel Craig's Bonds. Desmond Llewelyn was Q for every Bond except Craig. That's the nature of the beast as it exists. If Amazon doesn't totally buck tradition, it could easily bring back Fiennes for another go-around. And why not? He's already proven he can do it, he's a stellar actor, and he's arguably as popular as ever.

According to recent reports, he's also an actor who seems to really understand the role. "Spectre" was going to make M the villain until Finnes refused. That, to this longtime fan, demonstrates a real understanding of who M is. On top of that, Fiennes is very familiar with shouldering the weight of a major movie franchise, having previously played Voldemort in the "Harry Potter" movies. The only reason to not do this is if Amazon and Denis Villeneuve truly and completely want to hit the significant red reset button, which frankly seems likely.

It has been revealed that but if not? The debate could be settled before it even gets started.

In a recent development, last, but certainly not least, we arrive at the choice that's both decidedly out-of-the-box yet technically in the very box we're talking about. Pierce Brosnan previously played James Bond in four movies stretching from 1995 to 2002. His "GoldenEye" remains the most key box office hit in "James Bond" history, in addition to being a beloved entry in the franchise. But Brosnan's run was uneven and ended on a sour note with "Die Another Day." Perhaps playing M would give him a shot at redemption?

It has been revealed that let's get some things out of the way up top. Yes, it might be a little weird to have a former 007 play M, but it's not that much stranger than fully ignoring continuity in order to have the same actor play M for multiple Bonds. More importantly, Brosnan absolutely looks the part. The man, well into his 70s, still looks better in a suit than most of us could ever hope to. He's got an authoritative on-screen presence, but he's also got that charm that could work wonders for M.

Brosnan feels he was never good enough as James Bond. I would humbly argue that the scripts weren't always good enough for his James Bond, not the other way around. Why not use the past to help build a future for the franchise? Let Brosnan bring his experience to the role and mentor Robert Pattinson's 007. The meta nature of it all might add something to their dynamic.

The question of whether or not Brosnan would want to take on the role is another one entirely, but it's a wild card idea that's just inspired enough to be a whole lot of fun.

Danaher declared a strategic partnership with Microsoft on November 20, 2026, to develop a shared agentic platform for scientific instrument control and insight generation, with initial commercial applications expected in 2026 and additional agent-enabled instruments planned for 2027.

The platform is built on Microsoft Azure for secure, compliant cloud infrastructure and Microsoft Foundry for AI agents that interpret results and guide instrument control. Danaher’s goal is to give specialized products a reusable foundation while keeping the system open to agents and digital solutions in multi-vendor laboratories. That architecture links reuse across Danaher’s technologies with interoperability in laboratories that combine multiple suppliers. For those laboratories, the stated openness extends the interoperability goal beyond any single Danaher product or business. (Related: How Much Does a Will Cost? Complete Price Guide...)

One initial application is MetaXpress Acquire AI Assistant, Molecular Devices’ software for its high-content cellular imaging systems, designed to provide product support. The companies are continuing to develop the wider platform. That first example sits at the product-support end of the roadmap; the planned instruments are intended to extend into experimental setup, execution and analysis. Recent findings suggest that the instruments are being designed to translate scientific intent into experimental plans, instrument setup and methods. They would guide execution and troubleshooting while controlling hardware within defined safety boundaries and with user oversight. The design also includes interpreting results and identifying quality challenges in real time, then recommending corrective actions and next experiments for scientist review.

In a recent development, together, those functions would extend software assistance across the experimental cycle. The oversight model applies different constraints at different steps: instruments can operate hardware within set limits, while scientists assess the recommended corrections and subsequent experiments.

Sources indicate that in preliminary internal testing, Danaher reported, novice scientists conducting an AI-assisted experiment on a Leica microscope took up to 83% less time and up to 68% fewer steps to complete it than a novice working without the assistant. The assisted novices also produced high-quality data and completed the experiment in a time comparable to an expert. The reductions come from the novice-versus-novice comparison; the expert comparison concerns completion time.

Industry observers note that julie Sawyer Montgomery, Danaher’s president and CEO, remarked: “With Microsoft, we are building a common foundation, so our instruments and laboratories work together consistently, as one connected Danaher. Through context and learning, that foundation can continuously inform the next experiment and the best analysis.”.

The partnership sits within Danaher’s broader AI and autonomous-science strategy, connecting intelligence at the instrument with data, laboratory workflows and physical experiment execution. Intelligent instruments are the first step. As instruments and laboratory systems become more connected and interoperable, Danaher expects experimental results to help scientists determine what to do next, supporting increasingly connected and autonomous workflows under scientific oversight.

The planned instrument rollout will test whether one shared foundation can support reuse within Danaher and openness across multi-vendor laboratories while preserving the safety boundaries and scientist review built into the design.

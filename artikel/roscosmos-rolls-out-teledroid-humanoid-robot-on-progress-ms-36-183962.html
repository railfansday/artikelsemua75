According to recent reports, roscosmos debuted its Progress MS-36 cargo spacecraft toward the International Space Station on November 24, 2026, carrying a human-like robot for the Teledroid experiment. A Soyuz-2.1b rocket lifted off from Baikonur at 13:17 Moscow time with more than 2.4 metric tons of cargo.

The spacecraft reached its intended orbit, separated from the rocket’s third stage and deployed its antennas and solar-panel arrays as planned. Roscosmos expects it to dock with the station’s Small Research Module on November 26, 2026, at 15:10 Moscow time. The robot has therefore reached orbit, but its arrival at the station remains a scheduled next step. (Related: Latest Update: Towers of Fortune codes (November 2026)...)

Industry observers note that the prelaunch mission description identified Baikonur’s Site 31 as the launch site and listed equipment for Teledroid, Aseptik, Kaskad and Orbita-MG among the cargo. Provisions and other equipment for crew life and work make this a broader resupply flight, with the robot delivered alongside other research hardware. Roscosmos describes Teledroid as a robot avatar intended to work on the station’s exterior and assist cosmonauts. An operator aboard the ISS or at mission control can wear a special suit and perform movements for the robot to copy. Possible tasks include preparing a worksite before a spacewalk by opening casing and installing equipment, while some tasks are intended to be performed autonomously. The main goal is to reduce the time cosmonauts spend outside the station.

According to recent reports, A project record from developer NPO Android Technics notes the system was implemented jointly with RSC Energia to test operational-support technologies for cosmonaut spacewalks. Control is possible through an operator inside the station’s pressurized compartment or at a ground control point. The project consequently covers operator procedures and robotic execution as parts of the same exterior-work system.

In a recent development, the experiment’s equipment comprises a copy-type master device called the cosmonaut workstation, a computing module, the robot and an external camera that lets operators see the robot from outside. Progress MS-35 delivered several elements, including the workstation, to the ISS in September 2026. An October 28, 2026, update mentioned Pyotr Dubrov and Anna Kikina were installing that equipment and preparing initial tasks with virtual models while the physical robot was still absent.

Earlier crew-training material documented Dubrov and Kikina preparing to carry Teledroid outside the ISS, install it on the exterior and connect its communications during a spacewalk. They rehearsed those operations underwater. The upcoming delivery supplies the robot for an experiment whose control equipment and crew preparation began earlier; exterior installation and subsequent physical tasks remain ahead.

Those steps will test whether the avatar and autonomous modes can support preparation work while reducing the time crews must spend outside the station. Successful orbital insertion starts that phase, but the planned installation and operating tasks will establish Teledroid’s practical value to spacewalk support.

A mother and caregiver were arrested after they allegedly let a 3-year-old boy with curable cancer die by skipping two years of treatment that would have saved his life.

Destiny Schliefert and Debbie Sue Potter were arrested on Wednesday, November 18, and were both charged with second-degree murder, abuse or neglect of a child resulting in death, and four counts of first-degree child endangerment, according to court records viewed by Us.

Both Schliefert and Potter are presently in custody at the Christian County Jail.

Doctors noticed a cyst on the boy’s left kidney when he was born on June 1, 2023, according to their probable cause statements viewed by Law & Crime.

In March 2024, the boy was diagnosed with neuroblastoma, which is a rare cancer of immature nerve cells. The type of cancer is most common in infants.

Sources indicate that authorities said that the child’s last recorded visit to a medical center in Kansas City, Missouri, with his mother, Schliefert, was on May 14, 2024. Schliefert allegedly stopped bringing her son in for treatment after doctors advised her to return every 12 to 24 weeks for additional assessment and treatment.

The doctor who previously saw the boy noted his neuroblastoma “was survivable” if it was treated “with proper care,” according to Schliefert’s probable cause statement.

The boy reportedly went to a different hospital in Clinton, Missouri, for treatment, nearly one year later in April 2025. He allegedly weighed just over 23 pounds at the time, and the visit appeared to be his last time he saw medical professionals.

Schliefert and her son moved into a home in Ozark, Missouri, with Potter in July 2025. Potter was identified as “grammy” in court documents, and she took on caregiver responsibilities.

According to recent reports, authorities noted that the boy’s condition rapidly weakened. Schliefert has been accused of being aware of her son’s condition, and she reportedly searched for the “fastest way to get a toddler to gain weight fast” and “bulk up a toddler fast” in July, per the court documents. One day after Schliefert made her final search regarding her son’s health, the Ozark Police Department reported to the family’s home when they received a report that a boy who was “unresponsive and not breathing.” The boy was ultimately pronounced dead at the scene.

In a recent development, first responders “were very concerned and expressed suspicions of child abuse, based on the physical appearance of the child who passed away,” according to the probable cause statements. Additionally, police said the boy “appeared to be severely malnourished, undersized, obviously weak and dirty,” looking like a “third world starved child” and “having no muscle to his arms and legs.”. An investigation was launched into the matter, and police reportedly found the boy’s body in a bedroom while “completely covered with a black and red ‘AC/DC’ blanket.” Additionally, there were “several cockroaches, and other bugs” crawling around the boy’s exposed food and hand.

Authorities determined that the home was filthy and “unsafe for children.”.

An autopsy was later conducted on the boy, which found “bumps” on his rib cage that “appeared to be tumors.” The autopsy also determined that his official cause of death was “metastatic neuroblastoma in the setting of medical negligence,” and the manner of death was ruled as homicide.

A detective spoke with Schliefert on Wednesday, and she reportedly claimed that doctors told her her son would live a “long happy life.” She also claimed she called to reschedule appointments at the Kansas City hospital, but “the hospital never called her back.” (The doctor who treated the child rejected her version of events.).

Schliefert allegedly told officers that her son had his last doctor’s appointment in August 2025 and claimed she didn’t take him to see doctors due to her lack of medical insurance.

Recent findings suggest that while speaking to authorities, Potter acknowledged that the adults in the home were concerned about the child’s health. However, she insisted he was “still eating, drinking, and playing” and never appeared to be sick.

Industry observers note that it’s not right now clear if either Schliefert and Potter have entered pleas following their arrests. The Ozark Police Department did not immediately respond to Us’ request for comment regarding updates in the case.

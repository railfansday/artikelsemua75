Thom Tillis move comes after Michael LoSapio, nominated for Cisa role, told hearing ‘I don’t believe it was an attack’.

Senator Thom Tillis, the outgoing North Carolina Republican who is due to be replaced by a Democrat, has reportedly blocked a confirmation vote on Donald Trump’s nominee to lead a key US cybersecurity agency, Michael LoSapio, after he denied during a hearing that the Capitol was attacked on January 6 2021.

Tillis, who declined to run for re-election and after repeated clashes with Trump’s, confirmed to HuffPost on Friday that he has placed a hold on LoSapio’s nomination to serve as a director of the US Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Agency (Cisa). His block on the nomination will prevent it from being included in a package of nominees to be voted on next month, before the Republicans lose their majority in the Senate. At his Senate confirmation hearing on Thursday, LoSapio, the chief information security officer for Palantir, a technology firm founded by JD Vance’s mentor, Peter Thiel, was asked by senator Richard Blumenthal, a Connecticut Democrat: “Was there an attack on the Capitol of January 6 2021?”.

After initially replying, “I don’t know,” LoSapio remarked: “No, I don’t believe it was an attack.”.

Tillis told the Hill that the question of whether a mob of Trump supporters attacked the US Capitol in an attempt to overturn Joe Biden’s 2020 victory was not open for debate.

Recent findings suggest that “It’s binary,” Tillis told the outlet. “The minute you pretend like it didn’t exist or you can’t even acknowledge it, I don’t have any time because it’s not a very thoughtful person.”.

The agency LoSapio was nominated by Trump to lead, Cisa, plays a role in protecting US elections infrastructure from hacking.

The week after the 2020 election, as Trump falsely claimed that count had been rigged, Cisa was one of a number of federal agencies with oversight of election infrastructure to issue a statement calling the election “the most secure in American history” and noting that there was “no evidence that any voting system deleted or lost votes, changed votes, or was in any way compromised.”. After he returned to the White House last year, Trump signed an April 2025 presidential memorandum directing executive offices to take steps to address supposed “risks from Chris Krebs”, who led Cisa in 2020.

“Krebs, through CISA, falsely and baselessly denied that the 2020 election was rigged and stolen, including by inappropriately and categorically dismissing widespread election malfeasance and serious vulnerabilities with voting machines,” Trump’s memorandum claimed.

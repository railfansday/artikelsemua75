According to recent reports, it is a harsh reality of rewatching Game of Thrones that the groundbreaking series has aged terribly. The series changed the way we approach fantasy TV by proving that far from being whimsical and child-focused, the genre could be as gritty and intense as any gangster show, while its enormous budget brought dragons and battles to life in a way rarely seen outside of blockbuster movies. Even so, while Game of Thrones remains one of fantasy TV's best epics, it has been overshadowed by the factors that turned its original fans off.

According to recent reports, game of Thrones' infamously rushed ending destroyed the character development that had been masterfully built over the first few seasons, and the intense multilayered political scheming gave way to sexual brutality and violence that became the norm rather than the game-changing exception. Now, many high fantasy TV shows are surpassing Game of Thrones, while fantasy of all subgenres are aging better, with many much older series overshadowing Game of Thrones and newer ones learning from its mistakes. Game of Thrones ran into problems when the TV series started to overtake George R. R. Martin's books, while Philip Pullman's books provided a clear framework for the way the story could be told onscreen.

Recent findings suggest that game of Thrones' early seasons had some flashes of wisdom that remain some of the best fantasy TV lines of all time, but the action and spectacle began to outshine the script, losing the show's best features. Instead, the series became known for sexual violence and dragons rather than infinitely quotable lines and political maneuvering. In a recent development, game of Thrones occasionally paid fan service, but The Magicians treated communication with its audience in a wildly different way, which is virtually guaranteed to give the series longevity. The show has been compared to Harry Potter for grownups, and its writers ensured this was addressed by making hilarious references to the controversial series. This cultural reference might have dated the show, if not for the fact that the characters are also heavy readers whose commentary on the fantasy genre is timeless, with many fantasy staples still being used to this day, including in Game of Thrones.

The Magicians also changed the fantasy genre, but in a more subtle way. Game of Thrones indicated what could be done with an enormous budget, but The Magicians addressed the psychological part of magic that few shows included. Rather than presenting magic as the solution to a problem, the series took a far more realistic approach, with mental illness, anxiety, and depression not disappearing with the right incantation. This approach is sometimes addressed in modern fantasy, but it is central to The Magicians, making the series feel both authentic and comforting.

Recent findings suggest that by the time Game of Thrones ended, viewers had largely stopped caring about the characters, even Jon Snow and Arya, whose further adventures once looked set to be perfect spinoff material. This dissatisfaction left viewers with little else to discuss, resulting in a franchise that is now being kept alive by its superior spinoffs and excellent source material. In contrast, Over the Garden Wall might only have lasted for ten minutes, but it is still one of fantasy's most intriguing series as it ended its perfect run with unanswered questions.

Following the adventures of two brothers navigating a mysterious forest, Over the Garden Wall's story and true meaning are still debated, with its themes resonating on different levels with viewers of all ages. That instantly makes it endlessly rewatchable, with a whimsical dark fantasy art style that will not age, while it feels inevitable that modern special effects will. Over the Garden Wall's total runtime is just under two hours, forever making it a much easier commitment than a multiple-season show with a disappointing ending.

Arcane is a perfect example of a genre-bending fantasy that creates something entirely upcoming while still fitting perfectly into the fantasy genre. Based on the League of Legends video game franchise, but telling an original story, Arcane straddles sci-fi, steampunk, and high fantasy without wasting a second of its runtime. Many fantasy TV shows are better than Game of Thrones, but Arcane outpaces it in virtually every way possible.

Both seasons of Arcane have a 100% positive critics' rating on Rotten Tomatoes.

According to recent reports, the multi-award-winning two-part series built a world that felt real and tangible, never lost its grip on its character development, and introduced a soundtrack that was the perfect finishing touch, outshining most other great fantasy TV series. Game of Thrones became too big for itself, neglecting the subtlety that defined its early seasons and dividing its characters along typical hero and villain lines, while Arcane is a masterclass in writing antiheroes.

Pushing Daisies is a fantasy TV show that was canceled before its time, making it into a forgotten gem that deserved more than its two seasons. This has allowed the show to age well, as fans are still invested in the fates of the characters, who deserve a fairytale ending, perhaps more than many Game of Thrones characters. The show, starring Anna Friel and a pre-Hobbit trilogy Lee Pace, centers on Ned (Pace,) a baker with the skill to bring the dead back to life with one touch, but kill them again with a second.

Recent findings suggest that this unique plot sets Pushing Daisies apart from virtually every other fantasy, including Game of Thrones, which is still a stereotypical entry for the genre, with its dragons and royals within a medieval-inspired setting. Pushing Daisies' central tragedy, with Ned being unable to touch his reanimated true love, has been turned into a bittersweet comedy, and with no subsequent show that comes close to improving on its tone, it is likely to continue aging like fine wine.

Sources indicate that avatar: The Last Airbender has a lot in common with Game of Thrones. Both series are high fantasy, featuring magic and warring nations, with warlords, quests, different cultures, and redemption arcs. However, Game of Thrones was heavily criticized for its regular sexual assault scenes and violence against women that many saw as gratuitous and unimaginative, neglecting the many other ways to drive a plot forward.

Recent findings suggest that despite being suitable for viewers of most ages, Avatar: The Last Airbender is sometimes gritty and emotionally heavy, but it does not include the graphic and sexualized violence that becomes a recurring theme in Game of Thrones. This alone has allowed it to age so well that many people who watched the series as children are now showing it to their own children. Avatar: The Last Airbender's best episodes are mini-masterpieces on their own, all combining to make a series with a consistent tone and lasting appeal, including on a rewatch.

Characters in Game of Thrones and Adventure Time grow up onscreen, and the tone of both shows shift, but while Finn's story sees him deal with complex emotions that bring more layers to the story, the coming of age of characters in Game of Thrones tends to be centered on marriage and sexual exploitation. Adventure Time might have been aimed at children, but it becomes more emotionally rich and layered over time than Game of Thrones.

Adventure Time was nominated for 16 Emmy Awards, winning eight of them.

It has been revealed that set in the land of Ooo, the surreal and imaginative Adventure Time grows with its audience, building on its own lore through Jake and Finn's adventures. Ooo has a rich history of its own, with past tragedies and surprising backstories for even its biggest villains, and one standout factor that beats even Game of Thrones fan favorite characters. Adventure Time's best characters are multifaceted, with few conforming to one archetype, like Princess Bubblegum, who is a ruler and scientific genius, while Game of Thrones often stuck with standard fantasy types, like warriors, queens, and adventurers.

Not all aspects of Buffy the Vampire Slayer have aged well. The series might be one of the best 1990s fantasy TV demonstrates ever made, but it is still a product of its time, and there was a long way to go for the portrayal of same-sex relationships onscreen. That mentioned, Buffy the Vampire Slayer paved the way for other TV shows to build on the good work it started, and it was revolutionary at the time it aired. Episodes like "Hush" played with format and this creativity made the show into a legend.

Like Buffy the Vampire Slayer, Game of Thrones began as a boundary-pushing TV show, but it did not take its initial pioneering approach much further. Characters like Brienne of Tarth were the exception rather than the norm, while Buffy the Vampire Slayer normalized women saving themselves and the world, and this has allowed the show to age better than most other fantasy TV series despite not being perfect itself.

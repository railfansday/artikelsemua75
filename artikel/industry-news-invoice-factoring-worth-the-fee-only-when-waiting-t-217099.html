According to recent reports, invoice factoring turns your unpaid invoices into cash within days, but the factoring manufacturer keeps 1% to 5% of each invoice for every 30 days it goes unpaid. Factor only when waiting would cost you more than that fee, and pick non-recourse factoring if one customer going broke would sink your business.

A trucking, staffing, manufacturing, or wholesale business can finish a job and wait 30 to 90 days to get paid, while the bills for the next job come due now.

Industry observers note that with factoring, you sell the unpaid invoice to a factoring company, or factor, which typically advances you 70% to 90% of its value within days. The factor collects from your customer and sends you the rest, minus its fee. The U.S. Chamber of Commerce puts that fee at 1% to 5% of the invoice for every 30 days it stays unpaid. Say a trucking company factors $80,000 in invoices due in 45 days, with a 90% advance and a 3% fee. It gets $72,000 now and the last $8,000, minus the $2,400 fee, when its customers pay. That $2,400 is the same cost as borrowing the $72,000 for 45 days at roughly 27% a year. (Related: How we sped up transformer inference 100x for 🤗 API cus...)

The fee is worth paying only when the cash earns more than $2,400 in those 45 days, such as by funding loads the organization would otherwise turn down. If the $72,000 would sit in the bank until its customers pay, the $2,400 is wasted.

A bank line of credit usually costs less than factoring, so get a quote on one first if your business qualifies. Factoring fits when you can’t get one or your sales have outgrown your credit limit, since the factor looks mostly at your customers’ credit.

Recourse factoring costs less than non-recourse, but you repay the factor when a customer never pays. Non-recourse shifts that loss to the factor, so buy it if one significant customer going broke would sink you. It usually covers only a customer that goes broke, not one that disputes the bill.

According to recent reports, before you sign, check the contract for a monthly minimum you must factor, a term of up to two years, and a fee to leave early. Then get the fees for invoices paid in 30, 60, and 90 days in writing, since a fee charged every 30 days triples when a customer takes 90 days to pay.

You can tell that Miranda Lambert is from Texas, but, until today, she’d never performed for her hometown football team — or anyone in the NFL, for that matter. Singing a medley of her classic hits (which she affectionately called “bangers and mash,” i.e., a mashup of her bangers), Lambert made her halftime show debut during the Thanksgiving Day matchup between the Dallas Cowboys and the Philadelphia Eagles at AT&T Stadium.

According to recent reports, following a pretaped introduction by Reba McEntire, who confirmed Lambert as the “most awarded artist in the history of the Academy of Country Music,” the Grammy-winning singer took the stage to perform “Snakeskin Boots” (sorry, no Ella Langley cameo), “Kerosene,” “Gunpowder & Lead,” “Mama’s Broken Heart,” and “Little Red Wagon.” Lambert was backed by her band, along with a string section wearing black cowboy hats to mirror that of the entertainer: Lambert, in a black hat, wore a black dress adorned with blue fames. She also received an assist from an army of Dallas Cowboys Cheerleaders, who delivered their choreographed routine as Lambert sang. Recent findings suggest that lambert is a lifelong Cowboys fan who grew up in Lindale, Texas, about 80 miles east of Dallas. She now joins a cast of music’s biggest names as a halftime show veteran, including Beyoncé, Reba McEntire, LeAnn Rimes, Lainey Wilson, and Dolly Parton, who gave a memorable performance while wearing the iconic Dallas Cowboy Cheerleaders costume. Coined the “Salvation Army’s 30th Red Kettle Kickoff,” it was McEntire who performed at the inaugural broadcast, which kicks off the annual holiday fundraising drive. Lambert, true to form, announced her appearance from atop a longhorn bull. “It’s my first time ever to do this,” Lambert noted during a visit to the Calm Down With Erin and Charissa podcast. “I’ve been doing this 23 years, and it’s my first halftime show ever. Isn’t that crazy? I don’t have a lot of firsts in my career at this point, but I was like okay, here we go.”.

It’s been a packed year for Lambert: her album Crisco came out in October, and she just took home a handful of CMA Awards for her work as producer and writer on Ella Langley’s Dandelion and song “Choosin’ Texas.” Next up is the Grammy Awards, where she is nominated seven times and could potentially make history if she wins Producer of the Year.

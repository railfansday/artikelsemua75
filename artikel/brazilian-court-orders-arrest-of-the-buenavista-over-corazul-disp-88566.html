Brazilian Court Orders Arrest of the Buenavista Over Corazul Dispute

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that A Brazilian court ordered the arrest of Corazul’s the Buenavista at the Port of Santos after the cruise line’s exclusive Brazilian distributor claimed it was owed about $270,000 (R$1.48 million), according to a court decision dated Nov. 13.

Judge Frederico dos Santos Messias of the São Paulo State Court of Justice’s specialized maritime court granted the request from R11 Travel Viagens e Turismo against GLCL Global Cruiseline Ltd., the Cyprus-registered company that operates as Corazul.

The judge issued the order without first hearing from Corazul, the decision showed. (Related: IShowSpeed breaks down in tears after finishing GTA 6 i...)

The order barred the Buenavista from leaving Brazilian waters. The court noted the ship was anchored off Santos for repairs.

The judge set security at about $323,000 (R$1.78 million) and mentioned the ship would be released if Corazul deposited that amount or posted a bank guarantee.

According to the decision, R11 said Corazul owed about $1.27 million (R$6.96 million) under a four-year exclusive distribution agreement signed in March. That figure included about $780,000 (EUR 675,000) in unpaid quarterly marketing contributions. After subtracting about $1 million (R$5.48 million) in passenger funds it held, R11 put the net balance at about $270,000. (Related: Jimmy John’s reveals Christmas Vacation meal with exclu...)

Recent findings suggest that the filing, as summarized by the court, described a series of disruptions including the cancellation of the Buenavista’s European season in June; the cancellation of its Oct. 23 inaugural Brazilian cruise nine days before departure and complaints about onboard service on the Oct. 30 sailing. The company has since suspended operations and R11 has stopped selling cruises on the ship.

Recent findings suggest that R11 mentioned Corazul demanded $400,000 on Nov. 4 before it would operate the Nov. 6 departure. R11 refused.

The court noted that only two of the 11 sailings scheduled between Oct. 23 and Nov. 27 operated. Corazul also suspended operations until Nov. 30 and moved the end of its Brazil season up to March 5, 2027. The judge wrote that Corazul’s own communications showed a “serious liquidity collapse.”.

According to recent reports, the dispute is headed to arbitration in São Paulo under AMCHAM, the American Chamber of Commerce for Brazil. The decision stated that R11 must file within 30 days or the order lapses. Corazul has five days after being served to contest it.

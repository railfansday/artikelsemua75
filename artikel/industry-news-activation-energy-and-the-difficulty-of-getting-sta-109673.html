In a recent development, in chemistry, the concept of activation energy is the minimum amount of energy required to start a chemical reaction. Metaphorically, this concept can be applied to psychology, thinking about both formal behavioral activation and initiating typical life tasks. I (JS) have often felt like there is an activation energy for everyday things we need to do. How much energy would it take to get myself to start cleaning the bathroom or to start exercising or to make dinner when I feel like just having a bowl of cereal? How much energy would it take for our clients to start doing the things we talk about them doing in session? What would it take for them to get out of bed, practice a latest coping strategy, or talk with their partner or children in a more effective way? Sometimes it just feels impossible to get started, like there’s a barrier to overcome. (Related: PENN’s Interactive revenue rise rests on partner taxes ...)

In a recent development, I can’t speak to this concept in chemistry. It has been many years since I was in a chem lab. However, when difficulty following through comes up in psychotherapy, I have some ideas that relate to the concept of activation energy. And this comes up a lot. Very simply, we can either lower barriers, therefore reducing the metaphorical activation energy necessary, or increase the energy we have to initiate the intended activity or task.

In a recent development, to increase the energy you have, you might look at what gives you start-up energy (to activate), what drains you, and maybe what sustains your energy. Your schedule might be too full or just inefficient. There might be ways to rejigger things so that you have larger chunks of open time. Perhaps doing something that gets you excited and recharged can help propel you into doing something challenging. Maybe even playing energetic music while getting ready and doing the task. Planning to reward yourself after starting or completing the task may help you get excited—“eyes on the prize” and all that. Whatever strategy you come up with should be individualized to what works for you, what is doable, and what will help with follow-through.

Sources indicate that this holiday season, skip the mimosa and pour a poinsettia cocktail instead. It’s tart, sparkly and guaranteed to make any brunch feel like a holiday party.

Cranberries are a fast way to really make it feel like December has arrived. And this ruby-colored poinsettia cocktail, which gets its name from the brilliant red flowers in season at the same time, is our fresh go-to cranberry drink. Recent findings suggest that this festive holiday cocktail doesn’t get any flavor from the poinsettia (definitely don’t eat those), but it matches the color of the flowers with a mix of tart cranberry juice and a splash of triple sec (for orange-y sweetness). It’s topped off with chilled champagne for festive effervescence. No shaker, no muddler, no syrup simmering on the stove. Just a sparkler you can pour for a crowd faster than my uncle can bring up politics. (Related: Latest Update: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

Industry observers note that that’s exactly why this is my go-to pour for Christmas parties, New Year’s Eve countdowns or any cozy fall or winter get-together. You can pour a round for a crowd in the time it takes to find the bottle opener and a few fresh cranberries. A rosemary sprig makes every glass look like it belongs on a holiday card.

Pour the cranberry juice into a champagne flute or wine glass.

Editor’s Tip: Chill your glasses in the fridge or freezer for 10 to 15 minutes before pouring the cocktails. A cold glass keeps the drink crisp and helps hold onto the bubbles longer.

If you’re using it, add the triple sec to the glass with the cranberry juice.

Slowly top the glass with the chilled champagne.

Editor’s Tip: Tilt the glass slightly as you pour, just like you would with a beer, so the bubbles don’t foam up and spill over the rim.

In a recent development, if desired, drop a few fresh cranberries into the glass and tuck in a sprig of rosemary. Serve the cocktails right away while the bubbles are at their liveliest.

According to recent reports, poinsettias are best poured and enjoyed right away, since champagne goes flat rapidly once it’s opened and mixed. If you have extra cranberry juice and triple sec mixed up, cover it and refrigerate it for up to three days. Recork any leftover sparkling wine with a champagne stopper, and keep it in the fridge for a day or two.

Recent findings suggest that it comes down to how sweet you like your drinks. Pure cranberry juice is tart and tangy, and the triple sec adds just enough sweetness to balance it without dulling that pucker. Cranberry juice cocktail, on the other hand, is usually sweetened and/or blended with other juices, so it makes a softer, sweeter poinsettia. If you’re serving a mixed crowd, I like to stick with the pure stuff and set out a little extra triple sec for anyone who wants it sweeter (and doubly, a bit boozier).

They’re very close relatives. A classic mimosa is roughly equal parts orange juice and champagne, while a poinsettia gets a smaller pour of cranberry juice, so it tastes more like champagne with a cranberry accent. The triple sec also sneaks in a little orange flavor, giving a nod to the mimosa. Plus, cranberry-orange recipes are always a hit!

Sources indicate that chilling everything ahead of time sets the stage for the best poinsettia. Since this drink is built in the glass and served without ice, it’s the cold juice and cold champagne that keep it refreshing. Warm champagne also foams up more when you pour it and loses its bubbles faster. Pop the juice and triple sec in the fridge a few hours before serving, and give your bubbly at least three hours in the fridge or 20 to 30 minutes in an ice bucket.

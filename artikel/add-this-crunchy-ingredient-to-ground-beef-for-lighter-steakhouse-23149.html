The actual difference between a hamburger and a steakburger comes down to the quality of meat used in the grind, but the difference between a homemade steakburger and one from a steakhouse is all about a lighter texture. Melissa Clark, author of "The Massive Book of Dinner: Recipes and Rules (and How to Break Them)," tells us that achieving that characteristic is quite easy if you use ground cracker crumbs. (Related: Gift Tax Rules: How Much Can You Give Tax-Free?...)

In a recent development, "Any kind of cracker or breadcrumbs will make a burger lighter," Clark says. "Ritz and saltines work really well. They are very airy, which helps lighten the ground meat." Beef is rather dense, even if you avoid overworking it into a firm, puck-like consistency that's more similar to sausage than a tender patty. But a quarter cup of ground crackers per pound of meat can make a world of difference. (Related: Primary vs Contingent Beneficiary: What's the Diff...)

When meat cooks, it contracts and releases juice and fat. Crackers, however, absorb them rather than allowing them to seep out of the patty, preventing it from shriveling up too much. Since steakhouse burgers typically use thick patties, this also keeps them nice and moist. Whereas a thin sliver of well-crusted beef may be just what you want in a smashburger, retaining moisture is a vital component to keeping your steakhouse burgers not only light, but also at their peak flavor.

In a recent development, using plain, unsalted crackers can still keep your steakhouse burgers light, but it would be a missed opportunity for better flavor. While you also don't want to use something starch-free, like a nut- or seed-based variety, Melissa Clark is a major believer in picking a tasty brand and smashing them up properly.

Sources indicate that "Using crackers can add great flavor – Ritz, with their butteriness, are especially lovely," Clark explains. Flavorful but unobtrusive, Ritz crackers are the perfect addition to any savory dish because just about everything can benefit from their toasty richness. But if you want to get a bit experimental, you can also just as easily use Cheez-Its or anything seasoned. This is an easy way to avoid coming up with a specialty spice blend since you'll be adding enough to thoroughly flavor your meat.

Sources indicate that clark is also a proponent of using whatever method you can to grind the crackers into a fine powder. "Grind the crackers in a food processor, blender or tuck them in a plastic bag and crush them under a rolling pin," she added. Large chunks can make some bites unexpectedly soggy and, if using a seasoned cracker, a little too flavorful. Plus, if some parts of your blend don't have a starch, they may end up expressing their juices too much, leaving some bites juicy and light and others dense and dry.

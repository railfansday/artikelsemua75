The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping has begun to ignite the box office.

Lionsgate released the sixth film in the dystopian franchise in 4,018 North American theaters, and it grossed $56 million on Friday, including Thursday previews, as it heads for more than $120 million for the weekend. This would give it the third-best debut Friday in franchise history, besting the two Mockingjay titles ($55.1 million for 2014’s Part 1, $45.6 million for 2015’s Part 2) and 2023’s The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes ($19 million) but behind 2013’s Catching Fire ($70.9 million) and 2012’s The Hunger Games ($67.2 million). Sunrise earned an A- CinemaScore from audiences, putting it below the A grades of the Jennifer Lawrence-led initial 2012 movie that kicked off the series and 2013’s Catching Fire but matching the A- grades of the two Mockingjay films. The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping will notch the fifth-biggest opening in Lionsgate history, as it also stands at No. 5 among 2026 domestic openings for all studios. It represents the highest-grossing launch for the careers of stars Glenn Close, Jesse Plemons, Kieran Culkin and Elle Fanning. This weekend’s haul also brings the Hunger Games franchise’s box office total to roughly $3.437 billion to make it the studio’s highest-grossing franchise ever, topping The Twilight Saga. In a recent development, lionsgate had projected The Hunger Games: Sunrise on the Reaping to gross north of $80 million, but other sources expected that it would collect between $100 million and $130 million, which now looks to be the case. Adapting author Suzanne Collins’ prequel novel that was published last year, director Francis Lawrence‘s movie carries a production budget of roughly $150 million as it revitalizes the franchise in telling a story set 24 years before the events of Collins’ original 2008 book.

This opening represents a major comeback for the series after registering its lowest-performing entry with 2023 prequel The Hunger Games: The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes, given that Sunrise on the Reaping is looking to open with more than double its launch. Starring Tom Blyth, Rachel Zegler and Hunter Schafer, Songbirds & Snakes — telling the backstory for the villainous President Snow, played by Ralph Fiennes in the upcoming movie — disappointed, opening to $44 million domestically for the weekend before ending its run with $166.4 million domestically and a global sum of $361.8 million. It earned a B+ CinemaScore, the lowest for the franchise.

It has been revealed that sunrise on the Reaping holds a 67 percent critical score on Rotten Tomatoes, lagging behind the franchise’s first four movies but besting the 64 percent approval for Songbirds & Snakes. The fresh film has a franchise-best 95 audience score on Rotten Tomatoes. In his review for The Hollywood Reporter, chief film critic David Rooney wrote that it “delivers on emotion and brutal spectacle.”.

Sources indicate that the movie opened the day after the launch of the hotly anticipated video game Grand Theft Auto 6, which does not appear to be preventing the film from thriving. Joseph Zada stars as Haymitch Abernathy, providing the backstory for the character whom Woody Harrelson brought to life in the first four movies. The cast for Sunrise on the Reaping also includes Mckenna Grace, Whitney Peak and Maya Hawke.

Among other weekend titles, Amazon MGM Studios expanded I Play Rocky to 2,477 North American locations after two frames in limited release. Peter Farrelly’s Rocky origin story earned $3.8 million on Friday, including Thursday previews, as it heads for an estimated $9.3 million. Starring Anthony Ippolito as a young Sylvester Stallone, I Play Rocky landed a rare A+ CinemaScore, becoming only the second release this year to do so (after April’s A Great Awakening).

In a recent development, I Play Rocky holds an 89 percent approval rating from critics on Rotten Tomatoes and a 98 percent audience score. Rooney’s review described it as a “substantial-hearted movie about moviemaking, if not an artful one.”.

Industry observers note that in its sophomore frame, Paramount’s horror-tinged holiday offering Ebenezer is likely to claim third place with roughly $8.2 million for a decline of around 57 percent, bringing its domestic cume to $33 million. Johnny Depp marks his studio comeback vehicle with director Ti West’s reimagining of Charles Dickens’ A Christmas Carol, which opened atop last weekend’s box office.

It has been revealed that continuing to draw families in its third weekend is Warner Bros.’ The Cat in the Hat, as the animated adaptation of Dr. Seuss’ classic children’s book is expected to gross around $7 million for a domestic total of $47.2 million. This marks a respectable decline of roughly 38 percent for directors Erica Rivinoja and Alessandro Carloni’s movie that features the voice of Bill Hader in the lead role. It is set to face fresh competition in the animated space from Disney’s Hexed over the forthcoming Thanksgiving frame.

Gkids and Toho’s Godzilla Minus Zero is likely to close out the top five with $2.9 million in its third weekend to reach a domestic cume of around $54.7 million.

Focus Features opened Elsinore in six theaters in Upcoming York and Los Angeles, with director Simon Stone’s Oscar hopeful likely to collect approximately $240,000 to average $40,000 per screen. Andrew Scott stars in the biographical film about Scottish actor Ian Charleson, who navigates personal challenges while preparing for a pivotal performance of Hamlet. After earning acclaim at Telluride and TIFF, Elsinore holds an 87 percent rating from critics on Rotten Tomatoes, with Rooney deeming it “a hymn to the theater, powered by a towering lead turn.”.

Toward the bottom of the top 10 is Ramayana: Rise of a Legend, with Sony Pictures distributing the Prime Focus Studios religious epic that is expected to gross $2 million for its third weekend to bring its domestic take to $28.3 million through Sunday. Director Nitesh Tiwari’s project becomes the highest-grossing Indian film ever at the North American box office.

With Thanksgiving arriving next weekend, the holiday frame brings an array of new options, including Disney’s Hexed, Universal’s Meet the Parents sequel Focker-in-Law, Netflix’s The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth and Angel’s biographical Hershey.

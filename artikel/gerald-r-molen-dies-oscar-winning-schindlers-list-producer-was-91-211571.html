Gerald R. Molen, an Academy Award-winning producer behind Steven Spielberg‘s Amblin Entertainment, has died. He was 91.

It has been revealed that on Sunday, Molen’s niece Rachel Pullan announced that he died earlier that day. “This morning my great uncle, Gerald R. Molen, passed on to join my great grandma and his dear wife Pat on the other side of the veil,” she wrote on Instagram. “I am so happy for him, and also so very sad.

“Uncle Jerry was a man of great moral character and high integrity. He started doing work, moving set pieces and driving at a movie studio, and worked his way up to the position of producer. He worked on many great films, including Jurassic Park, Schindler’s List (for which he won the Oscar for best production), Rain Man, Minority Report and The Other Side of Heaven, just to name a few. He held to his beliefs and standards and industry where that was often difficult to do. Minority Report is rated PG-13 because Jerry told Steven Spielberg he would only do the movie if it wasn’t rated R, and Spielberg made it happen—it was worth it to him to have Jerry be his producer. Pullan added, “I’m glad I was able to go see Uncle Jerry a couple of times in the last few years before he passed. I will be watching Jurassic Park in his honor tonight. It was definitely one of his personal favorites stash as evidenced by the number of dinosaur she had on every surface in his house. Love you, Uncle Jerry— say hi to great Grandma for me.” Watch on Deadline.

In a recent development, born Jan. 6, 1935 in Great Falls, Montana, Gerald Robert Molen grew up in North Hollywood after his family relocated there.

In a recent development, in 1985, Molen began working with Spielberg as a unit production manager on The Color Purple, before going on to produce his movies Hook (1991), Jurassic Park (1993) and Schindler’s List (1993), which earned them the Best Picture Oscar. They also worked together on The Lost World: Jurassic Park (1997), Amistad (1997), Minority Report (2002) and Catch Me If You Can (2002).

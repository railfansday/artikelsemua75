UK FCA Bars Crypto Exchanges From Market Making on Their Own Venues

The U.K. will bar crypto exchange operators from trading against their own capital on the venues they run, while a fresh Treasury order clears independent market makers to trade on those platforms without a separate dealing permission.

The Financial Conduct Authority’s final rules say a firm “must not execute any orders against its proprietary capital” on a qualifying trading platform it operates. The FCA’s guidance spells out the effect: a firm “cannot act as a market maker” on its own platform. The restriction takes effect Oct. 25, 2027.

The FCA rule itself is not fresh. The regulator published it on June 30 in policy statement PS26/11, part of a five-document package that completed its crypto rulebook. What changed last week was the Treasury’s side of the framework.

It has been revealed that the FCA’s rulebook defines a market maker as a person who holds themselves out on a platform on a continuous basis as willing to buy and sell qualifying cryptoassets against their own capital at prices they set. That is the activity operators will be barred from on their own venues.

Industry observers note that there is one carve-out. An operator with the appropriate permission may still engage in matched principal trading to execute client orders on its platform, as long as it follows the platform’s non-discretionary rules. The FCA warned that this model does not rule out settlement risk, so firms should take steps to minimize it. (Related: Latest Update: How to train a newly introduced language...)

It has been revealed that the bar applies only to the operator’s own platform. Under the same rules, an operator with the right permission can still trade against its own capital outside the venue it runs. It must make clear to clients which capacity it is acting in, and it must manage conflicts of interest.

According to recent reports, the Treasury made the Financial Services and Markets Act 2000 (Cryptoassets) (Miscellaneous Amendments) Regulations 2026 on Nov. 18. They add a fresh Article 9UA to the U.K.’s crypto legislation.

The article carves two kinds of activity out of the regulated activity of dealing in qualifying cryptoassets as principal. One is activity carried on “otherwise than for the purpose of providing a service to another person.” The other is activity carried on “as a market maker on a qualifying cryptoasset trading platform.”.

In practice, a third-party firm quoting prices on a U.K. crypto platform would not need the principal-dealing permission for that activity. The text does not say who the market maker has to be, so the bar on operators comes from the FCA’s rules rather than from the Treasury order. The explanatory note says no impact assessment was prepared because no significant new costs are expected. (Related: Few-shot learning in practice: GPT-Neo and the 🤗 Accele...)

Sources indicate that the FCA’s rules also put new duties on platforms that rely on market makers. Operators must identify, document and monitor customers running market-making strategies. If they offer incentive schemes or other arrangements to liquidity providers, they must document and disclose the terms. The FCA stated it will not require formal contracts with every market maker.

Recent findings suggest that the change lands as exchanges work through the U.K.’s licensing process. The FCA opened its authorization gateway on Sept. 30, and firms that want to keep operating in the U.K. must apply by Feb. 28, 2027. Authorization is not automatic, the regulator said at the time.

Until the full regime starts, the FCA’s crypto oversight stays limited to financial promotions and anti-money laundering controls. That is the regulator’s own description. The U.K. has also been enforcing those existing limits, as HTX’s October settlement demonstrated.

The FCA’s June announcement framed the package as a broader bid to make the U.K. a global crypto hub. David Geale, the FCA’s executive director of payments and digital finance, said the framework means firms “can have both” regulatory certainty and room to innovate.

According to recent reports, the latest rules, including the ban on operators making markets on their own venues, take effect on Oct. 25, 2027.

Check Walmart’s $4 Generic List – Its Cash Prices Can Beat Your Copay

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that before your next refill, look up your prescription on Walmart’s $4 generic list. In most states, about 20 drugs cost $4 a month or $10 for 90 days, and dozens more cost $9 a month or $24 for 90 days. If you’re insured, ask the pharmacist for both the list price and your plan’s price.

A copay can cost more than a generic’s cash price. Walmart posts its list prices, so you can check before you pay.

Most of the $4 drugs are for heart health or blood pressure, such as hydrochlorothiazide, atenolol, and furosemide, and the rest include levothyroxine for thyroid problems and a few mental health drugs. Metformin for diabetes and simvastatin for cholesterol cost $9 for 30 days or $24 for 90, and a few drugs cost $15 for 30 days.

Industry observers note that antibiotics, antihistamines, and steroids aren’t on the list. Higher doses cost more, a 90-day supply may need your prescriber’s OK, and prices can run higher in some states, including California and Minnesota. Walmart pharmacies in North Dakota don’t offer the program at all. (Related: Industry News: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

Look up each generic you take by name and strength, and compare the list price with what your strategy charges for the same supply. Hydrochlorothiazide, for example, is $10 for 90 days. At a $15 copay per 30-day fill, the same 90-day supply costs $45 through insurance, so paying the $10 list price instead saves $35 every three months, though that $10 usually won’t count toward your deductible. On a high-deductible plan whose deductible you expect to meet this year, the insurance price can work out cheaper over the year, since what you pay through insurance counts toward it.

For a drug that’s missing from the list or costs more there than your copay, a prescription discount card may still cost less than your copay.

Once the list price wins, ask the pharmacy at the Walmart you pass most often to transfer your prescription, since the program requires your first fill in person.

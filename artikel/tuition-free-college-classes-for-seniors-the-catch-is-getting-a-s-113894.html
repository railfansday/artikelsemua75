Tuition-Free College Classes for Seniors – The Catch Is Getting a Seat

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In many states, public colleges waive tuition for residents once they reach 60, 62, or 65, so you can take regular college classes for little more than books and fees. Ask a nearby public college’s registrar for its tuition waiver for older students, and find out when waiver students may register.

Sources indicate that the other rules vary by state. Florida law lets the state’s public universities and colleges waive tuition and fees for residents 60 and older who audit classes, meaning they sit in without earning credit. At the University of North Georgia, Georgia residents 62 and older can take classes for credit or audit them, but they can’t register for fall or spring terms until the first day of classes. Kentucky law requires the state’s public colleges to waive all tuition and fees for residents 65 and older unless classes are full. Industry observers note that to find your state’s version, call the registrar or continuing education office at a nearby public college and ask for its tuition waiver for older students. Bring proof of age and residency, such as a driver’s license, and ask whether you can earn credit and which fees you still owe. Where no nearby school offers one, ask your state’s higher education agency which campuses do.

It has been revealed that if no seat opens, look up the nearest Osher Lifelong Learning Institute. The institutes, at colleges in all 50 states, run noncredit classes for people 50 and older, with no tests or grades, for fees that vary by institute. A community college’s continuing education classes and universities’ free online courses are other options.

For a waiver seat, call the registrar a semester ahead, since you may need to apply for admission by the college’s usual deadline before the waiver covers a class.

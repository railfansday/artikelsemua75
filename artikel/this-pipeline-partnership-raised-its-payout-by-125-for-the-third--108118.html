In a recent development, MPLX (MPLX -1.27%), a midstream firm spun off from Marathon Petroleum (MPC -0.20%) in 2012, operates over 10,000 miles of crude oil and light-product pipelines in 14 states. It pays a forward yield of 8.9%, and it's raised its annual payout by 12.5% for three consecutive years. (Related: All three northeast Indiana counties reject casino, lea...)

MPLX is a master limited partnership (MLP) that passes its taxable income through to investors rather than paying federal corporate income tax. These distributions are often treated in part as a return of capital, which reduces an investor's cost basis and defers their taxes until the position is sold. That structure can be more tax-efficient than earning dividends from traditional corporations, but it requires investors to report an additional K-1 tax form every year. According to recent reports, MLPs cover their distributions (not dividends) with their distributable cash flow (DCF). MPLX spent 64%, 65.3%, and 71.5% of its annualized DCF on distributions in 2023, 2024, and 2025, respectively. That ratio declined as it ramped up its investments and acquisitions in 2025, but it can probably still afford to raise its annual distributions for at least the next few years.

It has been revealed that MPLX has already generated a total return of 179% over the past five years, but it could still have more upside potential if oil prices stay elevated and its pipeline volumes hold steady. It's well-insulated from volatile oil prices -- since it merely charges companies "tolls" to use its infrastructure -- and it should remain a reliable income play for the foreseeable future.

Tech Report: Airport Food Is Optional – TSA Lets You Bring Lunch and an Empty Water Bottle

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Pack sandwiches and snacks from your kitchen and carry an empty water bottle to fill past security, since TSA lets solid food and empty bottles through the checkpoint. Spreads, yogurt, and drinks fall under the liquids rule, so each needs a container of 3.4 ounces or less.

Say an airport sandwich costs $13 and a bottle of water $4. That puts lunch for a family of four at $68, before snacks.

For a long day or a layover, pack food that keeps without a fridge. The USDA’s Food Safety and Inspection Service claims perishable food shouldn’t sit at room temperature more than two hours, or one hour above 90 degrees. Its list of foods that need no refrigeration includes bread, crackers, peanut butter, jelly, hard cheese, and whole fruit. For anything perishable, bring an ice pack frozen solid, since a slushy one counts as a liquid unless it’s cooling formula, breast milk, or baby food.

Most fresh fruits and vegetables can’t come along on a flight to the mainland from Hawaii, Puerto Rico, or the U.S. Virgin Islands. The USDA bars almost all fresh fruits and vegetables brought into the U.S. from abroad, even ones handed out on the plane, and the EU bars meat and dairy brought from outside it. Finish those foods before landing, and declare what’s left at U.S. customs.

When you do buy, choose a grab-and-go market over a sit-down restaurant, where a tip adds to the bill. Use any airport dining credit or lounge access your card already includes first.

Pack the night before: make the sandwiches, freeze the ice packs solid, and put the empty bottles in your carry-on.

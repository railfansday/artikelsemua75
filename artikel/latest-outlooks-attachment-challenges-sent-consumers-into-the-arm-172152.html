Microsoft recommended Classic or the web client while two rounds of fixes restored normal service.

A recent patch broke attachment handling for some Fresh Outlook customers, prompting Microsoft to recommend switching to the web or Classic version of its email client. (Related: How to Budget for a Wedding Without Going Broke...)

It has been revealed that things are going swimmingly for Fresh Outlook, the email client that Microsoft would very much like users to migrate to. A code change meant that for some users, opening or uploading email attachments failed. It took the tech giant two attempts to fix the problem. (Related: GTA 6’s Mandy actress responds to the internet going cr...)

Sources indicate that microsoft confirmed there were issues on November 20, as reports that customers were having problems with attachments began rolling in. By the end of the day, the company had added inline images in email to the litany of woe and "paused the rollout of a recent client update that may be contributing to attachment failures.".

The advice for affected owners was to switch to the web version, or use Outlook Classic to open or upload attachments and view inline images.

The good news was that Microsoft produced a fix. The bad news was that this fix only resolved problems with Office attachments and inline images, leaving other attachment types affected, according to incident notice EX1561765.

The Windows behemoth tried again with a "supplemental fix" and reported the remaining problems resolved on November 21.

Owners needed to close and reopen Upcoming Outlook for the fix to take effect.

Recent findings suggest that microsoft mentioned it had tested the fixes before deployment. Administrators might reasonably wish the original update had received the same attention. The preliminary root cause was a "recent update to the code which supports embedded web content unexpectedly caused impact.".

The clock is ticking for Outlook Classic. Microsoft is clear that New Outlook is the future, though Classic Outlook will remain available until at least 2029. The fact that the company had to suggest that affected consumers fall back on the old warhorse to perform that rarely used email activity – attachment handling – is less than confidence-inspiring.

Still, by the time Outlook Classic is finally put out to pasture, we're sure Microsoft's habit of sending out broken updates will also be a thing of the past. Right? ®.

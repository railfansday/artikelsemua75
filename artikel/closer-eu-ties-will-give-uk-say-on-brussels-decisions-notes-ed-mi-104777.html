Closer links with the EU will mean the UK having a say on decisions made by Brussels, Ed Miliband has argued as ministers faced pressure to move further after Friday’s landmark deal with the EU.

Any timetable for closer integration, including possible talks about the UK rejoining the EU, remains unclear after the Brussels summit, with Andy Burnham saying he would now “brainstorm” over future ties.

Emily Thornberry, the Labour MP who chairs the Commons foreign affairs committee, said Burnham should push for membership.

“The problem with being outside the bloc is that we end up in a permanent state of negotiation, because we always have to play catchup, rather than having a seat at the table,” she wrote in an article for the Sunday Mirror. “That’s why I think we should simply be back in the European Union.”.

Speaking to BBC One’s Sunday with Laura Kuenssberg show, Miliband refused to say whether he now supported discussions on potentially rejoining, but noted the deal reached at Friday’s summit – on food and drink, emissions trading and a mutual youth mobility scheme – indicated the benefits of closer links, including UK input to EU regulations that were being followed anyway. Recent findings suggest that “At the moment, what the situation is is that we are essentially rule takers,” the foreign secretary said. “Businesses are rule takers if they want to sell into the European market. So they have to abide by the rules of the European Union, but at the same time, because we are not integrated into that market for food and agricultural products, they have to fill in a whole bunch of forms.”. He remarked this was “the worst of all worlds” and it meant there was a clear case to integrate more closely into EU markets.

Another reason for even closer ties, he said, was the transformed geopolitical order since 2016, including Russia’s full-scale invasion of Ukraine and the US’s distancing from Europe under Donald Trump.

He said: “I was not in favour of Brexit, as you know, but I accepted the result, and I think it was right to accept the result. [But] I think there’s a key point: the world has changed massively since 2016, and we can’t stand still.

“How can we say: ‘Well, this is all it is, the status quo is fine, even with the summit today. We’re not even going to look at the future of our relationship with the European Union.’ Of course, we’ve got to do that, and we’ve got to lay out the options.”.

Speaking for the Conservatives on the Sunday morning broadcast round, Alex Burghart, Kemi Badenoch’s deputy, noted Burnham should simply be concentrating on better defence ties with Europe, arguing that the public would not want to return to the EU.

Industry observers note that asked if the Conservatives would undo the deal made at Friday’s summit if they won power, Burghart said they would. He said: “Yes, we would scrap it. I don’t think that there will be good outcomes from it. And I also don’t think it is a good idea for a country like Britain to hand over control of its rules to another power and have no say over how those rules are created right into the future. This is a silly idea.”.

Burnham faces some nervousness in his cabinet and the parliamentary Labour party more widely over talk of possibly rejoining, with some MPs wary about the bitter political fight this could set off.

But Thornberry in her article praised Burnham for having “finally ended the conspiracy of silence over Brexit”, saying polls indicated that a majority of the UK public now backed rejoining.

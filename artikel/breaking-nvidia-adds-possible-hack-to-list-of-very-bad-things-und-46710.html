📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Ransomware group sets a deadline for the chipmaker to pay up - or see its data leaked.

Finding a newly introduced CEO is just one of their problems. Nvidia is investigating an alleged breach following claims by ransomware group The Gentlemen's affiliates that they hacked the AI giant and stole sensitive information.

Industry observers note that the embattled chipmaker told The Register's that it was investigating a possible third-party data breach. “We have found no indication that Nvidia systems or networks were affected,” a spokesperson stated. “We continue to monitor the situation.". Industry observers note that the ransomware gang’s data-leak site, shared on social media and seen by The Register, doesn’t show any stolen files or indicate what information the group claims to have stolen. Nvidia first appeared on the criminals’ blog on Wednesday, with The Gentlemen promising “DATA INFO - SOON.”. (Related: Industry News: My Journey to a serverless transformers ...)

The post also included a countdown, ending November 28.

According to recent reports, while we can’t verify the criminals’ claims, the group, which emerged in mid-2025, is one of the most prolific ransomware-as-a-service operations. Its affiliates claimed more than 320 victims as of April, with the majority of these alleged ransomware infections (240) occurring in the first half of 2026.

Industry observers note that the actual count, however, is likely much higher. Check Point researchers accessed an affiliate’s live command-and-control server during an incident response case earlier this year. The server contained victim telemetry that revealed more than 1,570 victims, mostly located in the US, and primarily corporate and organizational environments.

According to an operational technology (OT) threat report from Trellix published on Wednesday, industrial ransomware claims jumped nearly 51 percent - to about 1,500 posts- compared to last quarter, and The Gentlemen’s affiliates were the most prolific claim posters during this time period. They accounted for about 16 percent of all posts.

It has been revealed that in February 2022, an Nvidia data breach exposed employees’ credentials and proprietary code. Extortion crew Lapsus$ claimed to be responsible for that intrusion and data theft.®.

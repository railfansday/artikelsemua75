Stephen King's horror adaptation gets a upcoming life.

It has been revealed that king's career began with his first novel, Carrie, published in 1974, which allowed him to become a full-time writer and later be named the King of Horror. King has a career spanning six decades and has published over 50 books and around 200 short stories, selling over 350 million copies of his work worldwide. Over the years, his novels have received many adaptations in movies, TV series, radio demonstrates, stage plays, and more. Now, a forgotten adaptation is returning in a brand-new way. In a recent development, the adaptation of King's novel from 1991, titled Needful Things, is getting a stunning new release. The 1993 movie Needful Things is set to get a 4K UHD and Blu-ray limited edition SteelBook release, with pre-orders open from December 1 ahead of its release on January 20, 2027. ViaVision Entertainment is releasing Needful Things in a whole new way with only 1,500 copies available. The movie has been restored from the original 35mm camera negative with both the 121-minute theatrical cut and the 191-minute television cut. Stephen King’s best-selling horror novel comes to life in Needful Things, the ultimate tale of buyer beware, starring Ed Harris, Max von Sydow, Bonnie Bedelia, and J.T. Walsh.

A new shop has opened in the quiet Maine town of Castle Rock, and its mysterious proprietor, Leland Gaunt, seems to stock exactly what each customer has always wanted. But Gaunt’s prices are steeper than they appear. Before long, he begins to set the whole town against itself - and only Sheriff Alan Pangborn suspects that the stranger’s wares are more dangerous than they look.

Sources indicate that the Needful Things release includes three discs: Disc One is the 4K UHD theatrical cut of the movie, Disc Two is the Blu-ray theatrical cut, and Disc Three is also a Blu-ray, but the 191-minute television cut includes interviews with film historian Dr. Simon Brown and screenwriter W.D. Ritcher.

Needful Things, released in 1994, starred Ed Harris, Max Von Sydow, Bonnie Bedelia, Amanda Plummer, Ray McKinnon, and J.T. Walsh. The movie adaptation was directed by Fraser Clarke Heston and achieved a 35% rating from critics on Rotten Tomatoes, with a 44% rating from audiences. Reviews from critics stated, "There's just not enough menace in this movie adaptation of the Stephen King novel," and "A devilishly fun and mysterious mix of the supernatural and personal stories of drama, crime, and all sorts of small town issues.".

King's novel was a commercial bestseller and sold over 1.5 million copies. Unfortunately, the adaptation only grossed around $15 million and failed to become a huge success.

Sources indicate that the digital release comes just days after “You Can See Everything” surpassed $100 million at the global box office, a staggering return for a nearly three-hour documentary that reportedly cost $7 million to produce. The film has grossed $93.5 million domestically, placing it among the highest-grossing documentaries in North American box office history.

According to recent reports, the documentary picks up 34 days before Holmes, the disgraced founder of blood-testing startup Theranos, is set to report to federal prison following her conviction for defrauding investors. What begins as an intimate portrait of the former Silicon Valley darling evolves into a three-year filmmaking experiment that interrogates the relationship between documentary subjects and their filmmakers, and the slippery distinctions between performance and reality.

In his review of the film, Variety’s Owen Gleiberman described it as “a shameless, fascinating, reality-TV-eats-its-tale look at entitled duplicity” and “everything a great movie should be: an experience unlike one you’ve had before, one that lures and enthralls you, and that leaves you feeling different when it’s over.”.

He also predicts that it could triumph at the Oscars because “anyone who votes for it won’t just be voting for best picture — they’ll be voting for the future.”.

Most password managers solve the same problem the same way: encrypt locally, then ship a synced copy to their cloud.

That model works. It’s also a huge blast radius. One vendor outage, one account takeover, one compliance surprise—and every device that trusts that sync plane is in the story.

Industry observers note that I wanted a different default: your vault lives on your devices. There is no Zafonyx Local account and no vendor cloud vault. Sync is optional, and when you use it, it stays on your local network—encrypted end to end—not a hosted vault backend you have to trust forever. That’s Zafonyx Local (formerly Password Safe Vault): a Flutter password manager with AES-256-GCM for the whole vault database, hardware-backed unlock keys where the OS supports them, built-in TOTP, platform AutoFill (or Windows auto-type), and desktop Quick Search for people who live in a keyboard. Security — what protects the vault.

Everyday use — unlock, generate, search, 2FA, fill.

Free plan (clear caps) and one-time unlock.

Multi-device, without “upload your vault to us”.

iOS, iPadOS, macOS, Android, Windows, and visionOS — 12 languages. Linux is coming soon.

Zafonyx Local is built with Flutter / Dart. Encryption uses AES-GCM on the vault database. Unlock keys lean on OS / hardware secure storage. Local sync pairs with post-quantum ML-KEM-768 and a one-time PIN so the session is negotiated on your network, not through a vendor sync API.

In a recent development, I’m not claiming “unbreakable,” and Zafonyx Local has no published third-party security audit yet. Encryption and decryption stay on your devices. The claim is a clear threat model: minimize who can ever see ciphertext of your vault, and keep sync under your physical / network control when you need more than one device.

It has been revealed that that’s fine. Different tools, different trust models.

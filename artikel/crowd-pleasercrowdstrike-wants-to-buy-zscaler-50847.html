Two cybersecurity giants could be joining forces. But would a deal make CrowdStrike a better investment?

The cybersecurity industry may be on the verge of a blockbuster deal. (Related: Industry News: The Reformer - Pushing the limits of lan...)

According to reports, CrowdStrike (CRWD -5.76%) is in talks to acquire Zscaler (ZS -5.29%), potentially bringing together two of the industry's most innovative companies in a transaction that could reshape the competitive landscape. (Related: Industry News: My Journey to a serverless transformers ...)

Investors reacted promptly sending Zscaler shares soaring roughly 17% in after-hours trading, while CrowdStrike shares fell about 8%.

For long-term investors, the question is what this combination could mean five or ten years from now.

Industry observers note that crowdStrike built its reputation protecting endpoints; the laptops, servers, and devices businesses rely on every day. Its Falcon platform uses artificial intelligence to identify and stop threats.

Zscaler approaches cybersecurity from another angle, pioneering zero-trust security that helps businesses safely connect employees to applications and data.

Put them together, and you potentially have something special: a comprehensive security platform protecting devices, consumers, networks, and cloud applications.

This is an incredibly powerful combo because cybersecurity customers increasingly want fewer vendors, simpler solutions, and better protection. And this combo creates an enormous opportunity.

First, cross-selling. CrowdStrike could introduce Zscaler's capabilities to its existing customers, while Zscaler could do the same with Falcon.

Second, competitive advantage. A combined company could become a more formidable competitor to Palo Alto Networks (PANW -5.09%) , strengthening its position in an increasingly consolidated industry.

Third, artificial intelligence. As businesses deploy more AI-powered applications and autonomous agents, protecting their digital infrastructure becomes increasingly complicated and increasingly essential.

It has been revealed that great acquisitions aren't automatically great investments. Zscaler carried a market capitalization of roughly $32 billion before the reports surfaced. That's a substantial price tag, and integrating two large technology platforms isn't easy.

CrowdStrike would also need to demonstrate that the benefits justify the acquisition premium, financing costs, and potential shareholder dilution.

In a recent development, cybersecurity isn't a passing trend. It's becoming essential infrastructure for the digital economy. A CrowdStrike-Zscaler combination could create a powerhouse positioned to benefit from that transformation for years. But remember: A great business isn't always a great stock at any price.

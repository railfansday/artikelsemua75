Sources indicate that zin Boats made a splash with fast, pricey electric boats. But most of its business is now coming from what’s under the floor.

The Seattle startup has launched Zin Power, a latest division that will supply its electric propulsion systems to other boat builders and to industries that rely on diesel engines, such as construction and mining.

The company also raised $5 million to scale up production and expanded its headquarters in the Interbay neighborhood to 11,000 square feet. (Related: Introducing Optimum: The Optimization Toolkit for Trans...)

According to recent reports, about 90% of the firm’s revenue comes from selling motors and batteries to customers in the marine and drone industries, founder and CEO Piotr Zin told GeekWire. A typical setup includes two motors and about 500 kilowatt-hours of battery storage. (Related: How we sped up transformer inference 100x for 🤗 API cus...)

“We noticed a steady demand for pleasure craft year over year, but at the same time a massive increase in propulsion sales,” Zin mentioned.

Customers include StanCraft Wooden Boats, which teamed with Zin on the 340 Hammerhead E, an all-electric wooden runabout. StanCraft delivered the first one to a customer this summer.

GeekWire first checked out the manufacturer in early 2024, when it was relaunching after a pandemic shutdown with its Z2R, a 20-foot carbon fiber runabout. Later that year Zin showed off the Z11, a 37-foot electric tender designed to shuttle passengers to and from megayachts at more than 60 mph. The company said boats on the platform would cost $1.75 million to $3.75 million.

Recent findings suggest that the company also offers the Z7 Convertible, a 22-foot runabout with a removable roof that it pitches for fishing, surfing and cruising. The boat has a top speed of 40 mph and a range of more than 80 miles.

According to recent reports, zin Power grew out of the drivetrains engineered for those boats. Zin sees the marine track record as a selling point for other industries that need high torque, including construction, mining and remotely operated vehicles (ROVs).

“The ocean is one of the harshest environments there is, so the technology has already proven itself under about the toughest conditions it could face,” Zin said.

Many of those machines still run on diesel engines, a technology more than a century old. Zin noted operators are eager to switch to electric.

“What’s interesting is that economics is driving the shift, not regulation or image,” he said.

In a recent development, electric systems cost up to 70% less to operate, according to Zin. He estimated that replacing a single 250-horsepower industrial diesel engine that runs eight hours a day could save as much as $1.5 million over 10 years.

It has been revealed that the newly introduced funding will pay for more manufacturing capacity, a dedicated sales and engineering team to work with boat builders, and support for customers converting from diesel. Zin calls it a seed round, with a larger raise planned down the road. The organization has raised $6.5 million to date, including a $1.275 million seed round in early 2024.

Zin Boats has nine full-time employees and brings on about a dozen contractors as needed.

Recent findings suggest that for now, each Zin Power system is built to order. The tech giant’s motors range from 100 to 600 kilowatts and deliver up to 2,700 pound-feet of torque, and its systems can run fully electric or as hybrids. A proprietary control unit that Zin Power calls the “Magic Box” manages everything from charging and cooling to software and safety.

Sources indicate that “Our goal is to keep delivering what we’ve built while developing simpler, more advanced plug-and-play systems,” Zin noted. “Eventually, we want a customer to be able to pull out a diesel engine and drop in a Zin Power system as a direct one-for-one replacement.”.

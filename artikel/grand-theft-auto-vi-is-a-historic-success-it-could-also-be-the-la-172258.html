Recent findings suggest that last year’s top-selling video game, Battlefield 6, sold 7 million copies in its first three days, and went on to sell an estimated 20 million copies. Grand Theft Auto VI blew both of those numbers out of the water in a single day. Day-one sales of the highly anticipated and glowingly reviewed title hit $3.79 billion, Take-Two Interactive Software announced on November 20, a number that’s larger than the gross domestic product of Greenland or Curaçao, and triple the opening weekend box office take of Avengers: Endgame. The tech giant sold 32.5 million copies before release, it added. Even for a title with lofty expectations, the numbers are shockingly high. Those numbers, though, might just be the beginning of a long, steep surge for the game—and the video game industry. That’s because as major a game as GTA VI is (with 70-plus hours needed to complete the main story and another 90 to 120 hours to finish all the side quests and in-game exploration, it’s certainly big), there are plenty of milestones still to come. The first of those could be the release of a PC version of GTA VI. While the console versions are selling briskly, the PC has become an increasingly important part of the gaming world. Last year, sales of PC games jumped 12% to $43.6 billion, landing just shy of the console industry’s 2025 sales totals, according to Michiel Buijsman of Newzoo Research. That means there’s a huge untapped audience for GTA VI—and with console prices showing no signs of falling in the near term, it’s an important potential source of additional sales. Sony, to date, has shipped more than 100 million PlayStation 5 units worldwide. Xbox has sold an estimated 38 million units of its most recent model. That’s a big built-in base, but it’s not expanding much these days. And with no big discounts this holiday season, sales aren’t likely to jump as they usually do at this point in the console life cycle. By 2028, however, the number of PC gamers is expected to top 1 billion. And while not all of those systems will be able to handle GTA VI’s advanced graphics and system demands, a PC release would still open up a broad upcoming audience. Wedbush Securities analyst Alicia Reese raised the firm’s estimate for PC sales in the first fiscal quarter of 2028 (which runs April 1, 2027, through June 30, 2027) from 12 million to 15 million units. A PC release next year would be somewhat surprising, though. The PC version of GTA V arrived 19 months after the console version, while the PC version of Red Dead Redemption 2 came out 12 months after its console release. GTA VI Online The financial spike from a PC release, however, is likely to pale in comparison to the eventual launch of GTA VI’s online component, analysts say. “While the timing of GTA VI online remains a mystery, it is eventually coming, and should serve as the key driver of higher sustainable earnings power for the company,” TD Cowen analyst Doug Creutz stated in a note to investors on November 23. “Reviewers have remarked over and over again that the level of detail and realism within GTA VI is astonishing. We think this bodes well for the eventual online expansion to be able to keep players engaged for long periods of time.” GTA Online has been a consistent moneymaker for GTA publisher Take-Two Interactive Software, with lifetime revenue topping $5 billion. While the current version continues to operate and generate impressive revenue, those numbers could eventually begin to shrink. An online version of GTA VI would give them a substantial boost. Wedbush expects the number of monthly people to reach 28 million in fiscal 2028. While all of that news is very, very good for Take-Two and Rockstar, the question overshadowing the industry right now is whether Grand Theft Auto VI will be the gaming industry’s last true blockbuster for quite some time. No title is likely to come close to its sales numbers, and nothing announced for 2027 has generated anything resembling its mainstream buzz. Plus, publishers have, by and large, become warier of committing hundreds of millions of dollars to big-budget games. Layoffs, meanwhile, are an increasingly common story among game studios, while mobile game revenues are growing by $1 billion or more each year, capturing more and more of players’ time. That’s going to make it difficult for any development team to create a title that impacts the pop culture landscape on a level anywhere close to what Rockstar Games has achieved.

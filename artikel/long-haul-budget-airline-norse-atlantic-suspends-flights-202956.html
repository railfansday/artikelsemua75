📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Norse Atlantic Airways may be no more.

The Norway-based long-haul budget airline remarked in a statement early Thursday that all flights were suspended "pending clarification as to whether a viable financing solution.".

"There can be no assurance that a viable solution will be found," Norse added.

The confirmation followed the cancellation of all Norse flights late on Wednesday, the flight tracking website FlightRadar24 showed.

Pakistan International Airlines, which contracts Norse to operate select flights, said Wednesday evening on X that Norse had suspended operations "without prior notice.".

Norse flew just one U.S. route at the time of its shut down: Orlando International Airport (MCO) to London's Gatwick Airport (LGW), schedules from aviation analytics firm Cirium show.

Sources indicate that during the prime summer months, Norse also seasonally flew from Upcoming York's John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK) to Athens International Airport (ATH) in Greece, LGW and Rome's Fiumicino Leonardo da Vinci International Airport (FCO).

At its peak in 2024, Norse served five U.S. destinations, including Las Vegas's Harry Reid International Airport (LAS), Los Angeles International Airport (LAX), Miami International Airport (MIA), from gateways in Europe.

Sources indicate that the collapse of Norse comes at a challenging time for airlines. Jet fuel is at historic highs, spiking after Iran war and other conflicts. Geopolitical tensions have closed significant swaths of airspace, including Russia and significant portions of the Middle East. And, high interest rates have pushed the cost of capital for any business to the highest levels in years. (Related: Industry News: huggingface_hub v1.0: Five Years of Buil...)

Norse warned on Wednesday that its financial situation was "critical" and said its continued operation was "dependent on securing financing to meet its short-term liquidity needs.".

Luis Gallego, the CEO of International Airlines Group that owns British Airways and Iberia, told the Financial Times in October that smaller airlines would struggle this winter due to the those financial headwinds.

According to recent reports, norse was one of the last airlines trying to make long-haul, low-cost work between the U.S. and Europe. Play Airlines, which attempted the same strategy via a connecting hub at Iceland's Keflavik International Airport (KEF), shut down in September 2025. And both Norse and Play were formed following the retrenchment and collapse of previous attempts; Norse from Norwegian Air's withdrawal from the market in 2021 and Play from the ashes of WOW Air that shut down in 2019.

Only independent French Bee and the budget arms of the larger European airline groups, including Discover, Level and Edelweiss Air, continue to offer lower-cost transatlantic flights.

According to recent reports, warren Buffett, perhaps the greatest investor of all time, once said: "It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair company at a wonderful price." However, even a wonderful company isn't worth buying at just any price. Earning outstanding long-term returns requires investors to buy stocks at the right price. On that note, let's consider one popular company on the market right now: Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +1.30%). The company arguably boasts a very strong business, but is its current stock price fair?

SpaceX is a leader in rocket technology. It helped pioneer reusable rockets and became the leader in space launch, contracting a significant amount of business from various U.S. government agencies. The tech giant has also become a leader in satellite-based connectivity. Its constellation of Low Earth Orbit (LEO) satellites is far larger than that of any competitor. It has been revealed that in addition, SpaceX offers various artificial intelligence (AI) services, including access to its Grok large language model and compute capacity through a network of data centers. It isn't just the breadth of SpaceX's business that makes it a great company. It has arguably built a robust competitive advantage. Consider the company's space launch business.

In a recent development, building rockets is capital-intensive, but that's just step one. They then have to go through multiple stages of testing before they can fly regularly. That's not to mention the expensive and time-consuming process of obtaining the necessary regulatory approvals that allow companies to legally fly rockets. SpaceX has already gone through all that with its Falcon 9, a partially reusable vehicle that has helped significantly slash space launch costs.

It has also made tremendous progress with its next-gen rocket, Starship, which has now undergone more than a dozen flight tests. SpaceX's rocket technology is superior to that of its competitors. That's why it is the market leader by far. Given the financial and regulatory barriers to entry, not to mention the time it takes to develop and build rockets, the company has a strong competitive edge. There is something similar going on with SpaceX's connectivity business.

Launching satellites into orbit takes time, a lot of money, and the appropriate authorizations. Also, service quality generally improves as the number of satellites increases. That's why it won't be easy for competitors to catch up to SpaceX here. Further, the company's financial results have been improving. In the third quarter, SpaceX recorded $14.6 billion in revenue, up 161% year over year.

In a recent development, the firm's Super Intelligence segment -- through which it offers various AI services -- posted $7.6 billion in revenue, up 830% year over year. Connectivity revenue was $5.2 billion, 69.5% higher than the year-ago period.

It has been revealed that and space revenue was $1.8 billion, up 6% year over year. On the bottom line, SpaceX recorded a net income of $4.4 billion, versus a net loss of $1.1 billion in the prior-year quarter. SpaceX's improving financials, strong lead and competitive moat across two of its business segments, and fast-growing AI business make it a robust company.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/25/26.

In a recent development, now for the second question: Is SpaceX attractive at its current price? Shares are trading at about $126 as of writing, and its market cap is $1.7 trillion. Based on its trailing 12-month revenue of $32.04 billion, the stock is trading at about 53x sales. That's very high, but the company deserves a premium, given the strength of the business and how fast it is growing.

Also, it's perhaps more telling to look at the forward-looking earnings multiple. SpaceX's current forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio, based on expected earnings over the next 12 months, is 44.2 -- that, again, looks high, and we have to factor in some uncertainty with the tech giant's prospects. SpaceX is significantly exposed to regulatory risks across all three of its businesses.

Also, its fastest-growing segment, SI, which now accounts for more than half of its top line, is the unit where its moat is arguably less established. The SI business also risks a significant slowdown, which would reduce year-over-year revenue growth and margins, given how much SpaceX is spending on its AI build-out.

With all that stated, I'd consider buying SpaceX at roughly 30x forward earnings -- that may undermine its prospects somewhat, but it would also give me a relatively healthy margin of error. But that means that at its current 44.2x forward earnings, the stock is too expensive, at least for me. I'd wait for a steeper decline to initiate a position.

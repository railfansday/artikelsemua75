One by one, the Faithful fell—and a upcoming Traitors winner was crowned.

Ironworker Shane Beatty hammered out his spot in TV history when he became the sole winner of The Traitors: New Blood, the fifth season of the U.S. series, during the emotional two-part finale on Nov. 19.

In a recent development, beatty, 32, made history as the first recruited Traitor to win the U.S. series. He joined Katie Fites in the turret after she recruited him in the Nov. 12 episode, following two consecutive Traitor banishments.

Thursday night’s two-part finale began with the banishment of Jay Vinnedge and the murder of Clyde Moser—two Faithfuls who had begun voicing suspicions about Beatty and Fites.

After the remaining contestants completed their final mission, the two Traitors survived the castle’s final Round Table, which ended with Kriste Lewis’ banishment.

According to recent reports, five contestants remained for the final banishment ceremony, but they all chose to continue banishing. Fites was sent out of the game, visibly shocked when Beatty turned against her. In the final vote, Victor Vollbrechthausen was banished, despite Beatty’s attempt to keep him in the game until the end.

With only three contestants left, Beatty revealed to Faithfuls Wyatt Gillespie and Abbey Benjamin that he was a Traitor before breaking down in tears.

“I’m sorry,” Beatty noted, his face in his hands. “I’m so sorry, guys.”.

In a recent development, beatty explained that he had started the game as a Faithful but was given an ultimatum to either become a Traitor or be murdered. Despite their visible devastation, Gillespie and Benjamin embraced Beatty.

“You don’t got to explain it, bro,” Gillespie noted. “I would’ve done the same.”.

With the $246,300 prize pot before him, Beatty told host Alan Cumming that the winnings were life-changing.

7. Contestants weren’t able to wade into every corner of the castle. "We use a section of it," Cotton explained to Variety. "We’ve taken a wing of the castle, and that’s their area that they can play the game within. They’ve got all the outside as well. They’re free to roam around.".

9. Game planning at the hotel, however, was strictly verboten. "You’re not supposed to talk about the game when the mics aren’t ready or the cameras aren’t on," season two player Dan Gheesling told Business Insider, "because if we say something that’s key and it’s not on camera, they can’t use it on the show.".

Even season two then-couple Larsa Pippen and Marcus Jordan weren’t able to share a room. "I think they would have wanted to," acknowledged executive producer Sam Rees-Jones, "but they weren’t allowed to because it would have given them a foot up in the game.".

"But the reason we like to do that is because it keeps an immersive bubble, keeps their heads in the game.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Keep health insurance even if you’re young and healthy. One emergency, like appendicitis, can bring a hospital bill bigger than your savings, while a plan caps what you pay for covered care each year. If the premium is the problem, check your price after subsidies before you go without.

Sources indicate that the premium is the cost you see each month. The bigger risk is a hospital’s list price, which is what patients without insurance are often billed.

Sources indicate that in 2024, hospitals billed Medicare an average of about $63,550 for the least complicated appendix surgery stays and were paid about $10,800 in all, according to Medicare data. The typical family had $8,200 in checking, savings, and similar accounts in 2025, the Federal Reserve found.

A health plan lowers that bill in two ways. Say a 27-year-old on a bronze plan, the type that usually has the lowest premiums, needs her appendix out. An in-network hospital is paid the rate her insurer negotiated, not its list price, and her share of covered in-network costs stops at the plan’s out-of-pocket maximum. Federal rules cap that at $10,600 for one person this year, not counting premiums. Without a plan, she gets neither.

Recent findings suggest that going without can also cost you at tax time. California, Massachusetts, New Jersey, Rhode Island, and the District of Columbia can add a penalty to residents’ income tax returns for months without coverage.

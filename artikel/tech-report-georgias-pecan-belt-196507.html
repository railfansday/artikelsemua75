According to recent reports, since an early-1900s planting boom, orchards along the Flint River in southwest Georgia have become a center of U.S. pecan production.

From the star of its namesake pie to a supporting player in countless side dishes, the pecan has earned a place on Thanksgiving tables across the United States. But before it becomes candied, chopped, or baked, it hangs from the branches of Carya illinoinensis—a species of hickory in the walnut family native to North America. Many U.S.-grown pecans come from orchards in Georgia, the state that has historically led the nation in production.

It has been revealed that one hotspot of pecan cultivation is in the southwest part of the state, around Albany, in Dougherty County. The OLI (Operational Land Imager) on Landsat 8 captured this image of the area and neighboring counties on October 23, 2026. That date falls within the pecan harvest season, which typically runs from October into December. In addition to pecans, other crops such as peanuts, cotton, and corn are also grown in the area.

Landsat observations like these contribute to the Cropland Data Layer, an annual map of crop types across the contiguous United States produced by the U.S. Department of Agriculture’s National Agricultural Statistics Service. The 2025 map demonstrates pecan groves clustered along the Flint River within the Coastal Plain. Pecan trees need abundant water, and they generally do well in the area’s well-drained sandy soils that sit above the Floridan aquifer system that growers can tap for irrigation.

In the late 1800s, Georgia had less than 100 acres (40 hectares) of pecan orchards. Then, in the early 1900s, southwest Georgia’s Dougherty and Mitchell counties underwent a pecan boom when thousands of acres were planted and sold to investors. By the end of the 1950s, Georgia led the nation in pecan production, producing over 17 million pounds, according to 1959 agricultural census numbers.

Industry observers note that in recent decades, pecan production has grown in other states, such as New Mexico and Texas. Georgia faced challenges after Hurricane Michael damaged orchards in October 2018, and Hurricane Helene dealt another blow when it swept inland in September 2024. Even so, USDA numbers show that the state’s production has topped 100 million pounds in each of the past three years (2023-2025).

Kirby’s request for private-sector involvement is bold, but far too generalized to give us insight into the seriousness of that wish. Airlines have a direct interest in a functioning air traffic control system. Disruptions strand their aircraft and crews, cost them money, and leave their employees dealing with understandably frustrated passengers. Their operational experience should inform the modernization effort.

But Kirby did not describe a specific governance proposal in this message. Asking to be involved does not, by itself, amount to a detailed initiative for privatization or airline control of air traffic services. I would want to see what he proposes before endorsing or rejecting it…and wonder what it is that United or other carriers can bring to the table that would actually help the FAA.

Sources indicate that there is also an obvious complication: a private contractor was already involved in the work at the center of this disruption. Private-sector participation alone does not guarantee competent execution any more than government involvement guarantees failure.

Kirby’s observation that this is not just about money is very true. New equipment requires funding. So do the people needed to install, maintain, and supervise it. But an appropriation cannot substitute for a sound implementation and oversight process.

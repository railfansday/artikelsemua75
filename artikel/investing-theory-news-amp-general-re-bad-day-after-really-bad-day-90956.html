X years of expenses in FI (TIPs, CDs, MMF) and pretty much everything else in equities.What do you use, or proposal to use, for that X ?I use 10 years. Currently still have a little more than that. I do not think I will let it get less than 10.Usually, 10 years is long enough for the market to go from one peak to another. I think, and hope. I will start getting nervous if the market drops for 5 years, and is showing no signs of going back up.Still living and learning. So curious about your thoughts.What I currently use, and what I plan to use, aren't (yet) the same thing. I concur with RocinanteRider and CloseEnough and you that 10 years seems a reasonable setup for FI to ride out a protracted market downturn -- I'm looking to get to that point but am not yet there.Was 100% equities for a long time, and nudged it down to 90/10 about 5-6 years ago and it drifted upward to 93/7 from market growth. It was only this past summer that I shifted 401K funds from equities to total bond funds in a quantity sufficient to equal 5 years' of anticipated retirement expenses. That felt good. Definitely liked knowing that I could avoid drawing down equities for a full five years if necessary. However, I don't think that's quite sufficient -- there are a number of reasons for this, but the duration of the lost decade (dotcom bust + GFC) as well as the 1968-1982 years gives me pause.To be straight, I don't really have this all figured out just yet. I'm leery about the idea of "not knowing what I don't know", as well as the fact that having been sanguine about the market plunges during dotcom bust, GFC, Covid, Tariffs, etc. does not necessarily translate to remaining sanguine once the Megacorp W-2 paychecks stop rolling in. Can't know what that's truly like until I retire. The main problem right now is that I'd like to get a TIPS ladder going but have no place to put it -- and so the plan is to put one together after retirement when the 401K is rolled over into an IRA and I can build a ladder out in that. Until then, stuck with "total bond funds" in the Megacorp 401k.So yeah, having spent (a LOT of) time reading BH forum threads and planning/preparing, 5X doesn't seem quite enough -- I think 10X makes a lot of sense, even if it's an arbitrary number. I tentatively plan to put about 50-60% of that into a TIPS ladder running to age 70 (when SS starts), where each rung covers nondiscretionary expenses, and the remaining 40-50% in I-Bonds, CDs, MMF etc. to cover the discretionary purchases.If I did that, that would result in an AA of roughly 65/35, comprised of 10X LMP (liability matching portfolio) and then an RP (risk portfolio) containing 'everything else'. The satisficer in me feels this is probably a pretty reasonable approach.-rwStatistics: Posted by rakish_weasel — Sat Nov 21, 2026 10:32 pm.

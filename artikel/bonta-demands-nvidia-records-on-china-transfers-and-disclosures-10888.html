California Attorney General Rob Bonta served NVIDIA with an investigative subpoena on November 19, 2026, seeking information on whether advanced chips or confidential information reached China and whether the tech giant misled investors and the public about the risk. The state will use the responses to evaluate possible violations of California law.

In a recent development, the California Department of Justice is examining actions by both NVIDIA and former CEO Jensen Huang. Bonta’s announcement describes an evidence-gathering investigation, not a completed legal finding or a filed enforcement action.

Recent findings suggest that those questions create two distinct lines of inquiry. One concerns whether advanced chips or confidential information may have reached China. The other concerns whether NVIDIA accurately described that risk to investors and the public, bringing possible technology transfers and securities disclosures into the same state investigation.

It has been revealed that california’s securities antifraud statute makes it unlawful to offer, sell, buy, or offer to buy a security in the state through a written or oral communication containing an untrue material fact. It also covers omissions of material facts needed to keep statements, in their circumstances, from being misleading. Bonta summarized the principle: “Companies cannot mislead their investors.”.

That licensed H200 program does not define the boundaries of California’s inquiry, which refers more broadly to advanced chips and confidential information. The filing nevertheless documents the controlled-sales framework and the practical limits of NVIDIA’s oversight after a sale.

According to recent reports, NVIDIA noted it has assisted and will continue assisting authorities regarding attempted diversion, but lacks physical control over its products after sale and must rely on customers’ and partners’ compliance programs. Reports of diverted controlled products, even unsubstantiated or untrue reports, and compliance failures by customers or partners could damage its business, relationships, and reputation.

The subpoena follows NVIDIA’s November 12, 2026 leadership announcement, in which Huang resigned as president, CEO, and director, effective immediately. Sources indicate that for Kress’s interim team, the subpoena connects possible transfers of chips and confidential information with the accuracy of NVIDIA’s public risk disclosures.

Rose was a 17-time Major League Baseball All-Star, but his legacy was tarnished by a lifetime ban.

US President Donald Trump has posthumously pardoned baseball legend Pete Rose.

"I have just given a Complete and Total Pardon to the Late, Great Pete Rose, a true Baseball SUPERSTAR & LEGEND," Trump wrote on Truth Social on Thursday.

Recent findings suggest that rose, who died in 2024 at the age of 83, pleaded guilty in 1990 to two counts of filing false tax returns. He was also given a lifetime ban from Major League Baseball (MLB) after an investigation revealed he bet on games.

The baseball star was posthumously reinstated to MLB, in which he played for 24 seasons, last year and made eligible for the Baseball Hall of Fame.

Trump also stated on Thursday that Rose's "record of 4,256 Hits in a Season will never be broken".

Recent findings suggest that in fact, Rose accumulated a record 4,256 hits - which MLB defines as a batter striking the baseball into fair territory and reaching base without doing so via an error or a fielder's choice - during his career, which is more than any other player. He played a total of 3,562 games.

It has been revealed that according to Trump, Rose "was treated terribly by Major League Baseball in not allowing him to go into the Baseball Hall of Fame. Politics, I guess".

The US Constitution gives a sitting president the broad power to grant a pardon, which represents legal forgiveness and ends any further punishment.

Industry observers note that because Rose's pardon was issued after his death, it is largely symbolic and does not change Rose's Hall of Fame status. The Department of Justice (DoJ) typically does not process posthumous pardon applications, though presidents have still issued them. It has been revealed that "Now he is dead, will never experience the thrill of being selected, even though he was a FAR BETTER PLAYER than most of those who made it, and can only be named posthumously. WHAT A SHAME," Trump added.

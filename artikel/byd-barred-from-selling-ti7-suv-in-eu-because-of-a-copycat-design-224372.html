Sources indicate that jaguar Land Rover (JLR) brought the case against BYD earlier this year after the Chinese automaker confirmed that it would launch the car in the United Kingdom in 2027. The preliminary ruling marks a significant setback in BYD’s recent explosive expansion into Europe.

It has been revealed that “In these summary proceedings, the preliminary relief judge rules that the Ti7 infringes the EU design of Jaguar Land Rover,” the court remarked in its November 20 ruling, AN reported. “The characteristic and distinctive elements of the design are reflected in the BYD Ti7.”. The Defender is JLR’s best-selling vehicle globally, with the automaker shipping more than 100,000 units last year. Both vehicles have boxy designs with flat rooflights, squared wheel arches, and, notably, body-colored panels in place of the rear side windows.

Sources indicate that unfortunately for JLR, the judge also ruled that the court does not have authority over BYD’s U.K. arm. To that end, JLR is reportedly planning to bring the same claim to the courts in England.

According to recent reports, according to AN, BYD “strongly disagrees” with the ruling and has prepares to appeal the result. “The BYD Ti7 is the result of BYD’s own independent design work and infringes none of JLR’s rights,” the firm remarked. “We will pursue every legal avenue to demonstrate that this decision is incorrect.”.

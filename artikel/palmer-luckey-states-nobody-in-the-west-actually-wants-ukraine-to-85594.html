Anduril founder Palmer Luckey speaks at Space Systems Command, Los Angeles Air Force Base, El Segundo, 6 August 2026.

It has been revealed that palmer Luckey says the US government will not let Ukraine use Starshield, because it fears Russia would destroy it. Starshield is the Pentagon’s military satellite network, and SpaceX helps run it. The Anduril founder made the case to Andrew Ross Sorkin at The Latest York Times’ DealBook Summit in New York on Wednesday.

“The US government isn’t even willing to stand up for Ukraine because they’re afraid of losing their constellation,” Luckey mentioned.

It has been revealed that he was defending Elon Musk, who spoke later that day and accused Zelenskyy of lying about Starshield speed limits. Luckey remarked he likes Musk personally, so people should take his view “with a pound of salt”. (Related: Rockstar accused of tracing furry artwork for GTA 6’s c...)

Recent findings suggest that his argument ran like this. Russia has hinted that it could destroy the civilian Starlink network if Ukraine keeps using it for attacks. The same satellites that cover Ukraine also orbit over the US. So Musk should not have to risk his whole network when Washington will not risk its own, Luckey remarked. (Related: Top Democrat asks Trump administration to stop using di...)

Recent findings suggest that “Nobody in the West actually wants Ukraine to win. They just want them to not lose for as long as possible, which is a bizarre and absurd warfighting position,” he stated.

Luckey mentioned he fully supports Ukraine, and that Anduril has worked there since the second day of the war. Who America sells weapons to is not his call, he added.

“I’m a dog on a chain and I need to be kept that way,” Luckey stated.

It has been revealed that luckey co-leads Project Meridian, the Pentagon’s 120-day study of future war, with Musk and Newt Gingrich. He said it looks at what war could look like in 2050, and that the MITRE Corporation runs it. The group met at Cheyenne Mountain the day before. It agreed to focus on ideas that have no champion in the Pentagon.

In a recent development, one example is weather control, he said, such as changing where rain falls at sea so forces get a clearer view of targets. He also took credit for pushing the Trump administration to rename the Defense Department the Department of War.

Sorkin read him a criticism from Wes Bryant, who until last year ran the Pentagon’s assessment of civilian harm. Bryant called Meridian a sign of oligarchy in government. Luckey said more than 20 people sit on it, including retired generals and admirals. Nobody attacked Eric Schmidt for joining the Defense Innovation Board under Joe Biden, he said, and the report will be public.

Luckey wants the US to resume nuclear testing, mainly to see what an electromagnetic pulse does to modern electronics. The models used today were built for transistors thousands of times larger than those in a phone, he mentioned. He is not confident his own systems would survive, even when built to the hardening rules.

“They were designed for the type of cell phone that Abraham Lincoln would have used,” he said.

He mentioned no AI model can be controlled, because every major model has been jailbroken within months. Data centres can still be shut off, he added. The AI in weapons is a different technology from chatbots, he argued, and more reliable than the older seekers it replaces.

“I am way more afraid of the civilian collateral side for dumb weapons than I am for smart weapons,” Luckey said.

Some companies have used chatbots for targeting and made mistakes, he mentioned, without naming them. China wants Jensen Huang out of Nvidia, he said, so he wants him back. Luckey still opposes selling chips to China.

Luckey also defended public executions. The state should rarely use the death penalty, he said. When it does, it should do so in public and without medical cover, so people can judge whether it got it right.

“Executions should be brutal. They should be violent,” he said.

He closed on his 2017 firing from Facebook, after he gave $9,000 to a pro-Trump group. He blames former Oculus colleagues, not Mark Zuckerberg, and now works with Meta on a combat headset for the US Army.

“It’s probably better for everybody that I got fired, but I have not let it go,” Luckey remarked.

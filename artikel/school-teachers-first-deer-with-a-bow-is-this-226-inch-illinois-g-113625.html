Industry observers note that southern Illinois school teacher Darin Hicks grew up in a hunting family, but he didn’t start hunting deer himself until just a few years ago. As his interest in deer hunting grew, he took special notice of any big bucks he spotted during his daily drives to and from school as a science teacher.

It has been revealed that “I started seeing a large buck, but never in the day [at first],” the 40-year-old Illinois native tells Outdoor Life. “We don’t use trail cameras. But I got plenty of [phone] photos the last couple years of a really good buck — but only a few photos during the day.”. (Related: Show, Don't Certify: How to Prepare for Your First Data...)

Hicks was mentoring his 9-year-old daughter, Josie, who wanted her first deer to be a buck.

Industry observers note that “I had her out in youth season, and she could have taken a lot of does but wanted a buck. One evening we saw a buck, but it was too dark [even though it was still legal light] and Josie didn’t want to shoot in that poor light.”. Hicks thinks the buck that he and his daughter spotted that evening during the youth season may have been the giant buck he arrowed weeks later.

It has been revealed that on Nov. 14 he hung a ladder stand near a wooded thicket full of rubs, scrapes, and plenty of trails. He set the stand for his dad, 77, to hunt during the upcoming Illinois gun opener on Nov. 20.

But that Saturday night Hicks’ wife, Katie, asked her husband if he wanted to bowhunt the stand the next day, before his father got in it the following week.

Hicks noted there wasn’t much time that next morning on Sunday before they left for church. Katie said he had a good two hours to hunt from dawn until leaving for church with his family.

According to recent reports, she had a good point, Hicks decided, and climbed into the stand the following morning of Nov. 15.

He sat for about an hour and spotted a doe with twin fawns at 150 yards. Suddenly the doe with young spooked and out stepped the giant buck Hicks had seen from afar for the last two years.

In a recent development, “I was shaking, found my grunt call and blew it once,” he says. “He turned and started walking toward me and my heart was pounding overtime.”.

The buck came steadily closer while Hicks readied for a shot — the first time he would ever draw his bow on a whitetail.

It has been revealed that “He walked toward me, stopped, and looked up at me at 15 yards, then he turned to head away,” Hicks says. “He started through some brush quartering away at 35 yards and I drew, released, and I heard a loud smack.”.

It has been revealed that the buck collapsed in his tracks. Hicks’ arrow had hit a bit high, but his 2-blade mechanical struck the spine and the buck didn’t get up.

Hicks managed to wrestle the nontypical out of the woods and, field-dressed, it weighed 187 pounds. His taxidermist aged the buck at 5 1/2 years, with a gross green score of 226 1/8 inches.

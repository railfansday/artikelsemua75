It has now been revealed that Newcastle United are set to host 2035 FIFA Women’s World Cup matches.

The United Kingdom was officially confirmed as the tournament’s host nation on Monday.

The Newcastle United Women’s team official social media declaring that the 2035 FIFA Women’s World Cup is: ‘Set to be the largest single-sport event ever staged in the UK, the bid features Newcastle among the host cities.’. (Related: Is Social Security Taxable? How to Calculate What You O...)

Newcastle United Women’s star Jordan Nobbs: “Having grown up in the North East, I know how much football means to people here and just how passionate the fans are about the women’s game. The FIFA Women’s World Cup coming to the UK in 2035 is another milestone for our sport, and bringing matches to this region would take the tournament to another level. We’ve seen what happens when the Lionesses play in front of crowds up here – the atmosphere is electric.”.

Jordan Nobbs adding: “Hosting these matches wouldn’t just be an incredible celebration of football; it would inspire the next generation of young girls across the North East by showing them that the biggest stage in the world is within their reach.”. BBC Sport state: ‘The English stadiums included in the 2035 bid are: Villa Park, The Amex, Ashton Gate, Elland Road, Hill Dickinson Stadium, Emirates Stadium, Selhurst Park, Tottenham Hotspur Stadium, Wembley, Etihad Stadium, St James’ Park, the City Ground, the Stadium of Light and Old Trafford.’.

They then add: ‘Cardiff City Stadium and Principality Stadium are the two Welsh venues, with Easter Road in Edinburgh and Hampden Park in Glasgow in Scotland. Northern Ireland missed out on co-hosting the men’s Euros in 2028 after the UK government mentioned in 2024 it would not provide funding to redevelop Casement Park in time – but a redeveloped Windsor Park is set to stage matches in 2035. A further three stadiums could be added – Birmingham’s new Powerhouse Stadium, Wrexham’s redeveloped Racecourse Ground, and a potential new Chelsea ground – which would take the tournament to 16 cities and 22 venues.’.

I don’t think any Newcastle United fans should be taking too much from this announcement with regards to whether or not a new stadium will be built, rather than redeveloping St James’ Park.

A new stadium could be built in time to host matches at the 2035 FIFA Women’s World Cup.

Whilst the club could also withdraw from hosting any games in these next few years and their fixtures given to one or more other stadiums.

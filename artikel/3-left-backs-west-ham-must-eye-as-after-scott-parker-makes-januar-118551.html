It has been revealed that west Ham must first navigate a busy December schedule before the January transfer window opens, but Scott Parker is already planning to strengthen his squad. The Hammers boss has identified two key positions he wants to address, with a upcoming striker and left-back both on his January wish list.

It has been revealed that the left-back role has been an issue for WHU this season after the departure of El Hadji Malick Diouf. Academy duo Ezra Mayers and Ollie Scarles have had opportunities in that role but have failed to nail down the spot.

It has been revealed that adama Boiro was brought in late in the summer window but he has managed just 13 minutes of league action since his arrival. There only other option in that role has been Morato but he is a natural centre-back. (Related: Industry News: Summer at Hugging Face...)

With that said, here are three left-backs that could solve Scott Parker’s issues at West Ham….

Harrison Burrows brings a different, more attacking dimension from left-back. At Sheffield United he has posted strong goal and assist numbers while operating as a left-back or wing-back, showing he can contribute in the final third as well as defend. (Related: How to generate text: using different decoding methods ...)

Industry observers note that that end product is useful for a West Ham side that needs more threat from deeper areas. Energetic in the press and capable of overlapping or underlapping, he adds balance to a back line that has often lacked a specialist left-sided option.

While he has no prior Parker connection, his Championship pedigree and creative output make him a sensible complementary target alongside more defensively oriented options. He would give Parker a player who can both protect and progress.

It has been revealed that lucas Pires stands out because of his direct experience under Parker. During Burnley’s 2024/25 Championship promotion campaign he was a regular starter at left-back, logging a high number of appearances in a side that prioritised defensive organisation and controlled possession.

Recent findings suggest that that familiarity with Parker’s demands is valuable for West Ham, who are still bedding in a new system. At Championship level he has shown reliability in one-v-one defending, solid positioning and the ability to recycle possession calmly.

Still only in his mid-twenties and currently at Burnley, he represents a realistic, modest-fee option rather than a high-risk gamble. His prior success in Parker’s set-up reduces the adaptation period and gives West Ham a proven, system-ready left-back who has already contributed to a promotion.

Industry observers note that harry Amass offers a high-upside profile that fits a promotion-chasing side. At 19 he has already accumulated meaningful Championship minutes on loan at Sheffield Wednesday and Norwich City, demonstrating he can handle the physical and technical demands of the division.

Athletic, left-footed and comfortable stepping into midfield, he provides both defensive cover and progressive carrying from the back. Limited first-team opportunities at Manchester United make a permanent or long-term move plausible, and he has been repeatedly linked with West Ham.

It has been revealed that for Parker, Amass represents a younger, more dynamic alternative who can grow into the role over multiple seasons while still delivering immediate Championship quality. His age, athletic traits and proven second-tier experience make him an attractive, future-proof target.

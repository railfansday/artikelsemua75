Industry observers note that arkansas Attorney General Tim Griffin has sued sweepstakes casino operator VGW and prediction market Kalshi on the same day, alleging illegal gambling under state law. The VGW complaint puts a dollar figure on the state's sweepstakes market and adds Arkansas to a growing list of states challenging the dual-currency model.

Arkansas has opened a two-front legal fight against online gambling it says is unlicensed. One lawsuit targets VGW, the tech giant behind Chumba Casino, LuckyLand and Global Poker. The other targets KalshiEX LLC and Kalshi Inc., the companies that own the Kalshi prediction market. (Related: Breaking: Illinois appeals Pacold injunction blocking i...)

Both cases allege illegal gambling and violations of the Arkansas Deceptive Trade Practices Act. Both also include an unjust enrichment claim. In each case the state seeks restitution, penalties, taxes and injunctive relief. (Related: PENN’s Interactive revenue rise rests on partner taxes ...)

The VGW suit attacks the Gold Coin/Sweeps Coin model that almost every US sweepstakes casino relies on. It names ten affiliated VGW companies. The complaint indicates VGW reported about $66 million in taxable Arkansas sales between July 2025 and June 2026 and paid about $6 million in sales and use tax over that period, Gaming.net reported.

Recent findings suggest that those figures show a company declaring sales to the state and paying tax on them. Yet VGW holds no Arkansas casino license, the state alleges. The complaint also accuses VGW of letting 18-year-olds play, even though Arkansas casinos require players to be 21.

The case lands less than two months after the New York settlement. New York Attorney General Letitia James revealed that deal on September 9, 2026. It covered Chumba Casino, Global Poker and Luckyland Slots. VGW’s coin-based games were already off the table for New Yorkers by then, after her office sent the manufacturer a cease-and-desist letter in June 2025.

Kentucky Attorney General Russell Coleman went to court a few months earlier, claiming VGW’s sweepstakes casinos break Kentucky gambling law. Florida has also moved against dual-currency sweepstakes since June 2026. Arkansas now joins Kentucky, Florida and Upcoming York on that list. For operators using the same coin structure, the pattern points to rising legal exposure state by state.

According to recent reports, the Kalshi complaint rests on a constitutional definition. The Arkansas Constitution’s definition of casino gaming expressly covers accepting wagers on sporting events. Kalshi has never held a casino-gaming license from the state commission, the state alleges. Nor has it held any other Arkansas authorization to take sports wagers.

The complaint expressly limits itself to state-law claims and relief under Arkansas law. That choice means the state is relying on its own consumer-protection statute rather than waiting on the CFTC. Money lost, betting that gets out of hand, and depression or other damage to mental health are dangers Kalshi understands its customers face, Griffin’s office said.

According to recent reports, filing both cases on the same day signals how Arkansas views the two products. The attorney general is treating sweepstakes casinos and sports event contracts as the same kind of unlicensed gambling. Other states may weigh whether to copy that approach.

It has been revealed that the VGW case was filed in White County Circuit Court. The Kalshi case was filed in Independence County Circuit Court.

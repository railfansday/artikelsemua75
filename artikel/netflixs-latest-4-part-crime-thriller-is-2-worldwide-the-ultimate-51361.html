According to recent reports, the Law of Beasts is based on real-life events and an elaborate moral dilemma with Poland's judicial system that permanently changed the nation's laws regarding incarcerated persons, especially violent criminals. The Beast Law was put into effect in January 2014, allowing courts to legally send convicts to psychiatric institutions after completing court-ordered federal prison sentences. The law was designed to prevent criminals still deemed dangerous to society from reintegration, which challenged Poland's established constitutional rights given to all citizens. The series is based on the impending release of a serial child murderer who killed four boys between the ages of 11 and 13. (Related: Porting fairseq wmt19 translation system to transformer...)

Sources indicate that the latest Netflix series is told between two overlapping timelines in 1988 and 2014, which mark the beginning and end of a child murderer, Mariusz Trynkiewicz's, 25-year prison sentence. Originally sentenced to the death penalty, Trynkiewicz received an amnesty in 1989 and received a reduced sentence, which allowed the possibility of his release. As that release date neared in 2013, there was a public outcry in Poland that called for further judicial action to be taken. This is how the Beast Law was conceived and implemented, forcing Trynkiewicz to be transferred to a psych hospital specifically for convicted felons. The law that still exists in Poland to this day.

In a recent development, the two protagonists of Netflix's upcoming The Law of Beasts series are Anna Bersz, played by Agnieszka Grochowska, and Agnieszka Grabska, played by Agnieszka Żulewska. Both characters are fictional and not based on real persons involved with the actual Trynkiewicz case, invented purely as narrative devices for the Netflix series. Anna is a police detective who led the investigation into Trynkiewicz in 1988 and became a government official by 2014, whose interest lies primarily in public safety. Agnieszka is a psychiatrist who is assigned to examine Trynkiewicz's mental state, siding with psychological analysis and reasoning, and is a believer in protecting individual rights.

Anna and Agnieszka are lifelong friends who come into great conflict over the release of Trynkiewicz, serving as two opposite ends of the moral and legal spectrum regarding the Beast Law. Anna thinks in black-and-white terms of good and evil, while Agnieszka understands psychological complexities that determine degrees of criminal responsibility. Even though they are fictional characters, they embody the central conflict within the real-life philosophical conundrum regarding Trynkiewicz.

It has been revealed that ultimately, with the Beast Law put into law, it's already clear whose perspective changed Poland's history. The real-life Trynkiewicz was sent to a psych hospital/federal prison hybrid before being sent back to prison in 2015 for possession of child pornography. He died in custody in 2025.

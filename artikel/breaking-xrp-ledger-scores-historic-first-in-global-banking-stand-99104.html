📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

XRP Ledger (XRPL) has marked a significant step in its journey to institutional adoption by becoming the first public permissionless layer-1 blockchain to see its principles of Architecture and Resiliency independently verified by the Global Layer One (GL1) Market Infrastructure Toolkit.

The foundation’s self-assessment was independently verified by the blockchain security firm CertiK on the responses and evidence submitted around the principles of Architecture and Resiliency.

The independent attestation report was released on Nov. 13, further strengthening the assertion of XRP Ledger's compliance with institutional financial infrastructure requirements.

This comes at a time when banks and asset managers are increasingly considering blockchain technology to enable tokenization of deposits, stablecoins, investment funds and other financial instruments.

The Global Layer One initiative is a collaborative effort between prominent financial institutions and policymakers to set standards for digital financial infrastructure.

Its main goal is to make cross-border transactions for tokenised assets seamless while ensuring that blockchain networks adhere to internationally expected standards for financial market infrastructure. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

The GL1 Market Infrastructure Toolkit assesses blockchain networks for adherence to 10 principles, including governance, legal foundation, settlement finality, architecture, operational resiliency, access, interoperability, and privacy. (Related: Introducing Optimum: The Optimization Toolkit for Trans...)

Interestingly, XRPLF self-assessed XRPL for all 10 principles.

CertiK independently verified it only for the principles of Architecture and Resiliency.

XRPLF also provided details on how requirements specific to private/permissioned networks are addressed by managing related risks for XRPL’s decentralized nature, open-source governance model, and protocol amendments.

XRP Ledger Foundation executive director Brett Mollin emphasized the need to give financial institutions tangible evidence instead of pitching ideas.

Mollin added that independent attestation from CertiK also gives financial institutions more confidence while vetting the network.

It does not mean that XRPL meets all the standards mentioned in the toolkit completely.

The XRP Ledger Foundation will continue to patch its assessment in line with the development of GL1 principles and XRPL protocol amendments that are activated.

Nonetheless, it's another step towards XRPL's bid to be a possible infrastructure for regulated financial institutions looking for blockchain-based settlement and tokenization.

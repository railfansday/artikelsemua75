According to recent reports, eli Lilly (LLY +2.94%) continues to crush it, thanks to the success of its existing portfolio of GLP-1 weight-loss drugs. But with its drug candidate Retatrutide, a "triple agonist" that mimics three key gut hormones, the pharmaceutical firm may have an opportunity to market it as a treatment for another common ailment: obstructive sleep apnea (OSA).

At least, that's an emerging takeaway, following the publication of recent phase 3 trial results in the Newly introduced England Journal of Medicine. OSA affects over 50 million Americans, with an estimated 80% unaware they have the disorder. Hence, there is tremendous financial opportunity for products targeting it.

In a recent development, two separate clinical trials, one for patients with type 2 diabetes and one for patients who do not have type 2 diabetes, demonstrated how weekly injections of Retatrutide significantly reduced their symptoms. Depending on the dosage, the treatment reduced OSA "episodes" by up to 34.3 per hour, from a baseline in the mid-50s per hour. (Related: Restaurant owners left in tears after discovering their...)

Patients receiving the drug also experienced moderately high weight loss, with trial participants losing as much as 22% of their body weight over the 80-week clinical trial.

Eli Lilly already markets its GLP-1 weight-loss drug, Zepbound, as a treatment for sleep apnea. Yet if Retatrutide is approved for sale and proves itself to be a stronger option among weight loss drugs, the same may potentially occur for this drug candidate. In turn, this could benefit Eli Lilly in the following ways.

Improved weight loss, sleep apnea, and diabetes treatments enable the firm to further deepen its competitive edge against rivals like Novo Nordisk and against drug companies just entering the GLP ring, such as Pfizer.

In turn, the company could continue to experience elevated levels of revenue and earnings growth. While an earnings miss, concerns about its nearly 30x forward multiple, or market changes could send Lilly shares down, a further advance of its key growth catalyst could help it stay one of the best-performing pharmaceutical stocks.

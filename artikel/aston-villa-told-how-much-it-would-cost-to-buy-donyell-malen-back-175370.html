Donyell Malen would cost £60million if Aston Villa wanted to sign him back from Roma in the coming transfer windows.

That is the opinion of former senior Villa scout, Bryan King, speaking exclusively to Football Insider.

The forward left the Villans in January, joining the Italian giants initially on loan before signing permanently in a deal worth a total fee of around £21.5m. (Related: What Is Considered Upper Middle Class? Income and Net W...)

Malen struggled for minutes at Villa, with Unai Emery seemingly unwilling to ever hand him a sustained role in the starting XI during his year at the club.

But in Italy, the Netherlands international has come alive, scoring a staggering 31 goals and providing six assists in just 35 games since arriving.

Malen has hit yet another level this season, scoring or assisting in his last eight games for Roma and netting four against Parma on Saturday.

Villa were held to a 0-0 draw at home to Sunderland on Saturday, marking a fifth consecutive game without victory for Emery's men. (Related: How to Budget for a Wedding Without Going Broke...)

They have failed to score in three of those games, and produced only one major chance against the Black Cats.

Goal-scoring has become quite an problem for Villa, and they may well only have themselves to blame.

"Isn't it funny. The boy they sold to Roma, he can't stop scoring goals," King told Football Insider. "He has scored 12 in the last 11 league games. He got four at the weekend!

According to recent reports, "They took the £2m loan in January, and they bought him in May. He's now a full Roma player. So, if Aston Villa wanted to buy him back, he'd cost £60m.

Recent findings suggest that "Seems like a funny bit of business to me. For a 27-year-old, okay, he might not have been hitting the buttons at Aston Villa, but if they'd have kept [Ollie] Watkins in him, I don't think they'd have goal-scoring problems.

According to recent reports, "They let Watkins go, and this is a 20-goal-a-season man. They brought in [Tammy] Abraham, who struggled. [Nicolas] Jackson's doing okay, but he needs support.".

While the decision to sell Malen has aged woefully, he likely never would have had the same opportunities if he had stayed at Villa.

Of his 46 starts, the 27-year-old started just 14 games and, for whatever reason, clearly was not a player Emery ever fully trusted.

"It was a good deal for us and for him, as he wanted to play as a striker, and we're getting along well with Watkins," the Spaniard said in April.

"I would have liked to keep him, but he wanted to play more and asked me several times. He got this opportunity, and I'm happy for him. He's an excellent player, and I think he can have a great career in Rome.".

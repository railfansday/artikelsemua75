The battle for the most prestigious title in chess begins later on Tuesday in Geneva, Switzerland.

India's 20-year-old defending classical chess (traditional, long format game) world champion Gukesh Dommaraju will try to claim the title for a second time, having won it from Ding Liren in Singapore two years ago.

His challenger is another up and coming talent from Asia, Uzbekistan's Javokhir Sindarov, also 20 years of age. The combined age of the two contestants is the youngest in the 140-year history of the title.

"I don't think I'm here to defend anything. I'm here to win it all over again. That's the mindset I'm in," Gukesh told reporters before the match.

He described his challenger Sindarov as "one of the very top players at the very top of his game, so for sure, it's going to be a real test.".

The first game begins on Tuesday afternoon in Geneva, a timing decision that raised questions in Asia, given that it means the match will take place late at night in the countries where the contestants hail from.

Sindarov will start with the white pieces in the first game, having picked a white chocolate knight in a blind draw to decide who would move first.

The players will play a total of 14 long games if required. In the event of a 7-7 tie at the end of that series, they would move into a series of short time control games to settle the match as tie-breakers. Another Supreme Court bench, this time led by Chief Justice of India Surya Kant, is set to hear the bail petitions filed by student activists Umar Khalid and Sharjeel Imam tomorrow, legal news website Bar and Bench reported.

A previous bench led by justice Aravind Kumar had denied them bail.

The duo have been detained for over five years without a full trial under anti-terrorism laws on allegations of orchestrating a conspiracy to trigger deadly riots in the Indian capital in 2020.

Khalid and Imam, both 38 years old, were detained after the riots in Delhi. Some 53 people — mostly Muslims but also several Hindus — were killed.

The case has often been highlighted by critics who say India's more than 200 million Muslims face discriminatory treatment under Prime Minister Narendra Modi and his Hindu nationalist party.

Ousted Bangladeshi Prime Minister Sheikh Hasina plans to return to her country after two years of exile in India, according to her spokesperson.

That's despite the Bangladeshi government saying she can only return from the neighboring nation through extradition.

Hasina's scheduled date of return is December 16 — the anniversary of the end of Bangladesh's 1971 liberation war with Pakistan, the spokesperson mentioned.

In June this year, Hasina vowed to return to Bangladesh while speaking at a correspondents' event in New Delhi via live audio feed.

In a recent development, "I know that they may put me in jail, or they may kill me, or anything may happen, but still I have to go back," she remarked at the time. "Fear cannot decide my duty to the people.".

This, however, is the first time she has named a date, setting the stage for potential legal and diplomatic tensions.

"Since Sheikh Hasina was flown to India on a Bangladesh aircraft on August 5th, 2024, she now calls upon the Government to arrange for ‌her air transport from Delhi back to Dhaka," the spokesperson, Syed Mozammel Ali, noted in a ‌statement.

"Having ​exiled her contrary to the Constitution under such circumstances, it is now the responsibility of the government to make possible her return to Bangladesh on Victory Day on December 16th.".

In a recent development, hasina, having been sentenced in absentia by a Bangladeshi court last year, faces the death penalty on returning home. She was run out by angry protesters in 2024 amid a deadly government crackdown on a student-led uprising. The UN noted close to 1,400 people were killed in the crackdown.

A man who was called into a police station for counselling over a family dispute allegedly pulled out a knife and went on a stabbing spree, Indian media reported Tuesday.

The 30-year-old reportedly walked into the station enraged that a complaint had been filed against him.

When a police officer attempted to pacify him, he allegedly took out a knife and stabbed the officer, Indian Express reported.

The man is then said to have gone on a stabbing spree inside the police station, injuring five officers before stealing a scooter to escape.

After a police chase, he allegedly stabbed four more personnel before authorities were able to restrain him.

According to recent reports, local media reported that he was brought under control only after several shots were fired at his legs. The man is currently receiving treatment at a hospital.

Saurav Das, one of the co-conveners of India's political pressure group Cockroach Janta Party (CJP), mentioned he was prevented from leaving New Delhi to deliver talks about the movement in the UK.

The 27-year-old has been one of the central leaders of several anti-establishment protests since the satirical party launched in May.

In October, he was detained for defying a security crackdown in New Delhi during protests against the controversial voter roll revisions. Das and other leaders, including India's political opposition, were calling for the resignation of India's election chief, amid other demands.

Das, who was scheduled to fly out early Tuesday, noted immigration officers told him that his passport had been flagged.

"This coward government did not allow me to board my flight to London where I was invited by prestigious universities like Oxford, Cambridge, LSE (London School of Economics), Edinburgh to deliver talks on the cockroach movement," Das remarked in a social media post.

"Indian students put their heart and soul to organize the events but this anti-intellectual govt was scared of me going to these prestigious institutions!" he wrote.

Das was due to speak at the Oxford Union society on December 1.

Recent findings suggest that the incident occurred hours before Human Rights Watch published a report critical of the Modi government. Its allegations included the weaponization of federal agencies against Modi's critics.

Earlier this month, India barred over 100 foreign speakers from attending a democracy summit organized by India's opposition. Those barred included a Norwegian journalist who had previously questioned the Indian prime minister about press freedom.

Prime Minister Modi's Hindu nationalist government has "systematically weakened" institutions meant to protect human rights and democratic freedoms, a scathing new report by Human Rights Watch concluded.

In a 95-page report titled 'So What's This Democracy We're Talking About?: Modi's Authoritarian Playbook in India,' the watchdog found that the right-wing Bharatiya Janata Party-led (BJP) government is undermining parliamentary procedures, suppressing opposition and weaponzing investigative agencies for political ends.

It also found that the government has weakened constitutional and statutory bodies meant to protect democratic freedoms and the rights of the people.

"Over the past decade, the Modi government has been hollowing out India’s independent institutions that are critical to checking executive power and protecting civil liberties," noted Elaine Pearson, Asia director at Human Rights Watch.

"The government has eroded the rule of law and promoted Hindu majoritarianism, rolling back human rights protections, undermining the rights of marginalized communities, and deepening India's authoritarian slide.".

The report further claimed the Election Commission of India and the Supreme Court — both independent bodies — failed to uphold fundamental freedoms, pointing to the elimination of millions of voter names from India's electoral rolls in a controversial exercise.

"The Modi administration's actions over the past decade are not isolated institutional failures but mutually reinforcing elements of an authoritarian playbook," Human Rights Watch noted.

"India's allies should press the Modi government to end its assault on independent institutions and restore checks on executive power," Pearson said.

The BJP or Modi's government is yet to comment on the report which was published Tuesday morning. However, both have dismissed similar criticisms in the past.

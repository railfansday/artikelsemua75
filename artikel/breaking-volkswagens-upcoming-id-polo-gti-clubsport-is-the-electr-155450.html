According to recent reports, volkswagen revealed the ID. Polo GTI Clubsport Concept, an electric pocket rocket with a massive rear wing and 362 horsepower.

We knew Volkswagen was cooking something “really spicy” up after teasing the latest GTI hot hatch earlier this week.

Industry observers note that volkswagen debuted the first-generation Golf GTI in 1976, which essentially defined the hot hatch. To celebrate the brand’s legendary performance badge, a signature of its sporty, practical hatchbacks, VW is entering a new era with its first fully electric GTI. The GTI has been “one of Volkswagen’s great success stories for 50 years,” Volkswagen’s technical development boss, Kai Grünitz, said. (Related: Breaking: AI for Game Development: Creating a Farming G...)

After opening pre-orders for the ID. Polo GTI last month, the brand’s first EV to wear the badge, Volkswagen introduced the Clubsport Concept, a souped-up two-door electric pocket rocket.

The fresh ID. Polo GTI Clubsport Concept indicates “how much more potential there is in the electric GTI world,” according to Grünitz.

In a recent development, it’s based on the production ID. Polo GTI uses the same MEB+ platform, but VW reengineered it with a larger electric motor to pump out more power.

Recent findings suggest that the Clubsport is powered by a 362 hp (270 kW) front-mounted electric motor (APP550) that’s typically used in the rear of VW’s larger EVs, like the ID. Tiguan and the ID.7. That’s up considerably from the 233 hp (166 kW) and 213 lb-ft of torque of the standard ID. Polo GTI offers.

Thomas Schmall, CEO of Volkswagen Group Components, remarked the APP550 motor is “one of the most efficient electric drives in its class.”.

Thanks to an electronically controlled front differential lock (VAQ), Volkswagen noted, the Clubsport version can sprint from 0 to 62 mph (0 to 100 km/h) in 4.7 seconds, beating the standard ID. Polo GTI by a full two seconds (6.8 seconds).

Volkswagen also fine-tuned the adaptive chassis control (DCC) to make it racetrack-ready and added new ceramic brakes to help slow it down.

According to recent reports, the Clubsport version uses the same 52 kW NMC battery as the ID. Polo GTI, which can provide up to 389 hp (290 kW), Volkswagen mentioned, enabling a top speed of 149 mph (240 km/h).

On the exterior, the Clubsport is a two-door model rather than a four-door. It’s 100 mm longer than the standard ID. Polo GTI, with each wing flared an additional 50 mm. It also gets a large motorsport splitter up front, a wider rear end, a fresh diffuser, and a massive carbon-fiber rear wing.

It has been revealed that inside, the cabin is tailored to the racetrack with a minimalist, stripped-down, two-seater layout. As such, there’s no infotainment system, just a center display to switch between drive modes.

The dash panel, side trims, and other interior elements feature dark ArtVelours microfibre with red topstitching, reinforcing the racetrack-ready interior.

Following the ID. Polo GTI, newly introduced ID.3 GTI, and Golf GTI Edition 50, the new ID. Polo Clubsport is the fourth model to debut this year under the performance badge.

Industry observers note that volkswagen opened pre-orders for the ID. Polo GTI last month, starting at 39,995 euros (about $45,000). In early 2027, Volkswagen will open orders for the ID.3 GTI, the second EV to wear the badge, and its first rear-wheel-drive (RWD) GTI.

And American compact car enthusiasts drool over more unobtanium.

It has been revealed that the company will showcase the ID. Polo GTI Clubsport concept car at the Essen Motor Show next week, starting on November 27.

Volkswagen didn’t confirm looks to launch a production version, but don’t expect it to reach the States.

Sources indicate that the standard VW ID. Polo and GTI are not sold in the US, so the souped-up Clubsport variant likely won’t be either. Hopefully, VW changes its mind and proves us wrong.

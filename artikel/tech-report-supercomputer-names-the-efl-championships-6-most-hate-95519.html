The 2026-27 Championship season sees a wide range of clubs taking part - from the fallen giants of the Premier League to the teams like Lincoln City, who are playing in the second tier for the first time since 1961.

And with that vast array of club sizes come different types of fanbases - some humble, some not so boisterous, and some that are very loud and confrontational. (Related: How to generate text: using different decoding methods ...)

Sources indicate that but out of the 24 Championship clubs in the 2026-27 season, who have the SIX most hated fanbases in the division, according to the Supercomputer? FLW takes a look...

Historically, Portsmouth have always been a club that naturally were in the top two divisions of English football, but a short decline in the 1970's, and a further one due to financial complications in the early 2010's have meant that the south coast outfit have had to really battle to become the club they are once more. (Related: Fine-Tune Wav2Vec2 for English ASR in Hugging Face with...)

Industry observers note that what has never left Fratton Park though is the feisty, loud fanbase that comes with them. Away teams simply do not like travelling to take on Portsmouth at their home ground as it can get quite intimidating at times, and that's proven by the very good home record in recent years under John Mousinho's management.

The Supercomputer has Pompey as the sixth most hated fanbase in the Championship though, mainly due to their apparent celebration of how good their own support is, with rival fans sometimes 'perceiving a superiority complex' when it comes to them as a whole.

If you've got a good fanbase, there's no reason why you shouldn't shout from the rooftops about it, but perhaps it can grate on others, if Portsmouth do indeed do that.

As far as the two 'Second City' clubs go, Birmingham City have certainly had a tougher time of things in recent years than bitter rivals Aston Villa, and the malaise under the previous ownership of the club saw attendances dwindle a bit during Championship stagnation.

The buyout of Blues by American hedge fund manager Tom Wagner though has seen the fanbase galvanised, even if they did have to drop into League One - that may have been the best for all though as it led to a 111-point title-winning season, and City are now 100 per cent on the right path again.

Sources indicate that but why are they the fifth most hated Championship fanbase? As per the Supercomputer, some of their fans have perhaps gained a sense of arrogance since the Knighthead takeover, with their wealth pretty much crushing most of the League One opposition when they were briefly down there, and they're one of the big financial hitters in the second tier too, with some lucrative sponsorship deals helping their revenues.

The Supercomputer also thinks that some Blues fans also think they're a Premier League-size club already and they should be at the level already - given how they've been doing in 2026-27, they may be there in less than a year's time.

For 12 years in a row, Wrexham had struggled to get out of the National League and back into the EFL - then came the Hollywood money of Ryan Reynolds and Rob Mac.

The significant acting duo decided to pump their money into the non-league North Wales outfit, with their takeover completed in February 2021, and by the end of the 2022-23 season - with a team much transformed due to the significant pulling power they now had - they were back in League Two after 15 years away.

Two more promotions have followed since then, record revenues have been delivered, and plenty of millions has been spent, with new billionaire investors on board as the Racecourse Ground undergoes somewhat of a transformation to allow the tons of new supporters from all over the globe to get to the matches.

Of course though, their rapid rise and huge media following has caused serious resentment from opposition fanbases, as the Supercomputer notes, and their online fanbase, particularly from the other side of the Atlantic Ocean, can be somewhat irritating if not understanding the traditions of English football.

Whilst they continue to spend money and gain attention, expect the Red Dragons to be maligned.

With three FA Cup wins in their history, West Ham are a sizeable club, there's no question about it, but for an outfit that have never won the top flight of English football, their can sometimes be seen as overly entitled and arrogant, according to the Supercomputer.

The Hammers were very much established in the top flight in recent years, and they even won the UEFA Europa Conference League in 2023, yet three years later, they dropped back into the Championship - armed with a 60,000-plus seater stadium, a far cry from the days of Upton Park.

What size of club West Ham actually are though can be deliberated, but there was much ridicule when mentions of Mauricio Pochettino, Antonio Conte and Julien Nagelsmann to replace Nuno Espirito Santo recently were aired by one fan - and that will have rubbed some up the wrong way.

Added to the fact they have a bitter rivalry with Millwall that can sometimes dominate the Championship landscape over other things, West Ham certainly aren't liked by many, as per the Supercomputer.

With a number of rivalries with other Championship clubs, Cardiff City are the second most hated fanbase in the division, as per the Supercomputer.

Their bitter rivalry with Swansea City has returned after one year away in League One, whilst Bristol City are a cross-Severn foe that there's no love lost for - add Wrexham into the mix too, and that's a number of clubs whose fans don't have much time for the Bluebirds.

The Supercomputer also notes that many Cardiff fans often claim superiority over the rest of the Welsh clubs, which will no doubt rub others up the wrong way, and given the events of the 21st century, that point could be hotly-debated with their biggest rivals in the form of Swansea, whose lengthy Premier League stint will make them lay a claim for that title.

Well, it wasn't going to be anyone else, was it?

For a number of years, Millwall have had one of the notorious fanbases in the country - both away from home and at The Den.

The Lions fans know they're not liked by many, and they sing 'No one likes us, we don't care' every match to make it very clear, and away supporters travelling to South Bermondsey don't exactly ever have a comfortable experience.

Jamie Vardy has revealed how a conversation with Nigel Pearson helped change the course of his iconic spell at Leicester City shortly after joining from Fleetwood Town in 2012.

Vardy's career story is one of the most talked about in English football, and it is one which very few other names in the game have been able to replicate since.

The Sheffield-born native enjoyed strong success in the latter stages of his career after biding his time in the non-league game with the likes of Stocksbridge Park Steels, FC Halifax Town and, of course, the Cod Army.

With Pearson beginning the first full season of his second spell at Leicester in 2012, the experienced manager broke the non-league transfer record for an outgoing player when Vardy signed for £1m after having netted 34 goals for the Lancashire side as they won the Conference title. What Vardy and the Foxes went on to achieve individually and collectively was unprecedented, but it wasn't always plain sailing for the striker in the East Midlands.

Vardy's first season at the King Power Stadium saw Leicester finish sixth, losing to Watford in one of the most iconic EFL games of all-time in the Championship play-off semi-finals. (Related: AI data center power demand will double by 2030 thanks ...)

However, the forward scored just four times in 26 regular-season appearances - 17 of which were starts due to competition with the likes of Chris Wood, David Nugent and Harry Kane, on loan from Tottenham Hotspur at the time.

Asked by fellow former striker, Emile Heskey, on how he sees his legacy in LE2, Vardy revealed his early struggles with the club and how Pearson blocked a potential exit.

Industry observers note that "It was so hard, I'll be honest," stated the 39-year-old. "I think, because I'd not done the academy route, it was working out exactly what I needed to do.

"What was good enough when I was at Stocksbridge and Halifax wasn't what I needed to be doing at Fleetwood, for instance, and then what I did there was totally different from what was to come.

Recent findings suggest that "I was just eating 'crap', which is what I did previously and it worked. But, you get found out and there's a lot you have to change," Vardy added.

Industry observers note that "That first season was a struggle, but when you start to realise what you need to do. The summer after the first season, I went on holiday, but my holiday's not just a holiday, I'm running in the streets, making sure I'm fit when I get back.

"That first season was a massive struggle, but when it clicked the second year, it was like 'I am meant to be here'," he continued.

"I fully doubted myself. I remember saying to Nigel Pearson and Craig Shakespeare, 'send me back to Fleetwood. I know I can score goals there.

"They stated 'it's not happening', we've bought you for a reason. Knuckle down. Keep going. It's a good thing they did say that.".

Ultimately, Pearson and Shakespeare made the right call, as after a 16-goal season during the Foxes' 2013/14 Championship-winning season, Vardy went on an unbelievable run of goalscoring exploits.

In November 2015, the forward broke the record for the most goals in consecutive Premier League games - 11 - which helped Pearson's successor, Claudio Ranieri, oversee City's 5000-1 top-flight triumph, with Vardy netting 24 times that campaign.

Five years later, he won the Premier League's Golden Boot with 23 goals, and as well as winning the 2021 FA Cup and Community Shield with the club, stuck around to help Leicester win the 2023/24 Championship title, before departing last summer after an overall record of 200 goals in 500 appearances in blue.

Vardy spent last season with Cremonese in Serie A, but after his summer departure came intense speculation over a return to Leicester or a move to boyhood club, Sheffield Wednesday, following David Storch's takeover.

Neither would materialise, but in September, the former England international agreed a contract with Burnley until the end of the season, although his current Championship spell isn't going well at all for the Clarets.

Vardy arrived just weeks before Nicky Hayen's dismissal in East Lancashire, which came after he netted his first goal in the 2-1 home loss to Wolves on October 16th, whilst Fabio Celestini's reign has got off to a winless start ahead of Sunday's grudge match against Blackburn Rovers.

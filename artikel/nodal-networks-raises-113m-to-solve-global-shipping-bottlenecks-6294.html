Nodal Networks Inc., a organization that builds artificial intelligence decision networks to manage the world’s shipping container terminals, confirmed today that it has raised $11.3 million in seed funding to help port and rail hubs reduce congestion and move more cargo. It has been revealed that freight networks move more than a billion shipping containers per year. They carry everything from clothing, electronics, furniture, raw materials for construction and perishable goods – the things that people rely on every day.

However, according to Sea-Intelligence Pte. Ltd., a Copenhagen-based shipping consulting and data analytics manufacturer, in September only half of the world’s container ships arrived on time and the late ones were a week behind. Oftentimes, ships can spend nearly half their time at port not working cargo. (Related: Transformer-based Encoder-Decoder Models...)

Nodal Networks believes the solution is better management, not new infrastructure, which will be key to providing better terminal capacity.

It has been revealed that “Watching ports through Earth-observation satellites, I started to see transport as a physical internet: Containers are the data packets, ports and rail yards are the routers, and congestion is the lag,” noted co-founder and Chief Executive Paul Albert-Lebrun. “So, we went looking for the routers. We spent eight months in ports, terminals and rail yards, and even sailed the North Sea aboard a container ship, learning the work from the people who do it.”.

Industry observers note that albert-Lebrun worked in satellite operations and later led product at Kepler Communications Inc., a space telecom company. Co-founder and Chief Commercial Officer Nic Westgate, by contrast, comes from mining engineering, approaching the problem from the opposite end.

Together, they combined their expertise in Malcom, the firm’s AI-powered platform for improving terminal operations, named after Malcom McLean, a North Carolina truck driver turned entrepreneur who pioneered modern container shipping.

In a recent development, the AI platform combines agents with specialized forecasting models that can see, understand and predict incoming shipments, timing and offloading metrics to follow congestion, track lost productivity and make better-informed decisions about container placement, equipment allocation and loading operations. The organization stated it’s already deployed in terminals across three continents but did not provide details on which locations.

Industry observers note that “There’s a lot of noise about AI in our industry right now, and I see a new tech giant pitching terminals almost every week,” stated Peter Levesque, an adviser to Nodal and former president of Ports America Inc. and CEO of CMA CGM North America LLC. “Most bring a general-purpose tool and look for somewhere to apply it, and few can answer the question every operator asks: what’s the return? Nodal got into the details, surrounded itself with the right people and kept the customer’s bottom line at the center of everything.”.

Levesque added that Nodal has proven itself in terminal after terminal by showing measurable results.

It has been revealed that according to Nodal, Malcom has already reduced truck time by one-half in one terminal after operators reassigned yard equipment. At another terminal, ship loading and unloading speeded up by 5% after the team pinpointed what kept cranes waiting for containers. In both cases, the firm said Malcom helped operators identify which decisions would have the greatest impact.

It has been revealed that the tech giant mentioned it would use the newly introduced funding to expand Malcom’s reach to more terminals within the groups Nodal already works with, support new customers and advance product development. It also intends to extend Malcom beyond the terminal, adding planning for shipping lines, railroads and trucking companies, becoming support for transportation networks where operators work together to exchange information.

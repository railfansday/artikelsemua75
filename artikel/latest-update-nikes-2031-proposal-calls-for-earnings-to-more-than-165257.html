On Nov. 17, Nike (NKE +2.55%) concluded its first investor day meeting in nine years. It came at a time of great uncertainty for the business. The brand's dominance is under threat. Revenue is declining. And profits have cratered.

Elliott Hill, chief executive officer since October 2024, laid out a clear financial target that gives investors some hope. By fiscal 2031, Nike prepares to report adjusted earnings per share (EPS) of $3.00 to $3.50. At the midpoint, this would be 106% higher than the $1.58 generated in fiscal 2026 (ended May 31).

Sources indicate that A profit outlook like this is encouraging, although the market doesn't see it that way today. Shares fell 10% recently and were were down 46% in 2026. Investors are pessimistic, as the turnaround will take longer than expected.

Assuming Nike is able to reach that adjusted EPS target in five years, here's what it could mean for the consumer discretionary stock.

It's good for investors to first understand what exactly Nike intends to do to right the ship. The next few years are about resetting the playbook.

Recent findings suggest that first, the business is narrowing its focus. The Full Stride Ahead strategy centers on five sports: running, training, global football, basketball, and outdoor. Together with sportswear and 23 priority countries, these areas are expected to drive more than 90% of the revenue growth through fiscal 2031 compared to fiscal 2027. (Related: Breaking: Distributed Training: Train BART/T5 for Summa...)

According to recent reports, second, the three brands (Nike, Jordan, and Converse) don't change, despite speculation that Converse could be sold. Performance sports should deliver about $4 billion of the targeted $5 billion in added revenue versus fiscal 2027. Sportswear, which encompasses the firm's lifestyle-based apparel and footwear, supplies the other $1 billion. (Related: Transformer-based Encoder-Decoder Models...)

Third, Nike is pushing resources closer to priority countries and cities. Greater China, once a key growth engine, is dealing with recent challenges. India is a focal point now.

Industry observers note that fourth, cost cuts need to do a lot of the heavy lifting. The Pace program targets about $2.5 billion in annualized savings by fiscal 2031, with impacts starting to be felt this fiscal year. That supports an operating margin goal of 12% to 14%, a massive improvement versus roughly 6% expected in fiscal 2027.

In a recent development, timing is where it gets interesting. Management sees revenue shrinking this year and posting a "moderate" decline in fiscal 2028. Market-level growth isn't targeted until fiscal 2029. And investors will have to wait until the turn of the decade for accelerated growth.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/23/26.

Nike shares trade at a price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of 16.5, based on trailing 12-month profits. Assume that the multiple holds steady for five years.

It has been revealed that if Nike earns $3.25 per share in fiscal 2031, the midpoint of its target, the stock would climb 106% to roughly $71. A $10,000 investment today would be worth about $20,600, before dividends. That's more than a 15% annualized gain, well ahead of the long-term average of about 10% for the S&P 500 index (^GSPC +4.73%).

It all sounds so easy. But the path is heavily back-loaded. Management guides for adjusted EPS of $1.15 to $1.35 in fiscal 2027. Getting from the fiscal 2027 midpoint to $3.25 requires 160% growth in four years. Investors would be paying to buy shares when profits are projected to fall in the near term.

Sources indicate that the market has doubts, as evidenced by the stock's recent dip. At about 10.6 times the 2031 EPS target, the stock looks cheap on paper. Morgan Stanley (MS +4.18%) analyst Alex Straton called the goals optimistic.

According to recent reports, that skepticism is fair. Turnarounds are defined by heightened uncertainty. The financial improvements might never show up. Or they could, but take a lot longer than expected. Nike is still a show-me stock with a lot to prove.

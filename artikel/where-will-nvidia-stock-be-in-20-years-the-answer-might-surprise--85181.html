Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) stock is at the moment the cheapest it has been in over a decade. Shares trade at just 16 times trailing earnings. That's even cheaper than the manufacturer's previous decade-low valuation set in early 2019. (Related: Meta’s ‘secret’ Black Friday sale just started, and it ...)

The organization's shrinking valuation has little to do with its current financial situation. The GPU maker generated $43.8 billion in free cash flow last quarter. Sales growth this year is expected to approach 100% year over year, with another 71% growth expected the following year. Profits are expected to rise in tandem, causing shares to trade at less than 10 times forward earnings.

It has been revealed that the macro environment for Nvidia is similarly bullish. Research from Apollo Global Management (APO -4.41%) indicates:.

Industry observers note that what, then, is dragging down Nvidia stock? There are three main culprits.

Sources indicate that first, the company is dealing with the ongoing fallout of CEO Jensen Huang's affair with an alleged Chinese spy. The company could face regulatory blowback, export restrictions, and weaker long-term leadership following Huang's exit.

Second, reporting suggests that Nvidia engaged in a form of circular financing, injecting cash into artificial intelligence (AI) start-ups to absorb excess GPU supply.

Third, investors are concerned about potential margin compression as both hyperscalers and other GPU producers invest aggressively to scale their own chip production.

But here's the thing: If you commit to a 20-year holding period, few of these current concerns will ultimately matter.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

Recent findings suggest that normally, it is difficult to forecast a organization's next couple of decades. Nvidia's situation is surprisingly straightforward. While the company's dominant market share in AI GPUs is driven by long-term planning and software ecosystem investment, sales growth is largely driven by industrywide demand. In other words, Nvidia is a high-quality boat in a tide that will rise for decades to come.

It has been revealed that "Nvidia has a wide economic moat," asserts research from Morningstar. "[I]ts competitive advantages should allow it to sustain profitability for 20 years," as its products and software are "needed to enable the exponentially growing market around artificial intelligence.".

How quickly is that AI market growing, and how long will it sustain those growth rates?

Industry observers note that "A massive wave of data center construction will have implications through 2050," stresses research from PwC. "The AI infrastructure cycle underway will likely dwarf previous investment booms in areas like railways, electrification, or the internet.".

In no scenario does PwC forecast data center spending to fall under $20 trillion by 2050.

"Across all three scenarios, capex rises significantly through 2050," the firm predicts. "Faster AI adoption pushes capex to about $50 trillion through 2050; a slower adoption path limits the cycle to roughly $20 trillion.".

While former CEO Jensen Huang was critical in developing Nvidia's current chip lineup and CUDA developer ecosystem, Nvidia's long-term future now relies just as much on industry trends as company-specific development.

Similarly, while near-term regulatory pressure or export restrictions may slightly reduce sales growth, the long-term global growth outlook remains intact.

Margin compression over time, meanwhile, is almost a certainty. But trading at less than 10 times forward earnings, much of these pressures are arguably already priced in. Compounding double-digit annual sales growth -- which should persist over the next two decades -- should more than offset margin normalization, especially considering the next few years should still generate industry-leading profitability.

Sources indicate that what will Nvidia be like 20 years from now? The manufacturer will almost certainly be much bigger than it is today. And given how cheaply the stock is priced, I'm fairly certain that patient shareholders will be pleased following a multi-decade holding period.

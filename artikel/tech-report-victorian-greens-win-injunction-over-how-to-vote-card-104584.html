The Victorian Greens have won a supreme court injunction to stop an independent candidate from handing out how to vote cards that mimic their branding and urge people to preference it last. (Related: Breaking: Illinois appeals Pacold injunction blocking i...)

The decision has been welcomed by the party’s leader, Ellen Sandell, who said the flyers distributed by people wearing the exact same colour shirts as genuine Greens volunteers had already misled voters. (Related: Nebraska voters appear to reject online sports betting ...)

“The last thing people need when they walk into a polling booth is dodgy how-to-vote material designed to trick them into voting the exact opposite way they intended,” Sandell stated.

The decision comes after Guardian Australia confirmed that people handing out material for the independent candidate Tosh-Jake Finnigan were initially offered $25 an hour to do so.

As prepoll opened on Wednesday morning, Finnigan’s campaign team handed out how-to-vote cards that used the minor party’s colour scheme, font and triangle logo with the banner “Vote 1 Green Values”.

The card instructed people to vote in a way that would ultimately benefit the Liberal party. It asked voters to give their first preference to Finnigan followed by an Animal Justice party candidate, another independent candidate and then the Liberals in fourth place.

It has been revealed that the Greens are determined to win back the ultra-marginal seat it lost to the Liberals at a February 2025 byelection with a 1.3% margin. Labor is running an open ticket in Prahran, which the Greens fear could deprive it of the preference votes it needs.

On Wednesday afternoon, the Greens state director, Martin Shield, requested Finnigan immediately stop distributing the material and threatened to take legal action if an undertaking was not given to do so in less than two hours.

Finnigan agreed to the request and produced an amended how-to-vote card within hours that included different colours with a revised banner that mentioned “Vote 1 Trans Greens Values”.

“Due to legal threats from the Greens party, I confirm that I am not an endorsed Greens party candidate,” a disclaimer on Finnigan’s revised how-to-vote card said.

It has been revealed that but the changes did not satisfy the Greens. On Friday afternoon, the party’s lawyers lodged papers in the supreme court.

At an initial hearing on Friday night, the Greens’ lawyer, Adam McBeth, alleged Finnigan’s team stood next to Greens party volunteers and Greens placards. He told the court the flyers were designed to mislead voters.

“There is no other reason for an independent candidate who has an infinite range of colours and styles to choose from to so closely mimic the Greens, including the way people paid to hand out were dressed and where they were directed to stand,” McBeth said.

Guardian Australia has confirmed an ad on Airtasker offering payment to those handing out flyers at pre-poll in Prahran was for Finnegan’s campaign.

“I need people to hand out flyers at election booths in Prahran and Richmond $25 per hour during the day from 18-28 November,” the ad remarked.

But the person who published the ad, Finnegan’s friend Dean Hurston, said no one was recruited through Airtasker as Finnegan had found volunteers elsewhere.

Justice John Watson granted the injunction on both how-to-vote cards on Sunday morning, after hearing testimony from the Victorian Electoral Commission on Saturday afternoon.

Finnigan, who was self-represented, mentioned they had gone above and beyond to quickly make changes to their initial how-to-vote card and accused the Greens of using “legal manoeuvres” to stop them campaigning.

“It is my belief that they don’t want me to hand out on Saturday, which will be one of the busiest days of prepoll,” Finnigan noted.

Finnigan told the court that they did not have enough money to print newly introduced cards. They mentioned their campaign team had not been instructed to stand next to the Greens at prepoll.

“I am someone who holds Greens values, of course I am going to have some kind of green shirt,” Finnigan said.

“The honest reason I went for that colour of green is because the colour is called Irish green and I am of Irish background. That’s why I went for it. I just like the sound of Irish green.”.

On Friday evening, after the supreme court action was lodged, the Victorian Election Commission (VEC) accepted Finnigan’s subsequent how-to-vote card and published it online.

Justice John Watson said the first how-to-vote card “looked like a pretty deliberate attempt to pass yourself off as a Greens candidate”, but he adjourned the matter until 3pm on Saturday to hear testimony from the VEC.

A decision on whether to grant an injunction will be made on Sunday morning.

It has been revealed that finnigan became Victoria’s first openly transgender councillor in 2023 when they were elected to the Colac Otway shire council after a countback. They resigned from the council in October 2024.

The former Labor field campaigner founded the Restore Democracy Sack Dan Andrews party in 2022. They unsuccessfully contested an upper house seat for the party, securing 1% of first preference votes.

Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) has experienced a stunning reversal following founder Jensen Huang's unexpected resignation amid controversy surrounding alleged connections to Chinese intelligence. Investors are increasingly questioning whether the semiconductor behemoth can maintain its technological edge without the executive who spent more than three decades building the company. Those concerns have contributed to a dramatic sell-off, even as Nvidia continues reporting ridiculously good financial results. (Related: Latest Update: Does Fluoride In Drinking Water Affect I...)

In a recent development, the magnitude of the decline is anything but trivial. Nvidia stock reached a record closing price of around $239 in early October but has fallen to roughly $136 as of Nov. 20. That represents a decline of roughly 43%, wiping out more than $2.5 trillion in market capitalization and pushing Nvidia from the world's most valuable public tech giant to fourth place.

NVDA Market Cap data by YCharts.

Nevertheless, Wall Street analyst Dan Ives thinks investors are overlooking Nvidia's underlying earnings power. Although he acknowledges that Huang's departure changes the company's investment thesis, his revised price target suggests meaningful upside remains.

Recent findings suggest that ives has been particularly vocal about the consequences of Huang's resignation. During a recent interview, he estimated that Huang contributed $1 trillion to Nvidia's valuation. In a separate appearance, Ives described Huang as the manufacturer's "heart and lungs," emphasizing his influence over Nvidia's technological roadmap and competitive position.

Ives says Nvidia stock is worth $225 without Huang and $300 with him as CEO. These figures provide an interesting way to quantify the so-called Jensen premium. The difference between Ives' two valuations is $75 per share, representing a 25% reduction from his original $300 target.

Recent findings suggest that the implied difference in market capitalization is actually considerably larger than the $1 trillion Ives alluded to. With approximately 24.2 billion shares outstanding, a $75 difference translates into roughly $1.8 trillion in equity value. At Ives' respective price targets, Nvidia would command a market capitalization of approximately $7.3 trillion with Huang and $5.4 trillion without him.

The difference in valuation illustrates the enormous importance Wall Street places on Nvidia's founder and why investors have reacted so negatively to his departure.

Dan Ives. Image source: Getty Images.

Huang's value extends beyond his responsibilities as CEO. Since co-founding Nvidia in 1993, he has overseen the tech giant's transformation from a graphics processing company into the dominant supplier of accelerated computing infrastructure. His investments in GPUs, CUDA software, and AI computing helped position Nvidia at the center of the technology industry's most vital transformation since the birth of the internet.

Perhaps Huang's greatest contribution has been leading Nvidia's culture of innovation. Huang consistently anticipated computing trends years before they became commercially relevant. This enabled Nvidia to develop new chip architectures and software ecosystems that competitors have struggled to replicate. This foresight helps establish durable competitive advantages and contributes to the organization's pricing power.

Sources indicate that huang also cultivated relationships with other technology executives, hyperscalers, governments, and AI labs. These supply chain connections have become key as Nvidia participates in data center build-outs that require complex financing arrangements and long-term strategic commitments.

Recent findings suggest that with all of that remarked, Nvidia's competitive advantages are not going to disappear just because Huang stepped away. The organization's hardware and software ecosystem, engineering workforce, customer relationships, and product roadmap remain intact.

Moreover, Ives acknowledges that Nvidia has a lead of two to three years over its competitors in AI chip technology. Although Huang's departure could narrow that advantage over time, the company's existing leadership provides a robust foundation for future growth.

Jensen Huang. Image source: Getty Images.

Recent findings suggest that despite reducing his Nvidia price target from $300 to $225, Ives maintains a bullish outlook on the company. At approximately $136 per share, his revised target implies roughly 65% upside, while his original $300 estimate implies 121% potential appreciation.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

Importantly, Ives' optimism centers on the insatiable demand for AI computing infrastructure and Nvidia's technological advantages. Following the tech giant's earnings report on Nov. 17, Ives wrote that the results answered a critical question: "Demand did not leave with him.".

Recent findings suggest that the financial evidence supports that assessment. Nvidia generated $115.8 billion in fiscal third-quarter revenue, representing 103% growth year over year, while data center revenue surged 110% to $107.6 billion. The company also reported a gross margin of 74.8%, demonstrating that its pricing power remains strong.

In a recent development, furthermore, Nvidia forecast $130 billion in revenue for the fourth quarter and raised its fiscal 2028 revenue growth outlook to 75%. This forecast suggests the company is poised for further expansion despite the leadership transition.

According to recent reports, the central takeaway from Ives' analysis is that Nvidia doesn't necessarily need Huang to remain an attractive investment. Although his departure has reduced -- for now -- the premium investors are willing to pay, his price target still suggests considerable appreciation over the coming year. For investors who can stomach short-term volatility, Nvidia's recent sell-off could represent an opportunity to buy the dip in the most dominant AI infrastructure business at a significantly discounted valuation.

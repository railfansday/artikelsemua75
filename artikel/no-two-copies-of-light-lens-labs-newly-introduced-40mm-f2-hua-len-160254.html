In a recent development, light Lens Lab’s founder, Mr. Zhou, collected vintage Chinese lenses long before he started his lens (and film and camera) company. His brand’s newest special project, the Light Lens Lab 40mm f/2 “Hua,” celebrates the best features of those classic optics.

As Light Lens Lab explains, the “Hua” is also a celebration of the tech giant itself.

“We are proud to announce a small, fun, but special project: the Light Lens Lab 40mm f/2 ‘Hua,'” the company notes. “A token of appreciation for all Light Lens Lab patrons and supporters since 2016.”.

Sources indicate that china has a rich photographic history and has been producing cameras and lenses en masse for decades. Light Lens Lab outlines the timeline and inspiration behind its new lens:.

Sources indicate that in December 1953, Wang Daheng and his colleagues in Changchun produced China’s first successful batch of optical elements. In the decade that followed, Shanghai produced the Shanghai 58-I, the first domestically produced camera from China, with Seagull twin-lens reflex cameras shortly following. Shanghai’s No. 2 Camera Factory also built the Hongqi 20, a 35mm rangefinder camera to showcase domestic precision engineering. Even now, with most of the company defunct, these companies and their skill set form the backbone of Chinese optical and photographic manufacturing excellence. Industry observers note that light Lens Lab says it created the latest 40mm f/2 “Hua” as a “tribute” to the Chinese manufacturers that came before it. Every copy incorporates select, restored glass elements from Mr. Zhou’s personal collection, making each lens genuinely unique. According to the company, the vintage elements have been cleaned of historical defects and recoated to match their original specifications. Each “Hua” gives “photographers a chance to make latest photographs with glass shaped by an earlier generation of Chinese lens makers.”.

It has been revealed that as far as PetaPixel is aware, this is the first time a upcoming lens has celebrated sample variation rather than sought to eliminate it. Two different 40mm f/2 “Hua” copies could render the same scene very differently, making the sample images below a less representative of what to expect than usual.

Every 40mm f/2 “Hua” features six elements in four groups, focuses down to 0.5 meters (1.6 feet), and includes a 10-bladed aperture ranging from f/2 to f/16.

The lenses also share an aluminum-and-brass barrel design based on the company’s current 50mm f/2 model. However, Light Lens Lab indicates it developed a brand-upcoming Black Chrome finishing process for this special release that will carry over to future optics.

Given the design philosophy and unique manufacturing process behind the Light Lens Lab 40mm f/2 “Hua,” these sample photos may not be truly indicative of all copies of the lens.

Industry observers note that orders start today, November 23, with shipping set to begin in December. Light Lens Lab notes that because this is a limited-edition release built with vintage glass elements, it will produce the lens in “small batches as capacity allows.” It is available in Black Chrome and Silver Chrome finishes for $250.

The manual-focus lens comes natively in Leica M-mount, making it easy to adapt to modern mirrorless cameras.

A week and a half after being forced out as Nvidia CEO, Jensen Huang has returned to the AI chip giant he founded, ensuring he remains the face of the manufacturer amid a wave of investigations.

Helped by pressure from President Donald Trump and support from Silicon Valley leaders, the board declared Monday that after a unanimous vote Huang was reinstalled as CEO, president and a director effective immediately.

Shares shot up 30% as trading began, leading a massive stock market rally after top indexes tumbled last week into correction territory.

Nvidia’s board said it received input from senior levels of the U.S. government and is confident that Huang’s reinstatement “poses no national security risk.”.

But that won’t put a stop to the myriad probes the company faces related to Huang’s affair with a suspected Chinese spy and sales of Nvidia chips that bypassed export restrictions on China.

The California attorney general’s office subpoenaed Nvidia as part of an investigation into whether the company or Huang enabled China to obtain advanced chips or confidential information—or misled investors about the associated risks.

The SEC is also investigating if the company misled investors in its public disclosures amid questions on whether Nvidia knew about its potential diversion of chips to China and whether its disclosures adequately addressed the risks.

Trump has also remarked the FBI is investigating Huang’s relationship with Megaspeed’s Alice Huang and any ties to Chinese intelligence. Beijing has rejected the allegations. Megaspeed has insisted it conducts legitimate and lawful business and that Huang officially left effective August 30, 2025.

The Justice Department is investigating whether entities linked to Alice Huang helped smuggle products from Super Micro, a top Nvidia customer, into China, according to Bloomberg. That’s after the Commerce and Justice departments stated they investigated Megaspeed over potential export-control violations starting in 2024.

Lawmakers in Congress are also looking into Nvidia, with Huang and the tech giant’s general counsel due to appear before three House committees about Chinese entities accessing advanced chips. Huang has agreed to testify before the Senate in a public hearing, and Sen. Elizabeth Warren stated Monday that he and Nvidia still owe Congress answers.

Meanwhile, Nvidia’s board is also in the middle of its own probe, led by independent directors and with help from outside counsel. The company said Monday that this “will proceed to conclusion.”.

While Trump and other administration officials have cast doubt on whether Alice Huang was working with Chinese intelligence, fresh reports have highlighted those links.

On Sunday, the New York Times reported she was in contact with an official of China’s top spy agency, the Ministry of State Security, from the spring of 2025 until the fall of this year—a period that overlaps with her affair with Jensen Huang.

Aston Villa took a "big step forward" in their 0-0 draw with Sunderland, so says Unai Emery.

Villa drew a third straight Premier League match as they were frustrated by the Black Cats at Villa Park. (Related: Meta’s ‘secret’ Black Friday sale just started, and it ...)

It is the first time Villa have drawn three straight Premier League games since 2014. (Related: Three 'e-waste' Nvidia V100s hit 100 tokens per second ...)

Villa attempted 700 passes in this match – their most on record (from 2003-04) in a single Premier League game, completing 624, their joint-most on record (also v Hull City in September this season).

They had 19 shots, but those chances were worth only 0.98 expected goals (xG) combined. Emery, though, was pleased with the levels demonstrated by his team on the occasion of his 150th Premier League game in charge of Villa.

Emery told Sky Sports: "A little bit frustrated, but how we are building our team and recovering our confidence in our style of playing, [this game] was a step forward.

"We were dominating, we were progressing with passes, and we were in a good position to control the game, but we didn't create many clear chances.

Industry observers note that "But this is the way and the idea we have to try and implement. We avoided the moment of them dominating us and avoided giving up chances to the opponent. We did a lot of good things.

According to recent reports, "It is not enough, but we know how we have to try and build the team and how we can feel better. One step forward, clear in the style. Keep going forward.".

Sunderland, meanwhile, have picked up more points in their last four Premier League games (five – W1 D2 L1) than they had in their first seven this season beforehand (four – W1 D1 L5).

Regis Le Bris' team moved up into 18th, though they will slip back down to 19th should Manchester United avoid defeat at Liverpool on Sunday.

In a recent development, "Being hard to beat was one of the main objectives. It was really vital to be solid, and I think the lads showed a great mentality," Le Bris told BBC Sport.

"We had a few balls to exploit, and even at the end, with just a bit more maturity and more confidence, it was possible to score a goal.

Recent findings suggest that "We showed we are a really good team when we want to defend together. I told them when you feel the opponents getting frustrated, it is a good sign, and it happened in the first half. A good point on the road.

"The points are important; sometimes you play well and don't get anything. It is the strength of the squad, the mindset and stability. I am really proud of that, as it is the foundation for the future.".

In a recent development, on 22 November 2026 NVIDIA released Alpamayo 2 Edge, a compact ~2B-parameter reasoning vision-language-action model that intends driving trajectories and can emit Chain-of-Causation explanations. It targets DRIVE AGX Thor, not a datacenter card. With the FP8 + TensorRT Edge-LLM recipe (DRIVE OS 7.2.7.0, CUDA 13.4, TensorRT 11.3.1.9) NVIDIA measures 160–190 ms pixel-to-trajectory latency, depending on how much reasoning is generated. Adaptive reasoning skips full traces during routine driving and emits NOMINAL tokens instead. The BF16 PyTorch path needs 16 GB+ and is slower (demo numbers revealed ~0.76 Hz); the optimized path is what makes the real-time claim.

It has been revealed that that latency number is the interesting part for anyone shipping live overlays on Windows. 160–190 ms from sensor to action is the same ballpark as a usable first partial from a streaming STT session once the audio has left the capture callback. The model side keeps improving. The capture side on Windows has not.

Industry observers note that audio-thread callbacks do nothing but copy samples into a lock-free SPSC ring (power-of-two capacity). No model work, no locks that can stall the callback. Consumer threads then resample to 16 kHz mono, run a cheap energy/VAD gate, and feed either a local streaming session or chunked local model. Industry observers note that alpamayo’s 160–190 ms is the model-plus-optimization stage on specialized silicon. On a laptop the equivalent stage is your local ASR or small suggestion model. If the capture period is a 40 ms Bluetooth shared-mode buffer plus engine APOs, the recognizer starts late regardless of how good the model is.

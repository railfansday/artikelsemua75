Megan Moroney made sure to give Taylor Swift her flowers — literally — after her highly anticipated 2026 CMAs comeback. And, no, the 14-time Grammy winner didn’t take it lightly.

“On cloud 9 at the CMA awards!” Moroney, 29, wrote via Instagram on Friday, November 20, alongside a carousel of images from the celebratory evening. (Related: My Journey to a serverless transformers pipeline on Goo...)

In one slide, Moroney revealed that Swift, 36, gifted her a personalized poster ahead of the big show.

Industry observers note that “Megan! Thank you SO much for these beautiful flowers,” Swift wrote in her message. “That was incredibly thoughtful and I hope you have a fantastic night! I’d send you flowers back but also all I have in my dressing room is merch. Just pretend it’s a lovely bouquet?”. (Related: Industry News: Designing Azure silicon on Azure silicon...)

It has been revealed that she concluded, “Go get ‘em!! Love, Taylor.”.

Sources indicate that moroney and Swift both attended the 60th annual CMAs on Wednesday, November 18, in honor of their respective nominations. Moroney was recognized in the Song of the Year and Female Artist of the Year categories, both of which she lost to Ella Langley. Langley’s “Choosin’ Texas” also triumphed as Single of the Year over Swift’s “I Knew It, I Knew You.”.

Amid their respective losses, Moroney and Swift each performed during the live broadcast. For Swift, her return marked the first time she sang on the CMAs stage in 13 years.

Dressed in a bedazzled Erdem minidress and cowboy boots, Swift mashed up the Toy Story 5 track with “Our Song” from her debut album. The pop star further leaned into her country roots by enlisting her former country collaborators Brad Paisley, Keith Urban, Little Big Town, Brooks & Dunn and Sugarland for brief cameos.

Recent findings suggest that swift famously got her start as a country artist before making the move to the pop scene with 2014’s 1989. Following the 2025 release of her 12th album, The Life of a Showgirl, Swift has dropped hints at embracing her twang-filled music history down the line.

Industry observers note that “I love my country roots more and more as I go further in this path,” Swift mentioned during a Grammy Museum retrospective in August. “I want to honor them more and more, the artist who shaped who I am, and that all happened in Nashville.”.

“I Knew It, I Knew You” marked her official return when it dropped in June.

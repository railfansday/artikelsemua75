Every VPS price you see is a first-term price. The question that decides what you actually pay is what happens when the term ends. VPS Hosting Radar hand-checked 19 intends from 15 providers against the same baseline — closest available plan to 2 vCPU / 4 GB RAM — and recorded three numbers per plan: the advertised first-term rate, the month-to-month rate, and the renewal rate, each backed by a dated screenshot. In a recent development, rows sorted by renewal increase, highest first. First term is the advertised rate and the prepay it requires; renewal is what you pay after the first term ends.

Two notes on the table: WP Engine's $35 renewal is provisional — its plans page demonstrates the $28 first term but no documented renewal rate, and the index marks it rather than guessing. Contabo's Cloud VPS 4 is €5.28 → €6.60 (+25%), quoted in euros with VAT included, so it's excluded from the USD comparisons and the 36-month ranking.

The 19 rows sort cleanly into three groups, and knowing which camp a host is in tells you more than any spec sheet:.

According to recent reports, camp 1 — List pricing, 0% increase. Vultr, DigitalOcean, Linode, RackNerd, Kamatera, Cloudways, Kinsta, Rocket.net, and SiteGround's cloud line. You pay the same list price in month 37 as after any first-month promo. Usage-billed infrastructure lands here structurally; Kinsta and Rocket.net choose to.

Camp 2 — Introductory pricing, +25% to +100%. WP Engine (+25%, provisional), Contabo (+25%), ScalaHosting Unmanaged (+25%), ScalaHosting Managed (+83%), InMotion (+93%), Liquid Web (+100%). The discount is real but time-boxed, usually tied to a 24- or 36-month commitment. Budget on the renewal — that is the real price.

Industry observers note that camp 3 — Long-prepay shared hosting, +200% to +502%. Hostinger KVM 1 (+200%), Hostinger Web Premium (+301%), SiteGround StartUp (+502%). The lowest headline numbers in the market, reachable only by prepaying one to two years, followed by the steepest increases.

Recent findings suggest that neither camp is dishonest by definition. Camp 3 can be the cheapest option over three years if you genuinely commit — Hostinger Web Premium's 36-month total is $323.64, the lowest in the whole index. The problem is that a pricing page highlights you the Camp 3 number while implying Camp 1 economics. And the 36-month ranking mixes product types: the two cheapest rows are shared plans, the two most expensive are a cloud instance and a per-site autoscaling product. A three-year total states what you will pay, not what you will get — read it alongside the Type column, never on its own.

According to recent reports, cloudways runs on infrastructure you can rent directly, which makes the managed premium measurable instead of theoretical. Same 4 GB / 2 vCPU instance:.

Recent findings suggest that the index's own caveat: Cloudways splits DigitalOcean instances into standard and premium CPU tiers, and its $54 row is the premium tier against DigitalOcean's $24 regular-CPU droplet, so a strictly like-for-like comparison would narrow the gap. Across every combination it could verify, the managed layer lands between roughly 1.9× and 2.3× the direct rental — so "it roughly doubles the bill" holds, but not as a precise percentage.

It has been revealed that whether doubling the bill is worth it comes down to one question: could you restore that server from a snapshot at 2am, with revenue down? If yes, you are paying roughly $30/month for something you can already do. If no, you are buying it cheaply.

One more cost that flips comparisons: what's bundled. Backups, staging, and email are included at some hosts and billed separately at others. DigitalOcean charges $4.80/month for backups, Vultr $4.00, Linode $5.00, InMotion $2.99, Cloudways $0.033/GB. Kinsta and WP Engine include staging; Cloudways includes staging but not backups or email. SiteGround StartUp includes email; Hostinger includes it for the first year only. A $20 VPS with a $5 backup add-on is a $25 VPS.

Industry observers note that the index tells you what things cost. The chooser tells you what fits. It asks four questions and returns a primary + alternative shortlist from a pool of 15 hosts — managed cloud, premium managed WordPress, or DIY IaaS — matched to your workload:.

Two things worth knowing about the tool: your answers stay in your browser — nothing is sent to its servers — and it does not invent a universal winner. The shortlist comes with tradeoffs and "wrong when" notes. Scoring weights and the full host catalog stay unpublished.

Industry observers note that for a dev lab or a side project where you run the OS yourself, start with the 0% camp: Vultr Regular 2 vCPU / 4 GB at $20/month, or DigitalOcean / Linode at $24 — the price you see is the price you keep. For the cheapest three years and a willingness to prepay, Hostinger KVM 1 at $389.64 over 36 months wins on dollars, but only if you budget the $19.49 renewal from day one. For an agency that wants a panel instead of a terminal, Cloudways at $54/month is the honest middle — roughly double the direct rental, and worth it if 2am restores aren't your job. For high-value WordPress, Kinsta at $35 or Rocket.net at $30, both with 0% renewal jumps and staging included.

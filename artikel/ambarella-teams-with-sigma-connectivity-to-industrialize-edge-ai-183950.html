Industry observers note that ambarella and Sigma Connectivity unveiled a strategic collaboration on November 24, 2026 to help manufacturers turn computer vision and edge AI prototypes into production-ready products. Ambarella will supply its low-power AI silicon and software tools, while Sigma Connectivity will handle the engineering and validation work needed for volume manufacturing.

The collaboration is aimed at original equipment manufacturers (OEMs) and enterprises adding AI to connected devices. Planned work spans early architecture definition and prototyping through validation and manufacturing readiness, with the companies intending to reduce engineering complexity, development risk and time to deployment.

Ambarella’s edge AI systems-on-chip (SoCs) combine camera perception, AI acceleration, CPUs and video encoding on one low-power chip. Its X7 AI accelerator brings the company’s third-generation CVflow engine to Arm and x86 host processors, extending the architecture beyond an integrated SoC. It has been revealed that that common CVflow architecture and shared software environment cover Ambarella’s edge AI SoC portfolio. Perception software and development workflows can therefore carry forward when a product line scales to higher performance or shifts to a more cost-efficient design, reducing the need to rebuild the software foundation for each hardware tier.

Sigma Connectivity’s engineers blueprint to use the Cooper Developer Platform and Ambarella Developer Zone, which combines optimized models, agentic blueprints, developer kits, documentation and technical resources in one environment, so customer programs can begin on a mature software foundation. Ambarella CEO Fermi Wang remarked the collaboration gives Sigma Connectivity’s engineers a platform with more than 50 million edge AI SoCs shipped. “The work performed by a design house helps determine whether an edge AI concept becomes a viable commercial product or remains a lab demonstration,” he said.

Recent findings suggest that sigma Connectivity brings more than 700 engineers and over a decade of experience supporting products from early concept and system architecture through design, verification, industrialization and volume production. Its teams work across camera and computer vision, optics, electronic and mechanical design, embedded software, connectivity, and AI and machine learning.

According to recent reports, for a connected edge device, the AI accelerator is only one part of the engineering task: cameras, optics, software, electronics, antennas and mechanical design must also be integrated. Sigma Connectivity’s design, test and verification laboratories allow prototypes and complete products to be assessed against performance requirements and compliance standards before mass production.

It has been revealed that the companies are targeting applications that depend on local AI processing, real-time perception, low latency and power efficiency. Examples include warehouse robots, drones surveying critical infrastructure and machine-vision systems on production lines. In each case, the device must perceive its environment, interpret sensor information and respond within a limited power budget and response time.

It has been revealed that ambarella and Sigma Connectivity plan to help customers integrate computer vision, multimodal AI and emerging Physical AI workloads, in which machines perceive and act in real-world settings, into purpose-built products. Sigma Connectivity CEO Bhavin Patel attributed the fit to Ambarella’s performance-per-watt silicon and Cooper’s validated models and field-proven software stack, combined with his tech giant’s end-to-end product-development capabilities.

The companies initiative joint customer engagements and will evaluate reference designs, proof-of-concept projects and reusable engineering frameworks. Sigma Connectivity’s offices in Europe, the Middle East and North America are expected to support regional OEM and enterprise customers as that work develops.

The collaboration’s practical test is whether those reference designs and reusable frameworks reduce repeated integration work across customer programs, turning Ambarella’s silicon and Sigma Connectivity’s engineering capacity into shorter development cycles for deployable edge AI products.

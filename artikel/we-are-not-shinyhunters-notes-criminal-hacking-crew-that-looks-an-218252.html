📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Inception, a newly introduced data theft and extortion gang that security researchers have theorized is a rebrand of the ShinyHunters group, “reject[s] this attribution” and claims it has no affiliation with any prior cybercrime enterprise.

In a lengthy statement posted Tuesday and shared on X, the extortionists insist that they are a new group, with new operators, and that their alleged social engineering attacks aren’t copying anyone - but rather just abusing the weakest link in an organization’s security: “a human being holding a phone.”. The rant follows a Monday report by Okta Threat Intelligence - along with social media speculation since Inception’s inception last week - that the newly emerged group is very likely ShinyHunters with a different name.

Okta’s researchers based their analysis on the two groups’ shared infrastructure, plus Inception’s nearly identical tactics: voice phishing, during which the scammers trick targets into entering credentials into phishing pages and approving multi-factor authentication (MFA) requests. (Related: Scientists find “field” of washing machines in ocean du...)

Inception apparently took offense to this. “We would like to address this once, clearly, and only once,” the crims' statement notes. “We are not ShinyHunters. We were never ShinyHunters. We are Inception.”.

In a recent development, the gang argues that its overlapping domain registrars, hosting providers, and DNS services don’t prove anything. These are commodity services, “available to anyone who can pay for them,” Inception wrote.

It has been revealed that plus, their similar methodologies don't necessarily make Inception a reboot. “That is not theft of tradecraft,” they wrote. “It is acknowledgment of a good idea – the same way every group after LockBit used a leak site, and nobody called them LockBit.”.

“Inception is a new group, with new operators, our own objectives, and our own name,” the statement continues. “What we share with the past is plumbing.”.

To be fair, the fresh crime crew has every reason to distance itself from ShinyHunters.

That gang’s September FBI hack - plus several earlier digital heists during which the criminals stole information belonging to millions of people, including cancer patients, university and K-12 students - put a massive target on its collective backs.

And while at least three ShinyHunters suspects were arrested in September and October, there's no confirmation that authorities have dismantled the entire operation.

Plus, as a ShinyHunters spokesperson told The Register in an earlier interview, they and others in the crew started off as GnosticPlayers before rebranding as ShinyHunters in 2020 - so a reboot isn’t beyond the realm of possibility.

According to recent reports, inception, for its part, indicates it “will not comment further on the ghost of ShinyHunters,” and it has its own work to do. And by work, it means copying ShinyHunters' playbook, stealing corporate data, and extorting victim companies for massive sums of money.®.

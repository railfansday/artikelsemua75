The claude riemann hypothesis story is everywhere today. On 20 November, Anthropic mathematician Levent Alpöge said in a statement on his website: "I believe I have, working with Claude, discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis" (Scientific American). It is still a claim. Experts are checking it now.

You don't need to know number theory to take something useful from this. The interesting part for anyone who builds with AI is how the claim is being checked.

From the two sources I read for this post:.

Lean can confirm that each step follows from the last. Scientific American notes that "challenges can arise in Lean" and that experts still need to verify that the hypothesis is properly coded. In other words: did the program prove the thing we actually meant? (Related: Smart People Can Still Be Bad At Maths — These 3 Skills...)

That is exactly the question you should ask about your own AI workflows. A passing check only tells you the output matches the spec you wrote. If the spec is wrong, the check is wrong too. (Related: The One Question About Suicide That Could Improve Treat...)

Recent findings suggest that most of us will never prove a theorem. But we hand AI tools more work every month. The way mathematicians are treating this claim (excited, but careful and slow to confirm) is a good model for any business using AI output.

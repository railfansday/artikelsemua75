6 Underrated Mountain Towns to Visit This Winter

Industry observers note that you know the big names. Jackson, Wyoming. Stowe, Vermont. Telluride, Colorado. These are the mountain towns that dominate our winter daydreams. The famous ski towns that make us consider taking a sabbatical, buying a van, and living the Aspen Extreme dream for three months in the heart of winter. But there are other winter adventure spots that deserve the spotlight.

According to recent reports, I cover mountain towns for Outside, and every year I start plotting out my own winter getaways right about now. Sure, I enjoy hitting up established destinations like Park City as much as the next skier, but the towns on this list get me excited because they fly under the radar. They don’t receive a ton of hype, but they have access to amazing powder stashes, lengthy trail systems, and resort lines that are far shorter than their better-known counterparts’. A couple of these towns are my longtime favorites, while others are still on my “must-go” list. Here are six often overlooked winter mountain towns that you need to visit once the snow starts to gather. (Related: Industry News: “Determined” woman completes 7 marathons...)

It has been revealed that A legitimate winter town in the South? Yes indeed. Boone is a vibrant college town that grows when Appalachian State’s roughly 20,000 students are in session. It has all of the hallmarks of a fun-loving mountain town, with plenty of dive bars and burrito joints to keep you satiated. But it also sits in North Carolina’s High Country, surrounded by 5,000- and 6,000-foot mountains, which get some of the heaviest snowfalls in the region. There are three ski resorts within 30 minutes of downtown and some of the most consistent cross-country skiing in the South on a series of 6,000-foot peaks near the Tennessee border.

Winter Adventures: You have three downhill resorts to choose from, but Beech Mountain Resort is the highest ski resort in eastern North America, with a summit elevation of 5,506 feet. It also has a bar, the 5506′ Skybar, at the top of the mountain and its own brewery in the base village. As for the terrain, the mountain has 95 acres and just over 800 feet of drop, so you’ll need to do laps to get in your vert. But the terrain park gets better every year, and skiers can usually count on about 80 inches of snow every winter. Full-day adult lift tickets start at $57 on weekdays.

But the best winter adventure near Boone is cross-country skiing Roan Mountain, a high ridge that tops out at 6,285 feet, gets heavy snowfall, and has a variety of terrain to ski, from high-elevation grassy balds to singletrack through rhododendron tunnels. The only catch is you’ll need to bring your own skis, as rentals are scarce in the South. Start at Carver’s Gap and head up the gated Forest Service road, which climbs gradually for about two miles to the Rhododendron Gardens. You’ll get long-range views in both directions and the option to hop onto more challenging trails that branch off the road.

Duluth is a longtime shipping and industrial city at the western tip of Lake Superior that has found new life as an adventure hub, with a world-class mountain bike trail system traversing the hills on the edge of downtown. But the city’s location in northern Minnesota means winter hits the city hard. Duluth gets about 86 inches of lake-effect snow each winter, and that snow sticks around because high temperatures stay below freezing on more than 100 days a year. The constant cold temps are ideal for ice climbing, and the consistent snow and cornucopia of mountain bike trails mean Duluth becomes a fat-biking and cross-country ski mecca during winter.

Recent findings suggest that need more reasons to visit? How about downhill skiing at nearby Spirit Mountain, which has 175 acres of terrain with 700 feet of vertical drop.

According to recent reports, winter Adventures: Northern Minnesota has a legitimate ski culture, and Spirit Mountain has some of the best blue cruisers in the Midwest, many of which have long-range views of downtown Duluth and Lake Superior. The mountain also has two terrain parks, one for beginners looking to progress and one for shredders looking to send it. There’s a welcoming uphill policy for anyone who wants to earn their turns on touring gear, too. Adult tickets start at $83.

But Duluth is a bike town, and when the snow falls that means it’s time to roll out the fat tires. Many sections of the Duluth Traverse, an epic 40-mile-long route that runs from one side of the city to the other, are groomed for fat bikes in the winter. The machine-built trails in Mission Creek, with their berms and rollers, are particularly fun with a layer of packed snow. Local outfitter Day Tripper offers guided fat-bike tours and rentals.

In a recent development, latest England got hammered with snow last winter, with the highest elevations in the mountains getting more than 200 inches of snow, and the tiny town of Jackson was poised to make the most of it. This picture-perfect village, which comes complete with a general store and covered bridge, sits on the edge of White Mountain National Forest with fast access to downhill skiing at Black Mountain and roughly 90 miles of cross-country ski trails scattered throughout the valley.

Recent findings suggest that jackson doesn’t have the cachet of some other ski towns in Fresh England, but American ski culture got an early boost here when Black Mountain opened in 1935, making it one of the first lift operations in the country. And that culture is still thriving. Case in point: check out Friday Night Lights, a weekly uphill party where skiers put on headlamps and ski to the top of the mountain en masse.

Recent findings suggest that winter Adventures: The Jackson Ski Touring Foundation grooms and maintains about 150 kilometers of trails, all surrounding a touring center with rentals and lessons. The trails traverse the community throughout three consecutive river valleys over 60 square miles. You’ll get views of Mount Washington from open meadows, tight singletrack through woods, and even the chance to hit trailside restaurants in certain spots. Day passes start at $29.

No trip to Jackson is complete without some laps at Black Mountain, a proudly independent ski resort with 140 acres of glades, narrow groomers, and nonexistent lift lines. You’ll get 1,100 feet of vertical drop, with the steepest terrain at the summit, and a lively pub (the Shovel Handle Pub) at the base of the mountain for après-ski activities. Adult lift tickets start at $49.

According to recent reports, pagosa Springs and the surrounding area suffered a flood this fall, but the town and Wolf Creek are open and actively asking for travelers to visit this winter as tourism is integral to their economy. When you visit, keep in mind that locals and businesses might still be dealing with impacts from the flood. Your money helps, but so does your patience and understanding.

The San Juan Mountains are home to some of Colorado’s most iconic mountain towns and most well-regarded ski slopes, but most skiers don’t explore the region beyond Telluride and Silverton. Dig deeper into the mountains of southwest Colorado and you’ll find tiny Pagosa Springs, which sits on the banks of the San Juan River. The hamlet is best known for its geothermal springs, but the mountains surrounding this hot-water hub are even more enticing. Pagosa Springs is 23 miles from Wolf Creek Ski Area, which is known as one of the snowiest mountains in Colorado, pulling down an average of 430 inches of snow every winter.

Want more variety? Summer waterfalls turn into ice-climbing playgrounds when the temperature drops, and the local Nordic club grooms more than 50 kilometers of skate and classic ski trails near town.

Winter Adventures: Forget the champagne huts and concierge service you find at many ski resorts these days. Wolf Creek is a no-frills operation that’s all about the snow and the terrain, with 430 inches of annual snowfall that blanket wide groomers and steep trees. OK, a lot of trees. Wolf Creek has a significant amount of expert terrain, which plays out in a series of steep bowls at the top of the resort and seemingly endless glades all over the mountain. And you won’t have to take out a loan to fund this trip; full-day adult lift tickets at Wolf Creek are right at $110.

It has been revealed that the 50 kilometers of cross-country ski trails begin in town, with a mile-long loop inside the town park that’s perfect for beginners. But this is a great chance to dip a toe into fat biking. There’s an 8.5-mile loop that’s groomed for fat bikes when snow allows, winding through a ponderosa pine forest. Pagosa Mountain Sport rents cross-country skis and fat bikes.

Recent findings suggest that park City, Salt Lake City…these are the ski towns that get the press, but Ogden? Ogden doesn’t typically make the list. But it should. This historic city, which was the first permanent settlement by people of European descent in the state of Utah, sits between the Great Salt Lake and the Wasatch Mountains, just 40 minutes north of Salt Lake City, but seemingly a world apart. Downtown Ogden is anchored by 25th Street, a historic main street with upscale Wild West charm, while three ski resorts known for deep powder are less than 30 minutes away. Powder Mountain anchors the trio, with more than 8,000 acres of terrain, but Snowbasin is continually ranked one of the best ski resorts in the country by Ski magazine and the smaller Nordic Valley has a local-hill vibe complete with night skiing. Bonus: You don’t have to fight Salt Lake City’s notorious canyon traffic to make your turns.

According to recent reports, winter Adventures: You’re here to ski, so get after it at Powder Mountain, the largest ski resort in the Lower 48. Powder has 2,800 acres of lift-served terrain with more than 2,000 feet of vertical drop, but the real gem here is Powder’s sidecountry. Head through the Powder Country or Woody’s World entrance gates from the lift-served side of the mountain and you’ll have 1,200 acres of ungroomed, off-piste action at your ski tips. The best part? A shuttle bus picks you up at the bottom of your line and takes you back to the front of the mountain, so there’s no boot-packing involved. Adult lift tickets start at $99.

It has been revealed that you might also want to try your hand at sledding AKA snowmobiling while you’re in Ogden. The Monte Cristo area, east of the Ogden Valley, has some of the best snowmobile terrain in the state, including a 63-mile groomed loop that’s perfect for beginners and has long-range views of the Ogden Valley. Club Rec North offers tours and rentals next to the trailhead. Tours with rentals start at $229.

Recent findings suggest that this former gold-mining post in the North Cascades really leans into its historic roots, with a downtown that’s lined with Old West-style false fronts and wooden boardwalks. And that western heritage is still alive and well. A number of outfitters offer horseback adventures and the annual Ride to Rendezvous, a four-day horse-packing trip complete with a wagon train that rolls into town for Winthrop’s ’49er Days parade. But make no mistake, Winthrop has found new life as a ski town. Well, a skinny-ski town. The Methow Valley surrounding Winthrop has one of the most extensive cross-country trail networks in the country with more than 120 miles of groomed, marked and maintained paths to explore.

Looking for a cozy vibe for the whole family? Take a spin around Winthrop Rink, the town’s outdoor ice-skating rink that serves as the social hub for the season.

In a recent development, winter Adventures: If you’re spending time in Winthrop, then you’re here to ski the Methow Trails. With more than 120 miles of obsessively groomed paths, there is something for everyone in this valley. Want to ski from lodge to lodge? That’s an option. Want a pretty loop around a lake? No problem. Want to spend the day climbing an obscene amount of vertical? They have you covered. The Methow Trails organization runs clinics and camps all season if you want to improve your kick-and-glide form or maybe try your hand at biathlon. Day passes start at $32.

Recent findings suggest that loup Loup Ski Bowl is a lift-served mountain with 300 acres that’s operated by a nonprofit. There are 10 runs with 1,240 vertical feet of drop. You’ll find beginner trails and expert trails that drop from the top of the mountain and a very welcoming uphill ski culture if you want to earn your turns. And you’ve got to check out the Ski Bowl’s luge course, where you guide an old-school sled down groomed trails. Adult lift tickets start at $80.

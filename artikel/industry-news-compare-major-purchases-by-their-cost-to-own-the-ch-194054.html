Industry observers note that before you buy a washer, a printer, a phone, or anything you’ll use for years, add up each model’s cost over those years. Count the price, the energy or supplies it uses, repairs, and any interest, minus what it’ll sell for used. Then buy the model with the lower total, even when its price tag is higher.

Recent findings suggest that printers show how far apart the two totals can get. Take a $100 cartridge inkjet and a $240 ink-tank printer you refill from bottles, both from the same maker at full price.

According to recent reports, A $37 high-capacity black cartridge for the inkjet prints about 380 pages of text, about 10 cents a page. An $18 bottle of black ink for the ink-tank model prints about 4,500, under half a cent a page, and the bottles in its box print thousands of pages before you buy any.

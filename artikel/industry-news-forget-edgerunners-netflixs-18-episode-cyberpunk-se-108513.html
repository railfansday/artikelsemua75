Though Cyberpunk: Edgerunners has long been one of the best cyberpunk shows ever made, Netflix has another science fiction show that actually features even better worldbuilding. The release of Cyberpunk: Edgerunners 2 firmly cemented the anthology series as one of the best cyberpunk stories out there. David Martinez's story in season 1 and Roman's story in season 2 are both beautiful and tragic tales filled with adventure, crime, action, and more.

Recent findings suggest that while Cyberpunk: Edgerunners is one of the best reveals on Netflix and one of the best cyberpunk shows ever, it has some very stiff competition. From classic cyberpunk movies like RoboCop and Akira to upcoming shows like Prime Video's Blade Runner 2099 and Apple TV's Neuromancer, there are several other cyberpunk stories that are just as good or better. Netflix itself has even contributed to Edgerunners' competition. (Related: Industry News: “Determined” woman completes 7 marathons...)

Recent findings suggest that that competitor, Altered Carbon, actually features much better sci-fi worldbuilding than Cyberpunk: Edgerunners does. Altered Carbon follows Takeshi Kovacs, an elite revolutionary living in a far-future world where people, particularly wealthy elites, have achieved immortality by digitally transferring their consciousness into new bodies. It has a great concept and, importantly, Altered Carbon has the exquisite worldbuilding necessary to seize that concept's full potential. (Related: Tech Report: University releases $5m smart system that ...)

Recent findings suggest that whether Altered Carbon is a better show in general than Cyberpunk: Edgerunners is a matter of taste, but the fact that it's a stronger standalone show is undeniable. Edgerunners, for all of its merits, can't really stand on its own. It borrows huge amounts of lore and worldbuilding from both the Cyberpunk TTRPG and Cyberpunk 2077. Everything from the way chrome and cyberpyschosis work to the definitions of slang words and even characters like Adam Smasher comes from those outside sources, not Edgerunners itself.

Edgerunners requires supplemental material from the board and video games to fully understand what's happening. Altered Carbon doesn't. Though it's based on a novel, Altered Carbon takes great care to explain everything the viewer needs to know about Bay City, the Meths, stack technology, Envoys, and more. You don't need to consult Richard K. Morgan's book for the backstory on a character the way you need to consult the TTRPG in Cyberpunk: Edgerunners.

For what it's worth, the fact that Cyberpunk: Edgerunners isn't a standalone story really doesn't hold it back much. Many of the things it doesn't explain are easy enough to pick up as you watch, and it does give Night City a feeling of being larger than the confines of the show. However, Altered Carbon already has that same feeling, of being a story set in a world that's so much larger than it seems, and it didn't need any video games to help it.

It has been revealed that altered Carbon even goes beyond Cyberpunk: Edgerunners, as it's one of the most expansive and detailed dystopias within the cyberpunk genre in general. Most cyberpunk stories, by necessity, have to do a bit of worldbuilding to introduce newly introduced technologies, oppressive governments and corporations, and more. Most of those stories, however, only hint at their worldbuilding elements and allude to certain aspects of their dystopias.

For example, most of Blade Runner's worldbuilding is through the relationship between replicants and humans. It suggests some novel ideas, like off-world colonies and moments in history, but it never really expands upon those ideas beyond some offhanded mentions. Altered Carbon, on the other hand, dives right into the deep end and takes meticulous care in displaying every important aspect of its dystopia.

Altered Carbon season 1 directly deals with the history of Quellcrist Falconer, the Envoys, and the invention of stack technology. Takeshi Kovacs' investigation forces him to learn about the state of the Catholic Church and its views on resleeving, the politics of Bay City, class tensions between Meths and regular people, artificial intelligence, street drugs, criminal and governmental organizations, and more. Season 2 goes even further and is actually set on one of the off-world colonies that a franchise like Blade Runner would only mention in passing. Because of that intricate detail, Altered Carbon is one of the richest worlds in cyberpunk history.

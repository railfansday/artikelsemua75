The coming week brings a number of key indicators and new research from the Realtor.com Economics team, despite the holiday schedule for Thanksgiving Day.

On Monday, new research from senior economist Hannah Jones shows that many Americans won't be traveling far to visit their parents for the holiday. That’s because a third of American adults under 35 years old still live in their parents’ home.

On Wednesday, senior economist Jiayi Xu has our third-quarter International Demand report, showing where home shoppers from different countries are looking at U.S. homes. (Related: Latest Update: How to generate text: using different de...)

Also on Wednesday, coming out early due to the Thanksgiving holiday, senior economist Jake Krimmel will analyze our weekly housing data for clues on the latest housing market trends in real time.

For economic indicators being released next week, we’ll see the S&P Cotality Case-Shiller Home Price Index on Tuesday, along with new-home sales and the Freddie Mac mortgage rate on Wednesday.

Industry observers note that the Case-Shiller Index is expected to show national home price growth holding near 2% through September, after the national index rose 2.1% year over year through August. Annual gains have climbed since the spring, but slower median sales price growth and softer contract activity this summer suggest the pickup may be running out of steam. (Related: Industry News: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

Recent findings suggest that new-home sales are likely to show another weak month in October, after falling 5.3% year over year in September. The existing-home segment saw softer pending home sales and home purchase closings last month as buyers are faced with affordability challenges due to high mortgage rates.

Despite more builders offering mortgage rate buydowns, the new-home segment is expected to be slowed down as well, as buyers’ budgets are stretched thin.

One thing I’ll be watching closely is the median fresh-home sales price, which has been lower than the median existing-home sales price for much of 2026, an intriguing result that mostly stems from where the new homes being sold are located.

The Freddie Mac average mortgage rate, which will come out on Wednesday instead of its usual Thursday, is likely to remain in the low-7% range, as the 10-Year Treasury yield remains elevated above 5% in advance of the expected rate hike by the Federal Reserve.

A key to the city is an award that cities give to people they want to honor.

“The Key to the City is presented as a symbol of civic recognition and Philadelphia’s appreciation for individuals whose achievements, service and influence have made a meaningful impact on the city and its people,” according to a release from the City of Philadelphia. In a recent development, but the key won’t open City Hall or any other public building. It doesn’t give the recipient special privileges or any authority, either. The tradition goes back to when cities had walls and gates that closed at night. Giving someone a key meant they could come and go without asking permission.

It has been revealed that in medieval Europe, leaders also exchanged keys during official visits or military surrenders. Handing over the keys sometimes meant giving up control of a city. Over time, the custom became a way to welcome or honor someone.

Recent findings suggest that today, cities give keys to athletes, public figures, volunteers, and local residents. Each city has its own rules for who receives one.

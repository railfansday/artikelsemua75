If you ever watch science videos on social media, you've probably been served a video of someone debunking the claims of a flat earther.

It starts with a clip of some belligerent dope demanding to know why, if the earth is so round, ships don't disappear over the horizon. A greenscreened science communicator then appears, rubs their forehead, and demonstrates us a video of a ship disappearing over the horizon. Checkmate.

It's good content, but if you linger too long on one of those videos, you'll get another, and then another, and pretty soon your whole feed will be debunking videos. Now you're enrolled in a never ending lesson plan which features such insights as 'an all-meat diet is not good for you,' 'the moon landing was real,' and 'hating women is not in fact based.'. The total effect of this is that the most ignorant and dishonest quacks and provocateurs get to set the level of discourse, and instead of investigating new metaphorical horizons in topics that interest you, you wind up watching people debate the existence of the actual horizon. It has been revealed that which brings me to Grand Theft Auto 6. Despite making $3.79 billion on its launch day, it's easy to stumble across confident social media claims that it's a total flop and everyone's going to refund it. According to popular X posts, GTA 6 doesn't even let you steal cars anymore, and there are hundreds of Metacritic user reviewers insisting that it's far-left propaganda rather than the mass-market entertainment product it obviously is.

Recent findings suggest that it's the videogame discourse version of flat eartherism. The millions of people playing GTA 6 do not think it is a "cuck simulator" because you can buy a woman flowers, but it's hard not to react to the few dorks making that claim, even though focusing on debunking them severely limits the depth of the conversations we can then have about the game. Good luck discussing your feelings if you dislike GTA 6 for normal reasons!

Sources indicate that over the past few days, I've entertained sparks of hope that GTA 6 could be the flashpoint that brings us out of this era of videogame discourse. The millions of people playing it and not reacting like buffoons are surely irrefutable evidence that culture war dunces don't represent the masses.

But then again, we're still pointing at videos of ships going over the horizon to prove that the earth isn't flat.

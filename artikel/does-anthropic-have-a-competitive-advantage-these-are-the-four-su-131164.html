Anthropic (ANT -8.50%) just made stock market history with its IPO.

In a recent development, at $91.6 billion, the company raised more cash in its debut than any company before it. That would seem to be a bullish sign for the stock, but after an initial pop, Anthropic closed Friday trading just 7% above its IPO price, and at one point the stock was trading just five cents above the $83 listing price. Industry observers note that the company had the misfortune of going public while the broader AI sector is crashing on fears surrounding Nvidia. Anthropic also has plenty of skeptics, who have called out risks like the $900 billion it has in purchase commitments, falling token prices, competition from cheaper models, and concerns about circular financing in the industry involving Nvidia. Those risks are all valid, but the company has also reported revenue growth of 1,200% year-over-year through the first three quarters of the year, and it's already reached a revenue run rate of $68 billion. No company this young has ever been this big. Over the longer term, Anthropic sees its technology playing a role in everything from curing diseases to designing chips to making robots and beyond. In other words, it's a high-risk, high-reward stock.

Anthropic is in a fresh, rapidly evolving industry, and one of the most pressing questions for investors at this point is whether it has a sustainable competitive advantage.

It has been revealed that investors typically look for one of four types of competitive advantage. Those include:.

Industry observers note that anthropic clearly isn't the low-cost provider. It's a frontier AI lab running on state-of-the-art chips from Nvidia and others. Distillation models like DeepSeek's R1 are an example of low-cost providers in generative AI.

It has been revealed that anthropic's brand is getting stronger and becoming more of a competitive advantage. Claude, its core intelligence platform and chatbot, now rivals OpenAI's ChatGPT in recognition, and its other products like Claude Cowork and Claude Code have become well-known as well. Anthropic's brand helps attract new consumers and keep existing ones. If Anthropic's brand is recognized as the best AI, that gives it a competitive advantage.

Recent findings suggest that at an enterprise level, switching costs do apply to a product like Claude, as they do to the rest of the software industry. A business that's paid for hundreds or even thousands of Claude seats is going to face friction if it wants to switch to another provider. At the individual consumer level, switching costs are minimal.

Network effects do not really apply to AI, as it isn't used to connect with other consumers.

Of these four, brand seems like Anthropic's best opportunity to build a competitive advantage, much like Apple's in smartphones and consumer electronics.

In a recent development, technology on its own isn't generally considered a source of sustainable competitive advantage, especially in a rapidly evolving field like AI. Anthropic took the lead from OpenAI in part by introducing more capable models, but the race between the two companies continues, and Anthropic is not guaranteed to maintain its lead.

Technology can help build a competitive advantage when it's combined with patents or trade secrets, regulatory barriers to entry, or a strong brand, and it's clearly a major reason for Anthropic's meteoric growth.

Being the technology leader in an industry is a competitive advantage, but it's often not seen as sustainable.

Anthropic sees its competitive advantage stemming from the flywheel below, which highlights how its usage improves its models, similar to Google Search, and also drives more investment, which makes its models better.

What Anthropic describes here is more about how its business model creates a flywheel that makes it better, rather than a true competitive advantage.

For a market leader, however, this kind of flywheel can drive a competitive advantage, as it has for Google Search, which learns and improves from the way consumers use the product.

According to recent reports, I don't think it's correct to say that Anthropic doesn't have a competitive advantage, even though none of the four main ones strongly apply. I think the combination of its brand, technology, and the improvement flywheels constitutes an emerging competitive advantage that will be tested over the coming months and years.

If the company can continue to gain market share, that should be interpreted as a sign that its competitive advantage is getting stronger, which makes the stock much more likely to be a winner.

We'll learn more over the coming months as Anthropic's first earnings report comes out, and the AI boom responds to its biggest test yet in the stock market, but if Anthropic can build a sustainable competitive advantage, it could become the most valuable tech giant in the world.

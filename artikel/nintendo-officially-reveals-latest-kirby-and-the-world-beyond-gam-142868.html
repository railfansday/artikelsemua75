📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Kirby fans have had plenty on their plate lately, and it seems Nintendo is going to keep the good times rolling. Last year brought a latest entry in the Kirby Air Riders series, along with a beefy expansion to Kirby and the Forgotten Land for the Switch 2 titled Star-Crossed World. Next up is Kirby and the World Beyond, a new open-ended 3D entry in the series that looks to pick up where Forgotten Land left off. The game is slated for early 2027.

The latest matter of Nintendo’s catalog highlights many of the game’s upcoming features. While many of these details were revealed previously or hinted at in the game’s reveal trailer, the article offers more specifics.

Recent findings suggest that as translated by Genki_JPN on X, the fresh game will let Kirby travel in an open-world environment using abilities copied from enemies. For instance, you can ride the blade from the classic Cutter power-up to quickly cross terrain. Kirby can also use the Bomb ability to make big vertical leaps and the Parasol to glide through the air. The article emphasizes that the game will have an expansive setting, suggesting it will be fully open-world and let players explore at their own pace. (Related: Latest Update: How to train a newly introduced language...)

A “mysterious girl” who was previously kidnapped by King Dedede will play an important role in the story. She’s purple with white hair and, in a rare move for this series, is humanoid rather than shaped like an orb. The article once again draws attention to how, after Kirby sends DeeDee flying, he leaves a “hole in the sky,” suggesting that Kirby is trapped in some sort of The Truman Show-like enclosure. (Related: Understanding BigBird's Block Sparse Attention...)

Recent findings suggest that “What awaits Kirby beyond the other side of the massive wall he destroyed with his newfound powers?” the article asks. This imagery invites many questions: Was Kirby abducted and transferred into this enclosure at some point, or has he always lived in this “fake” world? How does this tie in with the post-apocalyptic reveals from Kirby and the Forgotten Land, if at all?

Recent findings suggest that despite its cutesy aesthetic, the Kirby series has always been willing to go to unexpected places. By the end of a given adventure, this pink orb is usually battling interdimensional beings beyond human understanding with names like "Nightmare" and "Dark Nebula." World Beyond seems to be getting existential in a different way, taking things in a fourth-wall-breaking direction. We'll see how this all pans out next year when the game releases in Spring 2027.

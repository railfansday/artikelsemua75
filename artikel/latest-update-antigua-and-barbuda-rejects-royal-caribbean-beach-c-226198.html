The government of Antigua and Barbuda rejected a project for a newly introduced beach club from Royal Caribbean, according to a report by The Caribbean National Weekly.

The publication noted that the cruise line was planning to invest millions of dollars to build the new facility at Fort James. (Related: Find Orphan Pages and Buried Content: Build an Internal...)

Prime Minister Gaston Browne was quoted as saying that the project was a no-go due to the organization’s request for exclusive access to the beach.

On a radio show, he noted that the government was unwilling to restrict public access to the coastline in exchange for the investment.

Sources indicate that “They want exclusivity. And we mentioned no, because our people must have access to the beach,” Browne said.

Further details of the project, including the nature of the restrictions Royal Caribbean had requested, were not revealed by the prime minister.

Fort James is described by The Caribbean National Weekly as a popular beach near the country’s capital city of St. John’s.

In a recent development, royal Caribbean first unveiled plans for a beach club at Fort James in 2019, when it signed a memorandum of understanding with the government. The company is currently building beach clubs in Mexico and the South Pacific. (Related: Show, Don't Certify: How to Prepare for Your First Data...)

The manufacturer opened its first beach club, the Royal Beach Club Paradise Island in the Bahamas, in December 2025, followed by the Royal Beach Club Santorini in Greece in April.

Royal Caribbean faced concerns earlier this year with a similar project at Costa Maya, in Mexico’s Quintana Roo state.

As reported by Cruise Industry News at the time, Mexican authorities rejected the company’s initiative to build a private destination near Mahahual.

When explaining the decision, Mexican President Claudia Sheinbaum said that the project could be prejudicial to the region’s coastal ecosystems and the Mesoamerican Reef.

The cruise line planned to invest in building the private destination, which was to be called Perfect Day Mexico.

A petition asking the government to stop the project later reached over 5 million signatures on the Change.org platform.

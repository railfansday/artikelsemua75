Percy Jackson & the Olympians is a series of novels by American author Rick Riordan which was a massive success. The story is about Percy, a young boy who discovers he is a demigod, i.e. that his father was the Greek god Poseidon. He is then whisked away to a secluded camp in the wilderness, where other demigods train and learn to fight the ravenous monsters from Greek mythology that prowl the world, unseen by ordinary human eyes.

The series is perhaps most famous for its stellar Disney+ TV adaptation, as well as its disastrous early 2010s movies starring Logan Lerman. If you're a massive fan of the book series and how they seamlessly blend 21st century life with mythology, there are plenty of other books out there that you might also wish to check out.

It has been revealed that tristan Strong Punches a Hole in the Sky is, admittedly, a bit of a mouthful. Lengthy title aside, this novel by Kwame Mbalia was actually presented and approved by Rick Riordan himself, basically linking the book with the legacy of Percy Jackson. The "Rick Riordan Presents" branding basically focuses on similar stories that feature mythological elements from various cultures that intersect with real life, and this tale is no exception to that rule. Sources indicate that instead of being about Egyptian, Greco-Roman, or Norse mythology, which are arguably the most popular, this one features West African deities and folklore. This is combined with Black American folktales as well, creating a fascinating blended universe. The main character, the eponymous Tristan Strong, is actually incredibly likable. Plus, the story deserves points for bringing a mythology to life that rarely gets the chance to. Representing a rich, yet overlooked culture, combined with tween-friendly plotlines and lovable characters make this novel a real diamond in the rough. Caytlyn Brooke's Among the Hunted is the first novel in the Skyglass series, which is all about Greek mythology. It doesn't serve as a retelling of an existing narrative established in the mythos. Rather, it crafts a fresh story that is seamlessly integrated into the existing mythology, so that nothing feels out of place. As of yet, no other Skyglass books have been published, but it is implied that there are more on the way.

According to recent reports, among the Hunted centers on Kaitani, a nymph warrior who serves Olean. She has fallen in love with Hermes, the messenger of the gods, and is shouldering an unbearable guilt following the death of her closest friend. It's a horrible existence, made even worse by the fact that Zeus begins actively hunting Kaitani's sister. This causes her to embark on a quest for revenge, finally releasing the emotions she's been bottling up for 100 years. Now, she has just one goal: kill an immortal. This book is a bit more mature than typical YA novels, but it's still appropriate for teens and adults alike. If you're in this age group, and you love Percy Jackson and need some more Greek mythology, this is definitely something you should check out.

It might sound like a broken record to recommend this one, but Harry Potter and the Philosopher's Stone is still a great book for Percy Jackson fans. This is mainly due to the fact that Harry Potter basically revolutionized the whole YA genre, creating a market boom of similar stories, of which Percy Jackson was part of. However, the similarities go beyond surface-level trends, and go right down to the bones of each story.

Each series features a reluctant hero for a protagonist; a secluded, specialized academy for magical individuals; quippy dialogue; and various monsters from mythology. Both series have their fair share of adventure and comedy in equal measure. While Harry Potter doesn't feature any gods or deities from mythology, it has enough in common with Percy Jackson that it is often the next thing readers move on to when they've finished with Percy's story. In fact, many Potter fans also move straight on to Percy Jackson once they're finished.

Industry observers note that the Serpent's Secret is the first in a series called Kiranmala and the Kingdom Beyond, a YA trilogy based on Bengali folklore. The eponymous protagonist, Kiranmala, is an ordinary 12-year-old living in Upcoming Jersey, who has been repeatedly told by her parents that she comes from a place outside this Earth, and that she is secretly an Indian princess. Though she never believes the stories, it turns out they were right, as she finds herself confronted by a horrifying rakkhosh demon, which abducts her parents. This is made more convoluted when two princes arrive and whisk her away to an alternate dimension.

Rather than being about Hinduism as a whole, the novel draws inspiration from lesser-talked about Indian traditions, imbuing its narrative with winged horses, talking birds, and a vengeful Serpent King. It's one of the most thematically interesting worlds told in a YA novel, and it fits in perfectly with the massive YA boom that began in the late '90s and peaked in the late 2000s/early 2010s. Fans of Percy Jackson will absolutely love this one, especially since it features ancient traditions and stories blending with aspects of real life in the 21st century.

In a recent development, wolves is Book One of I Bring the Fire by C. Gockel. The book is primarily about Loki, the Norse god of trickery, and opens up with Loki waking up in a prison cell. This time, he has no idea what he's actually done to land himself there, but he does know one thing: his dear father, Odin, and brother, Thor, are up to something. Loki isn't sure what that something is, but he is sure that he doesn't like it one bit. Meanwhile, in a sleepy American Midwest town, a girl is on her way to her grandmother's house when her fate becomes intertwined with Loki's.

Recent findings suggest that this hidden gem is perfectly suited for fans of Percy Jackson. After all, it has gods from a dead religion interacting with ordinary people and existing in the real world. The series also features the signature sass and chaos that Percy Jackson became famous for, alongside a cast of memorable characters and a grand adventure. It might not be the best book ever written, but it's something that fans of Percy Jackson will absolutely want to check out.

Sources indicate that the Song of Achilles is a stand-alone novel that became a major hit with audiences. Written by Madeline Miller, this novel is a retelling of the Trojan War through the eyes of one of its most famed warriors, Achilles. Alongside Achilles is his lifelong friend, Patroclus, both of whom are trained by Chiron the centaur in the arts of warfare. When Helen of Sparta is kidnapped and the Greeks lay siege to Troy, Achilles and Patroclus answer the call. However, their relationship blossoms into something beyond friendship.

Indeed, the two do end up forming a romantic relationship, which was actually fairly commonplace in Ancient Greece. This book has a little bit of everything: there's romance, war, action, and adventure in equal measure, providing a fresh spin on an age-old story. The Song of Achilles won quite a few awards, including the Orange Prize for Fiction, one of the UK's most prestigious literary awards. If you're a teen or an adult, this book is an excellent recommendation for fans of Percy Jackson and the Olympians.

The Odyssey is the quintessential Greek myth, initially penned by Homer nearly 3,000 years ago. Rather than a novel, the book is formatted as an epic poem, which was a common storytelling medium at the time. While it wasn't technically considered fantasy when it first came out, it certainly fits the bill in hindsight. The story became so popular, even for the time, that it eventually became part of the official Greek canon, meaning it is an official part of Greek mythology.

Since the poem is one of the most famous things ever written, as well as one of the oldest surviving works of fiction, pretty much everybody knows the story by now. It's about a warrior named Odysseus who is returning home to Greece after spending years fighting in the Trojan War. This journey home is no easy feat, though, as Odysseus and his crew encounter numerous beasts and beings as they sail across the Greek islands. This is such an ideal pick for Percy Jackson fans because it's one of the main works that inspired it all in the first place. Fans will definitely recognize some of their favorite featured creatures from Riordan's series in the Odyssey, and will come to understand the origins of Greek epics.

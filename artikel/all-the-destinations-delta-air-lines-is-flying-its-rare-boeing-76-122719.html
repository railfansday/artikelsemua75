Right now, only United Airlines and Delta Air Lines operate the Boeing 767-400ER as a scheduled passenger aircraft, although a VIP-configured airframe also operates for the Bahrain Royal Flight (Bahrain Air Force). While the -400ER is now a previous-generation aircraft with relatively high fuel consumption, it remains competitive within the Delta and United ecosystems for several reasons.

These include being paid off (low capital and lease expenses); lower maintenance, tooling, and training expenses due to commonality with broader 757 and 767 fleets; and being able to fill very specific niches that no other aircraft on the market offers. Its closest modern equivalents are the 787-8 Dreamliner, the Airbus A330-800neo, and A330-900neo. That noted, the A330-800neo almost doesn't count, as it's so unpopular that almost no airline is seriously considering it anymore. Here is what to know about where Delta is flying its 767-400ERs, according to data from Cirium, an aviation analytics tech giant. Recent findings suggest that delta is one of the world's largest airlines by several measures, with a mainline fleet of 1,009 aircraft, according to Planespotters.net. It is also one of the primary remaining operators of the now-aging 767 family of passenger aircraft, with 57 examples in inventory. This is made up of 36 767-300ERs, whose average fleet age is now 29.7 years, and 21 767-400ERs with an average age of 26 years.

In a recent development, these were orders Delta placed itself. Delta inherited many aircraft from Northwest, but Northwest passed on the 767, citing its DC-10 fleet and cargo container compatibility—and instead chose A330-200s and A330-300s alongside its 747s and 757s. The 767-400ER was one of the least successful airliner variants Boeing has developed in modern times. (Related: Industry News: Scaling up BERT-like model Inference on ...)

Boeing sold 1,451 examples of its 767 family, of which only 59 were the 767-400ER. It is unclear if Boeing ever recovered its expenses and broke even on the cost of developing the stretch. Regardless, the variant is now operated almost exclusively by Delta and United, for whom the aircraft fills a particular niche, making it difficult to replace. Both airlines apparently plan to operate the type for as long as is feasible.

Sources indicate that according to Cirium data, Delta is operating 12,308 flights with its fleet of 767-400ERs this year, including return flights. They carry a total of 2.9 million seats and generate about 11 billion available seat miles (ASMs). The average route distance is 3,245 nautical miles.

The vast majority of these flights are operated from two Delta hubs: Hartsfield–Jackson Atlanta International Airport (ATL) and John F. Kennedy International Airport (JFK). ATL has 2,934 outbound flights and 700,000 outbound seats, and JFK has 3,183 outbound flights and 758,000 outbound seats. There are some limited short-haul routes under 500 nautical miles from Atlanta, but not from JFK.

The 767-400ER's regular routes are within the 2,000 to 4,300 nautical mile range, with the aircraft sometimes used to plug gaps on short routes or to run short routes between its longer-haul schedules. Overall, it is useful to think of the aircraft as a transatlantic workhorse as well as a lesser transcontinental workhorse. Only three pairings have more than 1,000 annual flights, and only one has more than 500 flights.

All of Delta's shortest 767-400ER routes under 1,000 nautical miles are from its Atlanta hub, ignoring the ATL-JFK pairing. The shortest route is Atlanta to Orlando at just 350 nautical miles, with a total of 62 flights in both directions (outbound and inbound) for the year. Just three nautical miles longer is the Atlanta to Tampa pairing, with 20 flights covering 353 nautical miles.

Delta conducted eight flights between Atlanta and Detroit (516 nautical miles) and about 206 flights between Atlanta and Miami (517 nautical miles), meaning Florida dominates the shortest-route destinations. After Miami, the next shortest route is ATL to JFK, with 175 flights at 660 nautical miles. The only other destinations under 1,000 nautical miles are from Atlanta to Minneapolis-St. Paul at 787 nautical miles, although this route only sees four flights.

In the 1,000-1,999 nautical mile category, from and to Atlanta, there are 136 flights with Phoenix (1,379 nautical miles), 20 flights with Las Vegas (1,518 nautical miles), four flights with San Diego (1,643 nautical miles), 145 flights with Los Angeles (1,691 nautical miles), and 12 flights with San Francisco (1,859 nautical miles). Besides Atlanta, there are two flights between Fresh York-JFK and Phoenix (1,871 nautical miles) and a single flight from Las Vegas to New York (1,953 nautical miles).

Moving above 2,000 nautical miles is the first very busy route, the transcontinental route between Latest York-JFK and Los Angeles with 1,617 flights at 2,151 nautical miles. JFK's 2,247-nautical-mile route to San Francisco sees 96 flights. The 767-400ER is one of the workhorses for Delta's transatlantic routes. It has the range to connect the East Coast with many of Western Europe's top destinations.

It has been revealed that next, transatlantic routes start from JFK. There are 173 flights (including return) to Dublin (2,763 nautical miles), 80 to Edinburgh (2,829 nautical miles), 137 to Lisbon (2,926 nautical miles), and 423 to London Heathrow (3,000 nautical miles). Going over 3,000 nautical miles, JFK's transatlantic pairings include Madrid (436 flights), Paris (1,117 flights), Barcelona (200 flights), and Zurich (483 flights).

These destinations are between 3,120 and 3,416 nautical miles. Finally, Atlanta sees its first transatlantic route to Dublin with 78 total flights. The other flight pairings under 3,500 nautical miles include New York and Nice (430 flights), New York and Milan Malpensa (387 flights), and a single flight from Atlanta to Edinburgh. ​​​​​​​.

Moving above 3,500 nautical miles, the first and only major route not to include Latest York or Atlanta is the 3,611-nautical-mile pairing between Cincinnati and Paris (250 flights). This is the same distance as Delta's New York to Venice pairing (451 flights). The next ten routes by distance (3,767 nautical miles to 4,132 nautical miles) are all from Delta's Atlanta hub.

In order of distance, these are London Heathrow (1,527 flights), Madrid (288 flights), Marrakech (127 flights), Paris (426 flights), Brussels (187 flights), and Barcelona (198 flights). After Barcelona, the routes are greater than 4,000 nautical miles and include Zurich (292 flights), Rio de Janeiro (315 flights), Nice (306 flights), and Milan (387 flights).

It has been revealed that the Atlanta-Rio de Janeiro pairing is the first non-European international destination by ascending distance. Delta also operates 18 flights on its Upcoming York-Rio de Janeiro pairing, which is only marginally longer than the Atlanta pairing. Next, there are 652 flights between Atlanta and Munich and 197 flights between Atlanta and Venice.

The top five longest Delta 767-400ER routes are all over 4,250 nautical miles, but the frequencies are low. The New York-Athens pairing sees 135 flights over 4,294 nautical miles. Next is Atlanta to Buenos Aires with 95 flights. Delta typically assigns A330s and A350-900s on its Atlanta to Buenos Aires route. After Atlanta to Buenos Aires, there are no more regular flights.

In a recent development, there are two flights between Las Vegas and London Heathrow, two flights between Newly introduced York and Buenos Aires, and a single flight from Paris to Las Vegas. The latter is the longest flight at 4,730 nautical miles. Delta's A330-900neos are the primary aircraft serving the Buenos Aires-New York route. It seems that with these routes (as well as very short routes), the 767-400ER is occasionally used to fill in when the aircraft typically assigned to the route are lacking.

According to recent reports, according to Boeing, the 767-400ER has a basic range of 3,580 nautical miles and a 5,190 nautical miles range at maximum optional weight, with a 3% fuel factor. Judging by how Boeing is actually flying the aircraft, it is best suited for ranges from 2,000 nautical miles up to around 4,300 nautical miles. Most of Delta's 767-400ER's highest-frequency routes are transatlantic, with the notable exception of the New York-Los Angeles pairing and, to a much lesser extent, the New York-San Francisco route.

In a recent development, santiago spreads across the valley floor, and above its eastern edge, where the streets of Peñalolén give way to the foothills of the Andes, a pale form rises from a carved terrace: nine curved wings leaning toward one another. At dawn the building takes on the cold blue of the cordillera behind it. By midday it is a tent of white light, by late afternoon it turns gold, and after dark the sequence reverses: the temple becomes a lantern.

According to recent reports, in October 2016, the Bahá'í Temple of South America opened to the public. In October 2026, the community that built it marked a decade with gatherings, talks, and tours across its grounds. Designed by Canada-based Hariri Pontarini Architects under the direction of Siamak Hariri, the temple has welcomed more than three million visitors in ten years.

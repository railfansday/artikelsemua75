Latest Update: Star Wars’ 2-Part Sci-Fi War Thriller Has One of the Best Seasons in TV History

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In 2022, Disney was having a pretty strange year with its franchises. There were some major wins, such as the hugely financially successful release of Doctor Strange in the Multiverse of Madness and Turning Red becoming a streaming hit. Its films also earned $4.9 billion at the box office that year.

According to recent reports, however, some franchises fared much worse with their fans than others. After the poor reception of The Book of Boba Fett in February and the controversy surrounding Obi-Wan Kenobi in May, Star Wars fans weren't particularly hopeful that Andor, Tony Gilroy’s gritty spy series, would restore the franchise to its former greatness.

In a recent development, those fans were wrong. Gilroy didn’t just renew fans' hope that Star Wars could be more than a franchise for children; he delivered a culturally relevant story about the sacrifices people make — love, comfort, and friendship — in the fight against tyranny, all within a single season. The series also expanded the background of a beloved character, Cassian Andor, so that even if a second season had never arrived, that story would have worked perfectly as a sequel to Star Wars' most beloved films. Recent findings suggest that though Andor’s second season is more critically acclaimed — and has a handful of awards under its belt — its 12-episode first season is immaculate at showcasing how the Empire’s oppressive grip destroys the hope of those living under its regime. It also kickstarts Cassian Andor’s ascent into the hardcore rebel fans know from Rogue One.

The series opens with Cassian (Diego Luna) searching for his sister on Morlana One, where he runs into trouble and kills two Pre-Mor security officers. On the run, he returns home to the planet Ferrix and tries to sell an Imperial Starpath unit to fund his escape, but he gets caught up in the machinations of the rebel organization known as the Axis Network, led by Luthen Rael (Stellan Skarsgård).

A survivalist first and foremost, Cassian is staunchly opposed to getting caught up in the rebellion against the Empire — right up until he can no longer look away from the Imperial machine and the horrors it inflicts. (Related: Breaking: The Age of Machine Learning As Code Has Arriv...)

Recent findings suggest that star Wars has always been a story about a group of revolutionaries fighting a tyrannical empire, but most of the audience’s time has been spent following massive-shot heroes and chosen ones like Han Solo, Leia Organa, and Luke Skywalker. There were always Jedi, and the good guys rarely, if ever, lost.

In a recent development, that isn’t the case for Andor, which, like its predecessor Rogue One, follows the little guys — the people who aren’t showered with public praise and given medals as rewards. Even in the brief time we spend with minor characters like the insurgents on Aldhani and the prisoners of Narkina-5, each is given complexity and a compelling reason to fight the Empire. No one is driven by the Force or has a secret, epic backstory. They're on the ground fighting for what they believe in, or in most cases, because they have no other choice.

It helps that Andor is possibly the most beautiful Star Wars has ever looked on television. From Aldhani’s cultural event in the Scottish Highlands — one of Star Wars’ most heart-stopping moments — to Ferrix, built brick by brick, Gilroy’s production values are unmatched. It doesn’t just look like a Star Wars show’s backdrop. Each planet feels lived-in, with communities that feel unique in both appearance and behavior.

Andor season 1 is Star Wars at its very best: an epic, winding tale of Cassian’s journey from thief and scoundrel to a man who would inevitably sacrifice himself to give the Rebellion those all-important Death Star plans. The people he meets along the way, from Mon Mothma to Luthen Rael, are equally brilliant at showing how far people will go to bloody the nose of the Imperial machine.

Industry observers note that andor’s second season is every bit as compelling as the first, with each trio of episodes in the 12-episode season spanning a year. Still, Gilroy’s original five-season blueprint being cut to two is evident in the leaps in character development between episodes, leaving some choices unearned and, frankly, at odds with what audiences see in Rogue One. It is still leagues above many Star Wars shows — though Rebels will always remain my favorite — but compared to how tightly contained season 1 is in telling Cassian Andor's story, season 2 just doesn’t compare to the masterpiece that is the debut season.

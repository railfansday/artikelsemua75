American Blocking Domestic Saver Awards Close To Departure (Within Six Days)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In May 2026, we saw American introduce a major fresh restriction for award travel, which had the biggest implications for those redeeming on American through partner programs, like Alaska Atmos Rewards or British Airways Club (in other words, anyone redeeming through a program other than American AAdvantage).

With this, American started to restrict its own saver level award availability close to departure. Initially, all domestic nonstop saver awards within six days of departure were pulled. In recent days, it looks like American has expanded this to all domestic connecting itineraries as well, so I’d like to cover those details (thanks to azbobcat for flagging this).

Sources indicate that historically, American has been by far the best of the “big three” US carriers when it comes to making award seats on its own flights available, especially on domestic and short haul international flights. American actually releases a good amount of saver award seats across cabins, and it’s one of the things that makes AAdvantage better than the competition. According to recent reports, however, in May 2026, we saw American introduce a major change to how it releases award space to partner frequent flyer programs. Long story short, American started blocking all nonstop, domestic saver award availability, for flights within six days (exactly 144 hours) of departure. (Related: IShowSpeed breaks down in tears after finishing GTA 6 i...)

As soon as a flight gets to the point where it’s 144 hours until departure, you’ll see that the airline “turns off” its saver fare classes — I’m referring specifically to the “U” (saver business class award) and “T” (saver economy award) fare classes, which show up when searching on ExpertFlyer, for example.

In a recent development, initially, this applied exclusively to nonstop, domestic award tickets, as connecting itineraries and all international itineraries (including short haul international ones) weren’t impacted by this. However, this seems to have now expanded to domestic connecting itineraries as well.

Industry observers note that so any wholly domestic itinerary exclusively on American is no longer bookable at the saver level within six days of departure. International itineraries continue to be bookable within six days of departure, whether traveling nonstop or with a connection.

What would cause American to suddenly “shut off” saver awards for domestic routes within days of departure?

Recent findings suggest that what’s interesting here is that the implications for AAdvantage members are limited. Yes, American has shut off awards in the saver fare classes, but that’s not to say that American isn’t making reasonably priced awards available within days of departure. American has long used some kind of dynamic award pricing concept for its own flights, regardless of whether or not there’s saver availability.

It’s just that any awards in the official saver fare buckets aren’t available, and therefore aren’t bookable with points belonging to partner programs.

So it sure seems to me that the intent here is to block award availability for those booking through partner programs, like Alaska Atmos Rewards or British Airways Club. It goes without saying that I absolutely hate this restriction, as someone who “funds” a vast majority of my domestic American travel using Atmos Rewards points. At the same time, I also have to say that I can’t blame American for this change, as the airline has just made it too easy.

Sources indicate that I’ve been booking basically all my travel directly into American first class using Atmos Rewards points — I just set ExpertFlyer availability alerts for “U” class, it opens up a vast majority of the time, and then I book. It’s not surprising that American finally got a bit savvier.

American is now blocking all saver awards within exactly six days of departure for all domestic itineraries. This means within that timeframe, there’s no “U” or “T” availability, which corresponds to the saver business and economy award fare buckets, respectively. Initially this only applied to nonstop flights, while now it also applies to connecting itineraries.

Industry observers note that for American AAdvantage members, the implications are limited, since American can still make reasonably priced awards available, just not formally in those fare buckets. The practical implications here are that partner programs just don’t have access to these award seats close to departure anymore.

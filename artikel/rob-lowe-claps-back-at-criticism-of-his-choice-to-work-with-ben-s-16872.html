Rob Lowe is standing by his decision to work with Ben Shapiro — despite attracting some backlash for the call.

Speaking on the “Second Thought” podcast this week, Lowe, 62, defended starring in Confession, a psychological historical drama written by Shapiro, 42, who is known for his right-wing political views.

According to recent reports, “It’s a really, really, really interesting assessment of not only where our world is, but where Hollywood is as well,” Lowe said on the podcast. “So, I read the script. It was written by Ben Shapiro. I called Ben and said, ‘This is fantastic.’”. (Related: See where votes are being counted in Senate and House m...)

It has been revealed that lowe mentioned Shapiro initially asked whether he knew a leading man in his 50s who wouldn’t be intimidated by the idea of working with him. Lowe was quick to volunteer himself.

“And I go, ‘Yeah, me,’” Lowe recalled telling Shapiro, adding that he was comfortable taking on the role.

Industry observers note that lowe continued, “And he goes, ‘You’re good with it?’ I mentioned, ‘I’ve worked with the most left-wing writers in the world. Why wouldn’t I also work with the most right-wing writers in the world?’”.

Confession, which is directed by Václav Marhoul, follows the interrogation of a prisoner in a Stalin-era Russian jail. The film also stars Nick Nolte, James Franco and Emile Hirsch.

Sources indicate that the film premiered at the Warsaw Film Festival in October, then received a one-night-only theatrical release on November 11. Confession dropped on The Daily Wire’s streaming platform the same day.

During his podcast appearance, Lowe also expressed frustration that the movie failed to secure a wider festival run.

It has been revealed that “And so, we make the movie, and the movie is what it is. It’s a movie,” he stated. “Look, it is challenging. It is a tough watch, but I see the movies that are celebrated out there. I see the movies that are picked — we got turned down by every single film festival, summarily frog marched out of any conversation.”.

Lowe went on to suggest that political considerations were behind the film’s rejection, saying, “I see what they’re picking up, and it’s all political, right?”.

It has been revealed that he added, “The thought would be we’d play in a film festival, we would get some buzz — never happened. And then you know, Netflix would pick it up or this one would pick. Crickets. Absolute crickets.”.

Recent findings suggest that on Nov. 19, much-anticipated AI giant Anthropic (ANT -2.34%) finally became available to regular retail traders through the largest initial public offering (IPO) in history. The listing valued shares at $83 each. That valuation gave Anthropic an eye-popping market cap of just under $1.5 trillion on a fully diluted basis. By the close of the first day of trading, its valuation reached $1.76 trillion, putting it in the Top-10 among the world's most valuable companies (by the weekend, the valuation had fallen to $1.65 trillion on a fully diluted basis). Without full dilution, the valuation is closer to $1.2 trillion, which still makes it a very valuable company.

According to recent reports, clearly, huge expectations are already baked into Anthropic's current valuation. That leaves potential new investors wondering how much room is left for continued stock price growth over the long term. Let's dig into the pros and cons of the stock to decide if it is a compelling buy.

NASDAQ · As of 11:49 AM ET 11/23/26.

Recent findings suggest that in late 2022, OpenAI released its groundbreaking large language model (LLM) ChatGPT, which wowed the market with its ability to answer user queries with humanlike speech patterns. Anthropic had been founded a year earlier with a very similar business model. But the company distinguished itself by an apparent emphasis on safety, coupled with a major push into the business-to-business side of the opportunity. (Related: Latest Update: How to train a newly introduced language...)

Industry observers note that anthropic's flagship LLM, Claude, has increasingly become the go-to solution for enterprise clients who want to use AI models to automate their internal workflows, code, and analyze large volumes of data. A study from Menlo Ventures has found that the model has already captured 55% of enterprise LLM spending, putting it far ahead of OpenAI's 31%. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

Recent findings suggest that anthropic's success is showing up in its financial results, with its S-1 filing reporting $16.3 billion in revenue for the six months ended June 30. That's an eye-popping 1,300% jump from the prior-year period, and management seems extremely optimistic about the future.

According to recent reports, anthropic CEO Dario Amodei remarked he believes AI technology could continue to improve exponentially, eventually reaching a level where one data center can be compared to a "country of geniuses in a datacenter" capable of revolutionizing every human endeavor. This would include curing diseases like cancer and Alzheimer's. If things go as expected, the economic effects could be life-changing for early investors who buy shares before these things potentially happen.

Industry observers note that at this point, there are few people who still doubt the potential power of generative AI (although most of us probably aren't as bullish as Anthropic's leadership). That said, a promising technology doesn't necessarily guarantee a profitable business model. We saw this during the dot-com bubble, when many early internet companies quickly failed, even though the internet as a technology has been a lasting success.

Sources indicate that there are some early warning signs in Anthropic's S-1 filing. While revenue growth is explosive, the company generated a staggering net loss of $80.7 billion in the first half of 2026, compared to just under $13 billion in the prior-year period.

Sources indicate that the good news is that most of this was due to a one-time $72.4 billion charge related to changes in the fair value of financial instruments issued to early investors. As Anthropic's valuation has grown, so has the estimated value of these obligations, resulting in a massive accounting loss. The good news is that this is not a cash outflow. Still, it can lead to substantial shareholder dilution as convertible notes are converted into additional units of stock, increasing the number of shares outstanding.

In a recent development, anthropic is an expensive stock. With a forward price-to-earnings (P/E) multiple of around 170, it trades at a substantial premium to the S&P 500 average of 18. While this is somewhat understandable given the company's explosive growth rate, it leaves plenty of downside risk if things don't go as expected.

There are also growing fears that AI itself is a bubble that won't generate enough revenue to justify the hundreds of billions right now being spent on data center equipment. So while Anthropic definitely belongs on your investment watch list, it may make more sense to wait for the dust to settle before actually buying the stock.

Resourceful PC owner repurposes CPU watercooler with dead pump as standalone LCD hardware monitor, vibecodes new software for module

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

AIO liquid coolers are notorious for shorter lifespans due to pump failure or leaks, which usually results in having to replace the entire unit. However, one enthusiast decided to salvage theirs uniquely, at least a part of it. Sharing their DIY project on Reddit, u/BodeeHav repurposed the LCD screen from a dead NZXT Kraken AIO into a standalone desktop monitor that displays live CPU and GPU temperatures and utilization after vibecoding entirely newly introduced software.

Sources indicate that according to the post, the pump on their NZXT Kraken AIO stopped working around a month ago. Rather than discarding a perfectly usable screen, they decided to dismantle the block and remove the LCD and its main board. They used the original black outer housing and 3D printed a stand to hold the LCD in place. To keep things tidy, they stripped the connector down to just its SATA power and USB pins. The display connects directly to the motherboard, presumably using the internal 9-pin USB header, although BodeeHav states they plan to adapt it for external USB. Getting the display working properly required more than just pulling it out of the pump block. BodeeHav initially used NZXT’s CAM software; however, he kept running into complications whenever the PC entered sleep or standby. To overcome this hurdle, they turned to AI and vibe coded an entirely new software as a replacement that offers full control of the LCD with various customization options. (Related: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Faster Language M...)

Industry observers note that bodeeHav has graciously made his entire project available on GitHub, where you can access the DesktopKraken software and an STL file for the stand. According to the official GitHub page, the software is a standalone Windows application that communicates with compatible NZXT Kraken Elite LCDs over USB without requiring NZXT CAM.

The software currently only offers support for the 2023 Kraken Elite and 2024 Kraken Elite V2, which use circular LCDs with a 640x640 resolution. Apart from CPU and GPU temperatures and utilization, it can also display clock speeds, power consumption, memory usage, and VRAM information. Additionally, the software can be used for brightness control, screen orientation, sensor logging, and it even offers support for custom images, animated GIFs, thermal warning gauges, and automatic recovery after Windows sleep or a USB disconnect.

While it may cater to a very niche audience, the project is a great example of how a little ingenuity can give failed components a second life.

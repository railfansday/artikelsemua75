Last month, Tesla (TSLA -7.64%) and Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -7.69%) each formed a special committee of independent directors to consider a merger. Elon Musk, the CEO and largest individual shareholder of both companies, can't participate in the talks.

A deal would combine the top space manufacturer and automaker by market cap. For Tesla investors, the biggest change may be how the market values the company's robotaxi business. (Related: Breaking: Illinois appeals Pacold injunction blocking i...)

It has been revealed that although both companies are richly valued, SpaceX stock has the higher market cap and valuation. It's worth $2 trillion and trades at 58 times trailing sales, as of Nov. 19. Tesla has a market cap of $1.5 trillion and trades at 13 times trailing sales. (Related: Latest Update: Arkansas sues VGW and Kalshi, citing $66...)

Tesla's valuation is largely tied to humanoid robots, robotaxis, and AI services such as full self-driving (FSD). But cars are still the core business, and Tesla's operating income declined 56% year over year to $718 million in Q3 2026. At the moment, robotaxi is part of a car company, which weighs on how the market can value it.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

According to recent reports, A merger would put robotaxi inside a firm with a rapidly growing AI business, with revenue that skyrocketed 830% year over year to $7.6 billion in Q3. Investors could start valuing it as an AI business, not an add-on to a car business.

It has been revealed that robotaxi is an expensive business that requires vehicles, chips, compute resources, and connectivity. Tesla's network is also expanding promptly, with nearly 2 million cumulative unsupervised miles logged by the end of September, growing about eightfold from the previous quarter. As the network grows, costs will continue to increase.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

SpaceX is spending heavily on AI, reporting $48.1 billion in capital expenditures over the first nine months of 2026. It's guiding for another $35 billion to $40 billion in Q4. Tesla is pulling back, revising its capex guidance down to about $20 billion in 2026, from more than $25 billion. SpaceX could provide the robotaxi business with more funding, its existing AI infrastructure, and connectivity through the Starlink network, all contributing to faster expansion.

Although robotaxi is an exciting business, it's not yet making a financial impact. Musk doesn't expect it to make a noticeable difference to Tesla's finances until around Q2 2027, and that may be an optimistic viewpoint. He previously expected robotaxi service to be widespread across the U.S. by the end of 2026. Tesla offers service in nine metro areas, so it's a long way to go, showing how reality often falls well short of expectations.

If there's a merger, robotaxi will become part of a company potentially worth $3.5 trillion or more. Investors could have a harder time tracking robotaxi's progress than they do now.

Sources indicate that merger talks are in the early stages, with no proposed terms. Tesla would also need approval from a majority of all outstanding shares, not just those voting, because Musk doesn't control the company's voting power as he does with SpaceX. A merger could make robotaxi a more valuable business, but for now, what's important is that it starts producing meaningful revenue in 2027.

The two things I'd be looking for right now if I were worried about the stock market would be dividends and a low beta.

Recent findings suggest that dividend income can be crucial for padding overall returns. The Vanguard Utilities ETF yields around 2.8%, which is more than double the S&P 500 average of just 1.1%. That extra dividend income is highly valuable in boosting overall returns. For example, the Vanguard Utilities ETF has risen by just 25% over the past five years, but when including dividends, its total returns are up around 46%. And that's right in line with the S&P 500's total returns, despite utility stocks offering greater long-term stability and less volatility.

Investors can find low-volatility investments by looking at their respective beta values, which indicate how closely they move with the broader market. The Vanguard Utilities ETF has averaged a beta of just 0.36 over the past five years, suggesting it doesn't move closely with the overall market at all.

Florian Wirtz credited his upturn in form down to Liverpool's development under Andoni Iraola after he played a crucial role in Sunday's 4-0 thrashing of Manchester United.

Liverpool got the better of their fierce rivals at Anfield with an emphatic performance, which was helped by Leny Yoro being sent off early on for a last-man foul on Alexander Isak.

Dominik Szoboszlai thumped in the resulting free-kick before Wirtz superbly volleyed past Senne Lammens from close range after being teed up by the lively Rio Ngumoha. (Related: Hugging Face Reads, Feb. 2021 - Long-range Transformers...)

United, in Mauricio Pochettino's first game in charge, aimed to shut up shop as Harry Maguire replaced Bryan Mbeumo, but their resolve was eventually broken in the second half.

A blistering counter-attack led by Bradley Barcola resulted in Ngumoha sweeping home, with Wirtz turning provider for Victor Munoz to conclude the scoring in the 82nd minute.

Since the start of the 2021-22 season, Liverpool's four wins by 4+ goals against United are the most any Premier League team has achieved against another in all competitions.

Wirtz was key to their success, creating 10 chances in open play for Liverpool, the most by a player in a Premier League match since Manchester City's David Silva against West Brom in March 2015 (11), and he could have easily had more than two goal involvements to his name. The German international has been under immense scrutiny since his big-money transfer to Merseyside last summer, but he now has eight goal involvements (three goals, five assists) in all competitions in 2026-27, and Wirtz believes he is close to being back to his best.

In a recent development, "I feel very good. It's also because the team develops and gets better," Wirtz said. "Being part of this, you also get better, and I'm enjoying that.

"It was definitely notable, also the table situation. A lot of teams at the top didn't win this weekend, so it's good we can use the chance.

It has been revealed that "The red card influenced the game, of course, but we take that. We earned that because of the run of Alex [Isak], so it's very good.".

Sunday's win was the quickest Liverpool had gone 2-0 up in a Premier League match against United, going two ahead after just 12 minutes and 40 seconds.

Ngumoha, who tormented Diogo Dalot all game, did brilliantly to tee up Wirtz's acrobatic finish before getting himself on the scoresheet to cap a fine individual display.

Aged 18 years 85 days, Ngumoha became the youngest player to score and assist in a Premier League game for Liverpool since Michael Owen on Boxing Day 1997 (18y 12d v Leeds United).

Recent findings suggest that "These records are really special, and just getting goals and helping my team is the main thing: to get three points. Hopefully I can score and assist many more," Ngumoha mentioned.

"I just like exciting people, when I show everyone what I can do and get everyone off their seats. Hopefully, I can keep going and keep my head on the floor and stay grounded.".

Liverpool also condemned Pochettino to the joint-heaviest defeat by a United manager in their first game (0-4), along with a defeat by the same scoreline for Lal Hilditch in October 1926 (0-4 v Bolton Wanderers in the top flight).

UPDATED 20:20 EST / NOVEMBER 26 2026.

Enterprise software giant SAP SE is reportedly planning to lay off at least 10,000 employees, or more than 9% of its workforce.

Bloomberg today cited sources as saying that the scope of the restructuring initiative is still subject to change.

According to the publication, SAP and a labor council in its home country of Germany have negotiated an “employment guarantee” that will run through 2028. That may mean the layoffs will mainly affect employees in other markets. The report indicates that SAP’s product, engineering and sales units are expected to see the largest cuts. Executives have reportedly also asked other departments to find cost reduction opportunities.

Recent findings suggest that the reported restructuring push follows a banner quarter for SAP. The company’s sales and adjusted profit both topped analyst expectations. Its €24.11 billion cloud contract backlog, a measure of future revenue, also came in ahead of the consensus estimate. (Related: Show, Don't Certify: How to Prepare for Your First Data...)

The planned layoffs are said to be connected to SAP’s efforts to integrate more artificial intelligence features into its products. The company is best known for its ERP, or enterprise resource planning, software. Enterprises use it to automate a long list of backoffice tasks and share data between teams. SAP also sells cloud services for other use cases such as sales automation and customer support.

At a May user conference, the tech giant introduced an AI interface called Joule Work for its applications. SAP says that the tool will enable workers to centrally access its products instead of navigating multiple screens.

Sources indicate that joule Work made its debut alongside a suite of AI agents dubbed Joule Assistants. According to SAP, they will use over 200 subagents to automate repetitive business tasks. Another latest offering, Industry AI, will automate sector-specific workflows.

The report that SAP is preparing for job cuts doesn’t come as a complete surprise. Last week, Chief Financial Officer Dominik Asam told attendees of an investor event that the tech giant expects its headcount to decline. Additionally, two of the software maker’s six directors will reportedly leave in connection with its shift to a more AI-centric strategy.

Recent findings suggest that it’s unclear when the job cuts will take place. According to Bloomberg, SAP historically unveiled workforce reduction programs in January. The software maker took that route with its last major restructuring initiative, which saw it axe about 10,000 roles in 2024. The initiative cost about €3 billion.

The manufacturer said in a statement that “SAP continuously reviews its organization to ensure it remains aligned with its strategy and customer needs.”.

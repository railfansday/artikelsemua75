It's Tuesday morning, investors, and one of the hottest stocks on the market today is... Kohl's Corporation (KSS +16.96%).

Why? Shares of the department store chain surged 13.4% higher through 10:35 a.m. ET this morning after Kohl's beat analyst forecasts for (checks notes) zero profit, reporting pro forma earnings of $1.76 per share instead. Kohl's also beat on sales, reporting $3.5 billion for Q3 2026 versus forecasts of $3.4 billion.

Sources indicate that the story is not quite as simple as that, of course. The zero-profit quarter that Wall Street analysts were forecasting for Kohl's was based on pro forma, non-GAAP profit. Kohl's crushed that estimate, but it also reported $2.15 per share in earnings calculated under generally accepted accounting principles (GAAP).

Even with more than half that money coming from a tax benefit, Kohl's quarterly earnings would have been nearly $1 per share. (Related: Compound Interest Calculator: See Your Money Grow...)

NYSE · As of 11:31 AM ET 11/24/26.

And judging from Kohl's guidance, investors can expect things to get even better -- for a short while at least. Kohl's grew its same-store sales just 1.2% in Q3, and total sales only 1.3%. It also improved gross and operating profit margins, helping to earn much more on the slight increase in sales. (Related: How to Start a Side Business While Working Full-Time...)

It has been revealed that how long can that last, though? Management sees sales growth accelerating slightly in Q4, but to no better than 2%. Full-year profits will probably be about $4.40 per share, valuing Kohl's stock at about 5.5 times current-year earnings.

This soundsvery cheap. Still, Kohl's expects to get half its profit this year from "tariff refunds" and "tax benefit," with about 20% of the remainder from selling off real estate. These are all one-time items that will be difficult for Kohl's to replicate in the future. I expect Kohl's stock to look very cheap by the end of 2026 -- but when 2027 arrives, it will look a whole lot more expensive.

Sources indicate that caveat investor. Kohl's looks like a value trap to me.

Sources indicate that upstart's (UPST -7.88%) days as a hot fintech stock have long since passed. It was trading near $400 per share back in 2021, but recently was changing hands for around $17 per share, a nearly 96% price decline. (Related: Latest Update: One Year Since the “DeepSeek Moment”...)

While shares in this artificial intelligence (AI) lending technology firm have seen brief partial rebounds in recent years, I’m looking more closely at fintech names with stronger growth stories, namely SoFi Technologies (SOFI -7.76%). (Related: Industry News: “Determined” woman completes 7 marathons...)

According to recent reports, at first glance, it may seem illogical that Upstart continues to tumble. After all, following steep revenue declines in 2023 and 2024, sales and earnings have improved dramatically. In 2024, revenue and earnings per share (EPS) came in at $628.8 million and -$1.44, respectively. In 2025, revenue surged to just over $1 billion, with diluted EPS at $0.45 per share, marking a massive swing back to profitability.

For 2026, analysts' estimates call for Upstart to report $1.35 billion in sales and EPS of $1.84. Alas, there is a reason the market prices Upstart at such a low multiple to those estimates.

First, there is high uncertainty over future growth. Although the company recently reported a 34% increase in quarterly revenue to $371 million, and diluted earnings of $0.33 per share, up 43.4% from the prior year's quarter, management walked back its full-year guidance. Previously estimating  $1.4 billion in revenue and adjusted EBITDA of $294 million, Upstart's full-year 2026 outlook now calls for $1.32 billion in revenue and adjusted EBITDA of $260 million.

Recent findings suggest that second, the recent pullback in AI stocks and tech stocks has likely intensified the situation. This sell-off has spilled over into the fintech space, including shares in SoFi, a tech giant more richly priced than Upstart but with far greater long-term upside potential.

Sources indicate that like Upstart, SoFi has recently reported strong growth. Last quarter, the digital-first financial services organization reported net revenue of $1.4 billion, a 44% increase from the prior year's quarter. Adjusted EBITDA jumped 70% year over year, to $470 million. EPS came in at $0.18, a 63% increase from the $0.11 in diluted adjusted EPS reported for Q3 2025.

Better yet, SoFi's management provided bullish guidance for the coming year. In 2027, the manufacturer anticipates 27% adjusted net revenue growth and adjusted earnings of $0.90 per share, adding that it could achieve these goals without raising additional capital. With SoFi Technologies changing hands today at around $13.50 per share, the stock is trading at around 15 times next year's estimated earnings.

Longer-term, if SoFi can continue to grow at a moderate clip, steadily becoming a major "financial supermarket," shares could climb further, to past highs in the $30s per share, and beyond. Contrast that with Upstart, which, while still guiding for further growth, is revising that guidance downward. Worse yet, although both SoFi and Upstart are involved in the personal lending space, Upstart arguably has greater exposure. SoFi started as a lender but has since expanded into banking, brokerage, and other financial services, with its ability to cross-sell bolstering confidence in its growth story.

That mentioned, it's still possible for Upstart to thrive while SoFi flounders. The sell-side remains divided on SoFi, perhaps reluctant to assign it a fintech valuation, given that it will eventually be classified as a bank stock. Upstart has tanked on poor guidance, but any sort of better-than-feared results and outlook could be enough to drive a relief rally. Nevertheless, if you're looking at either stock as a possible long-term investment rather than a trade, consider SoFi the better of the two.

Sources indicate that wall Street appears to love Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -7.69%) stock. The current consensus price target is around $267, suggesting 93% upside potential.

I'm not nearly as optimistic. In fact, I think there's a compelling case that investors should forget about SpaceX stock entirely -- at least for a few years.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

I love SpaceX as a business. I'm just not so sure about the stock.

My biggest concern is simply a matter of timing. To justify its $1.9 trillion valuation, SpaceX will ultimately need to source hundreds of billions in fresh capital, successfully commercialize its Starship rocket, significantly scale both terrestrial and orbital data center infrastructure, and likely begin producing its own AI chips. (Related: Introducing Optimum: The Optimization Toolkit for Trans...)

It has been revealed that all this will take years to fully realize. Yet today's valuation largely assumes much of this will occur as promised.

Sources indicate that to be clear, I'm not necessarily a SpaceX bear. In fact, I believe the manufacturer will play a key role in the AI economy. But its growth trajectory is full of execution risk, and it wouldn't be a surprise to see the company's growth roadmap take years longer than expected.

When sizable growth is already priced in -- SpaceX right now trades above 50 times sales while reporting negative profits and cash flow -- small shifts in sentiment can have a large impact on the stock price, especially if discounted cash flows are being counted on far into the future.

There are two likely paths forward for SpaceX. The first is that the company executes its growth proposal as expected, but returns to shareholders are blunted, given that much of that growth is already priced in. The second is that the company's growth journey doesn't go exactly as planned, leading to potential buying opportunities over the next few years.

I'm betting on the latter. So while I'll keep an eye on the stock's valuation, I'm largely forgetting about it as a near-term investment opportunity.

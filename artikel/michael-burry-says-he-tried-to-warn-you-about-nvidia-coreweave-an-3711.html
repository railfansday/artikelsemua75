Michael Burry, the investor made famous by "The Big Short," isn't shy about saying "I told you so.".

"Coreweave, Nebius, NVIDIA, semis, I tried to warn you all," Burry posted on X on Thursday afternoon. "I didn't say short. But I provided the information one would need to sell.".

He picked a good day for it. Nvidia (NVDA -3.30%) fell 3.3% on Thursday, CoreWeave (CRWV -6.11%) dropped 6.1%, and Nebius Group (NBIS -9.39%) sank 9.4%, as tech stocks gave up an early rally.

Here's what Burry saw, and what investors should do with it.

Michael Burry. Image source: Getty Images. (Related: Fine tuning CLIP with Remote Sensing (Satellite) images...)

Burry's warnings came in pieces. In mid-October, he shared a Wall Street Journal story arguing that CoreWeave's sell-off showed the pressure from rising borrowing costs, adding his own commentary: "Dumb da dumb dumb da.".

Industry observers note that coreWeave and Nebius are so-called neoclouds, companies that rent out Nvidia-powered computing to AI developers. Building their data centers takes enormous amounts of money, much of it borrowed, so higher interest rates hit them hard. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

In a recent development, on Nvidia, his case is about the long run. He says he expects Nvidia's competitive edge to shrink over the next five years. In September, he also argued that its chips "may be economically depreciating waiting in warehouses while GAAP depreciation remains at zero," meaning the hardware could be losing value faster than the accounting highlights.

The numbers have lent credence to his concern. CoreWeave closed Thursday at $44.91, down 76% from highs last year, and Nebius finished at $96.92, down 66% from its June peak. Nvidia has held up better but still sits 39% below its October high. The damage has spread across the chip industry, too: The VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH -2.62%), a basket of the biggest chip stocks, is down 33% from its June high.

As he laid out his long-term worries about Nvidia earlier this month, Burry mentioned he imagined "the whole market is going to think this is a heck of a trade fairly soon." But since then, the stock has fallen another 16%.

Recent findings suggest that nvidia's business, meanwhile, hasn't cracked. Its revenue jumped 103% to $115.8 billion last quarter, and management raised its growth forecast for next year. The stock's slide this month coincided with Jensen Huang's sudden resignation as CEO, rather than a drop in sales.

In a recent development, if you own neocloud stocks like CoreWeave and Nebius, watch how much they borrow and what that debt costs them. If you own Nvidia, watch whether customers keep buying at today's pace, and whether the chips they buy hold their value.

Recent findings suggest that the AI stocks that come out of this sell-off in good shape will be the ones with the best answers to those questions. For long-term investors, that's a better guide than any single post on X, even one from Michael Burry.

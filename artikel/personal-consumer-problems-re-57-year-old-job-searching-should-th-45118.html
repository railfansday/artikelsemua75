I could not find reliable firm numbers for the percentage of 55 year old women who use color to cover their grey. However, if I put together the numbers that I did find, an overwhelming majority do cover, highlight or tone the grey. This fits with my personal experience. It appears that most of us do a pretty good job at it since so many, presumably men, in this thread appear to be unaware.I'm not sure if most men are unaware of this, though maybe it's more obvious to us men who started going grey by our 30's and noticed, 'remarkably', there were almost no women in our age range for whom that was (visibly) true (I believe studies show average onset of gray being only a year or few later for women on average). The standard stat seems be to 70% of US women color their hair, but that's all ages/purposes and lower by late 50's.Which I think largely answers the question once you get past the title (which suggests it could be either sex) to the OP saying it's a woman. I'm *not* talking about societal attitudes toward age of men v women generally (much of which falls outside scope of this forum not only this thread). But on the narrow topic of late middle age people dying their hair in a business setting, I think few people give much conscious thought to whether or not a woman does, many more would still judge a man negatively for doing so. And I doubt dying one's hair by itself provides much camouflage of a people's age by their late 50's. I think the subjective effect is mainly on the person dying their hair rather than person observing it in a business setting.Statistics: Posted by JackoC — Fri Nov 20, 2026 10:39 pm.

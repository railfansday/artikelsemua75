Eoin Colfer, who is exec producing Disney+ animated series based on his ‘Artemis Fowl’ book series, is among the shortlisted authors for the first-ever Children’s Booker Prize. Other film and TV links evident in the selection include author and illustrator Shaun Tan, who co-directed the Oscar-winning animated short ‘The Lost Thing’.

Sources indicate that colfer is shortlisted for ‘War’, a graphic novel he co-wrote with Andrew Donkin and which is illustrated by Giovanni Rigano. Sources indicate that the eight-strong shortlist of titles for kids aged 8 to 12 comprises five novels, two graphic novels and an illustrated collection of short stories. Watch on Deadline.

Sources indicate that stars including Adeel Akhtar (Enola Holmes), Dafne Keen (His Dark Materials) and Aidan Turner (Poldark) will read from the shortlisted titles in a series of short films showcasing the books. The films for the grown-up Booker have amassed millions of views.

The judging panel overseeing the process includes Ghosts star Lolly Adefope and bookseller Sanchita Basu De Sarkar, as well as Frank Cottrell-Boyce, a writer and screenwriter whose film credits include Paddington in Peru and Kensuke’s Kingdom. They are joined by four child judges. The inaugural Children’s Booker winner will be confirmed at a daytime ceremony at the Young V&A Museum in London on Feb. 2, 2027.

The main Booker Prize is regarded as the single biggest accolade in the world for a work of literary fiction. The film and TV links are evident in the scores of Booker titles that have been adapted for the screen including Hilary Mantel’s ‘Wolf Hall’ and Thomas Keneally’s ‘Schindler’s Ark’ (published as ‘Schindler’s List’ in the U.S.).

The 2025 winner David Szalay told Deadline about a film adaptation of his Booker-winning novel ‘Flesh’ from the producers of Conclave. This year’s winner, M. John Harrison, also told us about his hopes for a film adaptation of The End of Everything.

Here’s the Children’s Booker Prize shortlist in full.

‘The Worst Thing That Could Possibly Happen’ by Guy Bass, illustrated by David Roberts (Little Tiger Press).

‘Tadpole Summer’ by Catherine Bruton (Nosy Crow). (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

‘War’ by Eoin Colfer and Andrew Donkin, illustrated by Giovanni Rigano (Hodder Children’s Books).

‘The Night I Borrowed Time’ by Iqbal Hussain (Puffin Press).

‘The Teller of Tall Tales’ by Liz Hyder, illustrated by Tom de Freston (Pushkin Children’s Books).

‘The Rambler’s Curse’ by Mikey Please (HarperCollins Children’s Books).

‘Séamus Almost Famous’ by Jessica Scott-Whyte, illustrated by Becka Moor (Gill Children’s).

‘Hometown’ by Shaun Tan (Walker Studio).

Bitcoin Price Slips to $60,000 as November ETF Outflows Hit Record

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The bitcoin price slipped back to near $60,000 early Tuesday, according to Bitcoin Magazine Pro data, as the Nvidia-fueled rally in stocks cooled and spot bitcoin ETFs posted another heavy day of outflows.

Bitcoin faded to near $61,100 on Monday afternoon after spiking as high as $64,500 on Jensen Huang’s return as Nvidia CEO. The slide continued overnight. Bitcoin fell below $60,000 and touched about $59,400 before edging back to the $60,000 line ahead of the U.S. open. The bitcoin price is now down about 7% from Monday’s high, and it remains less than $5,000 above last week’s two-year low near $55,100.

Industry observers note that monday’s ETF figure was the number traders were watching, and it came back negative. U.S. spot bitcoin ETFs shed about $569.7 million on Monday. BlackRock’s iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT) accounted for $509.3 million of that, while Fidelity’s FBTC was the only fund to take in money, adding $13.3 million. Industry observers note that it was the fifth straight day of outflows. November’s withdrawals now total about $4.71 billion, topping June’s $4.5 billion record for the worst month since the funds launched in January 2024. (Related: Industry News: I Asked About Dirty Dishes. You Delivere...)

It has been revealed that adam Haeems, head of asset management at Tesseract Group, had called Monday’s flow figure “the first real test of this bounce.” The pattern he flagged, with bitcoin falling during U.S. hours in step with ETF outflows, held again on Monday, and the bitcoin price has given back almost all of Monday’s gains.

Recent findings suggest that wall Street is also giving back some ground. Dow futures fell 0.5%, S&P 500 futures declined 1% and Nasdaq-100 futures dropped 2% early Tuesday. Nvidia (NVDA) slid about 5% in premarket trading after closing up 31.5% at $165.05 on Monday.

It has been revealed that monday’s rally was the strongest day for both the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq since April 2025. The S&P 500 gained 4.7% and the Nasdaq Composite closed 6.5% higher, even as bitcoin failed to hold its own spike.

“With Jensen’s return, it assures people massively,” Chris Beauchamp, chief market analyst at IG Group, told Reuters. “But it then opens up a whole list of questions that may not be answered very promptly.”.

Rates remain a headwind for the bitcoin price. Newly introduced York Fed President John Williams noted Monday that “there is still work to do” to bring inflation back to the Fed’s 2% target. Markets are fully pricing in a quarter-point hike from the current 3.75% to 4% range when the Fed meets Dec. 8-9.

It has been revealed that treasury Secretary Scott Bessent has pushed back on a bigger hike, but traders still see about a 5% chance of a half-point move. The dollar index is hovering just below a 20-month high, and Wednesday’s PCE inflation report is the last major data point before the December decision.

In a recent development, bitcoin-linked shares are retreating with the coin. Strategy (MSTR) dropped 3.2% in premarket trading after Monness, Crespi, Hardt & Co. cut its price target to $59 from $65, the lowest on Wall Street. Analyst Gus Galá kept a sell rating and projects bitcoin will trade around $50,000.

It has been revealed that that would sit well below the $75,446 average cost Strategy has paid for its 851,305 bitcoin, which it grew by 457 BTC last week. MSCI is also set to remove Strategy from its indexes on Nov. 30, which could force the sale of about 13 million shares. “The premium is gone, and we think a discount can persist,” Galá wrote.

Sources indicate that coinbase (COIN) fell about 3.5% to near $105 before the bell, and miner IREN slipped 4%. Traders will watch whether bitcoin can hold $60,000 through the U.S. session as consumer confidence data lands Tuesday.

At the time of writing, the bitcoin price is near $60,000.

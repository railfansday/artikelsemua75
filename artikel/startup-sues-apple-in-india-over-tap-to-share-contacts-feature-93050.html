Startup sues Apple in India over tap-to-share contacts feature

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Apple is being sued in India by a U.S.-based startup that alleges the iPhone maker’s NameDrop feature, which lets people share contact information by bringing devices close together, infringes its patented technology.

GG Technologies, the startup behind StayTouch, an app that lets users exchange contact details by bringing their phones close together, is seeking ₹15 billion (about $161.86 million) in damages and an injunction against Apple, per court documents reviewed by TechCrunch.

Santa Monica-headquartered GG Technologies filed the lawsuit in India in October after mediation proceedings initiated in April failed in September, the startup told TechCrunch. Pre-institution mediation is generally required before filing commercial lawsuits in India. The company also sued Apple in the U.S. in April over related patent infringement claims.

Sources indicate that in its 781-page court filing in the Bombay High Court, GG Technologies alleged that Apple’s NameDrop infringes its Indian patent on technology that helps phones exchange contact details with nearby devices without requiring manual pairing. The organization released StayTouch in 2019, the same year it first sought patent protection for the technology in the U.S. It filed its Indian patent application in 2021 and received the patent in July 2024.

Industry observers note that apple introduced NameDrop as part of iOS 17 in June 2023. The feature debuted on iPhones later that year, with support for compatible Apple Watches arriving in October through watchOS 10.1. (Related: Industry News: Designing Azure silicon on Azure silicon...)

“We are seeking an injunction to restrict the usage of the NameDrop feature which is native to iOS,” GG Technologies told TechCrunch. GG Technologies urged the court in its filing to restrict Apple from selling or importing iPhones and Apple Watches preloaded with NameDrop in India. The case has yet to be listed for a hearing.

In the U.S., the International Trade Commission opened an investigation into Apple’s alleged patent infringement in August, following a complaint from GG Technologies. The startup is seeking an order that could block imports of infringing Apple devices into the country, along with restrictions on their sale. Sources indicate that founded in 2018 by Gaurav Goel, a graduate of the Indian Institute of Technology (IIT) Delhi and a former Goldman Sachs investment banker, GG Technologies developed StayTouch as an alternative to exchanging business cards. The app is available on iOS and Android.

Apple did not respond to a request for comment.

According to recent reports, india has become increasingly important to Apple, both as a fast-growing consumer market and a major manufacturing hub outside China. The Cupertino firm has expanded its retail presence in the South Asian nation, where iPhones still account for less than 10% of smartphone shipments, giving considerable room for growth.

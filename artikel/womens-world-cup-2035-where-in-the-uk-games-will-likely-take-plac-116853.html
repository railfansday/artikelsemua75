Confirmation that the United Kingdom will stage a World Cup for the first time since 1966 is expected on Monday.

The 2035 Women's World Cup is on course to be co-hosted by England, Northern Ireland, Scotland and Wales, who are the only bidders for the tournament. (Related: Tech Report: Course Launch Community Event...)

A specially arranged virtual Congress of FIFA's member nations is set to rubber-stamp their hosting. (Related: Hosting your Models and Datasets on Hugging Face Spaces...)

World football's premier international tournament was last held on these shores in 1966, when England hosted – and won – the men's World Cup.

Noel Mooney, the chief executive of the Football Association of Wales, said: "Wales has been a football nation for 150 years and we have never hosted anything on this scale. World Cup football played here, in front of Welsh children, would change what this game looks like in Wales: in the clubs, in the schools, and in the number of girls who grow up seeing a pathway into it.

Industry observers note that “Anyone who has been to a Cymru game knows the atmosphere our supporters create in a stadium. To see that support for the best teams in the world would be something to see. We are looking forward to seeing what happens on Monday.”.

FIFA’s evaluation of the bid, published earlier this month, rated it 4.3 out of five.

Recent findings suggest that nineteen venues are proposed, with 14 in England, one in Northern Ireland, two in Scotland and two in Wales in a tournament which will feature 48 teams. One of those venues – Old Trafford – has been included in its current guise but any rebuilt stadium is envisaged to be a host.

A further three stadiums could be added – Birmingham’s upcoming Powerhouse Stadium, Wrexham’s redeveloped Racecourse Ground and a potential new Chelsea stadium – which would take the tournament to 16 cities and 22 stadiums across the four nations.

The 19 proposed venues set to stage the action in nine years’ time which were evaluated by FIFA as part of the bidding process.

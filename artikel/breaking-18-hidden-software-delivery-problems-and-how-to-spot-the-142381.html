📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that software delivery pipelines move code from development through testing and deployment, generating a wealth of data along the way. Yet even when engineering teams track metrics such as deployment frequency, build times and failure rates, less obvious problems can undermine software quality, reliability and efficiency.

Identifying these issues early requires looking beyond standard performance metrics to understand how effectively the entire delivery process is working. Below, Forbes Technology Council members highlight often-overlooked problems in software delivery pipelines and the signals that can help engineering teams detect them.

In data centers, no change touches live equipment without a written procedure and a tested back-out plan. Software teams rarely meet that standard. The hidden problem is rollback paths nobody has exercised. Track the share of releases whose rollback was actually run in staging before deployment. If that number is low, your safety net is an assumption, not a plan. - Jon Trout, Chekhub.

In a recent development, I believe the hidden problem is complexity accumulation. Every release adds code, integrations, exceptions and dependencies, while the pipeline continues to report progress. The signal I watch is cognitive load: how many systems, teams, decisions and business rules an engineer must understand to make a safe change. When that number keeps rising, innovation slows, risk compounds and agility erodes long before traditional delivery metrics reveal the problem. - Dr. Suresh Rajappa, KPMG LLP. In a recent development, work waiting in the system is a hidden problem—and one that existed before AI. The revealing signal is low flow efficiency (or, equivalently, high wait time as a percentage of lead time). This is exposed through value-stream metrics like lead and cycle time, PR age, and so on. This also shifts the conversation from, “How do we make engineers code faster?” to, “Why is valuable work standing still?” That is usually where the biggest delivery gains exist. - Jesse Stockall, Flexera.

Many engineering teams can tell you how quickly they release software, but not how much effort goes into fixing what was just released. That disconnect is often hidden until it starts affecting customers. One signal worth watching is change failure rate. When more deployments lead to incidents, rollbacks or urgent fixes, the problem is rarely a lack of productivity. More often, it’s a sign that quality issues are being discovered too late in the delivery process. - Dzmitry Lubneuski, a1qa.

It has been revealed that assuming AI-generated code is safe because it passes automated tests is a major blind spot. As pull request volume grows, human reviewers can start succumbing to review fatigue and rubber-stamping changes, allowing security risks and technical debt to slip through. Instead, teams must track hard-to-find bugs, codebase standards and cross-repo impact after code is merged, not just whether tests pass. - Itamar Friedman, Qodo.ai.

It has been revealed that the hidden problem is communication lag. With AI-assisted workflows, engineering is no longer the bottleneck. We’re shipping far more code and newly introduced work than internal and customer-facing teams can keep up with, and everyone is feeling behind. Here’s the signal: the gap between what’s shipped and what’s documented. AI can help close it, but humans must stay in the loop, and teams need to decide who owns documentation and where that line now sits. - Adam Farren, Canvas Medical.

One overlooked bottleneck is latency disguised as productivity. Especially with vibe coding and other AI methods, there will be more outputs, but not necessarily an increase in delivered value. Track flow efficiency: active work time versus end-to-end lead time. The gap reveals whether engineering or the delivery system is the bottleneck. AI is exposing the organizational gaps: process, data and other debts. - Neda Nia, Stibo Systems.

Watch how long it takes a upcoming engineer to ship with confidence. That lag is the hidden cost of pipelines nobody questions anymore. AI speeds up commits, but a pipeline that only the original author understands isn’t fast; it’s fragile. Time to confident ship is the signal that exposes it. - Marc Fischer, Dogtown Media LLC.

The hidden problem is who understands a change after it ships. Changes went up, but context went down. The signal is how many people get pulled into an incident before anyone can name what caused it. Count the Slack threads, not the deploys. One person answering every time means your pipeline is fast and your knowledge is a single point of failure. - Sanjay Gidwani, KOSMOS.

Every pipeline reports what it found. Almost none report what it found again. AI assistants generate from patterns, so recurring flaws reproduce at machine speed. The signal is recurrence by weakness class, team and pipeline stage. Scan counts show volume; recurrence highlights whether you’re improving. A high number in one team is a skills gap; a high number everywhere is systemic: an insecure default or a copied prompt. - Andrea Scaduto, SecureFlag.

Sources indicate that one hidden problem is work accumulating between development, testing and deployment. Teams may appear productive while releases slow down due to bottlenecks and rework. Tracking cycle time from code commit to production, alongside deployment frequency and failed releases, helps reveal where delays occur. Making these signals visible enables teams to address root causes and deliver software faster and more reliably. - Harsh Jangid, Coozmoo Digital Solutions.

A pipeline can look healthy while teams work around it. Hotfixes, skipped checks and manual deploys happen under pressure and rarely appear on dashboards. Track the bypass rate: the share of production changes that skipped a standard check or were deployed by hand. A rising rate shows teams have stopped trusting the pipeline, which is the real problem to fix. - Venkatesh Sankaran, Saguna Consulting Services LLC.

One hidden problem is validation debt. A delivery pipeline can stay green while teams repair data or reconcile outputs by hand. In large analytics programs, I have seen those workarounds survive several releases before surfacing as production defects. Track post-deployment reconciliation exceptions per release. A rising rate demonstrates the pipeline is passing code while shifting quality work downstream. - Govinda Rao Banothu, Cognizant Technology Solutions.

Measure what one push now costs in model calls. Pipelines invoke AI on every commit for review, test generation and summaries, and that spend lands on the pipeline rather than on any team. At Google, we tracked compute cost per job the way we tracked latency. Track cost per merged change the same way. If it climbs while merge volume holds flat, your automation is getting more expensive at the same work. - Prashant Jalan, Guickly.

Sources indicate that one hidden problem is accumulating risk from unresolved vulnerabilities and skipped quality checks as code moves through the delivery pipeline. Make it visible by tracking security findings, defect escape rates and rework at each stage. These signals help teams identify bottlenecks early, prevent production incidents and balance delivery speed with reliability. - Deepali Kaushik, NBCUniversal.

Measure deployment frequency broken down by team, not just by pipeline. Aggregate metrics make the organization look healthier than it is. When you break frequency down by team, outliers become visible: one team shipping daily while another hasn’t deployed in three weeks. That gap rarely means the slow team has nothing to ship. It usually means something in their path has quietly stopped them. Aggregate metrics hide it. Team-level visibility surfaces it. - Dan Haiem, AppMakers USA.

One hidden problem is flaky tests. Engineers learn to hit retry by reflex, so real failures slip through as noise, and the lost time never reveals up anywhere. The signal is the pass-on-retry rate: the share of runs that fail and then pass on the same commit with no code change. Track it per test and alert when it rises. - Srinivas Chippagiri, Salesforce Inc.

It has been revealed that one hidden problem is the growing cost of change. Teams celebrate faster deployments while overlooking how much effort it takes to make even a small modification safely. Track the time from code completion to production, including rework, failed tests and approvals. If that time keeps growing, you’re not accelerating delivery. You’re accumulating friction disguised as progress. - Emma McGrattan, Actian.

Mortgage delinquencies rise to 4.42% in Q3 2026

Mortgage delinquencies increased in the third quarter of 2026, with the share of loans at least one payment past due rising to 4.42%. That’s according to the Mortgage Bankers Association (MBA)’s National Delinquency Survey, released Thursday.

The seasonally adjusted delinquency rate for one- to four-unit residential properties was up 5 basis points from the second quarter and 43 bps from a year earlier.

In a recent development, foreclosure starts also increased, with the share of loans entering the foreclosure process rising 1 basis point from the previous quarter to 0.21%. The percentage of loans already in foreclosure reached 0.71%, up 4 bps quarterly and 21 bps year over year.

In a recent development, walsh said the deterioration in government-backed loans is contributing to higher foreclosure activity. The Federal Housing Administration (FHA) foreclosure rate reached 1.89%, its highest level since the first quarter of 2017, while the Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) foreclosure rate climbed to 1.06%, its highest since Q4 2016.

By delinquency stage, the 30-day rate increased 4 basis points from the previous quarter to 2.25%, while the 60-day rate rose 1 bps to 0.74%. The 90-day delinquency rate remained unchanged at 1.43%.

Recent findings suggest that performance varied by loan type. The conventional mortgage delinquency rate increased by 1 basis point from the second quarter to 2.73%, while the FHA rate declined 5 bps to 11.74%. The VA delinquency rate rose 11 bps to 5%.

Recent findings suggest that all three loan categories posted annual increases. Conventional delinquencies were up 11 bps from a year earlier, while FHA delinquencies increased 96 bps and VA delinquencies rose 50 bps.

In a recent development, the seriously delinquent rate, which includes loans at least 90 days past due or in the foreclosure process, reached 2.12% on a nonseasonally adjusted basis. That was up 6 basis points from the second quarter and 51 bps year over year.

FHA loans recorded the largest annual deterioration in serious delinquencies, with the rate rising 197 basis points. The rate increased 13 basis points for conventional loans and 60 basis points for VA loans.

Recent findings suggest that the five states with the largest quarterly increases in overall mortgage delinquencies were Wyoming, up 72 basis points, followed by West Virginia and Louisiana, both up 32 basis points. Arkansas saw its rate increase by 31 basis points, while Kentucky’s rose by 27 bps.

Walsh stated the overall mortgage delinquency rate closely tracks the national unemployment rate, which stood at 4.1% in October.

Although the labor market has remained resilient, Walsh pointed to several pressures on household finances, including wage growth lagging inflation, declining personal savings, and rising balances on credit cards, auto loans and student loans.

Borrowers who took out mortgages in recent years at higher interest rates may also face affordability challenges that make timely payments more difficult, Walsh said. Changes to mortgage relief programs over the past year, including the removal of some COVID-era flexibilities, could be adding to financial pressures for some homeowners.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that it's amazing what a difference a few lines of preamble can make to your understanding of a game. Out now on PC, Steel Gullet is a factory engineering puzzler that, while notable for its meticulous interest in thermodynamics, broadly compares to the foundational work of Zachtronics, together with more recent and focussed conveyor belt wranglers such as Modulus. Resources come in, and you arrange machines on a grid to transform them into something else, before sliding them into an output hopper.

The difference is that you're doing this inside a huge walking fortress city, which stamps across the surface of a largely unseen, devastated and uninhabitable Earth. Your resources are the refuse of a bygone human civilisation: knots of concrete and rebar, flaccid rubber hose and splintered plank, that must be separated out before you can manufacture anything. (Related: Primary vs Contingent Beneficiary: What's the Diff...)

Sources indicate that from a certain, carefully limited point of view, this transforms Steel Gullet into the kind of "Zen experience" I hope death-positive morticians have when really absorbed in their work. My task is to lay a civilisation to rest, by dissecting and cleansing the remains, readying a fallen objectworld for an after-life that extends somewhere beyond the map's huge rusty walls (walls that, perhaps appropriately, constrain your camera movement a bit too much for comfort).

According to recent reports, it makes me think of the vertigo I get when I look at devices such as, well, the computer I'm writing this on, and think about how many materials and histories of production lie entangled within it. Steel Gullet answers the desire for clarity and accounting there. Of course, the other part of the premise is that I am doing busywork for some kind of totalitarian organisation, G.R.I.D. But shh, no time to think about that! I'm trying to work out how hot rubber needs to be before it can be moulded. (Related: Dividends Aren't What You Think They Are...)

Recent findings suggest that thermodynamics is Steel Gullet's fixation, and a worthwhile one. Every material encased in each lump of salvage has different physical properties – a melting point, density, solubility, ferromagnetism, etcetera. You meed to exploit these differences to separate the pieces. Wood, for example, is more buoyant than metal and concrete, so if you feed the clump into an oil bath, the planks will float to the top. If there's rubber, you can run the clump through furnaces to melt and drain it, then cool and reheat the resulting puddle to create a malleable material.

Recent findings suggest that it's still just the usual automation game routine of funnelling greebly wotsits through various smashy boxes to produce a commodity. But the thermodynamics sim element both supports the above feeling of purifying a hopelessly muddled world, and makes for a surprising visual feast: I enjoy watching each material emerge from the inital jumble and assume different shapes. There are the usual supporting infrastructure elements, such as elevators, splitters and manual junction boxes. I dare say somebody will build a logic circuit in Steel Gullet, sooner or later - let's start the clock till they get Doom running on the thing.

Recent findings suggest that in terms of overall strategy, you alternate between sending outputs to hoppers that earn you cash, and sending them to hoppers that generate research progression. You'll also unlock new rooms, removing walls and exposing new output hoppers. As you're issued new work orders, you must break open your production lines to install new machines and redirect the flow. Fortunately, you can sell back components to keep your cash reserves healthy – or at least, you can in the demo.

Between chapters, you'll be invited to join the resistance against the aforesaid G.R.I.D., perhaps sending information to the rebels. But again, no time, no time! I have all this concrete I need to powderise, all these girders I need to liquefy and make new, all these crisp, gleaming circuitboards I need to manufacture. The city's hunger is as vast as it is holy. Read more on Steam.

Recent findings suggest that on its third-quarter 2026 earnings call, held on Oct. 28, 2026, Banc of California (BANC -2.11%) unveiled that balance sheet restructuring boosted its net interest margin, the difference between interest earned on assets and interest paid on deposits. Strategic balance sheet actions replace expensive wholesale funding with higher-yielding loans and core deposits. This establishes a durable foundation for long-term shareholder value.

Management shares three key takeaways on how this operational shift supports long-term value creation.

It has been revealed that management completed targeted loan sales and reinvested securities proceeds into higher-yielding assets to eliminate higher-cost debt. This shift reduces credit concentration risk while expanding profit margins.

Recent findings suggest that the bank is expanding business accounts that pay zero interest to depositors to replace volatile brokered deposits, high-cost funds gathered through third-party brokers. This relationship-driven approach stabilizes funding costs regardless of interest rate moves.

Recent findings suggest that new loans are originating at yields well above the existing portfolio average. As older, lower-yielding assets mature or repay, new originations systematically elevate the overall asset yield of the balance sheet.

Earnings momentum builds as balance sheet actions yield higher returns, and leadership isn't hiding its satisfaction with the trajectory. Deep commercial relationships, low-cost funding, and underwriting discipline support long-term value, while management prioritizes pricing discipline and expense control.

Expanding capital capacity positions the bank for sustained shareholder value creation.

From counterparty to payment: how separate tasks added up to the whole picture

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

I never planned to study how a business lives inside a bank. It happened gradually, task by task, until separate products came together into one client journey.

According to recent reports, in a large bank a designer usually gets one area: a service, a team and screens due by a deadline. That was my case too. The difference was that I took on whatever came my way and tried to understand each task more deeply than it required. (Related: Breaking: Large Language Models: A New Moore's Law?...)

It has been revealed that I went to analysts’ meetings, asked why a particular field existed and stayed on calls that formally had nothing to do with me. Over time colleagues from neighbouring teams started writing to me: “You already know how this part works, could you take a look at ours?” That is how one area turned into several.

It has been revealed that where a business meets the bank It started with B2B services for legal entities. Behind every form there is a core banking system, complex and conservative. Together with system and business analysts and the business side, we designed how a counterparty is created and registered and how it passes every check the bank is required to run. (Related: Breaking: Porting fairseq wmt19 translation system to t...)

This is the least visible part of fintech. Every state in the interface has to match a real state in the system, and “under review” means someone is actually checking documents. Here I learned to design agreements between people and systems first and to build the screens on to p of them.

Recent findings suggest that day-to-day banking Registering is only the start: after that a manufacturer needs to work. So the next tasks were about servicing: acquiring, everyday banking operations and everything a business uses once it is connected.

In a recent development, then came payments, and that is where I went deepest: SWIFT, cross-border transfers, Visa Direct, Mastercard’s cross-border services and control of international payments. There are many formats, limits and regulatory requirements, and every mistake costs real money.

At some point I realised I knew the whole client journey, from the first step to the last.

It has been revealed that when it all came together Our team was distributed, and every product had its own rhythm. Nobody asked me to design the end-to-end journey. But one day, working on yet another flow, I realised I knew how a tech giant comes to the bank, how the bank checks it, connects it, serves it and sends its money abroad.

That knowledge turned out to be the most valuable result of the work. When you know what happens before and after your screen, it is easier to see where a client will get stuck three steps later, which check will surprise them and which wording will make an operator’s day easier.

It has been revealed that advice for designers coming into fintech Take the tasks that look boring. Talk to analysts. Ask what the system does when a user presses a button. Depth in this domain comes gradually, product by product, and one day you start seeing the whole picture.

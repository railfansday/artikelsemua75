5 Greatest YA Movie Franchises Of All Time, Ranked

Young adult stories have always made for compelling movie adaptations, especially when you look at the massive YA movie boom that began in the early 2000s. Magical worlds, dystopian futures, complicated love triangles, and the supernatural surrounding various coming-of-age stories will always have strong entertainment potential, though some are better than others.

It has been revealed that the very best YA movie franchises turn relatable and familiar coming-of-age experiences into major cinematic experiences, balancing honoring the source material with offering newly introduced perspectives at the same time. As such, it's notable that our choices for the top five greatest YA movie franchises are all still ongoing with brand-new projects in some form or another, from Harry Potter to The Hunger Games. This ranking will consider a few different key factors, including overall quality, consistency, how well it adapts and expands the source material, world-building, and cultural impact. Disney and 20th Century Fox's Chronicles of Narnia movies brought C.S. Lewis' beloved fantasy novels to life, starting with 2005's The Lion, the Witch and the Wardrobe. Audiences were introduced to the Pevensie children who discover the magical land of Narnia and ultimately join the fight against the White Witch.

In a recent development, without question, the first movie remains the peak of the franchise, though 2008's Prince Caspian offered a compelling darker journey with the Pevensies' return to a very different Narnia. 2010's Voyage of the Dawn Treader also adapted the source material fairly well. However, the franchise lost momentum and stalled after the third film, so much so that the rest of the books in Lewis' series were never adapted in this iteration of the franchise. That's a large reason why this ranks so low on this list, despite still being one of the GOATs.

Looking ahead, it will be very interesting to see how Netflix and Greta Gerwig's The Magician's Nephew fares next year, a brand-latest take on the franchise that looks to be incredibly unique.

DreamWorks' How to Train Your Dragon franchise is one of the most emotionally satisfying animated movie sagas ever made, with its focus on a young outcast Viking named Hiccup and a dragon named Toothless. Managing to change his entire village's hatred of dragons, the franchise gets better with every entry as Hiccup gradually evolves into a great leader and hero for his people.

There's an incredibly rich coming-of-age story baked into the entire franchise that is also full of some of the greatest dragon designs and animated action sequences of all time (not to mention one of the very best movie scores as well). Having expanded into television, short films, and most recently the start of a whole live-action adaptation, the enduring popularity and audience engagement are a clear testament to its GOAT status, though here's hoping the rest of the live-action franchise lives up to its animated counterparts as the first movie did.

It may not yet have the same cultural footprint as the top three from a live-action standpoint, but it still delivers one of the most satisfying and complete narratives on this list.

It has been revealed that few YA movie franchises have had quite the cultural impact of Twilight. Based on Stephenie Meyer's bestselling novels, the supernatural romance series follows Bella Swan as she moves to the rainy town of Forks, Washington, and falls in love with Edward Cullen, a vampire struggling to reconcile his feelings with his dangerous nature. Their relationship becomes increasingly complicated as werewolves, rival vampires, and ancient supernatural politics enter the picture.

Industry observers note that the five-film franchise, which began in 2008 and concluded with Breaking Dawn – Part 2 in 2012, became a defining pop culture phenomenon. Kristen Stewart, Robert Pattinson, and Taylor Lautner became household names, while the love triangle between Bella, Edward, and Jacob inspired years of passionate debate among fans. The movies also helped establish a newly introduced wave of supernatural YA adaptations, proving that romance could be just as powerful a theatrical draw as fantasy action.

It has been revealed that admittedly, the Twilight films are uneven, particularly when it comes to their pacing and melodramatic dialogue. Their critical reception and inconsistent quality prevent them from ranking higher, especially when compared with franchises that offer more consistently strong filmmaking. However, their distinctive atmosphere, memorable characters, and enormous cultural impact are difficult to dismiss. Few YA franchises have inspired such a devoted fanbase or remained such recognizable cultural touchstones more than a decade later. Twilight earns its place through its influence, even if its individual movies don't always match its popularity.

Suzanne Collins' The Hunger Games novels provided the foundation for one of the most compelling YA movie franchises of the 2010s. Set in the dystopian nation of Panem, the series follows Katniss Everdeen, a teenager who volunteers to take her younger sister's place in a televised fight to the death. What initially appears to be a survival story gradually becomes a much larger examination of propaganda, class inequality, political manipulation, and revolution.

Industry observers note that jennifer Lawrence's performance as Katniss is central to the franchise's success. She's a compelling protagonist precisely because she isn't a traditional action hero. Katniss is resourceful and courageous, but she's also frightened, emotionally complicated, and frequently overwhelmed by the expectations placed upon her. As the series progresses from the arena to an all-out rebellion, the movies increasingly explore the psychological consequences of turning a young person into a symbol of resistance.

Recent findings suggest that the original four films delivered a satisfying adaptation of Collins' trilogy, with Catching Fire standing out as a particularly strong sequel. The franchise has since continued with The Ballad of Songbirds & Snakes, a prequel exploring the younger years of future villain President Snow. That expansion demonstrates just how much storytelling potential remains in Panem, even without Katniss at the center. Its excellent world-building, strong central performance, increasingly ambitious themes, and relatively consistent filmmaking put it just below the top spot. The Hunger Games is arguably the strongest YA franchise, aside from one that absolutely takes the top spot.

It's difficult to imagine any YA movie franchise surpassing Harry Potter. Beginning with The Sorcerer's Stone, Warner Bros.' adaptation of J.K. Rowling's novels introduced audiences to a magical world hidden beneath the surface of everyday life. Following Harry, Ron, and Hermione through their years at Hogwarts, the eight-film saga gradually evolved from a whimsical fantasy adventure into a darker story about friendship, sacrifice, prejudice, and the fight against tyranny.

Industry observers note that one of the franchise's greatest strengths is how naturally its characters and world grow alongside its audience. The early movies capture the excitement of discovering Hogwarts, with moving staircases, Quidditch matches, enchanted objects, and countless magical surprises. As the characters get older, however, the stakes become increasingly serious. The introduction of Voldemort's return transforms the series into an epic conflict that challenges Harry and his friends in ways they could never have anticipated.

The eight movies also benefit from an unusually consistent cast, with Daniel Radcliffe, Rupert Grint, and Emma Watson growing up alongside their characters. Supporting performances from actors like Alan Rickman, Maggie Smith, and Ralph Fiennes further enrich the sprawling ensemble. While the adaptations occasionally sacrifice details from the books, they successfully preserve the emotional core of the story and deliver a genuinely satisfying cinematic journey.

According to recent reports, beyond the original saga, the Fantastic Beasts movies attempted to expand the Wizarding World with mixed results. Nevertheless, the original eight-film series remains a remarkable achievement, combining memorable fantasy, compelling character development, and an overarching story that builds toward an unforgettable conclusion. Its consistency, iconic world-building, memorable performances, faithful adaptation of the central story, and extraordinary cultural influence make it the most complete franchise on this list. More than any other YA series, Harry Potter made audiences feel as though they were growing up alongside its heroes, securing its place as the greatest young adult movie franchise of all time.

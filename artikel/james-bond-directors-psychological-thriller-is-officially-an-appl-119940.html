Industry observers note that 15 years ago, there weren’t many movie fans that had heard of Denis Villeneuve, but flash forward to today, and he’s one of the most prominent directors/writers in all of Hollywood. Villeneuve is a sci-fi savant who is widely known and celebrated for his work on Dune and Dune: Part Two, but his work with the sci-fi genre extends well beyond Arrakis’ reach.

According to recent reports, four years before the first Dune movie, Villeneuve took the reins from Ridley Scott for Blade Runner 2049, which is one of the most beloved sci-fi films of the 2000s despite its underwhelming box office performance. Villeneuve was already red-hot off the back of directing Arrival, the critically acclaimed sci-fi epic starring Amy Adams and Jeremy Renner. The year before Arrival, he directed Sicario, which was written by Taylor Sheridan.

Next up for Denis Villeneuve after Dune: Part Three releases in less than one month is the next James Bond movie, which is now confirmed to star Robert Pattinson as 007. Villeneuve will have to rely on his years of experience as a director to helm the best Bond film possible, calling on elements from all of his past movies, even Prisoners, the 2013 crime epic starring Hugh Jackman and Jake Gyllenhaal.

Villeneuve spent $46 million on the making of Prisoners, but the film proved to be a worthy investment by grossing $122 million at the global box office — an impressive run for a director’s first American movie. 13 years after Prisoners left theaters, the film has finally found a streaming home on Apple TV, where it surged into the top 10 during the last week.

Recent findings suggest that in addition to Hugh Jackman and Jake Gyllenhaal, Prisoners also features other stars you’ll likely recognize, such as Viola Davis and Paul Dano. Aaron Guzikowski, who also wrote the Ridley Scott HBO Max sci-fi series, Raised by Wolves, penned the script for Prisoners.

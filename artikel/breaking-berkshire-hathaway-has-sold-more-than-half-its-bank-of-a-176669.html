According to recent reports, berkshire Hathaway's (BRKA +0.92%) (BRKB +1.02%) latest 13F filing was released last week, and, as in prior quarters, the "House that Warren Buffett Built" continues to pare down its position in Bank of America (BAC +0.54%). Following this sale, Berkshire now owns just 455.9 million shares, down from a peak of over 1 billion during mid-2024. Yet while the holding company continues to reduce this position, the motivation remains unclear. (Related: How to Start a Side Business While Working Full-Time...)

In a recent development, berkshire Hathaway's sale of its Bank of America position began two years ago, when Warren Buffett was still at the helm, and has continued since Greg Abel became CEO last January. Berkshire and its leadership have not made any explicit statements about this investment decision, leaving a wide range of possible reasons for the continued selling.

NYSE · As of 11:38 AM ET 11/24/26.

Industry observers note that on one hand, Berkshire may be concerned about interest rate trends and sees a strong chance that shares, already pulling back in recent months from the mid-$60s to the high-$40s per share, will continue to slide lower. On the other hand, Berkshire first built up its Bank of America stake well over a decade ago. In Buffett and Abel's view, it is time to take profits and reinvest in new opportunities.

As with other bank stocks, Bank of America has come under pressure amid concerns about rate hikes. So far, however, BofA has continued to deliver strong results, as seen in its recent Q3 2026 earnings release. The stock trades for just 9 times forward earnings, and at only a 20% premium to book value.

Sources indicate that it also has a forward yield of 2.7%, a more-than-decade-long track record of consecutive annual dividend increases, and dividend growth has averaged in the high single digits in recent years. Although interest rate news still warrants caution, I wouldn't automatically put this stock in the "avoid" pile just because Berkshire is selling.

Together AI Discloses Prompt Leak After Coding Agent Exposed Credential

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Together AI disclosed on November 21, 2026 that an unauthorized party downloaded stored samples from a subset of customer prompts submitted to its serverless inference service. An AI coding agent had exposed the private-repository credential used in the download while the tech giant was developing its public xorl GitHub repository.

According to recent reports, customers had authorized the prompts for routine software testing, and Together AI stored the samples in a private Hugging Face repository. They involved requests to the MiniMax M3, GLM-5.2 and Kimi K2.6 models, primarily on June 24, 2026, July 4, 2026, July 8, 2026 and July 14, 2026. The coding agent committed a credential that granted access to the private Hugging Face repository during xorl’s development. Together AI’s automated scanning detected the exposure, and the company reverted the commit. During that exposure window, an unauthorized party obtained the credential and used it to download the stored samples.

It has been revealed that the sequence makes clear that detecting and reverting the commit did not prevent the credential from being used during the exposure window. The model names establish which requests were sampled, while the repository credential explains how the stored prompts became accessible. Recent findings suggest that after the download, the party searched the samples for credentials to other services and tested whether they were valid. Five of the tested credentials were canary credentials associated with a Dragos security experiment. The searches and validity tests show that the activity extended to possible reuse of secrets contained in prompts, creating a risk beyond disclosure of the prompt text itself.

It has been revealed that together AI worked with an external forensic investigation firm and law enforcement. The manufacturer said its review has not identified evidence of additional prompt leakage beyond this incident, that it has no reason to believe the data was leaked further, and that it understands the data has now been deleted.

Recent findings suggest that customer impact still depends on the content of the samples. The review is ongoing to determine whether sensitive personal information or other customer data was exposed, and Together AI will contact affected customers directly as needed with findings relevant to them.

Together AI’s response includes scoped or short-lived credentials and keyless access where supported, credential checks on all pull requests, and updated development processes governing the use of AI coding agents. The manufacturer remarked the credential measures are designed to reduce the potential impact of exposure, while the other changes add controls around pull requests and coding-agent development.

Zero data retention, abbreviated ZDR, is now the default setting for fresh accounts. Together AI recommends that customers handling sensitive prompt data enable ZDR in their account settings and provides security@together.ai for questions about the incident or its potential impact on an organization.

The incident connects credential safety in AI-assisted development with the retention of customer prompts for testing. Together AI has documented controls for both, but the ongoing content review remains the decisive step in establishing what the exposure means for customers and which notifications are necessary.

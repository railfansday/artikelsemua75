📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Drama is a comedy — or so say the backers of the film, about an engaged couple’s darkest secrets, in their submission for the Golden Globes, which requires all films be entered as either A) a drama or B) a musical or a comedy. And that’s not even one of this year’s more questionable submissions.

Other comedy entries include I Play Rocky, about the making of Rocky (which competed at the Globes as a drama); Obsession, about a young woman’s loss of agency; Tender Loving Care, about the frustrations of two young women, including the decline of one’s father; Artificial, about AI growing out of control; and Project Hail Mary, about a man sent into space against his will to try to stave off the end of the world. Meanwhile, Club Kid, about a man-child who ends up with a child — widely described as an elevated version of Adam Sandler’s comedy Significant Daddy — has been entered as a drama. Yes, all of the aforementioned musical/comedy submissions have moments of laughter and all of the dramas have elements of drama — but still, these classifications are pretty eyebrow-raising, begging the questions: What is going on? The first Globes were held in 1944. There were no genre-specific Globes categories until the eighth, when the lead acting categories were split into two, one for dramas, the other for musicals/comedies. The following year, the same split was implemented for best picture. That category was reunified for the 11th ceremony, but the split was brought back for the 12th and every year since, helping to attract more stars to the ceremony.

Until 2023, Globes category submissions were made by studios and reviewed by the HFPA’s reminders list committee, which rarely overturned anything, even as dramas with music passed as musicals (Ray, Walk the Line and Rocketman), dramas with few laughs passed as comedies (Ghost, The Tourist and My Week With Marilyn) and comedies passed as dramas (All About Eve, Born Yesterday, Harvey and Sunset Boulevard — all in the same year). Once in a blue moon, the committee would reject a submission (Promising Young Woman as a musical/comedy), but it usually deferred to studios’ preferences.

In a recent development, frustrations burst into the open in 2015 after The Martian, a film about an astronaut stranded on Mars, was submitted and accepted as a musical/comedy. Judd Apatow, whose comedy Trainwreck was in contention that year, tweeted, “Trying to dominate the comedy category when you are really a drama afraid of dramatic competition is a punk move.” The Martian ended up beating Trainwreck for best picture, something Apatow was still smarting over when he presented at this year’s Globes: “My film Trainwreck lost best comedy to Ridley Scott’s The Martian. You know Ridley Scott, America’s favorite comedy director — director of such hilarious comedies as Gladiator, Blade Runner and Alien? I can’t wait till later tonight when best comedy is won by Hamnet.”.

According to recent reports, but ownership of the Globes had changed in 2023 (the Globes now share the same owners as THR), resulting in widespread reforms. This year’s rules declare: “Motion pictures shall be entered in the category that best matches the overall intent, tone and content of the film. Therefore, a motion picture that is primarily intended to have a serious or dramatic tone but contains comedic overtones or elements shall be entered as a drama as opposed to a comedy. Likewise, a motion picture that is primarily intended to have a comedic or satirical tone but contains dramatic overtones or elements shall be entered as a comedy as opposed to a drama.”.

It has been revealed that once the film submission period ends (Nov. 24 this year), an eligibility committee comprising current Globes voters (the quantity and names are kept private) evaluates every submission for “potential misclassification issues.” If found, they “may contact the studio … to discuss those issues and to resolve them informally.” Eventually, the eligibility committee meets with a review committee to discuss any outstanding concerns, and those committees — or at least members of them who have seen a film in question — vote to determine if a change is necessary; two-thirds must favor a change for one to occur.

In a recent development, why do people try to game the Globes categories? Because the nationally telecast Globes ceremony provides the biggest spotlight for awards contenders before Oscar nominations voting (the 2027 Globes will take place Jan. 10, and Oscar nomination voting starts the next morning), making it highly valuable for a film or individual to be represented at the ceremony as a nominee or, better still, a winner.

There usually are fewer standout musicals and comedies than dramas, meaning it’s often easier to land a nom or win if a drama can pass as a musical/comedy. (This year’s aforementioned musical/comedy submissions surely wanted to get out of the way of The Odyssey.) Another motivation: Studios with multiple films prefer to not have them pitted against one another. (A24 probably wants Club Kid to compete as a drama because it already has The Invite and The Debut up for best musical/comedy, just as Neon probably wants Artificial to compete as a comedy because it already has All of a Sudden, Fjord and Paper Tiger up for best drama.).

It remains to be seen whether the Globes’ eligibility committee will overturn some of this year’s submissions. (Reports suggest it already rejected Michael as a musical/comedy.).

Industry observers note that moving forward, it would make everyone’s life easier if the Globes consolidated its genre-specific categories — picture, actor and actress — into single categories with 12 slots rather than divided categories with six each. Or, if the Globes prefers to continue to have multiple categories for each in order to yield twice as many winners, it might consider retaining the “drama” label for one but changing the other to “genre,” which would better describe recent musical/comedy nominees like Poor Things, The Substance and Bugonia.

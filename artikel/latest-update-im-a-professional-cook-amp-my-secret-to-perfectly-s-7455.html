Industry observers note that gravy is one of the most problematic dishes on the Thanksgiving table. Every year, home cooks struggle and sweat over gravy that’s too thin, too greasy, or—worst of all—lumpy. All while your relatives watch and the turkey gets cold.

Making the gravy the day before is a surefire way to avoid the last-minute panic. But on holidays when prepping ahead just isn’t in the cards, one simple hack can solve your gravy woes: a $4 pantry staple that will thicken up any gravy in mere minutes—without any lumps. It has been revealed that the secret to perfectly smooth gravy is Wondra flour. Known to chefs and home cooks alike for its bright blue cylindrical can, Wondra is wheat flour that has been pre-gelatinized, dried, and finely milled so it dissolves more easily and thickens sauces faster than regular flour. This means you can whisk it directly into any gravy (no matter the temperature!) without worry about clumping—and it will thicken that gravy almost immediately. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

If you’re making gravy from scratch with no other thickener besides Wondra, I recommend starting with 1 tablespoon for every cup of stock and/or drippings. You can do this by heating the drippings or stock in a pot on the stove, then just whisking in the Wondra. It should thicken within a few minutes. If your drippings don’t have a ton of fat, I recommend whisking in a little butter too, to help with the flavor.

Recent findings suggest that if you’re using Wondra to troubleshoot a gravy that’s too thin, I recommend whisking in a few teaspoons at a time. The Wondra should do its thickening magic within a minute or two after it goes in, and then you can decide whether you need more.

While Wondra flour is great for thickening gravies and sauces, it’s far from a one-trick pony. I’ve used it to coat white fish (such as tilapia) before pan-searing to give it a beautifully thin, crispy crust—and help prevent the fish from sticking to the pan. It’s also a common ingredient in batters for fried foods, since its lower protein content helps form a lighter, crispier crust—and it’s also a magical way to get a flakier pastry dough. So even if you only make gravy once a year, that blue canister won’t go to waste.

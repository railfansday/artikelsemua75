Recent findings suggest that the major averages of U.S. equities surged on Monday after Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO, giving investors some hope that the relentless selling pressure seen last week may subside — at least temporarily.

Industry observers note that the relief rally came after the Dow Jones Industrial Average, S&P 500 and Nasdaq Composite ended Friday's session in correction territory. The S&P alone tumbled more than 9% after Huang's departure from the chipmaker was initially revealed on Nov. 12 through Friday. (Related: Industry News: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

According to recent reports, that sell-off may be over — at least temporarily — according to Wall Street experts. But key questions linger around Nvidia and the artificial intelligence trade that has carried the bull market.

"I think the AI trade is back on," noted Mark Gibbens, founder of Gibbens Capital Management. "I certainly believe this establishes a bottom and a floor in the AI trade and the recent market weakness.".

It has been revealed that he added that the news likely doesn't alleviate all concerns that have driven recent declines, including worries about data center approvals after the midterms and fears that the capital expenditures cycle may have peaked. But Gibbens thinks the "keystone" role that Nvidia plays in both financing the AI buildout and the technology's supply chain means a more stable picture of the chipmaker creates a brighter view of the overall market than before.

An encouraging sign that the correction may be over came even before Nvidia's Monday announcement, according to Art Hogan, chief market strategist at B. Riley Wealth Management. He noted Friday's selling, which was indiscriminate and affected sectors beyond just AI-exposed companies, indicated a bottom may have been building.

"Sometimes you just hit that crescendo where you know the market gets oversold, and I would argue that we'd be higher today, even without the Nvidia news," he mentioned. Indeed, futures tied to the major stock indexes were broadly higher on Sunday night, before any news regarding Huang's return. "So I think that sort of signals we've likely found a place where we priced in, at least the near term, as much of the bad news that was giving markets jitters over the course of the last several weeks.". (Related: Course Launch Community Event...)

According to recent reports, strengthening the case for a bottom was that the technology sector's forward earnings valuation was cheaper than that of consumer staples, Hogan remarked. The S&P 500 consumer staples sector trades at a 20.5 multiple, per FactSet. Tech, meanwhile, trades at 16.3 times forward earnings. Hogan said that discrepancy doesn't square with the earnings outlook for the former sector.

Industry observers note that jay Woods, chief market strategist at Freedom Capital Markets, said Huang's return created a relief rally for a market that was oversold. But he's not sure the headline alone can get the market out of its funk.

"I think the rally will probably fizzle out because now people will pull back and still have the same questions we had before he was replaced," Woods said. Those questions will likely play out in congressional hearings, he speculated. "The market needed this jolt to get us back on our feet, but it is still a counter trend rally in an otherwise near-term downtrend.".

Woods, though, stated that the lows for the year are likely in, thanks to Friday's wash-out that spread to all sectors beyond those dependent on Nvidia's role in AI.

According to recent reports, while investors celebrated Huang's return, the sell-off that accelerated after his departure started before worries about his position even began. That stemmed from concerns about financing the buildout, data center approvals after Democratic wins in the midterm elections and worries that hyperscaler capex may have already peaked or is about to.

That's why Peter Boockvar, chief investment officer at One Point BFG Wealth Partners, is still nervous about the "picks and shovels" companies involved in the AI trade, a group he indicates includes Nvidia.

Sources indicate that "We should enjoy this rally, and maybe it lasts a bit, but I think that picks and shovels trade is going to be solely dependent on what that rate of capex spend is going to be," he noted. "That, at the end of the day, is the story that those companies are going to have to deal with outside of bounces like we're seeing now.".

Traders at Goldman Sachs put it more succinctly.

Recent findings suggest that "Not sure yet whether good or bad that the fate of the market is almost equal to the fate of one person. But hey ... We are not picky here," they noted.

Industry observers note that even though Bitcoin (BTC) price rebounded to a local peak of about $64,478 on November 23, 2026, driven by the optimism of the reinstatement of Jensen Huang as President, Chief Executive Officer (CEO), and Board Director of Nvidia Corp. (NASDAQ: NVDA), the cryptocurrency had wiped out all the gains at the time of reporting.

At the time of writing, Bitcoin traded at about $61,354, returning to the range it hovered in before Nvidia’s announcement. (Related: How to Build a Healthcare Robot from Simulation to Depl...)

As a result, BTC’s average 24-hour trading volume surged more than 73% to nearly $45 billion at the time of publication. Notably, Bitcoin’s market capitalization was at $1.23 trillion, thereby ranking 14th among the global top assets according to data CoinPedia fetched from CompaniesMarketcap.

The main reason why Bitcoin’s bullish momentum faded immediately was due to a lack of spot demand amid a renewed short squeeze, a situation where rising prices force short-sellers to buy back their position.

It has been revealed that over the past 24 hours, the total crypto liquidations were more than $770 million, with nearly $520 million involving short positions, as per metrics from CoinGlass. Worth noting that Bitcoin recorded the highest liquidation in the crypto derivatives market today of $322.91 million, whereby 69.7% of these involved short trades. Although Bitcoin’s short squeeze catalyzed a strong rebound on Monday, low demand from investors in the United States failed the rally. As of press time, the Bitcoin Coinbase Premium Gap- the difference between BTC price on Coinbase and Binance- has remained negative since September 22, 2026.

Essentially, a negative Coinbase Premium Gap indicates relatively stronger selling pressure from United States investors.

It has been revealed that consequently, a sustained Bitcoin price rebound could be fueled by continued short squeeze amid renewed spot demand, led by the U.S. spot exchange-traded funds (ETFs).

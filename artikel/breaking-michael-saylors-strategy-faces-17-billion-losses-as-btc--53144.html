Strategy faces more than $17 billion in unrealized Bitcoin losses after BTC falls below $56,000.

The tech giant holds 850,849 BTC with an average purchase price of approximately $75,454.

Strategy notes its $35 billion Net Reserve gives it greater flexibility during another Bitcoin downturn.

Strategy, the largest corporate holder of Bitcoin, is sitting at more than $17 billion in unrealized losses after Bitcoin crashed below $56,000, sending the organization’s massive BTC holdings into deep losses.

The collapse has erased more than $1 trillion from the crypto market since October, while Strategy’s own stock, MSTR, hit a 52-week low.

Michael Saylor’s Strategy now holds 850,849 BTC after buying another 485 BTC last week. The firm has acquired its entire Bitcoin holdings at an average price of around $75,454 per BTC, bringing its total purchase cost to roughly $64.2 billion.

However, Bitcoin’s latest collapse has created a huge gap between what Strategy paid and what its holdings are worth today.

Sources indicate that BTC recently fell to around $55,580, its lowest level since September 2024. At that price, Strategy’s Bitcoin holdings were worth less than $47 billion, leaving the company with more than $17 billion in unrealized losses.

It has been revealed that these losses remain on paper as long as Strategy continues holding its Bitcoin. But the gap indicates how heavily the company’s fortunes now depend on Bitcoin’s price.

The growing losses have not changed Strategy’s core Bitcoin strategy. Michael Saylor continues to describe Bitcoin as the foundation of the company’s business.

“Bitcoin is the foundation of everything we do at Strategy,” further pointing to Bitcoin’s fixed supply and its role in moving and holding capital.

Recent findings suggest that remember why you ₿ought. pic.twitter.com/UxYdpFJutx.

Saylor also told Bitcoin supporters to “Remember why you bought.”Strategy says its financial position is now much stronger than it was during the 2022 crypto crash.

It has been revealed that “Today, our Net Reserve is $35 billion. All our BTC is unencumbered. And our conviction and optionality have never been stronger.”.

According to recent reports, this is a major change from 2022, when Strategy had a negative net reserve and around one-third of its Bitcoin was pledged as collateral. Despite those challenges, the company managed to get through the bear market and says it is now in a stronger position.

Bitcoin has also fallen below $56,000 for the first time in more than two years, losing 10% in a single day. Analysts are now watching $52,643 as the next support level, followed by the August 2024 low near $49K.

If Bitcoin falls to $49,000, Strategy could face around $25 billion in unrealized losses on its Bitcoin holdings.

The crash has also hit Strategy’s stock (MSTR), which has fallen 31% in five days and 47% over the past month, reaching a newly introduced 52-week low. MSTR is at the moment trading around $77.80, down another 10%.

In a recent development, however, Bitcoin’s fall is not the only concern for the company. MSCI has announced that it will remove Strategy’s Class A shares from its global indexes after November 30, 2026, adding further pressure on the stock.

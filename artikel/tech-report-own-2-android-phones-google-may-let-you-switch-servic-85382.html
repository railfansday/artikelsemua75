In iOS 27, Apple confirmed iPhone Handoff, a networking feature that allows users to switch cell service between two eSIM-enabled iPhones. Text strings in Google’s SIM Manager app suggest the tech giant is preparing a similar feature for compatible Android phones, according to Android Authority.

According to recent reports, code strings include “Use a SIM on two phones,” and note that the feature will be called Fast Switch. Little information is currently available about the feature’s limitations, including which phones or mobile carriers may not be compatible. Recent findings suggest that on Apple’s side, any iPhone in the US running iOS 27 with a T-Mobile or Verizon-provided eSIM can use iPhone Handoff. AT&T does not currently support iPhone Handoff. (Related: Intel Diamond Rapids reveals signs of life in SC26 demo...)

However, the code strings uncovered by Android Authority show that Android’s Fast Switch will work similarly to iPhone Handoff, in that consumers must be signed in to both devices under the same Google account for the feature to work.

Strings in Google’s SIM Manager app also point to no sharing between two people, so this feature isn’t a way to circumvent carrier fees by adding service to someone else’s phone for the price of one.

If Fast Switch works anything like iPhone Handoff, which it appears it will, one eSIM can be switched between two phones. So, calls, messages, location services, and other information synced to your Apple ID can remain consistent between two devices.

Some third-party apps, like banking, communications, or social media, may also remain consistent across the two phones, depending on the app’s syncing behavior.

Features like Apple’s iPhone Handoff and Google’s Fast Switch are ideal for people who regularly use two phones. Business owners and digital creators may want one phone strictly for business purposes and the other for personal use.

Local MP says legitimate criticisms of asylum housing plans are being co-opted by opposition parties ‘stirring up racial discord’.

Residents of a Suffolk village have voted to “leave the UK” in a referendum held in response to government plans to house 930 asylum seekers at a nearby former military base.

Approximately 500 people in Barnham were eligible to take part in the symbolic vote on Saturday. There were 257 votes for independence, 16 against and one spoiled ballot. (Related: YouTube ‘Co-Views’ try to guess how many people are wat...)

After the vote, Helen Beck, the chair of Barnham Residents’ Association, said Barnham’s flag would be a stone-curlew on a “Suffolk blue background”.

RAF Barnham was one of three Ministry of Defence sites named in June by the government as potential locations for newly introduced developments to house asylum seekers. (Related: Three 'e-waste' Nvidia V100s hit 100 tokens per second ...)

A similar referendum was held in Piddington, Oxfordshire in September, with residents also voting to leave the UK amid looks to house more than 1,000 migrants at another former military base.

Industry observers note that in Barnham, the intends have prompted protests outside the site. The referendum asked residents whether they supported seeking a “crown dependency status”, which is a similar system to that of Jersey, Guernsey and the Isle of Man.

John Bauer, the parish council chair, said this would allow the village to “retain loyalty to the crown” but remove itself from Home Office jurisdiction.

Recent findings suggest that while in opposition the Labour party had attacked the Conservative policy to house asylum seekers on military bases; once in government, however, the party backtracked. The Conservatives have also reversed their position, with Kemi Badenoch telling frustrated villagers “the Conservative party is on your side”.

“We know that the problem of the boats needs to stop and we have got a initiative to stop it,” she mentioned on Friday.

Earlier this month, it was confirmed construction manufacturer Kier would design the latest camp at RAF Barnham.

Recent findings suggest that before Saturday’s vote Labour’s Dr Peter Prinsley, the local MP, remarked he was against the development of the site. Prinsley said the looks resembled a “concentration camp”, and warned that planning permission may be difficult to attain due to environmental concerns about rare species living in the area – in particular, stone-curlews – with previous development applications nearby having been turned down.

In a recent development, “This is a symbolic referendum,” Prinsley remarked. “It’s not going to produce a binding result. I’m expecting that [the result] will be hugely in favour of becoming a crown dependency. There are 500 electors, so I’m expecting it to be 500 – nil.”.

He added that the government “is doing an awful lot to deal with the irregular migration problem”, and highlighted that net migration may come down to “more or less zero by the end of the year”.

In a recent development, but he added that he didn’t think “the large army camps in remote locations are ever likely to be appropriate for the distribution of people seeking asylum. The fact is that people who arrive here on small boats are not illegal migrants. They are people who are seeking asylum. Our political opponents have described these people as ‘illegals, criminals, rapists’, and all sorts of things that they’re not. They’re just people who have arrived on small boats. The government is getting a grip on this problem, but it’s a complicated problem.”.

Recent findings suggest that he remarked the proposed Barnham site had particular issues which may make it unsuitable. “It’s a remote location without any pedestrian access, it’s on a very hazardous main road. When it was used as military accommodation there were serious accidents on that road.

“It’s also a site of special scientific interest because it is the nesting site for stone-curlews, which will make any planning application very difficult – even a government planning application – because these are worldwide protections.”.

He suggested “disused office blocks in some of the larger cities” may be more appropriate places to house asylum seekers, with more local services available.

Industry observers note that “It feels like a concentration camp,” Prinsley added. “It doesn’t feel like a place that has a compassionate approach to people who are fleeing persecution and are being processed for asylum.”.

He also said legitimate criticism of the aims had been co-opted by opposition parties who are aiming to “stir up racial discord”.

A Home Office spokesperson said: “We recognise the strength of feeling in local communities and are listening carefully to the concerns being raised.

“The government is working on a fairer distribution of asylum accommodation across the country, with over £1bn saved in costs since the election.

Industry observers note that “This includes closing all asylum hotels and moving to alternative accommodation, including former military sites. These are designed to be self-contained, with 24/7 security and CCTV, to reduce the impact on local communities.”.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Lufthansa Group plans to lay off around 1,000 full-time workers at its flagship carrier as elevated fuel prices and service disruptions continue to undercut earnings.

The airline group disclosed the cuts in a letter sent to employees and seen by Dow Jones Newswire, according to The Wall Street Journal. The reductions will target Lufthansa’s hubs in Frankfurt and Munich. (Related: Tech Report: Intel continues layoffs with cuts to its I...)

Tech giant leaders noted their 2024 turnaround blueprint, initially expected to boost earnings by €2.5 billion over four years, is no longer enough to keep the brand on solid financial ground. (Related: Silicon Motion demos SM8466 PCIe 6.0 SSD controller, ex...)

“The year 2026 has set us back: the war in the Middle East, the still very high fuel costs, nine days of strike action, and our already very high cost base have intensified the situation,” the letter said.

Lufthansa failed to reach an agreement with its pilot union earlier this week, setting the stage for a possible work stoppage. The impasse has already resulted in two short strikes, one in February, the other in March.

Industry observers note that lufthansa Group has spoken openly about the damage caused by higher fuel prices over the last nine months. Over the summer, it retired aircraft, cut routes, and warned that layoffs were likely unless prices eased.

According to EuroNews, Lufthansa has lost on average about €20 million per month since the start of the year.

Nvidia Stock Plunge Wipes Out Nearly $500 Billion in One Day as SEC Reportedly Probes Its Accounting

By Jonathan Ponciano – Nov 20, 2026 at 4:07PM EST.

Nvidia's terrible month just got worse.

Sources indicate that shares of Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) fell 14% on Friday afternoon, to less than $126, after Reuters reported that the Securities and Exchange Commission is investigating the chipmaker's accounting. The drop erased roughly $491 billion in market value in a single session, taking Nvidia from about $3.52 trillion to about $3.03 trillion. Recent findings suggest that zoom out and the damage is even bigger. Nvidia closed at a record $239.24 on Oct. 6. At Friday's price, the stock is down about 48% from that high, and the firm has lost about $2.7 trillion in value in just six weeks.

Here's what we know, and what investors should make of it.

The Wall Street Journal first reported on Tuesday that the SEC was investigating Nvidia but couldn't say what the probe was about. On Friday, Reuters, citing two people briefed on the matter, reported that the agency's enforcement staff are examining potential accounting issues at the company. (Related: Industry News: Politics Takes A Toll On Your Health — W...)

Recent findings suggest that many questions remain unanswered. Reuters couldn't determine when the inquiry began or which accounting practices are under review. The SEC declined to comment, and Nvidia didn't immediately respond. Keep in mind that the SEC investigates plenty of companies that are never charged with anything.

The stock was already having a bad day before the report: shares were down more than 6% earlier in the session amid a broad sell-off in AI stocks.

The probe adds to a pile of problems. Nvidia co-founder Jensen Huang resigned as CEO on Nov. 11 following revelations about his personal relationships, including with a woman U.S. authorities suspect of spying for China, which China denies. The Justice Department is also reviewing Nvidia's planned $12.9 billion purchase of Hugging Face, according to Reuters.

It has been revealed that two areas could draw scrutiny. The first is how Nvidia reports where its sales come from. The tech giant breaks out revenue by where customers are headquartered, rather than where they're billed. On that basis, about 63% of last quarter's revenue came from U.S.-based customers. The Financial Times has reported that Nvidia made the switch as sales billed to Singapore approached half of its total, a bad look with Southeast Asia having become a hub for chips smuggled into China.

It has been revealed that the second is how Nvidia helps its customers pay for its chips. On Tuesday's earnings call, interim CEO Colette Kress noted Nvidia's guarantees totaled $118 billion at the end of the quarter, including $105 billion of credit support tied to a single data center campus. These guarantees are the kind of disclosure the SEC is watching closely at AI companies. When a chipmaker helps fund the projects that buy its chips, it's harder to tell how much demand would exist on its own.

It has been revealed that none of this has shown up in the numbers yet. Nvidia's revenue jumped 103% to $115.8 billion last quarter, and the organization expects about $130 billion this quarter. Management even raised its growth forecast for next fiscal year to about 75%. At about $126, the stock trades at roughly 12 times last quarter's adjusted earnings of $2.68 per share on an annualized basis, cheap for a company growing this fast.

Recent findings suggest that but cheap can get cheaper. Accounting questions hit at the heart of what investors pay for: trust in the numbers. Until Nvidia names a permanent CEO and the SEC's focus becomes clearer, the stock could stay volatile, even after a 48% drop.

Sources indicate that for long-term investors who already own Nvidia, there's no need to panic-sell on a report about an investigation. For anyone thinking of buying, it's smarter to wait for more clarity, or to build a position slowly, than to bet that the worst is over. There are plenty of other semiconductor stocks without a cloud of investigations hanging over them.

The alleged foreign agent was discovered with two Russian passports and a forged American passport.

A Russian man has been arrested in California's Silicon Valley for allegedly attempting to spy on employees of a technology and defence firm that supports the Ukrainian military.

Industry observers note that igor D'iu is accused of acting as an agent of a foreign government to conduct a covert operation in Palo Alto, near San Francisco. The BBC understands that the firm targeted was Palantir Technologies. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

D'iu is also charged with stalking employees and possessing falsified identity documents.

The organization is not named in charging documents, but is described as a publicly traded technology and defence company that produces software used by Ukraine in its war with Russia.

"The allegations in this case describe a state-sponsored operation to target and harass employees of an American technology and defense tech giant because of that company's assistance to Ukraine in its war with Russia," said US Attorney for the Northern District of California Craig Missakian.

"We will not tolerate any efforts - by foreign governments or otherwise - to turn Silicon Valley into a base for foreign intelligence operations.".

Prosecutors say that D'iu entered the US unlawfully via the Mexico border, after travelling there from Istanbul in June.

It has been revealed that he was allegedly recruited through the Telegram encrypted messaging app. His handlers referred to Russia as "'the Motherland' and directed D'iu to gather information about the company because it was supplying weapons used against Russia in Ukraine".

On their instruction, he purchased computers, phones, wireless networking equipment and remote access software - tools he needed for his spy mission. (Related: Fine tuning CLIP with Remote Sensing (Satellite) images...)

He then went to a coffee shop located near the manufacturer's offices in Palo Alto, where he photographed laptop screens of employees who were working there.

His handlers also allegedly "instructed him to position his laptop at designated locations near the company so they could remotely access it and attempt to collect information from company employees in the vicinity".

The suspect allegedly took photos of employees screens at a cafe near the office.

A source in the industry confirmed that Palantir was targeted, and added that the operation did not penetrate the company's private systems.

They added that the company has been "targeted extensively" by Russia over its involvement in Ukraine, and that the attacks are becoming "increasingly aggressive".

The source, who did not want to be named, noted that this was the first time a Russian operative was found while on US soil attempting to spy on the company.

"I think the surprising thing is that they decided to take the risk of sending someone to the US," they stated, in an attempt to get into the system "physically".

Palantir has not issued a statement in reaction to the charges.

D'iu also allegedly conducted surveillance of employees and their residences.

Industry observers note that on 3 September, he followed a vehicle driven by one senior executive for nearly 30 miles. He fled after she drove to a police station. Police later obtained evidence that he had followed other employees' cars on previous days.

During the investigation, officials discovered identity cards that had been falsified - including a US passport, California ID card, and a Social Security card with the fake name "Hugo Landry Johns".

A raid on his home in October in East Palo Alto turned up a handwritten notebook that contained numerous home addresses of employees "family members' names, educational and employment histories, and assessments of their strengths and weaknesses", prosecutors say. Two Russian passports were also found.

He faces two charges related his alleged work as a foreign agent, one charge of interstate stalking, and one count of fraud related to his identity documents. He faces over 15 years in prison if found guilty.

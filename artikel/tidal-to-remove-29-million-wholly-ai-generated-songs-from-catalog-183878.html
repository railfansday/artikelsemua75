TIDAL will begin removing all wholly AI-generated tracks from its catalog on December 9, 2026, Robert Andersen revealed on November 24, 2026. He mentioned the initiative would delete 29 million songs and leave the catalog clear of those tracks by the beginning of 2027.

Andersen’s X profile indicates he runs TIDAL at Block, while Block’s leadership page identifies him as the company’s principal designer. TIDAL’s official account confirmed the start date on X, writing, “Bye AI music. Starting December 9.”. (Related: How to Start a Side Business While Working Full-Time...)

Andersen remarked TIDAL tagged and demonetized all wholly AI-generated tracks a few months earlier to protect real artists’ authenticity, but that 43% of tracks distributed to the service in the preceding 90 days had been detected as AI-generated and 96% of AI-generated songs received zero plays. Those figures describe a large detected inflow with little listening for AI-generated material. The latest policy adds deletion to the earlier labeling and demonetization approach. It has been revealed that andersen estimated that TIDAL would reject 80,000 to 85,000 AI-generated songs per day, described the organization as the first digital service provider to take this step, and encouraged competing services to follow. The daily figure covers expected future rejections, distinct from deletions of music already in the catalog.

TIDAL’s terms, effective June 29, 2026, for the service outside Canada, the European Union, the European Economic Area and the United Kingdom, define AI-generated content as audio wholly or substantially generated by generative AI with limited or no direct human creative input beyond an initial text prompt or similar instruction. The unveiled removal applies to wholly AI-generated tracks, a narrower formulation than that contractual definition.

It has been revealed that those terms provide for an in-app AI indicator at track or album level. Identification and labeling are performed on a best-efforts basis using industry-standard detection technology; TIDAL cautions that detection can produce false positives and false negatives and does not warrant the accuracy or completeness of a label or its absence.

Under the same terms, TIDAL Upload tracks may be scanned for AI generation, AI-generated uploads are ineligible for monetization, and users who believe a track was erroneously tagged can contact support. The rules already give classification direct financial consequences for uploaders.

TIDAL’s published content guidelines say the service accepts AI-generated content that meets additional requirements. For AI-generated content, they prohibit material that impersonates or imitates an artist’s voice, likeness or identity without express authorization, misleads listeners about its origin or creation, or perpetuates streaming fraud, including inflated play counts and royalties diverted from legitimate artists.

Industry observers note that violations may be labeled, demonetized or removed without prior notice, while repeated or egregious breaches can bring account suspension or termination. Moderation combines automated scanning at upload with human review when necessary. Consumers can appeal content removals and account decisions, with human moderators reassessing the material and initial decision; account suspensions and terminations also receive human review before being finalized.

The announcement moves TIDAL beyond disclosure and demonetization toward using AI detection to determine catalog access. Since the published terms acknowledge mistaken classifications, the policy’s practical impact will depend on how reliably TIDAL distinguishes wholly generated music from human-directed work and how effectively its review process handles disputes.

David Tepper of Appaloosa Management is considered one of the world's top hedge fund managers, and through his investment prowess, he's amassed an estimated net worth of $28.5 billion. The Carolina Panthers' owner was reshuffling his artificial intelligence (AI) semiconductor positions in Q3, exiting positions in Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -13.25%) and Broadcom (AVGO -12.19%), while buying shares of Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%) and Micron (MU -13.89%). He also cut his stake in Alphabet (GOOGL -2.70%) (GOOG -2.59%) nearly in half.

In a recent development, while Tepper's track record speaks for itself, I think he got it wrong in this case. Here's why.

It has been revealed that while Tepper was buying Nvidia shares before Jensen Huang stepped down as CEO, the company is facing potential challenges beyond the loss of its CEO. Competitors have started to catch up, with Semianalysis recently reporting that Alphabet's Tensor Processing Units (TPUs), which it co-developed with Broadcom, are delivering better performance per dollar. Meanwhile, all of Nvidia's largest customers, outside of Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX -7.69%) are working on developing their own custom chips. NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

It has been revealed that the AI application-specific integrated circuit (ASIC) market is growing much more quickly than the GPU market, with Counterpoint Research projecting that custom AI chip unit shipments will surpass data center GPU shipments in 2028. Two of the biggest winners from this trend are Alphabet and Broadcom.

Alphabet developed its TPUs more than a decade ago, and its comprehensive ecosystem is starting to rival Nvidia's. Not only is TPU growth supported by Alphabet's internal use and its cloud offering, but it is also beginning to sell its solutions to external data center operators, such as Anthropic and OpenAI. (Related: My Journey to a serverless transformers pipeline on Goo...)

It has been revealed that bloomberg Intelligence believes that TPU sales outside of Google Cloud could grow from just $12 billion this year to over $135 billion in 2028. This is a huge revenue driver for both Alphabet and Broadcom, which appear to be two of the biggest winners in this rotation.

Even bigger news for Nvidia competitors, though, is that Anthropic (ANT -8.50%) revealed that its Claude Mythos model demonstrated that it can get models up to speed running on AMD GPUs in just two to three days without any human involvement, dramatically cutting down the six to 12 months it used to take a dozen engineers to get models running on different chips. This greatly narrows Nvidia's CUDA moat and opens the door for both AMD and ASICs to take market share.

The timing is great for AMD, as it recently started shipping its complete Helios systems, which combine its GPUs, CPUs, and networking into one package, to large hyperscaler customers. It's significantly cheaper than Nvidia's end-to-end server racks, opening the door to take more market share if it can show strong performance. The company's CPUs will also play a key role in Alphabet's new TPU 8tx AI system.

It has been revealed that with CUDA's moat weakening, Alphabet, Broadcom, and AMD, all of which have cheaper offerings, should benefit greatly in the coming years. Meanwhile, Nvidia could face not just a loss of share but also margin compression. This could lead to lower profitability even if it can grow its revenue. That's one of the major reasons I prefer the three stocks Tepper was selling over Nvidia.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

Sources indicate that as for the other semiconductor stock that Tepper was buying, Micron, I'd be careful. Memory has historically experienced very big boom-and-bust cycles, and, while this supercycle is certainly different, memory still tends to be a commodity.

Micron is also the most exposed of the massive dynamic random access memory (DRAM) makers to any changes in prices of conventional memory, with it having the smallest share of the high bandwidth memory (HBM) market, which has been the driving force of the industry.

Another area of worry is with NAND, or flash memory. While Micron derives the bulk of its memory from DRAM, NAND has grown to about a quarter of its revenue. Hong Kong-based GF Securities recently issued a note saying it expects this market to become oversupplied as soon as next year. While primarily a DRAM maker, Micron would not be immune to a big price pullback in the NAND market.

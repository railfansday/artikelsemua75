FY 2027 Transportation Proposal Puts EV Charging Investments at Risk.

It has been revealed that WASHINGTON, D.C. — On Monday, Senate Republicans released an Appropriations package that proposes to take back nearly $2 billion in federal funding already allocated to states to build out electric vehicle (EV) charging infrastructure under the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act and repurpose it for general transportation uses, putting critical clean transportation investments at risk. (Related: Build A Racetrack codes (November 2026)...)

Republicans already clawed back roughly $500 million from the National Electric Vehicle Infrastructure (NEVI) Formula Program in the FY 2026 appropriations bill. Now, they are proposing to further divert these funds, even as all 50 states, Washington, D.C., and Puerto Rico have approved aims for their use and are working to deliver critical charging infrastructure to their communities. More than half of available NEVI funding has already been committed to projects, and the proposal threatens to upend additional awards that states continue to make to charging developers.

According to recent reports, for example, Texas recently made an announcement of $226 million in awards that will support 321 stations. The Senate proposal would threaten Texas’s remaining NEVI funding, along with funding for states like Ohio, which has one of the largest numbers of operational NEVI-funded stations in the country. In response, Clean Transportation for All Director Katherine García released the following statement:.

It has been revealed that “It is unacceptable for Senate Republicans to once again seek to claw back critical EV charging investments. Trump’s day-one freeze of NEVI set states back, and they are only now back on track after we successfully challenged that freeze in court. This latest proposal threatens affordable transportation for American families, clean air for communities, and states’ efforts to build the infrastructure of the future. We have successfully defended NEVI against the Trump administration’s attacks, and we will continue fighting to protect the program and ensure these investments reach communities across the country.”.

Industry observers note that the NEVI program, established through the Bipartisan Infrastructure Law in 2021, provides funding to states to build a nationwide network of EV fast-charging stations. Protecting that investment is essential to expanding charging access, supporting EV adoption, reducing dependence on volatile gasoline prices, and cutting transportation pollution.

Since the Trump administration’s unlawful freeze of the NEVI program was lifted following successful litigation by Sierra Club, its partners, and a coalition of states, hundreds of millions of dollars in NEVI funding have been committed to charging projects.

Congress must preserve funding for EV charging infrastructure while providing dedicated resources for passenger rail, public transit, and other clean transportation priorities.

Tech Report: Billionaire Frank Slootman gives 22,081 Montana kids $1,000 each for their Trump Accounts

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Snowflake billionaire Frank Slootman and his wife, Brenda, are giving more than $22 million to children in their home state of Montana. The couple is putting $1,000 into the Trump Account of every Montana child born in 2023 or 2024, the U.S. Treasury Department announced on Nov. 19.

The gift covers 22,081 children, totaling $22,081,000. The Slootman Family Foundation stated the money has already been deposited through the Treasury Department and invested in the children’s accounts, according to NBC Montana.

It has been revealed that those children were born too early for the federal government’s own $1,000 deposit, which goes only to children born from 2025 through 2028. The Slootmans’ gift gives them the same start. At $1,000 a child, it is also four times the per-child amount in Michael and Susan Dell’s $6.25 billion national pledge.

Industry observers note that “Montana is the Last Best Place,” the Slootmans stated in a statement. “We want to give Montana children an even faster start in life. The money in your account is yours, it cannot be accessed by anybody, not even by you until you are at least 18 years old. What you can do is watch it grow, ask for contributions on birthdays, holidays and other special occasions to make your account compound in value even more.”. Gov. Greg Gianforte thanked the family for what he called “this significant investment.”.

According to recent reports, frank Slootman, Snowflake’s chairman and former CEO, led the cloud data company through its 2020 initial public offering after taking two other technology companies, Data Domain and ServiceNow, public. Bloomberg Law reported that the Bloomberg Billionaires Index put his net worth at $3.1 billion when the gift was unveiled. (Related: Breaking: My Journey to a serverless transformers pipel...)

According to recent reports, slootman, born in the Netherlands, has lived in Bozeman for years. When Snowflake eliminated its corporate headquarters in 2021, it named its Bozeman office its principal executive office because Slootman and the tech giant’s CFO lived there, Fortune reported at the time.

It has been revealed that the family foundation has given to Montana causes before, including $1 million to American Prairie Foundation, a conservation group assembling a large prairie reserve in the state, in 2022 and $1.5 million in each of the next two years, according to its tax filings. This gift is on another scale: it roughly matches the $22.7 million in net assets the foundation reported at the end of 2024, the latest year for which its filings are public.

According to recent reports, slootman has also backed Montana Republicans. While he was ServiceNow’s CEO, he gave the maximum allowed contribution to Gianforte, then the state’s congressman, CNBC reported in 2021. Sen. Tim Sheehy, a Montana Republican, publicly thanked the couple for the gift last week, calling it “an incredible bet on Montana’s future.”.

Created under President Donald Trump’s 2025 tax law, a Trump Account is a type of individual retirement account for children. Parents, relatives and employers can contribute, generally up to a combined $5,000 a year during childhood. Contributions from charities to a broad group of children, such as every child born in a state in a given year, don’t count toward that cap.

The money goes into low-cost funds that track broad U.S. stock indexes, such as the S&P 500, so its value rises or falls with the market. Investment earnings generally aren’t taxed until you withdraw the money.

According to recent reports, families generally can’t withdraw the money until the calendar year the child turns 18. After that, traditional IRA rules apply, and withdrawals can trigger taxes and early-withdrawal penalties.

According to recent reports, the Treasury Department has opened accounts for eligible children under 18 with valid Social Security numbers. Nearly 70 million children now have one, a White House spokesman told Fortune this month. Only U.S. citizens born from Jan. 1, 2025, through Dec. 31, 2028, qualify for the government’s one-time $1,000 deposit.

According to recent reports, some Montana children covered by the Slootmans’ gift may have already received $250 from Michael and Susan Dell. The Dells’ gift reached 25 million children born from 2016 through 2024, starting in ZIP codes with the lowest median family incomes. The Slootmans’ announcement said 118,523 Montana children are eligible for the Dells’ $250.

A Montana child who qualified for both gifts would have $1,250 from the two couples.

According to recent reports, parents can claim their child’s account through the official Trump Accounts app by verifying their identity and their relationship to the child. Once an account is claimed, relatives can add their own contributions. Fortune has a guide to who gets the $1,000 and how to claim an account.

It has been revealed that nearly half of manufacturers surveyed by the Philadelphia Fed stated they raised selling prices in November, while about as many reported falling fresh orders as rising ones. The US inflation report due Nov. 25 could shift expectations for the Fed’s December decision and affect the cost of benchmark-linked dollar loans used to finance Bitcoin positions.

The Philadelphia Fed reported on Nov. 19 that the selling-price index rose to 47 from 40.1 in October, its highest since June 2022. Some 48.1% of respondents mentioned they raised prices for their own goods, 1.1% lowered them and 50.8% reported no change. (Related: Industry News: One Year Since the “DeepSeek Moment”...)

The fresh-orders index fell to −0.4 from 4.3, its fourth straight decline. Roughly 32% of firms reported more orders and a similar share reported fewer. Shipments held up better, with that index rising to 25.7 from 15.3. (Related: At historic summit, Xi and Putin share global stage wit...)

Industry observers note that these regional diffusion indexes subtract the share of firms reporting a decrease from the share reporting an increase. The readings measure how widely prices and orders changed among the factories surveyed. An index of 47.0 is the gap between the shares reporting higher and lower selling prices, measured in percentage points.

The Bureau of Economic Analysis scheduled its October Personal Income and Outlays report for 8:30 a.m. ET on Nov. 25. It includes the personal consumption expenditures price index, a national measure of consumer prices for the month before the regional factory survey. Revised third-quarter GDP is due at the same time, so interest rates could respond to both releases.

In a recent development, the minutes of the Fed’s October meeting show why the inflation reading matters. The Fed held its target rate at 3.75% to 4%, and almost all participants judged that more increases would likely be appropriate under their baseline outlooks. In a discussion about inflation risks that included the AI investment boom, a majority remarked higher costs could feed through to consumer and business prices more widely and persistently.

The Fed’s policy rate influences other interest rates and borrowing costs. If October inflation comes in higher than expected and markets price in higher Fed rates, traders whose dollar loans reset against short-term benchmarks could face more expensive Bitcoin positions.

A cooler inflation reading could pull rate expectations lower heading into the Fed’s Dec. 8–9 meeting.

‘Michael’ Won’t Be Submitted for BAFTA Awards as Filmmakers Pass on Billion-Dollar Biopic’s Entry (EXCLUSIVE)

The highest-grossing biopic ever made is sitting out one of the biggest stops on the road to the Oscars.

“Michael,” Antoine Fuqua’s billion-dollar portrait of the King of Pop, will not be submitted for the 2027 EE BAFTA Film Awards, Variety has learned exclusively. That takes actors like Jaafar Jackson and Colman Domingo, along with the film’s entire craft team, out of the running before longlist voting begins. According to sources with direct knowledge, producer Graham King, Fuqua and various stakeholders made the call after consulting with Lionsgate, international distributor Universal Pictures and the film’s other partners. The sources cite “multiple factors” behind the decision but declined to say what they are.

Representatives for Lionsgate, Universal Pictures and GK Films declined to comment.

Sources tell Variety that Lionsgate and Universal supports the filmmakers’ decision.

Earlier this month, the organization sent film voting members its Recommended Viewing Groups for the 2027 awards, which divides every entered film into 15 groups. Each member is randomly assigned one. The British Academy encourages voters to watch as many entries as they can but asks those short on time to prioritize their assigned group and the films in their specialist craft category. “Michael” isn’t in any of them. It has been revealed that BAFTA’s Stage Two entry and deadline passed Nov. 19. A draft list of entered films goes out to entrants and members Nov. 26. The final list will be published on Dec. 4, the same morning Round One voting opens to set the longlists.

Among the films that entered are “The Odyssey,” “Artificial,” “Behemoth!,” “Project Hail Mary,” “Wuthering Heights” and “The Social Reckoning.” The year’s other box office heavyweights, “The Super Mario Galaxy Movie” and “Spider-Man: Brand New Day,” are also in the mix, along with two music documentaries, “Epic: Elvis Presley in Concert” and “Oasis: Don’t Look Back in Anger.”.

It’s an unusual move for a film that has spent the fall building an awards case.

The 80th BAFTA Film Awards reclaim its traditional spot ahead of the Academy this season. Last year, the British Academy confirmed nominations five days after the Oscars did, breaking a pattern that had held since 2001. This time, longlists arrive Jan. 7 and nominations Jan. 19, two days before the Academy unveils its own on Jan. 21. The London ceremony follows Feb. 21, three weeks ahead of the March 14 Oscars at the Dolby Theatre. For voters on both sides of the Atlantic, the BAFTA ballot is the first real read on the Oscar race, especially given the Academy’s large international voting bloc.

It’s also a departure from producer King’s own playbook. “Bohemian Rhapsody,” his last music biopic, won Rami Malek the BAFTA for leading actor on the way to four Oscars, which he repeated.

Industry observers note that king has criticized the British Academy before. After February’s ceremony, Tourette’s syndrome campaigner John Davidson involuntarily shouted the N-word while “Sinners” stars Michael B. Jordan and Delroy Lindo presented the night’s first award, and the BBC aired the slur despite a two-hour tape delay. Both organizations apologized.

In a recent development, “For years, BAFTA and the BBC have repeatedly displayed cultural insensitivity and tone deafness,” King said at the time in a statement posted to his GK Films’ Instagram account. “Their inability to take accountability for their actions or inactions is reckless and unacceptable.”.

The sources did not say whether the episode factored into this year’s decision.

It has been revealed that the news also comes after the Golden Globes rejected the studio’s bid to place “Michael” in the comedy or musical categories, a submission that came with a letter from Fuqua, pushing the film into the more crowded drama race. An FYC screening at the DGA reportedly drew a standing ovation.

Recent findings suggest that at the Gotham Awards on Nov. 30, Fuqua, King, Jaafar Jackson, Colman Domingo and Nia Long will accept the Musical Tribute Award.

The commercial case was settled months ago, with a sequel currently in development.

“Michael” opened in April to $97 million domestically and $217 million worldwide, a record debut for a music biopic. In July, it became the first biopic to cross $1 billion globally. It’s Lionsgate’s biggest release ever, topping the $865 million of “The Hunger Games: Catching Fire” and the studio indicates it’s the first film from an independent distributor to reach the milestone.

Sources indicate that the critics’ reception was much harsher than audiences’. The film right now holds a 38% score on Rotten Tomatoes. However, viewers have consistently singled out Jaafar Jackson’s star-making turn as his uncle; audiences gave it an A- CinemaScore.

Sources indicate that written by John Logan, “Michael” traces Jackson’s rise from the Jackson 5 in the 1960s through the Bad World Tour in the late 1980s, closing before the first allegations of misconduct became public in 1993. Jaafar Jackson leads a cast that includes Domingo as Joseph Jackson, Long as Katherine Jackson, Miles Teller as attorney John Branca and Mike Myers as record executive Walter Yetnikoff.

Skipping BAFTA doesn’t affect the film’s Oscar eligibility, and the Globes, Critics Choice Awards and guild circuits remain in play. But it gives up one of the season’s biggest televised platforms, along with one of the few chances for a breakout performance to reach an international voting body before Oscar nominations voting opens.

Whether the decision ultimately costs “Michael” a trip to the Oscars remains to be seen.

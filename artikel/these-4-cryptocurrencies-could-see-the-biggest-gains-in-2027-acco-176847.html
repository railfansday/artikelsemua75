It has been revealed that arthur Hayes, the co-founder of the now-defunct BitMEX crypto exchange, is expecting that a quartet of cryptocurrencies -- Ethereum (ETH -5.33%), Zcash (ZEC -15.26%), NEAR Protocol (NEAR -8.63%), and Venice Token (VVV -0.94%) -- will rally, though he gave no timeline for when that might occur. He dubbed those coins the "Four Horsemen" in a post on X (formerly Twitter), calling for price targets ranging from 53% to 108% more than as of Nov. 23. He also expects the rally to come before the artificial intelligence (AI) bubble ends.

Hayes also said that his "conviction is meh," regarding the group, but he will probably not be completely wrong, so let's analyze his argument in a bit more detail.

Let's start from the point on which Hayes is very likely to be correct. (Related: How to Start a Side Business While Working Full-Time...)

In September, Hayes called Ethereum his "number one pick" because "everybody hates it." That checks out, as it's down 38% during the past 12 months, and sentiment among investors, including longtime evangelists, is in the dumps. Even if it's disfavored right now, it's still the financial center of gravity for the crypto sector, with $128.6 billion in stablecoins on its chain, as well as $37.1 billion in capital locked in the decentralized finance (DeFi) applications in its ecosystem.

CRYPTO · As of 11:41 AM ET 11/24/26.

Sources indicate that all of that capital on its network means it could just be a matter of time before the Ethereum ecosystem produces some new projects or perhaps even entire new segments that restore enough enthusiasm to raise its price. What's more, the Ethereum Foundation is working on a set of privacy features that will let consumers do some of their DeFi business without revealing their financial details. An early version of its wallet privacy toolkit was released in May. (Related: How to Budget for a Wedding Without Going Broke...)

Zcash will likely also remain an emerging leader among the major cryptocurrencies because it enables holders to keep their transaction details and wallet balances private in its "shielded" addresses. Its price is up 58% this year, but the more important point is that it's also up by 76% compared to 12 months ago, despite the crypto bear market that started in October 2025, unlike nearly all other altcoins. The long-term thesis for buying Zcash is based around the rising demand for financial privacy, which isn't likely to go out of fashion anytime soon.

Industry observers note that zcash also stands to benefit from the success of NEAR, which enables a single account to swap assets across more than 35 blockchains, and, since February, users can make their transfers confidential. Bridging Zcash's privacy via NEAR additionally means that NEAR itself will get more utilization by way of extending privacy where Zcash's shielded pools would not otherwise be able to go.

Separately, Venice is a private AI platform, and its staked tokens can be locked to the network to mint credits for its AI computing services. So, like Zcash and NEAR, and to a lesser extent Ethereum, Venice sells privacy.

According to recent reports, investors should probably not rely on Arthur Hayes' (or anyone else's) bullishness to guide their own investments. In June, Hayes dumped the three cryptocurrencies he had dubbed his "Holy Trinity" in May, which had also included Zcash and NEAR. His Nov. 16 post states he now holds the four in "small to medium" size.

More importantly, these are risky investments.

Even by the standards of crypto, where everything is high-risk, buying maturing altcoins like NEAR and Zcash, and emerging coins like Venice, is an especially hazardous prospect considering the persistent market turbulence and the still-lingering crypto bear market.

Ethereum is probably the asset with the strongest investment thesis of the four at the moment, but investors should keep their expectations low; a lot of its growth over the near term might just stem from its price rebounding from overly pessimistic levels.

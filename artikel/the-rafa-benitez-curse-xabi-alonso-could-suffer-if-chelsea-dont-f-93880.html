It has been revealed that as the half-time whistle blew on another dire afternoon for Chelsea, boos emanated from parts of Stamford Bridge. They were on level terms with Leeds at this point; it was about to get a hell of a lot worse for the Blues fans in attendance as they watched their team, playing without purpose, get overrun.

The outpouring of anger from what was left of the home support was far more unanimous an hour or so later. Leeds had run out 4-1 victors, a scoreline that frankly flattered Chelsea. Barring a flash of brilliance from Emmanuel Emegha for the opener, the hosts had been absolutely outclassed - and it hadn’t looked particularly laborious for the visitors. Like Tottenham fans a month earlier, the travelling Leeds contingent found themselves “ole-ing” at their opponents’ expense, enjoying total control at a ground they hadn’t previously won at this century.

Chelsea were embarrassed in a grudge match, again - and patience feels like it is really beginning to run thin. “I understand their disappointment for sure and we share that with them and whatever we can do is work, try to give our best to turn it back and to do much better,” Xabi Alonso mentioned. He is fighting fires three months into the season.

Industry observers note that this is a fourth defeat in five for Alonso, a run that started with the sacrilegious home defeat to Spurs. That sort of result turned the fanbase against Antonio Conte in 2018, less than a year after he’d guided them to the title - so it was imperative for Alonso to regroup his side promptly in order to avoid meeting the same end. That he has not done. Victory over a pitiful Manchester United team aside, Chelsea have failed to come close to inspiring in recent weeks. And now, amid this atrocious run of form, they actually have more Premier League losses to their name than crisis-ridden United. “We are in a fragile moment mentally,” admitted Alonso, whose side is spiralling down the table.

Recent findings suggest that the team looks systemically lost. In defence, Alonso has flip-flopped between a back three and back four on a gamely basis, though the confusion doesn’t seem like it’s hindering Chelsea’s opposition more than it does themselves. They are persistently look open and passive - and against a team like Leeds, they were bullied, just as they were against Sunderland. This goes for their midfield, too, especially without Moises Caicedo - who was withdrawn through injury midway through the first half.

Industry observers note that and things aren’t much better going forward. What was initially touted as one of the most dangerous front threes in the league has turned toothless. The absence of Joao Pedro continues to be painfully detrimental, with Chelsea missing what he offers in the build-up along with his clinical finish. For parts of the first half, it felt like Emegha might be the solution Alonso has been searching for, finally playing a natural No 9 after three league matches of opting for alternatives. But the Dutchman faded and Chelsea ended the game with just an xG of 0.56. (Related: Industry News: Using & Mixing Hugging Face Models with ...)

It has been revealed that before the visit of Leeds, Alonso stated: “I always know my best team for the next game we have.” It’s how he justified making the second most line-up changes of any other manager in the top-flight: 24. That rose to 28 on Saturday afternoon, and the result that followed confirmed that Alonso is still some way off finding his best team, or at least one with a clear identity.

It has been revealed that this is obviously a cause for concern, because the Chelsea job is not known for having much of a grace period. The club’s reactionary ownership and culture of change is an occupational hazard that Alonso would have been fully aware of when he put pen to paper last Spring. But usually, the fanbase is more forgiving, especially in early days of a project.

One notable exception to this comes to mind, though. The interim appointment of Rafa Benitez in 2012, replacing fan favourite Roberto Di Matteo, was met with chagrin due to his Liverpool past. Fans never took to the Spaniard, even after he led them to the Europa League title and a third-place finish. His tenure was ultimately short-lived, with his rival Jose Mourinho preferred the following summer.

According to recent reports, as Chelsea’s form metastasises, this buried anti-Liverpool sentiment is beginning to effervesce at the surface. Discontent towards the team could not even wait until full-time against Leeds, and fingers are being pointed at the manager.

Alonso arrived with a certain aura, the Spanish footballing mastermind that had turned Bayer Leverkusen into Bundesliga invincibles just two years earlier. But the honeymoon period is over; Alonso now must produce results to prevent it from boiling over, and risk falling to the same fate as his countryman and former boss.

It has been revealed that chelsea welcome Liverpool in three weeks’ time, a fixture which could determine whether Alonso sinks or swims. He cannot afford more disappointment in front of his own supporters against an arch-rival, not least his first English love. It remains to be seen whether this relationship is salvageable.

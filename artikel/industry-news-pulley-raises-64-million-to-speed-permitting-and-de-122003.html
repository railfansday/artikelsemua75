📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Pulley, a development technology organization that helps multifamily and commercial development projects navigate permitting and approvals, has raised $64 million in fresh funding to expand its AI-driven services, the company announced this week.

Recent findings suggest that the financing includes a $41 million Series B round led by Jump Capital with participation from Catalyst Investors, and a $23 million Series A round led by CRV and Susa Ventures. Pulley remarked the capital follows 5x year-over-year growth in annual recurring revenue and will support both expansion of its permitting business and fresh products aimed at compressing pre-construction timelines.

Pulley has partnered with multifamily developers like Wood Partners, as well as large retailers, restaurants, banks, and manufacturers, including Amazon, Starbucks, JLL and Meta. (Related: The Partnership: Amazon SageMaker and Hugging Face...)

Recent findings suggest that pulley operates in entitlement, due diligence and approvals, a part of the development lifecycle that has grown increasingly complex for multifamily and commercial real estate. The tech giant estimates that 80% of project time is now spent before construction begins, as developers navigate zoning, environmental review, design iterations and building codes that vary widely across jurisdictions. Industry observers note that “The speed at which we build is one of the most vital challenges facing America over the next decade,” stated Charlie Jacobson, Pulley co-founder and CEO, in the announcement. “To remain a global leader in AI, bring manufacturing back home, reach energy independence and sustain job growth, we need to build orders of magnitude faster. The single biggest lever to building faster is to get started faster.”.

Pulley stated it combines AI-powered tools with a nationwide team of more than 75 licensed experts and a proprietary regulatory database to help clients secure approvals “faster and more predictably.” The company reports that it has supported $21 billion in commercial construction projects across all 50 states and thousands of cities and counties, saving customers a combined 11,750 weeks of project time.

Recent findings suggest that pulley’s services include diligence, approval strategies, construction plan reviews and coordination with local agencies. The organization also participates in private plan review programs, allowing it to approve building plans directly in place of local jurisdictions in more than 2,000 cities and counties, according to the announcement.

It has been revealed that the organization mentioned customers typically see just one round of comments from permitting authorities per project, and more than 40% of projects are approved without any feedback. Pulley reported that projects on its system have received 65% fewer review comments this year compared with 2025, as its models and rules engine learn from each submission.

With the new funding, Pulley prepares to extend beyond permitting into other pre-construction decisions that affect cost and schedule. New capabilities will combine zoning and permitting data with utility availability, local sentiment and other constraints to help owners and developers evaluate sites and programs.

Pulley is also rolling out AI-assisted drawing review designed to flag building code compliance complications earlier in design, reducing redesign cycles and surprises during plan check.

It has been revealed that lengthy permitting and entitlement timelines are a major driver of project risk and carrying costs for residential and commercial developers, particularly in high-growth markets where local agencies are understaffed and regulations are changing rapidly. For build-to-rent sponsors, single-family developers and multifamily owners, a slow or unpredictable approvals process can derail capital aims and delay unit delivery into tight markets.

Tools that standardize and partially automate permitting, codify jurisdictional rules and centralize reviewer feedback are increasingly seen as a way to protect returns and reduce soft costs. Pulley’s growth and new capital signal continued investor interest in technology focused on the “paper side” of development rather than just construction itself, and could accelerate adoption of private plan review and AI-enabled compliance checks across more jurisdictions.

Rivian Is Inviting Owners To Beta Test Its New Point-To-Point Assisted Driving Tech

🌐 Source: Insideevs • 📅 17 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (628 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Sources indicate that rivian started gauging interest for its Autonomy Early Access Program last month.
  • Sources indicate that the automaker updated its website recently to outline the requirements it will use to decide whether or not to grant access to the program.
  • It has been revealed that for starters, Rivian is only accepting applicants from either California or Florida.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that rivian started gauging interest for its Autonomy Early Access Program last month. They asked owners to let the manufacturer know if they were interested in being one of the first drivers to get their hands on its point-to-point driving software. This week, it will begin reviewing those applicants and, if chosen, owners will be welcomed into the Early Access beta—with one catch.

Sources indicate that the automaker updated its website recently to outline the requirements it will use to decide whether or not to grant access to the program. It turns out that it's not as easy as just clicking an "I Agree" button or paying for some sort of subscription. Rivian actually has a strict set of requirements that it uses to pick its testers.

It has been revealed that for starters, Rivian is only accepting applicants from either California or Florida. That's expected to change later, but for now, Rivian says that owners outside of these states can express their interest so it can decide where to expand the program later on.

Sources indicate that then there are the vehicle requirements. Owners must have one of the automaker's latest Rivian R2 electric SUVs, or an R1 vehicle on its Gen 2 platform (sorry, Gen 1 owners). You also need a paid Autonomy+ subscription, and Rivian will consider how frequently you use its existing autonomy features while driving.

Industry observers note that you also have to be a safe driver. Rivian notes it will review your driving safety score, which evaluates in-car events like forward collision warnings, true collision events, and "other safety-related metrics." RivianTrackr says that the program terms state that the automaker may also look into your driving history to determine if it is free from "DUI, reckless driving, license suspension, major moving violations or at-fault accidents.". Rivian's own terms note that to remain eligible and enrolled, participants will be expected to use the point-to-point features weekly, provide feedback via surveys, log issues (if they arise), and share certain data under a Non-Disclosure Agreement. RivianTrackr says the agreement includes location and camera data.

According to recent reports, the entire idea behind the Early Access Program is to focus on Rivian's Point to Point autonomy, a Rivian spokesperson confirmed to InsideEVs. And while it doesn't have a deadline for how many folks it will enroll in the program (or how long the program will last), Rivian says it prepares to scale it rapidly and make the features available for new vehicle test drives at a later date. Industry observers note that while most of the program details and point-to-point features are tight-lipped as of now, it likely won't be long before we get to hear some of the juicy details. Rivian's AI & Autonomy Day is coming up fast on December 9th, which Rivian states is "all about" Autonomy+.

So if you're an onlooker, you may have to wait another three weeks before some key details are shared. But if you're an owner who happens to meet all of the criteria to join the Early Access Program, Rivian says you can register your interest via your Rivian account for a chance to be selected to join.

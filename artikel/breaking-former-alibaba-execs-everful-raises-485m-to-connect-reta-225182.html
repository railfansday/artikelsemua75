Everful, a San Francisco- and Singapore-based startup that connects smaller retailers and brands with Chinese factories and manages the process from order to delivery, has raised $48.5 million in Series A funding.

The round was led by Vertex Ventures Southeast Asia & India and January Capital, with participation from existing investors Square Peg and Lightspeed.

Sources indicate that song, a former Alibaba executive, and serial entrepreneur Kevin Han, who sold a previous manufacturer to Amazon, co-founded Everful in 2024. The company began as a jewelry wholesale business before evolving into a multi-category, factory-direct sourcing platform. (Related: Latest Update: F1 star Max Verstappen played 40 hours o...)

Recent findings suggest that retailers and brands often rely on intermediaries, face high minimum order requirements, and experience long sourcing cycles, which tie up capital in inventory before sales trends are clear. Song noticed the growing gap between fast-changing consumer trends and traditional wholesale sourcing models while working as a venture investor at Bessemer Venture Partners, where she mostly focused on cross-border sourcing and meeting numerous startups in the sector. That same gap shaped Everful’s goal to simplify buying directly from factories through an AI-enabled platform. (Related: Runaway horse visits McDonald’s before leading police o...)

Industry observers note that the B2B sourcing industry is highly competitive. Big Tech giant Alibaba connects international buyers with manufacturers and suppliers, while Faire, a San Francisco-based wholesale tech platform, enables retailers to purchase wholesale inventory directly from brands. According to Everful’s co-founders, its key differentiator is responsibility for the sourcing process, rather than simply facilitating transactions.

“We take care of everything,” Han mentioned. “We handle sourcing, order consolidation, quality control, and logistics. That’s something the marketplaces don’t offer.”.

Everful is built for smaller retailers and brands without dedicated sourcing teams, Song said, and it also verifies the factories it works with. While peers also offer services such as quality inspection, logistics, and AI-powered sourcing, Everful aims to integrate these capabilities into a more streamlined purchasing experience, creating an end-to-end process that reduces the work retailers must handle themselves.

Han added that Everful uses AI agents to automate labor-intensive sourcing tasks, including tracking consumer trends, identifying suppliers, coordinating orders across factories, and managing logistics.

It has been revealed that because Everful sources from Chinese factories, ongoing U.S.-China trade tensions and tariffs bear directly on its business. Song said the co-founders have been thinking about geopolitical concerns since day one of building the firm, and that this has shaped Everful’s strategy from the outset, leveraging her experience at Alibaba and Bessemer Venture Partners.

She mentioned the Trump administration’s suspension of the de minimis exemption, which had allowed eligible shipments valued at $800 or less to enter the U.S. duty-free, as one example of a shift in trade policy.

Song explained such changes could favor a model that combines overseas sourcing with local retail sales. “We’re empowering local retailers and brands,” she noted. “Long-term cross-border commerce will be cross-border B2B sourcing plus local selling.”.

Everful aims to use the Series A round to expand its presence in the U.S. and Europe, particularly the U.K., France, and Italy, while investing in AI to automate product customization and other sourcing workflows. It also aims to deepen integrations with e-commerce platforms, building on its Shopify integration as it works toward becoming an end-to-end infrastructure platform for global sourcing.

The startup employs about 70 full-time staff globally, including 45 in research and development. It has also established an AI operations team to connect engineering and business functions.

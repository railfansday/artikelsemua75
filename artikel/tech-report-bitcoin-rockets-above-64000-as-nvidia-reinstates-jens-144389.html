Bitcoin jumped more than 4% in an hour to $64,427 after Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO on Monday.

The sudden rally triggered $424 million in crypto market liquidations within an hour, according to CoinGlass, as traders betting on further declines were caught by the price reversal.

Short positions accounted for $376 million, or 92.2% of the liquidations, while bullish traders lost $31.58 million.

In a recent development, bitcoin led the losses with $188 million in liquidations, 95.6% of which came from short positions. Ethereum followed with around $80 million, with shorts accounting for 92.9%.

Together, the two largest cryptocurrencies accounted for nearly 64% of the $403.98 million liquidated during the hour.

Sources indicate that that decline had briefly pushed Bitcoin below $60,000 following Huang's abrupt departure from Nvidia on Nov. 12, which triggered turmoil across technology stocks and added pressure to an already struggling crypto market.

Monday's reversal also lifted Nvidia shares by roughly 33% to around $170 in premarket trading, reversing part of the losses accumulated during the leadership crisis.

Nvidia's decision to reinstate Huang marks a dramatic reversal from its position last week, when board Chairman Harvey Jones ruled out bringing the organization's co-founder back as chief executive.

The board voted Monday unanimously to restore Huang as president, CEO, and a director after discussions that satisfied members about his candor and ability to lead the organization.

Nvidia also said senior US government officials provided input during the deliberations, giving directors confidence that Huang's return posed no national security risk.

“The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers.”.

Huang's return followed mounting pressure from shareholders, technology executives and President Donald Trump, who had publicly supported reinstating the Nvidia co-founder.

Strive CEO Matt Cole welcomed the decision, saying Nvidia was the largest single holding among Strive Asset Management's clients, with nearly $100 million invested through its ETFs.

Cole stated Strive had prepared to submit a shareholder proposal requesting an advisory vote on Huang's reinstatement before Monday's market opening, but the board acted before the proposal became necessary.

Huang, who had previously expressed willingness to return, said he was honored to resume leadership of the tech giant. Nvidia CFO Colette Kress, who served as interim CEO, will return to her previous responsibilities.

However, Huang's reinstatement does not end the investigation into reports that he was targeted by a foreign intelligence operation.

Nvidia noted its independent board committee would complete that investigation while a newly established national security and export control committee oversees trade compliance and related risks.

The manufacturer is also recruiting a chief compliance officer and will continue cooperating with government investigations.

Meanwhile, Bitcoin's rapid recovery marks its second sharp Nvidia-linked rally within a week.

The cryptocurrency briefly reached approximately $64,600 following Nvidia's stronger-than-expected earnings last week, only to surrender most of those gains within 35 minutes.

Monday's advance returned Bitcoin to within roughly $173 of that previous high, with more than $372 million in forced short closures accompanying the move.

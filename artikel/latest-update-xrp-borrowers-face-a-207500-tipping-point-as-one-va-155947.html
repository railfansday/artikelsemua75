📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that A single Sentora vault supplied virtually all the Ripple USD (RLUSD) in Morpho’s Ethereum market for loans against wrapped XRP on Nov. 23.

The market showed about 7.1 million RLUSD in outstanding loans and 1 million RLUSD available for new loans. Borrowers use FXRP, a token backed by XRP, to borrow the RLUSD stablecoin.

Sentora’s lending decisions can change both how much credit is available and the interest rate on existing loans.

At about 13:08 UTC, Morpho’s market dashboard put the Sentora RLUSD Main vault’s contribution at about 8.15 million RLUSD, or 99.99% of the market’s supply as displayed.

Borrowers had used 87.45% of the funds supplied to the market, a measure Morpho calls utilization, and the dashboard revealed a variable borrow rate of 4.99%.

Industry observers note that earlier on Nov. 23, one wallet posted 3,000 FXRP as collateral and borrowed 1,500 RLUSD in the same transaction, according to Morpho’s indexed activity. (Related: Industry News: ‘Minecraft Mommy’ goes viral on TikTok f...)

Flare’s FXRP documentation describes FXRP as a tokenized representation of native XRP, and its FAssets system verifies XRP Ledger transactions used to mint FXRP and supports redemption for native XRP.

Industry observers note that flare’s August announcement describes how holders can mint FXRP on Flare, bridge it to Ethereum through the FAssets Portal or Stargate, and post it as collateral in this market. Anyone already holding the market’s FXRP on Ethereum can start at the collateral step. (Related: Industry News: Twitch streamer’s PS5 catches fire after...)

It has been revealed that A borrower’s position can be liquidated if its debt exceeds 77% of the collateral’s value, as measured by the market’s price feed. A drop in collateral value can push a borrower closer to that threshold even without taking out another loan.

In a recent development, in a separate Nov. 23 transaction, the market log highlights the Sentora vault withdrew 7,147.88 RLUSD. The vault’s allocation history shows the same transaction moving funds in two other lending markets, one backed by USDe and the other by syrupUSDC.

A later withdrawal of 10,498.74 RLUSD from the FXRP market appears in both logs.

The FXRP market made up just 2.41% of the funds allocated by the Sentora vault, which reported 338.36 million RLUSD in deposits and 95.27 million RLUSD in liquidity across the vault.

Recent findings suggest that in October, the same market had about 8.53 million RLUSD from Sentora and a 10 million RLUSD allocation cap. By Nov. 23, the cap was 15 million RLUSD, while Sentora supplied about 8.15 million.

It has been revealed that the available funds for fresh borrowers were the 1 million RLUSD already in the market. Morpho’s liquidity API highlighted zero additional RLUSD available at about 13:10 UTC through its Public Allocator, which can move funds from participating vaults when their settings permit it.

In a recent development, other borrows or allocations can change the amount available before a transaction executes. That same reading revealed that about 207,500 RLUSD of additional borrowing would bring the market to 90% utilization with supply unchanged.

It has been revealed that upcoming loans could continue above the 90% utilization target while funds remained available. Morpho’s borrowing-rate curve steepens above that point.

Recent findings suggest that balances and allocation capacity could move before execution. The dashboard and API readings were captured about two minutes apart.

In the zoom, the first 20.3% represents 207,480.83 RLUSD divided by 1,022,276.09 RLUSD from Morpho’s liquidity API. Labels round the underlying values.

Balances and allocation capacity could move before execution. The dashboard and API readings were captured about two minutes apart.

It has been revealed that the 90% mark is the reference point for Morpho’s interest-rate model. Borrowing can continue above that level while funds are available, with a steeper rate curve above the target.

If Sentora takes unborrowed RLUSD out of the market while debt stays constant, utilization rises, and the variable rate can climb without anyone taking out a fresh loan. Adding supply with debt unchanged gives new borrowers more room and can ease rates for existing borrowers.

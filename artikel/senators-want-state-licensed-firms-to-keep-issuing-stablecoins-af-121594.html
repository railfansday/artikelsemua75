Senators want state-licensed firms to keep issuing stablecoins after Jan. 18

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Six senators are asking Treasury to let qualifying state-regulated stablecoin issuers keep putting newly introduced tokens into circulation while their states’ rules are under federal review. The GENIUS Act takes effect on January 18, 2027. Treasury’s proposal recognizes temporary relief for eligible federal applicants that get a waiver. It leaves open what happens if a state’s regulatory framework still awaits certification.

Industry observers note that sens. Cynthia Lummis and Kirsten Gillibrand led the bipartisan November 17 letter, which Lummis’s office released November 18. They asked Treasury for temporary protection during certification for issuers in states that will have a qualifying framework in place before January 18, 2028. They warned that a clearer federal route could steer issuers toward a federal license. (Related: Industry News: Hyperparameter Search with Transformers ...)

The proposed Section 3 rule would recognize relief for two groups with applications pending when the law takes effect. One is a subsidiary of an insured depository institution whose parent has applied on its behalf. The other is an applicant seeking to become a federally qualified payment stablecoin issuer. The primary federal regulator must grant a waiver, and its terms must cover the relevant Section 3 restriction. Relief can run for up to 12 months from the law’s effective date.

The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency (OCC) has a separate final rule that requires a written request and a substantially complete issuer application pending on or before January 18, 2027. It can grant relief on public-interest grounds or in extraordinary circumstances. Its licensing page claims it has yet to open applications or waiver requests and is awaiting clearance for its application forms.

Sources indicate that treasury’s proposal treats the first transfer that gives someone outside the issuer the right to use, transfer or redeem the token as issuance. An issuer could create tokens for its own inventory and still face a restriction when it tries to put those tokens into circulation.

The OCC waiver rule gives active issuers a route to request temporary relief before the January deadline.

It has been revealed that treasury asked whether issuers should get relief before a state files for certification or while a review is pending. Four state regulatory and legislative groups asked for such protection in October. New York’s Department of Financial Services offered a different reading. It states qualifying issuers can keep issuing if their state’s rules are substantially similar and the state files within a year, until any final denial. It wants Treasury to confirm that reading in the final rule. (Related: How to generate text: using different decoding methods ...)

In the preamble to its interim rule, the Stablecoin Certification Review Committee described the state option for issuers with $10 billion or less in consolidated outstanding stablecoin issuance as requiring a state filing and committee approval. The committee has 30 days to decide once a complete certification has been submitted. It will seek to expedite initial reviews after the Act takes effect for states that had a qualifying regime in place by January 14, 2026. New York says it expects to qualify for expedited review. The initial state filing is due within a year of the Act taking effect.

According to recent reports, the committee says it will start accepting certifications once the forms clear federal review. The paperwork record still reveals the certification forms under review. If Treasury treats committee approval as a prerequisite and offers no temporary relief, issuers that depend on the state route could have to wait to issue upcoming units.

Treasury’s proposal recognizes express federal waivers. It leaves the treatment of issuers under pending state certifications open.

A January pause could affect upcoming supply before the general platform-sales rule applies.

Foreign-issued stablecoins face separate platform requirements from January 18, 2027.

It has been revealed that the two branches map the federal waiver in proposed § 1523.4(a) and Treasury’s question about relief while state certification is pending. Newly introduced York’s legal reading comes from its October 5 comment. The dates are statutory milestones.

Foreign-issued stablecoins face separate platform requirements from January 18, 2027.

In a recent development, fresh York’s approvals show why the legal issuer matters. The state’s Greenlist and charter register identify Ripple USD (RLUSD) and WisdomTree Dollar (USDW) as approved for issuance in the state. Ripple’s November 16 terms name Ripple Custody & Trust Co., the trade name of New York-chartered Standard Custody & Trust Firm, LLC, as the RLUSD issuer.

The OCC charter record shows that Ripple National Trust Bank’s charter became effective October 26. Ripple said Standard Custody would continue issuing RLUSD, with the separate national bank authorized to manage reserves and provide custody.

Recent findings suggest that wisdomTree identifies a different Newly introduced York trust, WisdomTree Digital Trust Firm, LLC, as USDW’s issuer. For either coin, a federal waiver would have to cover issuance by the legal issuer.

In a recent development, ZUSD still appears on the Greenlist. Its issuer, GMO-Z.com Trust Company, Inc., removed the mint function in May during a voluntary wind-down. The issuer stated direct redemptions ended November 11 and that remaining balances are subject to mandatory redemption. ZUSD’s wind-down was already underway months before Treasury published its proposal.

Under the GENIUS Act, January 18, 2027 is the effective date for the U.S. issuance restriction. If a state-regulated issuer has to pause, the immediate effect would be on its ability to supply fresh units to customers and distributors. Trading already-issued tokens and redeeming them with the issuer are separate activities.

According to recent reports, starting July 18, 2028, digital asset service providers such as exchanges generally can offer or sell payment stablecoins to people in the U.S. only if the issuer is permitted or an exception applies.

In a recent development, A separate foreign-issuer provision applies from the Act’s effective date. It conditions U.S. platform availability on the foreign issuer’s ability and commitment to comply with lawful orders and reciprocal arrangements. Section 18 also provides a route for foreign issuers from comparable regimes that register with the OCC and meet other conditions.

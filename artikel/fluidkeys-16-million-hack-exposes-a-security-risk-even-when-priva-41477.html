It has been revealed that fluidkey said it restored its privacy wallet on Nov. 20, a day after taking it offline following unauthorized transfers.

Sending funds requires an app patch, and users who spot an unfamiliar transfer can look up their own receiving address or transaction hash on the relevant blockchain.

In a recent development, the company announced the return of its web, iOS, and Android apps in v1.79.22. It said older versions would stay offline and told customers to open Settings and select Update to re-enable transactions. (Related: AI for Game Development: Creating a Farming Game in 5 D...)

It has been revealed that fluidkey attributed the incident to flaws in a third-party logging tool and noted customers’ private keys stayed on their devices, and said it took services offline on Nov. 19. In its upgrade that evening, it said it had seen no further unauthorized transactions since the shutdown and was working with SEAL 911 to investigate and trace funds. (Related: Industry News: One Year Since the “DeepSeek Moment”...)

Its initial warning to avoid the site and app came before the new version was available.

Blockchain analytics firm Bitquery traced roughly $1.6 million in payouts to wallets it linked to the incident across six chains, including Base and Ethereum.

The independent Fluidkey drain tracker, maintained by pepebndc, put the total near $1.63 million across seven chains in its Nov. 20 morning snapshot. That estimate includes suspected destinations and excludes likely owner withdrawals.

Bitquery says the tracker labeled one Polygon payout as an owner withdrawal, linking it to the incident after following the recipient’s activity on Base.

The firm also separated the incident-linked payments from about $349,000 sent to wallets with an established history during its hour-long scan.

That group includes ordinary payments before the drain and about $277,000 sent during it to an older wallet across Ethereum and Base. Bitquery interpreted the latter as one user moving their own funds, and left another $65,000 unclassified.

Fluidkey’s standing recovery guide describes SARA as a last-resort option for verifying addresses or accessing funds. It warns that moving funds outside the regular app may impair app functionality.

In a recent development, in its incident revision, Fluidkey remarked it would contact affected consumers individually and publish a full post-mortem. It warned about impersonation and said its staff would never contact users privately through social media, and directed users to its official website for support.

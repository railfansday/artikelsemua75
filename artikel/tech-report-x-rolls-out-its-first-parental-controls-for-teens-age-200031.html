Tech Report: X rolls out its first parental controls for teens aged 13 to 17

🌐 Source: Thenextweb • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (470 words, 📖 Standard)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • X is rolling out its first parental controls, for teens aged 13 to 17.
  • Parents can also filter words, turn off sensitive media, and choose who can message the teen.
  • Mridul Singhai, X’s product engineering lead, framed the feature around the teen’s privacy, as Tom Warren reported for The Verge.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

X is rolling out its first parental controls, for teens aged 13 to 17. A parent or guardian can see how much time a teen spends on X and set a daily limit. They can also approve newly introduced followers and limit the For You feed to accounts the teen already follows, according to X’s Help Center.

Parents can also filter words, turn off sensitive media, and choose who can message the teen. They can see which accounts the teen chats with one-to-one, but they cannot read those messages.

Mridul Singhai, X’s product engineering lead, framed the feature around the teen’s privacy, as Tom Warren reported for The Verge.

Industry observers note that “Parents get guardrails, not a window into their teen’s private life. Simple, transparent, and built to grow with families,” Singhai stated. (Related: Breaking: Get your VLM running in 3 simple steps on Int...)

The parent starts the link from Parental Controls, and X reveals a QR code and a link. The teen scans the code or opens the link and sees what the parent will be able to do. Then the teen accepts or declines. An invite expires after 72 hours.

A teen can have one supervisor at a time, and a parent can supervise up to five teens. X uses the birth date on each account to decide who is an adult and who is a teen. X may also ask some users to prove their age with a selfie check or an ID check.

In a recent development, the daily limit is set in 15-minute steps. Once a teen reaches it, X locks for the rest of the day and resets at midnight local time. (Related: Tech Report: AI for Game Development: Creating a Farmin...)

It has been revealed that parents cannot see searches or viewing history, post as the teen, or sign in to the account. Every change shows up in an activity log that the teen can also see. A teen can ask to end supervision, but the parent decides.

Michael O’Herlihy, X’s head of safety, said the tools balance what parents and teens need, Digital Trends reported.

“Parents want practical tools, and teens deserve room to grow,” O’Herlihy mentioned.

Sources indicate that X admits the platform is not aimed at children. Teen accounts already run in a protected mode, which restricts sensitive media and blocks targeted ads, The Verge reported. Rivals such as Instagram’s teen accounts and TikTok have offered similar tools for a while, according to Digital Trends.

Sources indicate that the controls arrive days after Virginia and Oregon sued Musk’s SpaceX over Grok’s sexual deepfakes. In September, Ireland’s media regulator opened an investigation into X’s age checks. The regulator also questioned whether X’s parental controls met minimum requirements, Digital Trends reported.

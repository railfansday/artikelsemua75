A former Premier League star who represented both Manchester United and Liverpool has named the club he believes is bigger, offering his verdict on a debate that reveals no signs of going away anytime soon.

The Reds, thanks to the hugely successful managerial reigns of Bill Shankly, Bob Paisley and, more recently, Jurgen Klopp, are the most successful club in English football history, having won a joint-record 20 league titles, as well as twice as many Champions League trophies as their fierce rivals 30 miles east. However, with the Red Devils enjoying the most dominant period of the modern era, winning 11 league titles under Sir Alex Ferguson during a time when football's commercial landscape was rapidly evolving, the argument from their corner is that they boast more social media followers, a larger global fanbase and, consequently, greater financial muscle in the transfer market, alongside a bigger stadium.

Sources indicate that clearly, this is no open-and-shut case. With plenty of grey areas when it comes to measuring the overall size of a football club, one former player who experienced life at both sides of the divide once attempted to settle the score, even bringing Real Madrid into the conversation for good measure. (Related: Latest Update: Taco Bell is bringing back the Steakhous...)

Recent findings suggest that paul Ince - one of England's greatest midfielders ever - played 81 times for Liverpool and 281 times for Man United. Revealing his pick for the bigger club out of the two, he remarked, as per All Football:.

"Man United's got to be the biggest club, as much as I love Liverpool. From a historical point of view, Man United with the Busby Babes, all the stuff that's gone on historically.

It has been revealed that from winning trophies... Liverpool had their time with European Cups and that, but I think if you looked at the biggest club, even from a financial point of view, you'd say Manchester United is the club that everybody talks about globally. There's Liverpool, Man United and Real Madrid but the biggest club has got to be Man United.".

Ince played for the Red Devils from 1989 to 1995 under manager Sir Alex Ferguson, helping end the club's four-year trophy drought and 22-year top-flight title wait in the 1992/93 season, which also saw him win the club's Player of the Year award.

A year later, he would help United retain that title, adding to a trophy cabinet that also consisted of a League Cup (1992), and the European Cup Winners' Cup (1991).

Michael Owen is the most famous example of players to have represented both sides. The former Liverpool striker became a household name at Anfield, scoring 158 goals in 297 appearances and winning the Ballon d'Or in 2001. After spells at Real Madrid and Newcastle United, he moved to Old Trafford in 2009, where he spent three seasons, winning a Premier League title in 2010/11.

Other notable players include Peter Beardsley, who played for United early in his career before becoming a Liverpool star in the late 1980s, and Phil Chisnall, who made the controversial move directly from United to Liverpool in 1964.

More recently, Gabriel Heinze was set to play for both clubs, although his proposed move from United to Liverpool in 2007 was famously blocked by Sir Alex Ferguson, with the Argentine instead joining Real Madrid.

There have also been players such as Tom Chorlton, Arthur Riley and Fred Hopkin who crossed between the clubs during earlier periods of their histories.

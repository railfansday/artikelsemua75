Industry observers note that back in the 1990s, Michael Bay was really cooking. Before the slog that was 2001’s Pearl Harbor and his divisive Transformers movies that simultaneously hurt and enhanced his career by sidelining him for decades, Bay debuted as a director with a trio of really exciting movies that prioritized a fun experience for the viewer. First there was 1995’s Bad Boys, the Will Smith and Martin Lawrence buddy-cop film that became a popular franchise. He closed out the decade with the genre-defining end-of-the-world disaster film Armageddon. And in between those two, he gave us a tightly crafted thriller that is currently free to stream (with ads) on Pluto TV.

The Rock stars Sean Connery in one of his last great roles as John Patrick Mason, a cunning British Army officer who was imprisoned 30 years earlier without a trial​​​​​​​, for stealing classified U.S. information. He’s also the only man to ever escape Alcatraz, and the U.S. government​​​​​​​ is now offering him his freedom if he’ll help a team of Marines break into the long-shuttered prison he once broke out of. Alcatraz, nicknamed “The Rock,” has been taken over by rogue Marine general Francis X. Hummel (Ed Harris) and a brigade​​​​​​​ of loyal soldiers. Hummel is bitter about the government covering up some Marine deaths during Operation: Desert Storm and now he’s demanding restitution for the families (and himself). He’s also holding Alcatraz tourists hostage​​​​​​​ and has deadly chemical weapons pointed at San Francisco, which he’ll fire into the city if they don’t comply. Recent findings suggest that that’s why the government also pairs Mason with bookish FBI agent Dr. Stanley Goodspeed​​​​​​​, a chemical weapons expert played by Nicolas Cage.

In a recent development, as a duo, Connery and Cage have wonderful chemistry in the film. While Connery was well into his 60s at the time (as was his character), Mason​​​​​​​ was an adept ex-spy who was specially trained by the British intelligence service MI6​​​​​​​ and is still very capable for his age. This contrasts nicely with Cage’s character, who has no field experience and is nervous on the mission, but proves himself to be brave, capable, and adaptable. While not overly comedic, their relationship follows the typical buddy movie bromance where both gain respect for each other over the course of the film and they become trusted partners by the end of it.

Industry observers note that the movie is also relentlessly entertaining. It begins with a heist by Hummel’s forces to get the missiles, followed by their takeover of Alcatraz. Being the most knowledgeable person alive in regards to Alcatraz, the government calls upon Mason, who soon escapes in a wild car chase through San Francisco, just to get a little face time with his estranged daughter (Claire Forlani). Despite tearing apart the city with a stolen Hummer, Mason is quickly forgiven and the mission to sneak into Alcatraz proceeds normally. Once they get to the island, the movie has one tension-filled action set piece after another​​​​​​​. It’s what Bay does best.

In a recent development, the movie has no subtext and little subtlety​​​​​​​, but that’s honestly fine. It knows what it is and embraces its status as a fairly mindless action movie. There is some of the clunky dialogue that sometimes plagues Bay’s movies, but it’s not distractingly bad, I think because The Rock has three excellent leads who know how to work with subpar​​​​​​​ material and still create a compelling character.

According to recent reports, I rewatched The Rock last night; it was my first time seeing it in more than a decade.​​​​​​​ Not only did it still hold up as a fun watch, but I kept in mind the internet fan theory that Mason is actually Connery’s James Bond using an alias, having been captured by the U.S. for stealing some intelligence on behalf of MI6​​​​​​​. And while I’m not a Bond expert, it all does seem to line up pretty well with Mason’s personality and backstory. While the theory certainly isn’t canon to The Rock or the Bond films, it’s an interesting, added lens through which to watch the film.

It has been revealed that finally, another great thing about this film is that it was made during an era when all of these explosions were done with practical effects. While digital effects have certainly come a long way, you can feel the difference in a movie like The Rock. It’s the kind of thing that makes you long for ‘90s action films and kind of makes you wish that Bay never got involved in the Transformers franchise at all.

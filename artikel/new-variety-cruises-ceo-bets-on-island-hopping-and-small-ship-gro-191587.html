Steve Reynolds has spent his first few months as CEO of Variety Cruises building the systems he believes will carry the family-owned small-ship tech giant through its next stage of growth.

Reynolds was appointed in late October, with Filippos Venetopoulos, the third-generation head of the 77-year-old tech giant, moving to executive chairman. Reynolds joined from Discover Qatar, the destination management company of Qatar Airways Group, where he was senior vice president. He said he found a brand with a strong product and a story not yet fully told.

“The brand represents a style of travel, and as a style of travel it’s quite unique,” Reynolds told Cruise Industry News. (Related: Industry News: Retrieval Augmented Generation with Hugg...)

At its heart, he explained, Variety is an island-hopping product, and that kind of trip can be hard to manage alone.

According to recent reports, “It sounds all very romantic, island hopping, until you actually do it,” he mentioned. “If you want to do it using ferries, transfers and hotels, it can be tiring and logistically tough.”.

He compared the model to river cruising, where guests unpack once and wake up somewhere new.

His test for a small ship is simple.

“If you can’t swim off the back, it’s not a small ship,” he noted.

In a recent development, variety operates eight ships, with Greece as its core market. In 2027 the firm will add a Croatia product that also visits Montenegro. The Balearics, Corsica and the Italian islands are under consideration, depending on seasonal deployment and fleet logistics.

Recent findings suggest that north America is Variety’s lead source market, with solid contributions from the U.K., Europe, Australia and Newly introduced Zealand. Charters remain vital, especially alumni and educational travel such as Road Scholar, and tour operators also sell the product under their own brands. Direct bookings are also growing.

The near-term goal, Reynolds said, is to fill the existing fleet year-round before deciding whether to build, lease, buy or expand capacity.

Variety has started building a data warehouse to centralize customer and operational data, improve guest communication throughout the trip and use AI to automate processes and connect systems.

On his second day, Reynolds wrote about a dozen priority themes on his office whiteboard, including hyper-personalization, 2027 budgeting, reporting dashboards and guest quality metrics.

Much of how the business has always worked is good, he stated, but “if you can underpin that with strong principles around governance and using data to make and validate decisions, they tend to be much better decisions.”.

According to recent reports, reynolds mentioned southern hemisphere travelers who usually connect through Middle East hubs have become more cautious and are booking later. Europe is less affected, and North American demand for the Mediterranean, Seychelles and Tahiti remains steady.

He urged travelers to book while promotions last.

“My hope is in 12 months’ time we will have sold to an extent that there’ll be fewer of those deals available,” he said.

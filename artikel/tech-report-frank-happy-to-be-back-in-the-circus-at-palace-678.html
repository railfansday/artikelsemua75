Thomas Frank is happy to be "back in the circus" after speaking at his first news conference as Crystal Palace manager.

The 53-year-old, who was appointed on a two-and-a-half-year deal on Monday, has been out of work since he was sacked by Tottenham in February, less than eight months after taking the job.

But, rather than dwell on that disappointment, Frank hopes to use it as a learning exeprience as he attempts to improve Palace's fortunes and lift them off the bottom of the Premier League table. (Related: Few-shot learning in practice: GPT-Neo and the 🤗 Accele...)

"If you don't learn from any of your jobs, I don't think you reflect, I don't think you improve, and I don't think you get better," stated Frank.

"Of course there's a lot of things I have been reflecting over. The details, that will be for another time, but I can say I've learned, definitely.". (Related: Course Launch Community Event...)

The Dane remarked he could easily have extended his break from football, which he used to travel to Japan and Australia, as well as "visiting friends and family, eating too well [and] drinking too much wine" back in his homeland.

It has been revealed that "I was very aware I didn't want to do anything in the summer. I put a statement out there, I needed more time," he added.

"I can actually, honestly say I would have been fine if it was three, four, five, six, seven months more, because it's good to be back in the circus, but it's also unbelievably good not to be in, especially if you enjoy life, which I do.

"I was spending time with family and friends and doing all that, but I think the club, the way Palace is, how well Steve Parish especially spoke about the club and ideas, Matt Hobbs and Iain Moody and these guys, too, helped convince me it was a good idea.

"I'm a little bit surprised by this feeling I have in the body, how good it feels.".

How Frank can restore reputation and save Crystal Palace.

Frank's Palace reign begins with a trip to Coventry City on Saturday (15:00 GMT) and he states he returns to the dugout unbruised despite the way his stint at Spurs ended.

Industry observers note that "I think we all have an ego," the former Brentford boss remarked. "Some are better at controlling it than others. For me, it's not the critics, it's more what I can learn, what I can prove, how I can get better.

"There's a lot of clever people out there, but the interesting thing is they only know 10 per cent of the truth. That's a tenth of the truth at Brentford, there's a tenth of the truth at Tottenham, and that's just the way it is.

"If you can't handle that as a coach or a player, then you shouldn't be in this business, and I'm very happy to be back in the game.".

Mauricio Pochettino will never succeed as the Manchester United manager while he is forced to deal with the board's rotten decisions.

It is sad but painfully obvious to those watching United be torn apart by Liverpool in a defeat that felt so inevitable as soon as the manager's self-defeating teamsheet was revealed.

Chief executive Omar Berrada claimed recently that the back line was not a concern for the board, while insisting the team are improving and heading in the right direction.

Just over one week later, Man United have been dispatched 4-0 against their biggest rivals in a pitiful display which could have ended in an even wider scoreline.

They now have, statistically, the worst defence in Europe's top five leagues, with 34 goals conceded across all competitions so far this season.

While none of that can be placed on Pochettino's shoulders after just three training sessions, they are the same problems which have seen three previous managers sacked. (Related: Tech Report: Distributed Training: Train BART/T5 for Su...)

Erik ten Hag, Ruben Amorim, and most recently Michael Carrick, have all met the same fate under this board and they are showing no signs of changing any time soon.

Former Man United assistant manager Mick Brown told Football Insider on 16 November, shortly following Pochettino's appointment, that he is destined to fail at Old Trafford.

Rather than being an indictment on the manager, though, the concerns are with the board and the chief decision-makers who will be key to any success United have.

If Berrada and his head of recruitment Jason Wilcox genuinely believe that this squad is good enough to compete, and that they've made the right calls, they must be ousted.

The CEO speaking about the depth of options in defence, after they were forced to play two 20-year-olds in the closing stages against Aston Villa, was bizarre to begin with.

After a 4-0 defeat to Liverpool, there can be no more excuses for the board, and they have got to change their stance or risk being left behind.

Pochettino has been thrown into a nightmare situation, regardless of the allure the club still has, and will now be given an impossible job to turn their situation around.

Unfortunately, should Man United continue to struggle, the blame will fall at his feet regardless of whether he is truly at fault, while any improvements will be in spite of the board.

It is not just the fans who have been left raging at the board and the decisions made in recent weeks, as key players have also felt compelled to speak out against them.

Lisandro Martinez was the first and most vocal of them, as he directly tore into Wilcox and Berrada, questioning their transfers and suggesting they had thrown Carrick under the bus.

Carrick ultimately paid the price for that, but after players spoke out on social media following his departure, it is clear he is not the problem as far as they are concerned.

Martinez's comments, while misjudged in the moment, echoed the feelings of the majority of Man United supporters, and Pochettino will now face the same issues.

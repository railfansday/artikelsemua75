📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Shares of Nvidia $NVDA jumped 25% before the opening bell once its board brought Jensen Huang back as chief executive, and U.S. stock futures climbed sharply.

Contracts on the S&P 500 rose 4.3%, while those on the Nasdaq $NDAQ-100 climbed 7%. Dow Jones Industrial Average futures were up 1,229 points, a 2.6% increase.

President Donald Trump had written on Truth Social that he wanted Nvidia's board to bring Huang back as its chief executive, according to CNBC. Several additional companies tied to artificial intelligence gained as well before the opening bell. Micron $MU Technology was higher by 4%, while Advanced Micro Devices picked up 3% and CoreWeave leapt 5%.

Recent findings suggest that the Dow, the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq Composite have all entered a correction after a sell-off fueled by investor anxiety over artificial intelligence stocks. The Nasdaq is now down on a year-to-date basis. The Nasdaq-100 briefly traded in bear market territory, 20% below its Oct. 6 closing high, during Friday's session, but it closed just short of that mark.

The decline was the Nasdaq Composite's and the Dow's worst week in over six years, while the S&P 500 had not fallen as much in a single week since April 2025, according to CNBC.

The week is shortened. Markets are closed for Thanksgiving, and the session after the holiday ends with the closing bell at 1 p.m. ET.

It has been revealed that in Europe, markets rose in morning trade. The Stoxx 600 gained 0.9%, Germany's DAX rose 1%, France's CAC 40 added 0.8% and the U.K.'s FTSE 100 climbed 0.6%.

According to recent reports, most Asian markets finished the session in the red, with the slide in regional technology shares continuing. South Korea's Kospi lost 3.3% to close at 4,708.54, and Australia's S&P/ASX 200 slipped 0.3% to finish at 8,570.6. China's CSI 300 ended flat at 3,958.29. Markets in Japan did not open because of a national holiday.

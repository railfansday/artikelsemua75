Follow below for developments from the NATO Parliamentary Assembly on Monday, November 23:.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy called on Monday for fresh four-way negotiations with Russia – and involving European countries – to begin as soon as possible.

"We must do everything to make sure that the four-party meeting – Ukraine, Europe, America and Russia – happens as soon as possible," he told the 72nd NATO Parliamentary Assembly in Berlin via video link.

"We have heard from our partners that they want to try to make it happen before Christmas," he said. "We hope it happens sooner. We are ready to do this literally tomorrow.".

The Polish government intends to summon the Russian ambassador in response to a violation of its airspace which saw three Russian cruise missiles crash on its territory on Friday, the Polish Foreign Ministry said on Monday.

The security authorities and the military must first complete their investigations into the incident, a ministry spokesman told the Polish Press Agency (PAP).

On Saturday, Foreign Minister Radoslaw Sikorski stated that Warsaw would convey its "strongest displeasure" to the Russians over the violation of Polish airspace.

Following examination of debris found in southeastern Poland, state prosecutors stated that all three were Russian CH-101 cruise missiles.

The missiles are believed to have been manufactured in the fourth quarter of this year at an arms factory in Dubna, approximately 140 kilometers north of Moscow.

NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte said on Monday that Poland and allied countries were still working to establish whether the crossing of Russian missiles into Polish territory on Friday was intentional or not.

"It is ​clear ‌that this is a dangerous incident," he told ‌a ​meeting of the alliance's parliamentary assembly in Berlin.

"I will never ​speculate," he noted.

Western military intelligence services believe Russia is planning to station a nuclear weapon in space by the end of this year, German media reported on Monday morning.

According to public broadcasters NDR and WDR, as well as the Süddeutsche Zeitung broadsheet, intelligence chiefs believe Moscow intends to fire a satellite armed with a nuclear warhead into orbit. (Related: Industry News: Fine tuning CLIP with Remote Sensing (Sa...)

Intelligence regarding the intends was reportedly shared among NATO member states recently and German Defense Minister Boris Pistorius is said to have been informed by experts from Germany's armed forces, the Bundeswehr.

A Bundeswehr spokesman reportedly declined to comment on the reports, acknowledging only that Russia does possess such capabilities and warning that a nuclear explosion in space would have devastating consequences for global satellite communications.

"It would effectively mean: back to the stone age," he stated.

The German Ministry of Defense noted only that it was continuously observing global developments in space while the Russian embassy in Berlin said it had no information regarding such plans.

The United States Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA) warned in October that Russia was developing a satellite capable of transporting a nuclear weapon and, according to the upcoming report, a satellite codenamed "Sputnik-S" is believed to have been in storage in the Moscow area for several years.

Back in February 2022, the same month as the full-scale invasion of Ukraine, Russia rolled out its "Kosmos-2552" satellite into orbit, considered by western intelligence at the time to be a test designed to measure the effects of radiation in space.

Industry observers note that should Moscow indeed launch a nuclear weapon into space, it would constitute a breach of the 1967 Outer Space Treaty which prohibits the stationing of weapons of mass destructions in Earth orbit. Russia agreed to a continuation of the treaty at the United Nations General Assembly in December 2024.

Space is nevertheless becoming increasingly militarized, with the United States admitting in September 2025 that it had positioned offensive non-nuclear weapons in orbit.

Polish Prime Minister Donald Tusk called the latest violation of NATO airspace on Friday an "intentional provocation.".

The incident occurred as Russia was carrying out a wide-ranging attack on Ukraine.

But it also coincided with European military drills in Poland, intensifying concerns about Moscow's intentions.

Following the most recent violations of Polish airspace by Russian weapons, German Foreign Minister Johann Wadephul has emphasized NATO's determination to defend itself.

"We will defend every centimeter of NATO territory," he said on Monday as the 72nd NATO Parliamentary Assembly continued in Berlin.

Addressing Russian President Vladimir Putin, he added: "Don't be deceived: we may be a defensive alliance but we are prepared to defend our countries and our alliance.".

According to Polish state prosecutors, the three objects which crashed in southeastern Poland on Friday were Russian CH-101 cruise missiles.

Wadephul stated he was in close contact with Polish Foreign Minister Radoslaw Sikorski and assured Warsaw of his full solidarity.

Later, at approximately midday local time, NATO Secretary-General Mark Rutte is expected to address the assembly before heading to an official reception with German Chancellor Friedrich Merz in the afternoon.

Parallel to the continuing NATO Parliamentary Assembly in Berlin, representatives of the alliance's member states are also set to convene in Brussels on Monday to discuss the recent violations of Polish airspace by Russia.

Russian drones and missiles have frequently penetrated the sovereign airspace of Poland, a European Union and NATO member, in the course of air strikes against neighboring Ukraine over the past four years.

According to the Polish government, three Russian missiles entered Polish airspace on Friday, crashing in the south-east of the country.

Airspace violations have also been reported in other NATO and EU countries including Romania, Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia.

On Saturday, Finland reported a suspected airspace violation by a manned Russian military helicopter near the southeastern town of Loviisa.

Moldova, which is not in the EU or NATO, has also reported airspace violations, most recently this weekend.

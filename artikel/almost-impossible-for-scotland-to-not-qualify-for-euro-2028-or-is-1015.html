"It's almost impossible not to qualify.".

The statement is enough to make any Scotland fan uncomfortable, but that's been the relaxed theme going into this Nations League campaign among some outside of Hampden.

But, in the bleak, barren and grim aftermath of Scotland's 4-0 scudding by Switzerland in St Gallen, "almost impossible" almost feels like a challenge.

According to recent reports, scotland began with three shots at reaching the finals, where they would play at Hampden Park if they made it. We're now down to two.

Sources indicate that they could have qualified for the Euro 2028 finals with help from the Nations League. Not straightforward, but absolutely achievable.

Finish top of group B1 containing Switzerland, Slovenia and North Macedonia, then prevail in the play-off.

In Sebastien Pocognoli's first six games in charge, he failed to deliver that, his new-look squad and system only offering seven points. (Related: Porting fairseq wmt19 translation system to transformer...)

Not only that but that third-place finish has further repercussions.

Scotland's next route lies in the Euro qualifiers, with the draw taking place in Belfast on 6 December. Can't wait.

Pocognoli's team will go into pot three for that, making a shot at progression that much more difficult.

That means the Scots could be drawn in a group with the likes of Serbia, Turkey, Czech Republic, Switzerland, or Austria as their pot 2 opponent.

For example, Scotland could be in a group with Spain, Switzerland, Kazakhstan and Azerbaijan. Please, no.

The top 12 group winners and the eight best runners-up go straight through, with the rest of the runners-up going into a play-off with the best Nations League non-qualified group winners.

If Scotland make a hash of that, then do not fear...

Uefa is reserving two spots for the two best-ranked host nations who have not qualified after the completion of the qualifying group stage.

It's now feasible this may come into play.

Going on the assumption England - who have just turned over world champions Spain - will qualify, that leaves Scotland, Wales and Ireland. Despite the qualifying draw being in Belfast, Northern Ireland aren't a host nation.

If one of them qualifies through other means, then happy days.

If they do not, then the Scots will need to hope they are not the lowest-ranked team among the three or they face being locked out of their own house party.

Britain’s financial watchdog has a message for companies building AI agents that handle people’s money: you may need its permission to operate.

The Financial Conduct Authority set out its expectations for agentic AI on Tuesday, covering banks, tech providers, and social media platforms alike.

Sources indicate that the regulator says companies are moving from testing AI agents to deploying them. It is already talking to companies planning personal AI agents for UK consumers. Some of those products will need FCA authorisation at launch, it warns, and others will grow into needing it later. (Related: Course Launch Community Event...)

We’ve set out expectations for firms looking to develop and deploy personal AI agents in the financial services sector. (Related: Deploy Hugging Face models easily with Amazon SageMaker...)

AI agents can widen access to financial tools and information, but can also blur the lines between regulated and unregulated activity, and the protections consumers receive.

It has been revealed that the line gets crossed when an agent stops giving information and starts acting. Once it starts transactions, moves money, arranges borrowing, or makes investment calls, the firm behind it must work out which of those activities need approval.

The FCA has the power to step in where regulated services run without approval, and indicates it will use it if needed. The point, it says, is to guard against scams and make sure consumers get support and redress.

Tech companies get the bluntest passage. Describing a business as a technology provider does not remove the need for authorisation, the regulator claims. Parliament sets the boundary, not branding. It also notes a wrinkle of the AI era: the personalised context that large language models carry can make the legal questions harder, not easier.

Banks and other regulated firms keep full responsibility even when the AI comes from outside. Senior managers answer for the models in their area. The questions get very concrete: should a firm accept a payment ordered by an AI agent, or one from a digital wallet an agent controls?

There is help on offer too. The FCA’s AI Lab supports firms developing and testing agentic products. The regulator also aims an Agentic Policy Sprint for firms, policymakers, and tech providers. No date is set, but interest can be registered now.

The page lands as part of a coordinated push. Britain’s four digital regulators issued a joint statement on AI agents in October. On 16 November, they wrote to ministers, warning that several personal AI agents are about to launch in the UK and that some developers have yet to engage.

“We want to be explicit that existing legal and regulatory frameworks apply to personal AI agents,” the four regulators wrote in that statement.

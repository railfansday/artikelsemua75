"We spent a little bit more one-on-one time with each of these birds to get them trained to loud noises, a lot of people present, flashing lights — just being handled — so when they get to the president, hopefully they'll act like they have some sense," Scott Rightsell told Fox News Digital about raising the birds for Tuesday's National Thanksgiving Turkey Pardon. Recent findings suggest that rightsell, who owns and operates Deans Gap Farm in rural Fort Seybert, West Virginia, with his wife Becky, found out during the summer that he would raise the turkeys for the ceremony. He received the young turkeys, called poults, in July — shortly after the celebration for America 250.

The turkeys were named Freedom and Feathers through a White House public opinion poll, beating out the options for Commander and Chief or Honey and Ham.

Freedom and Feathers, the two turkeys chosen for the 2026 Thanksgiving Turkey Pardon at the White House, on a farm where they were raised in West Virginia. (National Turkey Federation).

Sources indicate that "We've had them from the middle of the July until now," noted Rightsell. "The turkeys weigh probably 40 to 45 pounds right now.".

He added that besides the aforementioned training, the birds receive the same treatment as all the others that he raises on the farm, which he and his wife founded in 2015.

"Being on national TV, that's a massive deal," Rightsell stated when asked what he was looking forward to most about his trip to the nation's capital this week.

"I mean That's probably what we're looking forward to the most is being on national TV, actually meeting the president, which everybody does not get to do that. And getting to converse with him and the first lady, that's gonna be a real treat for us.".

From left to right, Becky Rightsell, Secretary of Agriculture Brooke Rollins, Scott Rightsell, Gov. Patrick Morrisey, R-W.Va., and Christy Puffenbarger pose for a photo in West Virginia. (National Turkey Federation).

The National Thanksgiving Turkey Pardon has been a tradition for 79 years, recurring annually since President Harry Truman occupied the White House, according to Christy Puffenbarger.

Sources indicate that puffenbarger is this year's National Turkey Federation chairwoman, a position that rotates annually. She's the first woman to hold the role.

In a recent development, typically, the pardon event is held in the Rose Garden. However, Trump will host the pardoning on the latest South Lawn helipad Tuesday afternoon. A White House spokesperson tells Fox News Digital that Trump will deliver remarks about "how well our nation is doing" ahead of the holiday.

First lady Melania Trump, Vice President JD Vance and a litany of Cabinet secretaries, including Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent, Secretary of War Pete Hegseth, Secretary of Agriculture Brooke Rollins, Transportation Secretary Sean Duffy and Education Secretary Linda McMahon. Attorney General Todd Blanche will also attend. So will latest White House chief of staff Stephen Miller.

It has been revealed that both turkeys receive a pardon, but which turkey participates in the pardoning ceremony depends on the American people, who vote for their preferred bird on the White House's website. Voters are still deciding Tuesday morning which fleshy fowl will be pardoned later today.

Recent findings suggest that neither bird will end up on anyone's Thanksgiving dinner table. The turkey that doesn't receive a pardon remains behind the scenes at the White House, "for moral support," as Puffenbarger put it. If the chosen bird gets stage fright or cannot carry out the ceremonial duties, the second bird is on standby.

Freedom and Feathers, the two turkeys chosen for the 2026 Thanksgiving Turkey Pardon at the White House, on a farm where they were raised in West Virginia. (National Turkey Federation).

It has been revealed that "What I'm really looking forward to, obviously, like Scott and Becky, is meeting the president and his wife and representing this industry, and really [I] would like to thank the president for continuing this tradition," Puffenbarger told Fox News Digital. "Because I do think it is a very nice event. It showcases not only our industry, but the tradition of turkey at Thanksgiving.".

Puffenbarger indicates the industry as a whole will provide 30 million pounds of turkey to Americans enjoying the Thanksgiving holiday this year.

"And to be able to showcase it at a positive, fun event like that ... it'll be quite an honor for myself and my family," she mentioned.

After spending their first few months of life like any normal turkey, the royal treatment for Feathers and Freedom began on Thursday.

They left for Washington, D.C., after a star-studded send-off from a local elementary school in Brandywine, West Virginia, where Becky Rightsell is also a teacher.

Secretary Rollins attended the sendoff, as did West Virginia Governor Patrick Morrisey.

Recent findings suggest that "It’s so humbling for West Virginia to be named to have two of the turkeys that get pardoned. This is history in the making, only the second time that’s happened," Morrisey told WDTV during the ceremony.

According to recent reports, freedom and Feathers, the two turkeys chosen for the 2026 Thanksgiving Turkey Pardon at the White House, walk on a red carpet at the Willard Intercontinental Hotel in Washington, D.C. (Department of Agriculture).

Sunday night, the birds arrived at the five-star Willard InterContinental Hotel in Washington, D.C., where they received "presidential treatment," according to the Department of Agriculture (USDA), which posted photos of the turkeys sitting on the beds in a hotel room.

It has been revealed that A red carpet was rolled out as they strolled through the lobby of the hotel to much fanfare. Once in the room, the birds were greeted by a layer of crunchy fall leaves lining the floor.

But the fun doesn't end after Tuesday's pardoning. Instead, the turkeys will be whisked away for their next adventure.

"Immediately afterwards, another cool thing, these birds will get on an airplane and fly to beautiful College Station, Texas, where they will retire to live out the rest of their lives at Texas A&M University," Puffenbarger explained.

Freedom and Feathers, the two turkeys chosen for the 2026 Thanksgiving Turkey Pardon at the White House, sit on beds at the Willard Intercontinental Hotel in Washington, D.C. (Department of Agriculture).

Friday night, Texas A&M's football team is scheduled to host rival Texas, the fifth-ranked college football team in the country.

Feathers and Freedom will be the guests of honor at the game.

"I don't know how much they know about football, but I feel sure they will have a good time," Puffenbarger said.

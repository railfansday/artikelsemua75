Rick Berman says he got over Star Trek fans giving him grief about ruining the franchise "a long time ago.".

Berman was selected by Paramount as Star Trek executive producer in 1987, and he fully inherited Gene Roddenberry's mantle after the creator's death in 1991. Under Berman's aegis, the Star Trek franchise rapidly expanded with Star Trek: The Next Generation, Star Trek: Deep Space Nine, Star Trek: Voyager, and Star Trek: Enterprise, as well as four TNG feature films. In the 18 years Rick Berman ran Star Trek, fans took issue with many of his decisions, but time is the fire in which those complaints burn, to paraphrase Star Trek Generations' villain, Dr. Tolian Soran (Malcolm McDowell). It has been revealed that rick Berman was a guest of two of his former employees, Brent Spiner and Jonathan Frakes, on their podcast, Dropping Names... And Other Things. As actors and as a director, Spiner and Frakes played key roles in executive producer Alex Kurtzman's era of Star Trek on Paramount+, and they discussed the "grief" fans gave Berman that he "ruined Star Trek," which is now mainly directed at Kurtzman. Today, fans are sentimental about the "Berman era." Read their quotes below:.

Brent Spiner: “You got so much grief through the years…”.

Rick Berman: “Wait, what grief are you referring to?”.

Brent Spiner: “Grief just because ‘You ruined Star Trek.’ You got that moniker.”.

Jonathan Frakes: “Now, they want they cherish [the Berman era]: ‘We’ve gotta get back to the Berman era.’”.

Brent Spiner: “They call it the Berman years. Come on! It went to J.J. [Abrams], and everybody was down on J.J. until it became Alex [Kurtzman]. Whoever holds the mantle has ruined the entire enterprise.”.

Rick Berman: “I got over that a long time ago.”.

Brent Spiner: “But so have the fans, because they’ve come full circle that the Berman years were wonderful.”.

Recent findings suggest that brent Spiner is right that "whoever holds the mantle" as the person making the substantial creative decisions about Star Trek bears the brunt of ire from unhappy fans. After 60 years, Star Trek fans span different generations, with conflicting ideas of what Star Trek is "supposed to be." However, a sizable segment now looks past the flaws of Rick Berman's era and holds up his run as the gold standard of what Star Trek is "supposed to be.".

From 1987 to 2005, Berman oversaw more than 600 episodes across four Star Trek TV series, as well as four Star Trek: The Next Generation movies. Many of the decisions Berman was responsible for that were controversial at the time, such as the first Black and female captains to lead Star Trek series and making Enterprise a prequel, for example, are now widely accepted. Time has diluted much of the ire once directed at Berman in the pre-social-media age. (Related: Latest Update: Retrieval Augmented Generation with Hugg...)

Industry observers note that for longtime Trekkers, the Berman era is now a golden age when Star Trek series ran for seven seasons with 22 to 26 episodes a season. It was a dearly missed abundance compared to how relatively little Star Trek there is in the streaming era. Berman's shows are also held up to have superior writing and better stories, with the characters' Shakespearean-like dialogue more musical to the ears than the modern vernacular in Paramount+'s Star Trek shows.

Time will tell if, 20 years from now, Alex Kurtzman's era, and the highlights and movie he oversaw, will be as fondly remembered as Rick Berman's Star Trek is today. History seems to bear that out, and many fans are already poised to be against the next era of Star Trek coming under Skydance. The good old days of Rick Berman's 18 years of running Star Trek may be remembered as even more halcyon in the years to come.

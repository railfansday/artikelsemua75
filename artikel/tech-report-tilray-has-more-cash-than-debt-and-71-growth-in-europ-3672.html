According to recent reports, for more aggressive growth investors, Tilray Brands (TLRY -8.00%) appears to be moving in a positive direction as a business. It may be worth buying if you have a long time horizon. However, more conservative investors will probably want to remain on the sidelines because the company still hasn't achieved one crucial goal: sustainable profitability. Here's why this financially strong cannabis seller is worth buying for some investors, but not for others.

Industry observers note that when Tilray first went public, its goal was to take advantage of the increasing legal access to cannabis. There was significant excitement in the market around the marijuana sector at the time, and the stock rose sharply alongside the rest of the sector. But the reality didn't live up to Wall Street's expectations. Competition was intense, the legalization trend was slow, and illicit marijuana sales remained robust. Tilray and the rest of the sector fell sharply. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

Industry observers note that tilray decided to move in a different direction. The goal now is to be a "global lifestyle and consumer packaged goods tech giant leading at the nexus of the beverage, cannabis, and wellness industries." Effectively, the company is looking to operate more like a consumer staples company than a pure-play cannabis business. The biggest shift was the acquisition of alcoholic beverage brands. In the first quarter of fiscal 2027, revenues from Tilray's alcoholic beverages stood at $101.5 million, while its cannabis revenues totaled $56.1 million. You could easily argue that Tilray is now more of an alcohol firm than a marijuana company. However, the bigger story is the diversification that the company has created. That should attract growth investors who want exposure to cannabis, but who don't want to own a pure-play cannabis company.

The truth is, marijuana is a growing industry. The problem is that companies have had a hard time translating that growth into profits. Tilray is looking to resolve that problem by diversifying into fresh areas that have a more stable history, notably craft brewing. That creates a foundation on which it can continue to build its marijuana operations.

That foundation, meanwhile, is increasingly strong. Notably, it ended the first fiscal quarter of 2027 with a net cash position. That requires a little parsing, because it means the firm's cash balance of roughly $215 million exceeds its long-term debt of $118 million. That's not quite as good as a debt-free balance sheet, but Tilray has a very strong financial position right at the moment.

In a recent development, meanwhile, the company's growth in Europe has been a strong point, further adding to its diversification. In the first quarter, European revenues increased by 71%. From a high-level perspective, Tilray is a much stronger business today than it was not that long ago. If you are a growth investor with a long-term focus, you may want to take a closer look. It could be a good way to add marijuana exposure without having to own a pure-play.

Recent findings suggest that while there is a lot to like about Tilray and the direction the business is moving, there's one key issue that remains unresolved. Tilray continues to bleed red ink on the bottom line. That single fact should probably keep more conservative investors on the sidelines. The direction the business is heading looks good, but until there are sustainable profits, an investment here remains a risky one. Buying it requires believing that Tilray's consumer-staples shift will eventually lead to a profitable and growing business. Only a more aggressive growth investor will likely be willing to make that bet.

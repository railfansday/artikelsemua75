If you're still contributing to your retirement savings, you could consider directing all fresh contributions to short term bonds/money market funds. This way, you can pre-fund one or two years of retirement and not sell your equities. You want to guard against sequence of returns risk in early retirement. You could consider a couple other options: 1. Just withdraw from your bond funds for the first 10 years of retirement. This should protect you against a good amount of the sequence of returns risk. However, if equities either only keep pace with inflation or exceed inflation, your portfolio will become more aggressive. If you're uncomfortable with that, see option #2 below. 2. Do what someone already recommended for retirees who are nervous...shift your portfolio now. Maybe move down to something between 30/70 to 50/50 with your bonds on the short end. My parents are a decade older than you and your husband and are facing a similar situation (though they'll likely have fewer years in retirement). I recommended that they take money from their bond funds if their stocks are down in early retirement. They're around 45/55 in terms of allocation, but they also both have over $45,000 per year in SS income. I keep having to remind them that they're going to be fine. It sounds like you're also going to be fine, regardless of what happens with equities, so try to approach this from a position of confidence and strength.Statistics: Posted by Curb Fan — Sun Nov 22, 2026 11:04 pm.

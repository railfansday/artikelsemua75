Monday Night Football was briefly plunged into darkness during a Monday, November 23, game between the Washington Commanders and the Cincinnati Bengals.

“Somebody turned out the lights,” ESPN broadcaster Joe Buck confirmed to Monday Night Football viewers seconds after the power shut off in Northwest Stadium during the telecast.

The Commanders were lining up for a passing attempt in the third quarter on Monday when most of the lights in the Landover, Maryland, venue suddenly turned off.

“The lights are out and we’re not home,” Buck joked. “They do it a lot around the National Football League where they turn the lights on and off.”.

He then humorously guessed, “Somebody leaned against the wall!”.

“We’ll see how long this takes to get them up and running,” Buck’s cohost Troy Aikman chimed in.

Buck initially offered a grim timeline for when the power might come back on, recalling that it “took forever” to resolve technical faults “back in the day.”.

The situation was made all the more nerve-wracking because NFL Commissioner Roger Goodell was watching the Monday Night Football game from one of Northwest Stadium’s luxury suites.

“We’re being told it’s power-related and not the stadium,” Buck informed viewers as cameras panned around the dimly-lit stadium.

After Monday Night Football cut to a commercial break, there was good news for viewers and both NFL teams as the lights came back on.

Play resumed moments later, with Buck later relaying to viewers that the power outage was caused by a mistimed maintenance snafu. (The Commanders ultimately beat the Bengals, 22-20.).

It has been revealed that “If you want to know the reason why the lights went out earlier, the official word is the lighting vendor never disabled the regularly scheduled maintenance for 10 p.m.,” Buck announced. “It’s a weekday thing, and they forgot it was a Monday night game.”.

According to recent reports, the coronavirus pandemic turned two little-known mRNA companies into household names. Moderna (MRNA +1.29%) and BioNTech (BNTX -2.26%) brought their vaccines to market, collected billions of dollars in revenue, and then watched demand fade in recent years. Both stocks fell significantly from their highs, and both companies have spent the past few years trying to prove they're more than coronavirus vaccine makers.

This year, their paths have diverged. Moderna's stock has soared as investors rushed to bet on the company's promising personalized cancer vaccine program. BioNTech, meanwhile, saw its stock price slip and prepared to say goodbye to its founders.

So, heading into 2027, which of these mRNA pioneers is the better buy?

Recent findings suggest that first, a quick recap. Both companies have faced the same problem: the coronavirus vaccine was a windfall that couldn't last. Moderna's product sales peaked at more than $18 billion in 2022, and BioNTech's reached a high of about the same level in 2021. Each responded by cutting costs and shifting toward oncology. BioNTech has more than 25 phase 2 and phase 3 clinical trials ongoing in that treatment area. Moderna's personalized cancer vaccine candidate, intismeran autogene, is involved in four phase 3 trials and several earlier-stage trials.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/19/26.

In a recent development, bioNTech has real strengths. It has more than $17 billion in cash and investments, and its lead candidate, pumitamig, is a bispecific antibody it's developing with Bristol Myers Squibb. The firm is evaluating pumitamig in seven global pivotal studies across several cancer types. Meanwhile, BioNTech recently had mixed results in a phase 3 trial of an antibody-drug conjugate with DualityBio. Though the candidate met its primary endpoint, its impact may be "limited," the companies mentioned.

The coronavirus vaccine remains BioNTech's only commercialized product for the moment as we wait for the pipeline to potentially produce others. And the company will see its CEO, Uğur Şahin, and chief medical officer, Özlem Türeci, leave at year-end. A latest CEO takes over by Feb. 1. That's a lot of change during a critical stretch. Moderna, by contrast, has several commercialized products. Third-quarter revenue rose 29% to $1.3 billion, and net income was $162 million, a big swing from a loss a year earlier. Management raised its 2026 revenue growth outlook to a range of 15% to 25%, up from an earlier forecast of up to 10%. The biotech company now has five approved products, including a flu vaccine approved in the U.S. this year. A limited launch has already delivered more than 110,000 vaccinations, and Moderna is preparing a broader rollout for the 2027-2028 respiratory season. (Related: Hugging Face Reads, Feb. 2021 - Long-range Transformers...)

The bigger prize is oncology, and in a phase 3 melanoma trial, intismeran plus Merck's Keytruda reduced the risk of recurrence or death by 42% compared with Keytruda alone. Intismeran is also in studies for lung, bladder, and kidney cancers, so melanoma could be just the first of several revenue streams.

Next year offers several potential catalysts. Moderna and Merck mentioned they'd be in contact with regulators about filing for intismeran approval, so investors should watch for submission news. Moderna will also provide formal 2027 financial guidance with its fourth-quarter report, and it's still awaiting FDA guidance on refiling its flu and COVID combination vaccine. Finally, in Europe, Moderna expects an opportunity to gain share as a competitor's COVID-19 vaccine contract expires.

Moderna isn't risk-free. The tech giant still must gain regulatory approval for intismeran and prove the candidate's strength in several other clinical trials, and the stock's huge run raises expectations. Investors may see volatility around 2027 guidance.

Sources indicate that bioNTech has the cash and a promising pipeline, and it may reward patient investors down the road. But its biggest opportunities are further out, and it's navigating a leadership overhaul along the way. Moderna offers a rarer combination: growing sales today, a cancer therapy that could reach the market soon, and a broadening vaccine lineup. That's why Moderna is my pick between the two, to buy and hold for the long term.

Is Altria's 5.6% Dividend Yield Too Good to Be True Heading Into 2027?

Sources indicate that one of the great dividends of our age is paid by a tech giant making products so unfashionable they're barely visible anymore. I'm talking about Altria (MO +0.68%), the latest iteration of a storied tobacco enterprise that created and still sells the iconic Marlboro brand in this country. (Related: Dividends Aren't What You Think They Are...)

Recent findings suggest that several decades ago, it was rare to visit a household, office, or restaurant where at least one occupant wasn't lighting up. These days, it's normal to go days, weeks, even months without encountering any smokers. The cigarettes that remain the hard core of Altria's production to this day aren't a growing market. So it's worthwhile to explore whether the company's famously high-yield dividend -- which at the moment pays out at 5.6% -- is sustainable for much longer. Here's my take on that.

According to recent reports, given the many restrictions on traditional tobacco consumption in the U.S., plus the increasing health consciousness of its population, it's a wonder Altria ekes out any growth at all. In the company's third quarter, its net revenue only crept up by 1% year over year to $6.13 billion. If we strip excise (or "sin") taxes out of the equation, we get a meatier growth figure of 3.6%, with revenue net of such obligations of $5.44 billion. But that improvement was largely due to price increases, as total shipment volume of smokeables sank by 2.9%.

Revenue net of excise taxes, by the way, is considered the yardstick top-line figure for tobacco companies, so I'll stay with that throughout this piece without repeating the descriptor.

Sources indicate that of that $5.44 billion, smokeable products -- anchored by Marlboro -- pulled in $4.76 billion, or 88% of the total. Almost all of the remainder ($667 million) consisted of oral tobacco products, the revenue for which rose only marginally during the period. There's one bright spot there, however, and that's the company's promising on! and on! PLUS nicotine pouches, which collectively experienced a 13% increase in shipment volume.

Years ago, Altria sensibly tried to pivot toward products with a brighter future, specifically next-generation tobacco offerings seen as more innovative and (relatively) healthier. But its attempts to develop a vape in-house flopped, as did its acquisitions of stakes in existing vape manufacturers (most notably the spectacular flameout of the once-promising Juul Labs). These days, the manufacturer's "all other" category covers the tiny sales of such goods, which amounted to $12 million in the quarter.

Non-GAAP (adjusted) profitability, like revenue, didn't bounce sharply higher for Altria. It improved by 4% to $2.55 billion in the quarter. This, however, makes for a sky-high net margin of 47%. Most companies would be satisfied with a percentage in the low double digits. What's the secret sauce with Altria?

In a recent development, it's about the core product. Cigarettes are addictive, so users not only come back time and again to buy them, but they're also willing to put up with the frequent price raises by makers like Altria (and to fork over the always-high excise taxes to boot). Given that, there's little or no reason for a manufacturer to innovate the product, and to top it all off, cigarettes are cheap to make.

Put all of this together, and you have a good that generates high profits at very modest and steady costs. That's why, even in this day and age, tobacco companies continue to be cash-generating machines. Altria's statement of cash flows is always a thing of beauty, with high operating flows and tiny investments resulting in a mighty gush of free cash flow (FCF). This was only slightly down year over year in the third quarter, to almost $3 billion. That's far more than the under $1.8 billion the company needed to fund that high-yield dividend, plus stock-boosting share repurchases totaling $400 million.

American smokers aren't going to change their habits much, so Altria felt confident enough to raise its full-year 2026 earnings guidance. Adjusted net income should rise by 4.5% to 6% over the 2025 figure to $5.66 to $5.74 per share, the tech giant believes. All that cash flow has to go somewhere, so the company's board authorized a $1 billion expansion of its existing share repurchase program, which had $265 million remaining. The initiative has also been extended, with the upcoming deadline being Dec. 31, 2027.

Industry observers note that both of these moves strongly indicate Altria will keep its now 57-year (!) dividend raise streak alive, maintaining its status as a Dividend King (one of the few stocks that have raised their payout for at least 50 consecutive years). After all, its payout is one of the stock's great draws, and management is acutely aware of its importance. I predict, with the same level of confidence as Altria's leaders revealed the guidance raise and the share buyback news, that it'll continue to deliver those high-margin profits and impressive levels of FCF.

And with that, this perpetually high-yield dividend is extremely safe, in my opinion.

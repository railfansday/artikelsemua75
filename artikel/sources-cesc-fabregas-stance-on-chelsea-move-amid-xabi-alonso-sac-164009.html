Como manager Cesc Fabregas would not be willing to take the Chelsea job in the middle of the season, sources have told Football Insider.

The Blues are long-term admirers of their former midfielder, whose Como side are in promising form in both Serie A and the Champions League at the moment.

Fabregas is understood to be "right at the top" of Chelsea's list of targets to potentially replace Xabi Alonso, as pressure continues to build on their current boss.

However, sources say that the 39-year-old would only be willing to take the job at the start of a summer, giving him the entire pre-season to get his ideas in place. (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

Como are also hugely reluctant to lose their boss mid-season, given that they are competing both domestically and in Europe.

Alonso's position at Chelsea remains secure for now, but they are keeping a close eye on Fabregas in case they decide a change is needed.

Chelsea's fans are rapidly losing patience with Alonso, having chanted against him during last weekend's heavy 4-1 home defeat to Leeds United.

The Spaniard is under ever-increasing pressure at Stamford Bridge, and now faces a pivotal month to save his future.

Football Insider revealed on Monday that the Chelsea hierarchy are expecting wins from their next two games against Nottingham Forest and Crystal Palace.

Alonso's side then face a three-game run against Liverpool, Manchester City and Aston Villa, which is expected to "make or break" the boss' future.

Chelsea are adamant that Champions League qualification is an absolute minimum for the season, and Alonso could lose his job if they fall out of contention.

The West London outfit are eighth in the Premier League at the moment, after back-to-back defeats.

Former Tottenham scout Bryan King told Football Insider on Tuesday that Chelsea are playing like a team who know that their manager is about to get sacked.

Th ex-recruitment man believes that the dressing room feel that the "writing is on the wall" for the boss' future at Stamford Bridge.

King suggested that the players are not "running through brick walls" for the manager, which he feels is one of the early signs of Alonso losing the dressing room.

Results haven't been good enough in recent weeks, and Alonso could soon pay the price by losing his job.

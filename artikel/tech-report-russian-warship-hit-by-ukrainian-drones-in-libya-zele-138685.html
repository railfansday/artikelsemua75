How Ukraine drone attacks on Russian ships unfolded.

A Russian warship was sunk by Ukrainian drones at a port in Libya on Sunday, President Volodymyr Zelensky has said.

There was an explosion on the warship at the Benghazi naval base in the early morning, while it was docking for fuel and supplies ahead of its return to Russia, local authorities stated.

Zelensky added that Ukraine's attack hit a second Russian vessel, a cargo ship which he claimed was being used to transport weapons.

Sources indicate that the local health ministry stated it had received injured people from a commercial ship that was targeted off the coast of the port. It is not clear whether this is the same vessel as the one Ukraine has claimed responsibility for. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

Following the strikes, the Kremlin warned of an "escalation of tensions in the region", but declined to comment directly on the attacks in Libya.

Libya's two governments - the UN-recognised government in the capital, Tripoli, and a rival administration in the east - have both condemned the attack.

The Tripoli foreign ministry said the country would not accept being drawn into the conflict.

It mentioned foreign forces would not be allowed to use Libyan ports and civilian infrastructure for military purposes.

The eastern government, based in Benghazi, called it a violation of maritime safety and an attack on their sovereignty. (Related: Latest Update: Transformer-based Encoder-Decoder Models...)

Seven naval drones were deployed to target and sink the corvette Merkury, one of Russia's newest ships, Zelensky mentioned in a statement on Monday. He added that a Ka-27 combat helicopter was also destroyed.

The Ukrainian president noted the second ship was the MV ANGARA, a Russian container carrier.

The attack came the day after a Russian cargo vessel was hit by a drone attack in the eastern Gulf of Finland. The Sparta II had been bound for Russia's Baltic exclave Kaliningrad when it was hit on Saturday morning.

Finnish defence forces noted Ukraine was likely to be behind the attack. Kyiv has not commented.

Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov told reporters on Monday that the "expansion of terrorist activity in the Baltic" was a "dangerous trend" and has said "it is, of course, leading to an escalation of tensions in the region.".

When questioned over the attacks in Libya, Peskov said information would be provided by the country's defence ministry and added: "We paid attention to Zelensky's statements about it".

Peskov declined to comment on the strikes on Russian ships in Libya and said that that information would be provided by Russia's Defence Ministry.

The UN mission in Libya expressed "serious concern" over the attacks on Sunday, calling for "an immediate, independent and transparent investigation".

"Libya cannot afford, and must not become a battleground for foreign conflicts," the UNSMIL wrote on X., external.

Industry observers note that videos posted on social media and geolocated by BBC Verify show the ship that was struck in the morning engulfed in flames and smoke. Satellite imagery from Planet Labs PBC also indicates a boat on fire at 05:47 GMT at the Benghazi port.

Satellite imagery showed a ship on fire at the port.

US-based intelligence firm Vantor collected high-resolution images that it mentioned showed Project 20380 Steregushchiy-class corvette (Merkury) - a Russian navy ship - moored in the port on Saturday, and on Sunday showed most of the same ship capsized and sunk with only the forward bow still visible.

Neither of Libya's rival administrations has formally declared support for either side in the Russia-Ukraine war, but they have contrasting ties with Moscow and Kyiv.

The head of the Libyan National Army, which controls much of the east of the country, has longstanding links with Russia.

The eastern-based Government of National Stability (GNS) noted the Russian ship - which it did not name or identify - was using the Benghazi port within the framework of an existing maritime agreement.

Sunday's incident is under investigation, and authorities are coordinating with their Russian counterparts to determine the source of the suspected attack and who is responsible for it, GNS added.

It mentioned Libya was not party to the Russia-Ukraine war, or any other conflict beyond its borders.

GNS has accused the Tripoli-based Government of National Unity (GNU) of hosting Ukrainian military personnel and allowing them to operate from Libyan territory against Russian targets.

Separately, the GNU warned against hosting foreign forces and allowing them to use facilities in a statement published by state media.

It noted such activity raises "serious fears" about "risks that may result from involving ports and civilian facilities in activities related to external conflicts, and the resulting threat to the lives of citizens and the safety of national facilities and assets".

Libya fractured into violence and instability after its leader Muammar Gaddafi was overthrown in 2011, leading to the opposing governments.

Benghazi is more than 2,000 km (1,243 miles) away from the Russian capital Moscow.

In March, Russia accused Ukraine of targeting the Russian tanker Arctic Metagaz in the Mediterranean between Libya and Malta with "uncrewed sea drones" launched from the Libyan coast.

As well as having an economic impact on Russia, hampering the ability of their so-called shadow fleet of tankers to export oil and gas, attacks like this are seen as humiliating to the Kremlin, according to Dr Jalel Harchaoui of the Royal United Services Institute (RUSI).

But it is not only Moscow that will want to react to this latest incident. The ship was in the region as part of a deal with the government of eastern Libya - allied with Khalifa Haftar, commander of the Libyan National Army.

According to recent reports, harchaoui noted Haftar will take the attack as a personal slight. "You're going to have a response by the Haftar family. They will ask questions about who is hosting the Ukrainians.".

Many people have an old Nokia Android phone in a drawer. A Nokia 6.1, a Nokia 3.1, a Nokia 1 that a family member used for two years. The question we get is simple: can that phone be a security camera that keeps recording with the screen off?

The short answer: on most Nokia phones running Android 9 (Pie) or newer, yes. On a few older models, there is a hidden app killer you must check first. (Related: Latest Update: Stop treating agents as one-off projects...)

This matters more for Nokia than for any other brand, and the reason is a bit of history.

According to recent reports, the volunteer site Don't Kill My App tracks how hard each phone brand stops background apps. Today it gives Nokia (HMD Global) a score of 0 — the best score, the same as stock Android. Samsung, Xiaomi, OnePlus and Huawei all get 5, the worst.

But it was not always like this. The site notes it plainly:.

It has been revealed that "HMD Global/Nokia was the main reason this website came to exist. They had the most aggressive app killers preinstalled on their phones.".

Recent findings suggest that the old problem was a preinstalled app called Power saver (package com.evenwell.powersaving.g3), made by a third-party organization, Evenwell. According to the site's (now struck-out) history, on Android Oreo and Pie Nokia phones it stopped background apps after about 20 minutes with the screen off, and even apps you had excluded from battery optimization were stopped. (Related: Industry News: Introducing Optimum: The Optimization To...)

For a camera phone, that is the worst case: you set it up, the screen goes dark, and twenty minutes later recording ends.

The good news, again from the site:.

"HMD Global claims to disable Evenwell powersaving apps on all devices running Android Pie or newer.".

It has been revealed that that change happened in August 2019. So the first question for your old Nokia is not the model. It is the Android version.

Sources indicate that open Settings and look for About phone (on some versions it is inside System). Find Android version.

If the phone indicates a system update, install it before you start. On an old Nokia, the update is not just security — it may be the thing that removes the app killer.

Roberto Di Matteo has admitted that he was ‘shocked’ West Brom sacked him back in 2011 as they battled to stay in the Premier League.

The Italian had a successful playing career, and he turned to management after retiring, initially starting out with MK Dons. (Related: Tech Report: School sends out minibus to catch absent p...)

A move to Albion would follow, and Di Matteo would also work for Chelsea, Schalke and Aston Villa, with the high point undoubtedly coming with the Blues, who he won the Champions League and FA Cup with as interim boss.

Whilst nothing will surpass winning those trophies with Chelsea, Di Matteo really made his name as a manager during his time at The Hawthorns with West Brom.

After making the step up from League One outfit MK Dons, Di Matteo built an Albion side that won promotion to the Premier League in his first season at the club.

They did so by playing a brand of football that the fans really enjoyed, and they carried that into the top flight. Whilst an opening day 6-0 defeat at Chelsea was concerning, the players quickly adapted to the Premier League. (Related: Tech Report: GTA 6 players report game-breaking crashes...)

The Baggies had picked up 15 points from their first nine games, which included a memorable 3-2 win at Arsenal, a 2-2 draw at Old Trafford, and they also picked up a point from Tottenham.

So, Albion were performing very well, and they continued the entertaining style of play that had made them such a good watch the season before.

However, that form didn’t continue, and Di Matteo was ultimately dismissed in February, after a miserable period of seven defeats in nine.

And, speaking to Mick McCarthy and Tony Pulis on The Managers podcast, Di Matteo couldn’t hide how disappointed he was at the decision, with Albion outside the relegation zone at the time.

“I was a bit surprised and shocked. We’ve all worked in smaller clubs, so when you have injuries to one or two key players, it’s difficult. Up until the Christmas period we were looking really good, but Jonas Olsson, the centre-back, got injured for a while, and one or two others were not available.

Recent findings suggest that “In the Christmas period, you play every two or three days and you don’t have a deep squad, so you struggle a bit. But, again, we were coming out of it, and I was really shocked.”.

You can understand why Di Matteo was surprised at the decision, as he will have been tasked with keeping the team in the league, and he will argue he was on track to do that.

Plus, this was the first tough spell he had in charge of Albion, so he would’ve hoped that he had enough credit in the bank to be given an opportunity to turn things around.

Yet, football is a ruthless business, and the Albion board needed to make what they felt was the best decision for the club, and the reality is that, in hindsight, it was a call that paid off.

They had been on a terrible run over a period of a few months, and they would say the trend was pointing towards relegation.

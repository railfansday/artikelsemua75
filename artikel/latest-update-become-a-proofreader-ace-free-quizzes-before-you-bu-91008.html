For more practice, the publisher of The Chicago Manual of Style posts free quizzes called Chicago Style Workouts, with no sign-up required. Try the editing and proofreading quizzes first, then the ones that compare Chicago style with AP, the Associated Press style that journalists follow. According to recent reports, pay for a course only once your scores stay high, and only for one that trains you for the niche you want. Proofread Anywhere, for example, teaches court transcript proofreading in a course of its own, with a free seven-day introduction to try first.

Industry observers note that check what each niche pays before you pick one. In the Editorial Freelancers Association’s 2026 rate chart, members’ median proofreading rates run 2.5 to 4.5 cents a word for academic work, 2 to 3 cents for business and marketing copy, and 1.2 to 2 cents for fiction.

Then proofread three short samples in your niche, so clients can see the errors you catch. Look for clients on freelance marketplaces, in writers’ groups, and by pitching small businesses and publishers directly. Quote your price by the word or the page.

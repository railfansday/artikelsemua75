The top of the betting market's power ratings is like a hot nightclub.

The gatekeepers have something of a one-in, one-out policy, where there's only room for so many. But … if you're playing well enough, the limits will be pushed, and we'll find room for you. (Related: The Reformer - Pushing the limits of language modeling...)

After nine weeks of the NFL season, there were four teams considered a cut above:.

Due to mediocre results, preseason lounge regulars — the Rams and Seahawks — have been left out in the coat room, while the Chiefs are always lingering in the front bar.

Then Joe Burrow and his crew demonstrated up, blazing with a five-game winning streak, and an utter destruction of a division rival on prime time in Week 10.

The Bengals got their invite past the velvet rope, but now it's up to them to behave so as not to get bounced back out.

A week ago, the lookahead line for this matchup was Bengals -3.5, so there's a very easy measurement as to literally how much has changed after one game, since the Commanders' own Thursday Night Football win shouldn't have hurt the already-modest perception of Washington — three full points.

The Bengals offense has put up 30+ points in each of its last five games, but with a predominantly passing-centric offense, let's take a look at the pass defense metrics of their opponents, by Success Rate (adjusted for garbage time and high-leverage plays like turnovers and fourth downs):.

After a season where the Commanders defense was a legitimate liability, they've quietly shown improvement, not allowing 30 points since Week 3, and the opponent Success Rate on dropbacks against Washington is 43.6% (seventh in the NFL).

One of those games was against fellow club member, and offensive equal, San Francisco, which carried a similar point spread of 49ers -6.5 in Week 6, but that was at home, and the Commanders got inside the number and then almost took the lead late.

The unfortunate part about getting into the betting market's VIP room, is that you're asked to perform at a high level to cash tickets for your bettors.

The Bills barely scraped over the 16.5 points required of them, while the Bears couldn't cover the 11.5-point spread they were favored by over the Saints, and the Jaguars were exposed by the Giants — the first functional team they've played in weeks. Of course, the 49ers (constants in the "club" by covering spreads at a high power rating) continued to prove they belong, crushing the Vikings 35-10 on Sunday night. Industry observers note that the other issue the Bengals' recent victims have had is that their offense had little chance of keeping up, even if they could slow Burrow in the passing game. Quarterbacks Tyler Huntley, Malik Willis, Cam Ward, Michael Penix Jr. and (advanced-age) Aaron Rodgers don't have the efficiency or second receiving threat that Jayden Daniels has.

Since returning from his early-season injury in Week 5, Daniels and the Commanders have an adjusted EPA/Play just behind the Cowboys and Broncos — and ahead of the Lions.

At home, still in divisional contention and on Monday night, look for this to be closer than you might think having seen Cincinnati in action last Sunday night.

Bet: Commanders +6.5 (-114, Bet365).

Chase Brown under 46.5 rushing yards (-115, Bet365).

According to recent reports, brown is like a closer who is there to throw a changeup, like the Trevor Hoffman of running backs. However, if Cincinnati doesn't face the short-yardage situations it might have against the lesser defenses they've played of late, or have a massive halftime lead, Brown's usage or efficiency might come down.

During the five-game winning streak, Brown topped out at 57 (with 36 yards on three carries in one second-half drive) and 55 yards (thanks to a meaningless, end-of-regulation carry for eight yards).

Tee Higgins over 5.5 receptions (+108, DraftKings).

According to recent reports, the Commanders have struggled keeping the ball out of the hands of No. 1 receivers, as CeeDee Lamb, Jaxon Smith-Njigba and Puka Nacua combined for 32 receptions against Washington.

That might be a general point of emphasis for Dan Quinn, facing Ja'Marr Chase on Monday, but the problem is the Bengals have two top-tier wide receivers.

Recent findings suggest that tee Higgins' yardage totals have tailed off recently as Chase has had his way, but even as the second option, Higgins has five catches on six targets in a pair of blowouts. With a closer game, and more looks, he should get back to hauling in the 6+ catches he had in four of five before that.

Jayden Daniels over 223.5 passing yards (-113, FanDuel).

Whether it's as part of staying connected with the Bengals on the scoreboard, or trailing, if the game does start the way others have for Cincinnati opponents, Daniels should be spreading the ball around through the air.

Daniels has gone over this total in three of his last four games, with the other coming in a game with a division rival, the Eagles, where Philadelphia's offense didn't provide any kind of pressure to air it out.

Higgins was also held out of the end zone last week, after a run of five touchdowns in three previous games.

On his way to a third-straight 10+ touchdown season, scoring more often than not as long as Burrow is in the lineup, we'll take Higgins at around even-money.

TE Mike Gesicki (+190, DraftKings).

Only the Lions allow more production (yardage and touchdowns) to opposing tight ends than the Commanders, and if there was one throw that Burrow would have liked to have back during an exquisite prime-time performance, it's the one he air-mailed by about 18 inches to Mike Gesicki in the back of the end zone. After five straight games with a score, back-to-back without one feels like a drought, but we know Gesicki will get a look or two.

RB Jacory Croskey-Merritt (+135, Bet365).

The Commanders also get their No. 1 runner back on Monday, and maybe, David Blough will shift away from his the torturous goal-line play-calling we were subjected to against the Giants, with a tailback he's more comfortable handing the ball to in those situations?

Jacory Croskey-Merritt is coming off IR for the first time since an 11-carry, 82-yard performance, with a touchdown in Week 5, so we'll assume he's getting the primary role back.

In his rookie season, "Bill" had eight rushing touchdowns, so that's 10 in 22 career games.

TE Chigoziem Okonkwo (+240, FanDuel).

No team has allowed more yards per catch to the tight end position than the Bengals, and (eventually) that translated to a Pat Freiermuth touchdown last week. With four explosive catches, that's one shy of the team lead for Washington, so this might be as good an opportunity as Okonkwo has had all season to get his first touchdown.

WR Treylon Burks (+310, Bet365).

The ball was briefly in Burks' hands at the end of the aforementioned game with the 49ers, but he hasn't scored since getting in the end zone in two of his first three games this season.

Burks' size (6-foot-2, 225 pounds) makes him a different type of option in the red zone, compared to the other receivers — Stefon Diggs and Antonio Williams — who are under six feet and less than 200 lbs.

Not scoring recently lengthens Burks' odds, while a high game total suggests anyone is a little more likely to score than usual, creating value on a longer shot.

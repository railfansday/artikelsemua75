Valve Warns of a Possible Steam Machine Price Hike

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Video game hardware manufacturers have been impacted by the computer component crisis, with Nintendo, PlayStation, and Xbox all raising the prices of their consoles this year. PC gaming giant Valve has increased the cost of its Steam Deck, and its PC-but-looks-like-a-console Steam Machine ended up being more expensive than Valve originally intended. Now, Valve has warned that its Steam Machine might get a price hike while opening up purchases for the cheapest model.

Sources indicate that in an update posted on Thursday, Valve remarked it has fulfilled all reservations for the 512 GB Steam Machine model, which costs $1,049, and has now opened it up for purchase to anyone while supplies last. Nice! "However, we are continuing to encounter supply constraints for hardware components, which impacts our ability to source parts." Ah, nuts. Once the 512 GB model is sold out, Valve will mark it as out of stock instead of opening up a new waitlist. (Related: How to train a latest language model from scratch using...)

Sources indicate that according to Valve, there are more customers on the 2 TB model ($1,349) waitlist than its current production can meet. "Even when we are able to build more it will be at smaller quantities than we planned," Valve remarked. There are more people on the 2 TB model waitlist than units made, so Valve is closing the waitlist. Those on the waitlist can purchase the 512 GB model, but they will be removed from the 2 TB model waitlist. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

Recent findings suggest that but here's the real kicker: Because PC parts continue to increase in price "at unprecedented levels," Valve is unsure how many Steam Machines it will be able to make, and unsure of how much they will be priced at when they're available. "The 2 TB model in particular will be especially constrained." Valve notes it will share more information in January.

For now, PC gamers contemplating purchasing a Steam Machine will have to wonder if they should pull the trigger now on the 512 GB model (especially before scalpers gobble them all up), take their chances on the 2 TB model waitlist, or hope for good news come January.

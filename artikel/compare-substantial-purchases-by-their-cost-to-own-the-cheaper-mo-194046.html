According to recent reports, before you buy a washer, a printer, a phone, or anything you’ll use for years, add up each model’s cost over those years. Count the price, the energy or supplies it uses, repairs, and any interest, minus what it’ll sell for used. Then buy the model with the lower total, even when its price tag is higher.

Printers show how far apart the two totals can get. Take a $100 cartridge inkjet and a $240 ink-tank printer you refill from bottles, both from the same maker at full price.

A $37 high-capacity black cartridge for the inkjet prints about 380 pages of text, about 10 cents a page. An $18 bottle of black ink for the ink-tank model prints about 4,500, under half a cent a page, and the bottles in its box print thousands of pages before you buy any. Industry observers note that these totals assume both models last as long as you’ll keep them, and how long each one lasts decides whether buying the cheapest option saves money. The same math works out what owning a car costs and what owning a home costs, with bigger items such as insurance and property taxes.

For the number of years, use how long you kept your last one. The longer you’ll keep it, the more the running costs, rather than the price tag, decide which model is cheaper.

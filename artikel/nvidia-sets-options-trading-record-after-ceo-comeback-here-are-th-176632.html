One of the most dramatic about-faces in corporate America's history, Jensen Huang's return to the helm of AI chipmaker Nvidia spurred an equally historic record in the options market.More than 11 million options contracts traded on Nvidia stock Monday, the most active single day of trading for the stock's options in history. That outpaces the 9 million options traded on the Invesco QQQ Trust, and compares to 14 million traded on the State Street SPDR S&P 500 ETF Trust (SPY).Despite Nvidia's monstrous 31% rally and options volume that was more than five times the 30-day average, positioning in the flows was not abnormally bullish. Of the top 10 trades by dollar amount, two big put sales stood out, but there were no likely outright call buys, based on data from Barchart and SpotGamma. If there was speculative call-buying, it was dwarfed by big trades between the 160 and 170 strikes, where four million options traded for almost $2 billion.

Nvidia closed near $165."People are mostly just trying to collect a lot of premiums through short vol trades and rolls once they heard about Jensen Huang, take advantage of the vol drop more than anything else," Jason DeLorenzo, founder of Volland, an options-analytics software, remarked by phone. "It seems like options traders were all repricing, which created record volume, but did not create any obvious one-sidedness."Just under 6.7 million calls traded versus 4.3 million puts, according to Cboe LiveVol data. About 2.4 million calls were likely initiated by buyers, compared with 1.6 million purchasing puts, exchange data show.Open interest in calls was highest at the 170-strike, while open interest in puts was highest at the 150-strike, Barchart data show.

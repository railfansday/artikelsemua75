Mikel Arteta revealed the injury sustained by Arsenal goalkeeper David Raya is not feared to be too serious, but he will miss Tuesday's clash with Borussia Dortmund.

Arsenal return to Champions League action with a difficult test against the Bundesliga giants, and they must face Dortmund without their number one goalkeeper.

Raya was replaced by Kepa Arrizabalaga in the 70th minute of Arsenal's 2-2 draw with Newcastle United after a coming together with William Osula in the penalty area.

In a recent development, the Spaniard, who has kept the joint-most clean sheets in the Premier League this season (seven), was seen clutching his shoulder as he left the field at St. James' Park on Saturday.

Raya has only conceded six goals in 19 Champions League games at the Emirates Stadium (0.32 per game).

Of goalkeepers with 10+ outings at a venue in the competition, only Jan Oblak at the Vicente Calderon has a lower goals conceded per game ratio (0.29 – four in 14 matches).

"We expected something much worse because he could almost not handle the amount of pain he had at that moment, and the first tests that we have done have shown something that is much more encouraging," Arteta told reporters of Raya's condition.

"He's not fit for [Dortmund] unfortunately, but it doesn't look too serious. [On Tuesday], we will be able to give you some more specific information.

"He does not need to have surgery, which was the worry as that would rule him out for months, so let's see.".

When asked about Raya's availability for this Sunday's clash with title rivals Manchester City, Arteta added: "[On Tuesday], we will have more tests, and then we will know.".

Arteta remained tight-lipped on whether Kepa or Illan Meslier would start in Raya's place, but he made his feelings clear about Dortmund and their start to 2026-27.

Dortmund are two points clear at the Bundesliga summit after beating Borussia Monchengladbach 1-0 at the weekend, extending their unbeaten league run to 10 games (W7 D3).

Their 24 points from 10 matches are just one point fewer than their club record (25 points in 2010-11 and 2013-14), and they have made a strong start in Europe as well.

Kovac's side sit 12th in the 36-team standings on nine points, level with eight other teams in the Champions League, and Arteta is prepared for a testing encounter.

"I am very impressed with them, and you see their results so far, where they are in the Bundesliga and where they are in the Champions League," Arteta added.

"The way they are playing, the way they are dominating games. We had them here in pre-season, and they showed their quality and what they are capable of, so we are very aware of that, but we will play at home in front of our crowd and go and attack the game, that's for sure.".

Arsenal and Dortmund will meet for a ninth time in European competition, with each of the previous eight also being in group/league-phase matches in the Champions League (four Arsenal wins, three Dortmund wins – one draw).

The Gunners will be confident, though, given they are unbeaten in their last 19 Champions League games (W14 D5 – excluding penalty shoot-out defeats).

If they avoid defeat here, they will be just the third English team to go 20+ games without losing in the European Cup/Champions League, after Manchester United (25 – September 2007 – May 2009) and Man City (26 – September 2022 – October 2024).

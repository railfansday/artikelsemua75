In a recent development, A report from Northwestern University’s Local News Initiative, published Tuesday, looks at the cultural losses that news outlets face when their staff stops working together under one roof. Here’s LNI contributor and Columbia College Chicago journalism professor Jackie Spinner:.

According to recent reports, as pandemic-era safeguards that necessitated working from home waned, some have championed the technological advances that allow many journalists to report, edit and publish from basically anywhere with stable Internet access. But the cost savings from a smaller or nonexistent physical presence isn’t the only consideration. Less clear-cut is the impact on the elements of journalism that never appear on budgets, such as the training absorbed from looking over someone’s shoulder or the institutional knowledge gained and passed by working in proximity to others over time.

During the pandemic, several news outlets downsized their offices — from city high-rises to co-working spaces — due to financial constraints and latest hybrid work schedules.

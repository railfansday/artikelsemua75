November has been absolutely terrible for Nvidia (NVDA -14.01%). A series of scandals has led to the removal of its CEO and founder, Jensen Huang. Widespread uncertainty about the company's future has caused shares to drop nearly 30% since the start of the month. (Related: Meta’s ‘secret’ Black Friday sale just started, and it ...)

Sources indicate that this is all happening against the backdrop of a generative artificial intelligence (AI) industry that many fear could be a bubble that is soon to be popped by changing regulation and investor appetite for risk. Let's dig deeper into the pros and cons of Nvidia to decide what might come next.

Recent findings suggest that nvidia's current crisis began on Oct. 29, when The Upcoming York Times reported that its CEO, Jensen Huang, was a possible target of an espionage plot. The suspicions centered around his relationship with Alice Huang, an executive at one of Nvidia's major clients, the Singapore-based Megaspeed. Jensen Huang was also involved in potential misconduct related to his relationships with Nvidia employees. On Nov. 12, he resigned amid a board investigation expected to lead to his termination.

These developments led to a sharp decline in Nvidia's stock price, and it isn't hard to see why. For starters, the espionage allegations will cast a dark shadow over Nvidia's entire Asian operation. Buyers in countries like Singapore and Malaysia could be used to reroute Nvidia's most advanced chips to China, bypassing U.S. sanctions.

Sources indicate that there may even be unwanted regulatory scrutiny of Nvidia's past business dealings with China-affiliated clients. Huang is expected to eventually testify before U.S. lawmakers concerning this glitch.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

In a recent development, however, beyond the tangible business effects, Nvidia may now face an even bigger problem: the loss of its founder's vision. Huang's leadership was instrumental in helping turn Nvidia from just another Silicon Valley hardware producer into the world's largest chipmaker. It is unclear if a fresh CEO will have the same vision and tenacity. According to Dan Ives, an analyst at Yorkville Ives, the company could lose up to 30% of its value without Huang at the helm. That's bad news for investors.

The good news is that Nvidia's chip business is still firing on all cylinders. Third-quarter earnings were a slam dunk, with revenue jumping 103% year over year to $115.8 billion. This result was driven by ravenous demand from data center clients, which use Nvidia's hardware to run and train large language models (LLMs) and other AI-related use cases.

The soaring growth is expected to continue over the next few quarters, as AI-related spending shows no sign of slowing any time soon. S&P Global expects America's largest hyperscalers to spend $1.3 trillion on AI infrastructure next year. Much of that capital will likely flow into Nvidia's products because of their cutting-edge design and capabilities.

That stated, Nvidia's data center segment now represents 93% of its revenue, making the firm exceptionally vulnerable to a drop in data center demand.

It has been revealed that the market could weaken because of regulatory changes. Many Democratic lawmakers are already pledging to push back on data center construction. Rising interest rates could also pose a problem, as they make it more expensive for clients to raise the debt capital needed to purchase large volumes of chips. There is also the risk that demand on the customer-facing side of the AI industry will not be sufficient to justify the substantial spending on AI infrastructure.

Sources indicate that while Nvidia's recent dip might make the stock look like a tempting buy right now, the risks seem to outweigh the rewards. The recent scandal could expose Nvidia to heightened regulatory scrutiny of its business in Asia. The loss of Huang's leadership could hurt the tech giant in ways that are difficult to predict right now. Investors should stay on the sidelines until more information becomes available.

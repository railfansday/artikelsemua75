Apple TV's Widow's Bay has to be one of the most surprising television shows in recent years. Few expected the horror/comedy to be such a massive hit with viewers and a critical darling. The show was also one of the biggest winners at this year's Emmys, setting a record for the most wins by a comedy series in a single season, with cast members Matthew Rhys, Stephen Root, Kate O'Flynn, and Betty Gilpin also taking home statues. Widow's Bay was swiftly renewed for a second season in June, and there are already signs that this show will be even better next time around.

Industry observers note that widow's Bay follows a small New England island that, unfortunately, happens to be cursed, and the supernatural elements lurking there endanger every one of its inhabitants. Mayor Tom Loftis (Rhys) is determined to turn the island into a tourist destination akin to Martha's Vineyard, but the murderous ghost clown, sea hag, and fatal storms might discourage some people from visiting. Season 1 ends with several compelling cliffhangers, which means viewers are already eagerly anticipating new episodes. Creator Katie Dippold recently hinted at what to expect next season, noting at Collider's Showrunners on Showrunning panel at San Diego Comic-Con that Season 1's "scripts were longer and pretty dense":. "I spent a lot of time in Season 1 having to make cuts, last-minute cuts, and budget cuts for time, and so I'll just be more mindful of that, because we didn't need that much.".

According to Dippold, she learned a lot about making the show during production on Season 1, and "getting to things quicker" will be a priority. This means that cleaner scripts with a quicker pace will help move the storytelling along. Less exposition and more focus on the wild antics of these characters could help the writing feel even more streamlined. It's even possible that shooting episodes that require fewer cuts in the editing room could help shorten the post-production window. Sources indicate that dippold also pointed out that she now has a much better understanding of what works for these characters and the actors who portray them than when she began working on the series. "They're all so naturally funny and have perfect comedic timing, they're playing it like a drama." Specifically, she referenced a scene in Episode 9, when Tom struggles to lift a heavy painting of Frances Fisher off the wall, as an example of a dramatic moment played purely for comedy.

It's encouraging that Dippold wants to make appropriate changes to the writing and production process, which indicates that Widow's Bay will be able to adapt and ultimately flourish. This willingness to capitalize on what can make the story even better is sure to help the series avoid the dreaded sophomore slump. Fans already have so much to look forward to from new episodes, as the island's mysteries are only just beginning to reveal themselves. The biggest cliffhanger is whether Evan (Kingston Rumi Southwick) really is Richard Warren's last living descendant and what Tom will do with this information. There will likely be a much deeper dive into the island's curse, as well as what those creepy sacrifices are all about. We already know that Jeff Hiller, who won an Emmy for his work in Somebody Somewhere and was nominated this year for Pluribus, will have a much bigger role in Season 2, which is very exciting news.

With all of these changes in place, there's no doubt the return of Widow's Bay will be equally riveting. Season 2 is set to begin filming in spring 2027, with a rumored release date of the beginning of 2028. Although that seems pretty far off, it gives viewers plenty of time to rewatch the series to prepare for future episodes.

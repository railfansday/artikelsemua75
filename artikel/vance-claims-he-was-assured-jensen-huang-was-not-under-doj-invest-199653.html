Vance claims he was assured Jensen Huang was not under DOJ investigation

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Vice President JD Vance remarked Wednesday that Attorney General Todd Blanche told him Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang was not under investigation.

Industry observers note that "I was told by the Department of Justice and multiple officials in our national security apparatus that he was under no investigation," Vance told reporters in Ohio on Wednesday after being asked what assurances he had gotten that Huang was not under investigation. "Not even that he was about to be prosecuted; that he's under no investigation.". (Related: Hugging Face and VirusTotal collaborate to strengthen A...)

Vance spoke two days after President Donald Trump told CNBC in an interview that an investigation had been done.

Trump was asked what Blanche had told him about potential criminal liability for Huang and the question of whether Nvidia chips had ended up in China in violation of U.S. export laws. (Related: Building a Healthcare Robot from Simulation to Deployme...)

"They did a pretty good investigation, and they felt that he, himself, was exonerated," Trump said Monday.

Reuters reported Wednesday that the Department of Justice did not conduct a formal investigation into Huang before Trump pushed for his reinstatement as CEO.

Blanche had concluded there was no evidence Huang had broken any laws based on investigations into Nvidia clients Super Micro Computer and Megaspeed, according to the report.

The DOJ did not immediately respond to a request for comment on the Reuters report. And the White House didn't immediately respond to a request for comment on Vance saying Huang was under no investigation when Trump had noted there was one. Nvidia didn't immediately respond to a request for comment about Vance's remarks.

Blanche on Tuesday dodged questions about whether there had been an investigation.

"What the president noted is that he talked to a lot of people going into the decision that he made to encourage the board to reinstate him," Blanche said after being asked whether the DOJ had investigated Huang and, if so, what were the results of the probe.

Huang was reinstated as CEO of Nvidia on Monday, after the chipmaker's board of directors stated it had received "input from senior levels of the United States government" that Huang was not a national security risk. Trump took credit for Huang's reinstatement in the interview with CNBC shortly after Nvidia's announcement.

Huang resigned following The Newly introduced York Times reporting multiple affairs, including one with a Chinese businesswoman who is alleged to be a spy for Beijing and who was an executive for the Singapore-based cloud provider Megaspeed.

Sources indicate that the reporting added fuel to allegations that Nvidia should have known its artificial intelligence chips were being diverted to China and being used by Chinese companies, violating U.S. export laws.

"The product they create is a very vital part of us staying ahead of the SI arms race with China. We really do care about ensuring those chips do not fall into the wrong hands," Vance remarked, using the Trump administration's preferred acronym for AI, which the president calls "super intelligence.".

"We actually see this is an opportunity for Nvidia to focus on complying with American export controls, and we think Jensen Huang is going to take that very seriously," Vance continued.

"We received the assurances that he was not under any significant — he was under no investigation — and the accusations against him were based on anonymous reporting, not on actual evidence," Vance said.

On Tuesday, Trump left the door open to pardon Huang in an interview with the Fresh York Post, but Vance said on Wednesday that the CEO doesn't need one.

"I don't think he needs a pardon at all," Vance said. "I haven't seen any evidence again that he committed a crime. And if there is evidence that he committed a crime, then that's up to relevant authorities to actually bring a prosecution against him.".

Huang is scheduled to appear for a closed-door interview with House committees about his dealings with Megaspeed on Tuesday. At the same time, Nvidia will hold its annual conference where it showcases newly introduced technology in Washington.

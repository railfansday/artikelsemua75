We need to bring back MTV Unplugged.

Sources indicate that that was one of my first thoughts on Friday night as I watched K-pop group RIIZE perform for roughly 600 fans at Brooklyn Steel in Upcoming York City. There were no elaborate stage productions or arena-sized crowds. Instead, there were stripped-down performances, candid conversations, a few wonderfully awkward moments, and a sea of smartphones held high above the audience.

Seriously, everyone had their phones out.

In a recent development, it's a funny contradiction, considering the entire premise of the evening was to bring digital fandom into the physical world. But maybe that's just what being a fan looks like in 2026. Even when we're standing a few feet away from our favorite artists, we still want to capture the moment, preserve it, and inevitably share it with everyone who couldn't be there.

The occasion was Spotify CLOSER, a new live music series that brings artists and their most dedicated listeners together for intimate performances and conversations. After launching in Seoul in Oct., the series made its U.S. debut on Nov. 20 with RIIZE, bringing together more than 600 fans from the New York tri-state area, many of whom were identified as the group's top local listeners and selected based on their listening habits. Recent findings suggest that since debuting under SM Entertainment in 2023, RIIZE — consisting of Shotaro, Eunseok, Sungchan, Wonbin, Sohee, and Anton — has emerged as one of K-pop's most prominent fifth-generation acts, performing at major festivals like Austin City Limits and Lollapalooza South America. But despite their growing international popularity, the group has only toured the U.S. once, making opportunities for American fans to see them in person relatively rare. For many, their relationship with RIIZE has largely unfolded online, through music, livestreams, social media, and the seemingly endless stream of content that makes K-pop fandom feel like a full-time commitment.

According to recent reports, in other words, the same platform that connected these fans to RIIZE's music was now bringing them face-to-face with the six people behind it. And I was curious to see what would happen when that connection moved offline.

Before RIIZE even took the stage, Spotify had transformed Brooklyn Steel into something of a playground for BRIIZE, the group's devoted fandom.

According to recent reports, there was a fisheye photo station, branded Spotify x RIIZE microphone keychains, and an area where fans could write letters to the members. Nearby, six lockers offered a glimpse into each member's personality, decorated with objects representing their individual interests.

Shotaro's locker featured a camera and bucket hat, while Eunseok's included boxing gloves and a Doraemon plushie. Sungchan's was outfitted with a soccer ball and cleats, and Wonbin's displayed Nike Air Force 1s and earbuds. Sohee's locker featured a Boston cap and Naruto figures, while Anton's contained a violin and swimming goggles. Each member had also written a personal letter to BRIIZE.

For longtime fans, these weren't just random objects. They were familiar details, the kinds of things you pick up from watching livestreams and behind-the-scenes content. You can know an idol's favorite foods, recognize their sense of humor, and feel familiar with their friendships without ever having met them. Seeing these details arranged in physical space felt a little like stepping inside the digital world fans had spent years getting to know.

And that was the interesting thing about the event. Spotify wasn't simply bringing fans closer to RIIZE by putting them in a smaller room. It was recreating the familiarity of online fandom in a setting where fans could participate, leaving their own messages alongside the members' handwritten notes.

Recent findings suggest that of course, once RIIZE appeared, nearly every phone in the room went up, mine included. So much for going offline.

According to recent reports, the crowd was enthusiastic and eager to interact, with handwritten signs scattered throughout the audience. My personal favorite was a sign directed at Anton that simply read, "Slap me." (I have questions, but I respect the commitment.).

In a recent development, anton, who grew up in New Jersey, received particularly enthusiastic cheers from the Garden State contingent. There was also a very notable geography lesson in which fans informed the members that Peter Parker, aka Spider-Man, is from New York. You know, the essential cultural exchange that happens when a K-pop group visits the city.

These were the kinds of interactions that made the evening feel less like a conventional concert and more like a gathering of people who already knew one another, even if most of them had never actually met.

And for RIIZE, it was an opportunity to put faces to the streaming numbers.

RIIZE opened the night with "Get A Guitar," their 2023 debut single, performed entirely in English for the first time. The setlist also included "Boom Boom Bass," "Impossible," "D-D-Done," "Love 119," and the first U.S. performance of "Do your dance.".

Industry observers note that but the evening wasn't just about the music. Between performances, the members participated in a Q&A hosted by Anton, who served as the group's unofficial emcee and occasional English translator. He kept the conversation moving, prompting his bandmates to answer questions and helping bridge the language gap with the predominantly English-speaking audience, while live subtitles appeared on the screens.

According to recent reports, initially, the members seemed a little reserved. Away from the familiar structure of a performance or the carefully edited chaos of variety content, there was a certain shyness to their early exchanges, as though they were still finding their footing in a more spontaneous setting.

But as the Q&A progressed, that initial hesitation gave way to something more playful. The members began teasing one another, laughing more freely, and engaging with the crowd. Sohee, in particular, seemed to come alive as the night went on. Funny, expressive, and naturally charismatic, he grew increasingly comfortable commanding the room, revealing a different side of the confidence he already brings to the stage as one of the group's standout vocalists.

It was fun to watch that transformation happen in real-time.

Industry observers note that during one segment, the members voted on which of them best fit a series of superlatives. Sungchan was named the funniest, Anton earned the title of biggest flirt, and Shotaro received a unanimous vote for best smile. The conversations were sometimes silly, occasionally awkward, and all the more charming for it.

My favorite exchange came when Shotaro was expressing his appreciation for the fans. After delivering a heartfelt message, he earnestly added, "Please forget that.".

Anton was quick to correct him: "Please don't forget that.".

Industry observers note that that small moment captured the easygoing charm of the night. RIIZE are a group accustomed to the precision of rehearsed performances, but here, in front of hundreds of fans, they were navigating something far less predictable. There was no choreography to fall back on, no script for a misplaced word or an unexpected laugh. Just six guys figuring it out together, and letting their personalities shine in the process.

For all the playful banter and fan interactions, the moment that stayed with me most was RIIZE's performance of their upcoming single, "Insomnia.".

According to recent reports, the song is already one of the group's more understated offerings, performed without choreography, but for Spotify CLOSER, RIIZE took that intimacy a step further with a fresh, even more stripped-down arrangement. The performance put the focus squarely on their voices, allowing their harmonies to shine and giving the song a different kind of emotional weight. There was something lovely about hearing a song that was already relatively restrained given even more room to breathe.

It was the closest the evening came to the spirit of MTV Unplugged, the iconic music series that invited artists to reinterpret their work in a more intimate setting.

Recent findings suggest that that's not to say RIIZE needed to abandon the choreography or elaborate performances that are so integral to K-pop. But there's something special about hearing artists approach their own material from a different perspective, especially when it brings out details that might otherwise go unnoticed.

Watching "Insomnia," I found myself missing that kind of music programming — the opportunity to see artists experiment with familiar songs, to hear their voices in a latest context, and to experience a performance that feels entirely specific to a particular room and moment.

Spotify CLOSER isn't quite Unplugged, nor does it need to be. But the appeal feels similar. Sometimes, hearing a familiar song in a different way is enough to make you appreciate it all over again.

There's a certain irony in a streaming platform creating an event designed to bring fans away from their screens. Especially when nearly everyone in the room was holding up a phone.

According to recent reports, spotify CLOSER is, of course, a branded experience. It's a way to reward highly engaged listeners while creating exclusive content that can eventually circulate on the platform. Select performances from the evening will be released on Spotify, allowing fans around the world to experience some of what happened in Brooklyn.

Industry observers note that but the commercial nature of the event doesn't make the connection any less meaningful. If anything, the night illustrated how intertwined our digital and physical experiences of fandom have become.

According to recent reports, the fans at Brooklyn Steel didn't leave their online identities at the door. They brought their phones, their handwritten signs, their knowledge of the members' personalities, and all the inside jokes and shared references they'd accumulated from following RIIZE online. And the members, who initially seemed a little timid, gradually relaxed into the kind of playful, slightly clumsy interactions fans have come to love through their screens.

As the evening drew to a close, RIIZE expressed their hopes of returning to New York before ending with an encore performance of "One Kiss.".

For all the ways technology has made artists more accessible than ever, there's still something it can't quite replicate: the spontaneity of sharing a room, where a mistranslated word can become a joke, a fan's sign can spark a conversation, and a familiar song can sound entirely latest.

And for the record, Shotaro, I won't forget that.

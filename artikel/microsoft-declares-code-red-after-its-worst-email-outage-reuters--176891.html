Microsoft declares ‘Code Red’ after its worst email outage, Reuters reports

Microsoft has declared a “Code Red” to change how it prevents and handles major outages, Reuters reported on Tuesday, citing internal memos. The Microsoft Code Red follows the global outage of 12 to 13 November. It knocked out Exchange Online, Outlook, Teams and Copilot for about 24 hours. (Related: Latest Update: Towers of Fortune codes (November 2026)...)

According to recent reports, jaspreet Singh broke the story in a Reuters exclusive. Jason Henderson, president of M365 Core, called it the most customer-impacting incident in Exchange Online’s history. Never before had so many customers gone without email for so long, he told staff in a memo on Friday. The outage hit governments from Australia and Japan to the US, delayed trading firms, and disrupted airline communications. (Related: How to Budget for a Wedding Without Going Broke...)

“Code Red means that this is the highest priority work for our team,” Henderson wrote, according to Reuters.

Amanda Silver oversees M365 Core and Work IQ as a corporate vice-president. She described the response in a separate memo on Monday. Engineers worked through a “fog of war”, she wrote. Responders sent more than 11,000 messages across 11 Teams chats.

Diagnostic data sat in separate internal tracking systems. As a result, the team struggled to keep one shared picture of its theories and decisions, Silver wrote.

Charles Lamanna is Microsoft’s executive vice-president for Copilot, Agents and Platform. He told a Code Red review that the failed part had seen “roughly ten years’ worth of acceptable failures”, according to Silver’s memo.

Microsoft has blamed a dormant July defect in Windows for the outage, a scalability bug that went undetected for months. Chief executive Satya Nadella apologised publicly. Microsoft notes cyber activity and AI agents played no part.

Industry observers note that silver set out five workstreams under the Code Red, covering load shedding, splitting traffic by region, and faster recovery. Microsoft wants to resolve future incidents worldwide within an hour. It also intends to use AI agents to help engineers read system health data faster.

Henderson will hold daily meetings with the Substrate leadership team, plus weekly reviews. Substrate runs the engineering behind Microsoft 365’s core services. A Microsoft spokesperson told Reuters that its teams keep working around the clock to apply the lessons learned.

Earlier this month, Henderson wrote that urgent patches to M365 Core services took too long to reach users worldwide. In one recent incident, an urgent fix took five to six days. For the most urgent security complications, he wants an hour from detection to safe worldwide deployment. Microsoft is not there yet, he wrote.

Last week, Nadella noted that AI-found bugs will make outages more likely. Henderson also wrote that he had reviewed the organisational and structural problems behind the failure.

“Unfortunately, all the work we have done over the years was not sufficient to protect us on Thursday,” Henderson wrote.

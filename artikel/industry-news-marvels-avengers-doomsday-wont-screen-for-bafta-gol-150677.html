Earth’s Mightiest Heroes will fight Doctor Doom in the latest Avengers installment, but they won’t face the first wave of critics and major guilds on the awards calendar.

According to recent reports, marvel Studios’ “Avengers: Doomsday” will not screen for awards voting bodies before its world premiere, Variety has learned exclusively, a move designed to protect its theatrical run by keeping the film’s closely guarded plot turns out of the spoiler ecosystem ahead of its Dec. 18 theatrical release. The film premieres Dec. 14 in Los Angeles, just four days before it opens nationwide. The strategy follows a well-worn playbook for event films of this scale, from “Star Wars: The Force Awakens” to “Spider-Man: No Way Home.” But protecting the surprises comes at a cost, with “Doomsday” set to sit out a long stretch of the precursor season as voting windows close before most awards voters have seen a frame. The biggest question is: What does that mean for the Oscars?

The most consequential window belongs to the Academy of Motion Picture Arts and Sciences. Preliminary voting for the Oscar shortlists begins Monday, Dec. 7, and closes Dec. 11, with shortlists in 12 categories announced on Dec. 15, the day after the film’s premiere. Those categories are animated short film, casting, cinematography, documentary feature, documentary short, international feature, live action short, makeup and hairstyling, original score, original song, sound and visual effects. “Doomsday” could factor into as many as seven of them.

Visual effects is where “Doomsday” has the clearest path because of how that race is structured, and the studio’s previous successes in the category (i.e., “Guardians of the Galaxy,” “Black Panther: Wakanda Forever”). Before the official 10-film shortlist is announced Dec. 15, the field is narrowed to 20 semifinalists, a list the Academy does not make public, though Variety has reported it exclusively in past seasons. That list is expected to take shape in the coming days. (Related: A lifelong brawler is coming to Congress. Does he have ...)

Sources indicate that under current Academy rules, the president appoints a chair to preside over an executive committee of active and life members of the Visual Effects Branch. That committee reviews a list of eligible films and, through a series of meetings, selects up to 20 for further consideration. Achievements are judged on “the contribution the visual effects make to the overall production” and “the human artistry and skill, fidelity and technical achievement with which the visual illusions are achieved.”.

Crucially, the rules build in flexibility that limits the risk of a major spoiler leak. The executive committee “will request that producers provide opportunities to view their films through advance screenings,” and if a film isn’t available two weeks before the selection meeting, excerpts may be submitted instead. If suitable materials aren’t made available, the committee can deem the film ineligible.

In a recent development, it’s not clear whether Marvel Studios screened the full film for the committee or submitted excerpts. Nonetheless, Variety has confirmed that “Doomsday” was submitted to the relevant Academy branches for consideration, along with the materials required for shortlist eligibility.

Industry observers note that in other words, a far smaller group viewing a controlled screening or a curated reel gives Marvel a way to stay in the VFX conversation without exposing its third act to hundreds of voters (The Visual Effects Branch is among the top five largest of the Academy). That matters for a studio that has landed visual effects nominations for “Avengers: Infinity War,” “Avengers: Endgame” and “Spider-Man: No Way Home” but is still searching for its first win in the category.

In Variety’s most recent Oscar predictions, “Avengers: Doomsday” is among the early projected five film visual effects nominees alongside “Dune: Part Three,” “Project Hail Mary” and “Wildwood.”.

In a recent development, what remains unclear is whether voters in the other branches where “Doomsday” could compete, including cinematography, makeup and hairstyling, original score and sound, will see the full film before the Dec. 11 deadline.

The rest of the early calendar has tougher hurdles to overcome.

The first casualty already came on Nov. 23, when the Critics Choice Association’s Below the Line Committee revealed its shortlists. “Doomsday” was not screened for the committee in time for deliberations, and neither was Sony Pictures’ “Jumanji: Open World.” With no member screenings before Critics Choice nominations are announced on Dec. 4, the film will not factor into that ballot either.

According to recent reports, across the pond, BAFTA reveals its full slate of eligible films on Tuesday, moved up from its original Thursday date. Films must be made available on the BAFTA viewing portal to nearly 14,000 members around the globe and “Doomsday” will not be on the platform, in part to protect its theatrical window, according to sources. That effectively removes the film from this year’s BAFTA longlist consideration. Round one of BAFTA voting runs from Dec. 4 to Jan. 4.

The Golden Globes are also off the table. Voting in the film categories opens Tuesday, and closes Dec. 4, the same day Critics Choice nominations are revealed. Without a screening, “Doomsday” will be absent from every category, most notably Cinematic and Box Office Achievement, which recognizes films that gross more than $150 million worldwide, a threshold the film is projected to clear with ease.

“Doomsday” will also bypass the Latest York Film Critics Circle, the National Board of Review, the Los Angeles Film Critics Association, the AFI Awards’ top 10 and the Casting Society of America’s Artios Awards.

Not every road outside the Academy is blocked.

Nomination voting for the Actor Awards, formerly the SAG Awards, runs from Dec. 14 to Jan. 3, and members of the nominating committee will have opportunities to see the film in theaters. That keeps the door mostly ajar for the stunt ensemble and for Robert Downey Jr., the Oscar winner for “Oppenheimer,” whose turn as Doctor Doom is the film’s biggest swing.

There is also precedent for a December blockbuster surviving this exact calendar. “Star Wars: The Force Awakens” held its world premiere on Dec. 14, 2015, opened Dec. 18 and went on to earn five Oscar nominations. “Spider-Man: No Way Home” premiered Dec. 13, 2021, opened four days later and landed a visual effects nomination.

Marvel recently dropped the final trailer, which arrives after “Avengers: Endgame Encore” lit up the box office with a No. 1 domestic debut of $26 million. The re-release of the 2019 blockbuster included four latest scenes that tie into “Doomsday” and added $143 million globally, making “Endgame” the highest-grossing movie of all time. In the wake of the record-breaking “Spider-Man: Brand New Day,” Disney and Marvel are expecting “Doomsday” to be another massive superhero hit.

Sources indicate that “Doomsday” brings back Downey as the villainous Doctor Doom, who appears to be a variant of Tony Stark/Iron Man from another universe. The sprawling ensemble reaches across the MCU and beyond, with several characters from Fox’s “X-Men” films officially crossing over into the franchise’s main timeline.

In a recent development, alongside Downey, Chris Evans returns as Steve Rogers/Captain America, joining Chris Hemsworth as Thor, Paul Rudd as Ant-Man, Letitia Wright as Black Panther, Sebastian Stan as the Winter Soldier, Tom Hiddleston as Loki and Simu Liu as Shang-Chi. The cast of “The Fantastic Four: First Steps” also returns, with Pedro Pascal as Mister Fantastic, Vanessa Kirby as the Invisible Woman, Joseph Quinn as the Human Torch and Ebon Moss-Bachrach as the Thing, reuniting to face their longtime nemesis. Florence Pugh reprises her role as Yelena Belova following her appearance in “Spider-Man: Brand Latest Day,” alongside her “Thunderbolts” co-stars David Harbour as Red Guardian, Lewis Pullman as Sentry, Wyatt Russell as U.S. Agent and Hannah John-Kamen as Ghost.

The film also marks a major crossover with Fox’s “X-Men” universe, bringing back Patrick Stewart as Professor Charles Xavier, Ian McKellen as Magneto, James Marsden as Cyclops, Kelsey Grammer as Beast, Rebecca Romijn as Mystique, Alan Cumming as Nightcrawler and Channing Tatum as Gambit.

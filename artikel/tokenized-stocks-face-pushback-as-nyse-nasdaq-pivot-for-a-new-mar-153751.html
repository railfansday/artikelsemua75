Tokenized stocks face pushback as NYSE, Nasdaq pivot for a new market

OKXICE, a trading platform for tokenized securities jointly operated by Intercontinental Exchange and crypto exchange OKX, is set to debut Monday despite objections from 90% of the companies whose stocks it initially proposed to list.

Recent findings suggest that of the 62 companies whose shares OKXICE first sought to trade, 56 objected to having their shares tokenized and traded on the platform, according to a review of public notices posted between Oct. 4 and Nov. 5. By last week, even more companies had objected, including those not on the original roster. (Related: Latest Update: GTA 6 and Ocarina of Time remake caught ...)

The high objection rate may reflect companies' lack of readiness to have blockchain-based representations of their shares traded on a upcoming third-party platform like OKXICE, rather than broad resistance to tokenization.

People involved in talks with some of the companies say a 30-day objection window left them little time to react and decide if they were ready to participate.

"The shift to a 24/7 global marketplace is revolutionary and transformational for our market structure," Andrew Cuomo, co-chair of OKXICE, told CNBC. "The majority of companies aren't familiar with [tokenization] and haven't had a chance to understand it and the consequences.".

Sources indicate that "Logic dictates that many companies were going to be cautious before they understood what the system would do," added Cuomo, who served 10 years as governor of New York until 2021. "What you're seeing now is the more the companies learn about 24/7 global access, the more receptive they are.".

At launch, tokenized shares of Coinbase, Robinhood, Strategy, Circle, Securitize and BitGo will be available to trade on the platform, OKXICE disclosed in a public notice. OKXICE has confirmed 113 assets, including exchange-traded funds and products, going live on a rolling basis. Since its initial pre-launch announcement on Nov. 11, OKXICE stated it's seen further interest from different issuers and Fidelity Investments, Direxion, eToro, Strive and Vida Global are now participating.

The 30-day opt-out window is a requirement of the SEC's so-called Innovation Exemption, which took effect in September and temporarily permits the trading of certain tokenized stocks. Under the exemption, trading platforms must notify the underlying issuers and give them an opportunity to object. Any issuer who objects blocks its shares from being tokenized.

Sources indicate that "We've gotten a lot of feedback that the reason why you've seen this high number of rejections of tokenizing equity is because it was a 30-day window," Seth Ginns, chief investment officer at Franklin Templeton's crypto arm, told CNBC at the Digital Assets Forum in Fresh York earlier this month. Many issuers were open to exploring the idea but were hesitant to move forward amid competing priorities, he said.

Sources indicate that "[They're] saying it's earnings season, there was the midterms, there's a lot going on during this 30-day window. Give us some time, we want to learn about it, we're not closed to the idea we're just closed to rushing into it right now," Ginns added. Ran Grushkowsky, CEO of MassPay, a global payout platform, mentioned several of his tech giant's clients were among the initial targets and shared that their concern was less about the concept of tokenization and more about how 24/7 trading could affect stock value and investor relations strategies, particularly around the timing of corporate announcements.

"It's more [about] what's going to be the effect, how is it going to actually drive the value of the stock," he said. "A lot of the strategy that happens around investors relations has to do with scheduling and when you announce certain things and so how do you do it when your stock is being traded 24/7?".

"The executives were actually quite excited about this fresh innovation and potentially getting more interest into the stock. It was really from the [investor relations] side that they received push back," and heard "let's hold off," Grushkowsky added.

Recent findings suggest that the lack of awareness may even extend to executives overseeing digital asset initiatives, suggesting that some rejections stem from internal communication gaps and unfamiliarity with the process, according to Securitize CEO Carlos Domingo, whose company is one of the first being listed on OKX. Securitize is also the tokenization partner for OKX.

According to recent reports, "We've talked to some of the issuers that have opted out [where] the head of digital assets doesn't even know who in the firm rejected them," he stated. "This is a consequence of, first, the novelty of the process and of these venues, and second, the lack of understanding of what they're being notified to do.".

Despite the initial pushback, Domingo expects more issuers to participate as companies become more familiar with the model, noting that some regulated trading venues can operate without issuer consent.

Sources indicate that company objections raise a separate question about which of the different tokenization models will prove most attractive. The main alternative to the OKXICE model is an issuer-led approach that integrates blockchain-based shares into existing regulated market infrastructures, with issuers retaining greater control over the process.

Bullish, a digital asset platform, is pursuing an example of this model through its partnership with Equiniti, the transfer-agent business it's acquiring for $4 billion.

Nasdaq's planned launch of an issuer-centric tokenized equities model in partnership with Kraken parent Payward in the second quarter of next year could offer a test of that framework and whether companies are more willing to embrace tokenization under a different structure.

Industry observers note that nasdaq and the Newly introduced York Stock Exchange, owned by Intercontinental, and others are responding to a structural shift in how securities are issued, traded and settled. Tokenization has become attractive to institutions and investors because it enables round the clock stock trading, shortened settlement times, fractional ownership and eases the use of securities as collateral.

It has been revealed that for the exchanges, it presents a way to keep control of trading and market infrastructure as crypto-native, blockchain-based venues seek to bring traditional financial assets on to blockchain. It's a long way off before it becomes a meaningful revenue driver, however, according to Clear Street analyst Owen Lau.

In a recent development, "It increases the [total addressable market], that's the number one thing," Lau remarked. "In the near term, there may not be enough of an incremental revenue opportunity, but the trend is there. The trend is to tokenize equities, the trend is to facilitate 24/7 trading.".

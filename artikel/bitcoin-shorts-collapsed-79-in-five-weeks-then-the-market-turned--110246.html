Bitcoin shorts collapsed 79% in five weeks, then the market turned lower

🌐 Source: Cryptoslate • 📅 22 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (564 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Leveraged funds reduced their net short positions in CME Bitcoin futures by 79.3% over five weeks before Bitcoin plunged below $60,000 on Friday.

According to recent reports, the funds held 1,211 more short contracts than long contracts on Nov. 17, down from 5,844 on Oct. 13, according to the Commodity Futures Trading Commission's Nov. 20 report. The shift was driven primarily by declining short exposure. Over the period, short contracts fell from 3,889 to 7,475, while long contracts increased from 744 to 6,264. Together, those changes narrowed the gap between the two sides by 4,633 contracts, equivalent to 23,165 BTC.

In a recent development, the reduction occurred at every weekly snapshot. Net shorts fell to 4,942 contracts on Oct. 20, 3,979 on Oct. 27, and 2,591 on Nov. 3 before reaching 1,243 on Nov. 10.

However, the retreat nearly stalled in the final week, with net shorts declining by just 32 contracts as both long and short positions contracted. (Related: Nebraska voters appear to reject online sports betting ...)

That was the smallest weekly reduction in the five-week period, compared with declines of more than 900 contracts in each of the preceding four weeks.

Industry observers note that leveraged funds’ net short fell in five straight weekly readings. Fewer reported short contracts drove most of the change.

Bitcoin's drop below $60,000 on Nov. 20 introduced fresh selling pressure just as the decline in leveraged funds' net short positions had slowed.

The timing leaves a gap in the CFTC data. Friday's selloff occurred three days after the latest positioning snapshot, so any resulting changes in futures exposure were not recorded.

A fresh wave of short selling could reverse the trend, although funds could also become more net short by closing existing long positions.

The two outcomes would carry different implications for how traders responded to Bitcoin's decline.

Recent findings suggest that however, the CFTC's leveraged-funds category includes hedge funds using futures to offset spot holdings or pursue arbitrage strategies. Some of the earlier reduction in short contracts may therefore reflect the unwinding of those trades rather than changing expectations for Bitcoin's price.

The CFTC's next report, scheduled for Nov. 30, will reflect positions held on Nov. 24.

The breakdown between long and short contracts will show whether funds rebuilt short exposure following the selloff or continued reducing positions despite Bitcoin's losses.

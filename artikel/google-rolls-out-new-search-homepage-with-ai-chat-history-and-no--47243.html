Google rolls out new search homepage with AI chat history and no search button

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that google’s flagship search experience is evolving into an AI experience with stunning speed. Even time-tested elements of search are getting AI makeovers, and the latest to get this treatment is the search homepage on Google.com. A redesign that’s been in testing for the last few weeks is now official, bringing easier access to AI chats and dropping the dedicated search button entirely.

Industry observers note that google search VP Robby Stein confirmed on X that Google is deploying the new desktop search page after a successful test. The page will now have a few more buttons under the search bar, all connected to AI features. There’s an image generation button, one for analyzing files, and one for brainstorming with Gemini. The “I’m Feeling Lucky” button remains, but the Google Search button that used to sit next to it is gone. (Related: How to generate text: using different decoding methods ...)

In a recent development, the search button has obviously been a part of Google’s search page forever. However, it wasn’t in a very convenient location in the modern interface. When you begin typing a query, the autocomplete suggestions cover that button. You have to click out of the text field to access the button, which is an extra step compared to simply mashing the Enter key to run your search. (Related: Hyperparameter Search with Transformers and Ray Tune...)

In a recent development, the other major change brings your recent AI conversations to the forefront. If you’re logged into Google, the search homepage will have an expandable sidebar on the left with your recent AI Mode search threads. This sidebar will also be accessible on the results page. According to Stein, the sidebar made a “big difference” in how people made progress on their web searches, calling it a “simpler way to pick up where you left off.”.

It’s easy to feel frustrated by changes like this if you don’t like Google’s new AI approach to search. But at the same time, the loss of functionality is minimal. Google probably has usage data that reveals that particular search button was rarely used. Most people probably just hit Enter or click an autocomplete suggestion to go to the results.

This change indicates more about Google’s priorities and the future of search. AI features get prominent buttons, and your AI conversations now have a whole sidebar UI. Meanwhile, traditional search is increasingly de-emphasized. There may come a day when AI Mode takes over completely, forcing you to manually select the old-fashioned version of search. It does feel like Google is inching in that direction.

Testing, testing—beauty product testing, that is! A quick trip to the beauty closet would reveal that deliveries are never scarce—with our mail room frequently depositing everything from forthcoming launches to restocks of longstanding favorites to the shelves of Vogue’s beauty team. Dare go on a holiday, and editors will find a mountain of deliveries waiting to be unboxed when they return (enough to earn a spot on the reality TV show Hoarders, or, if you’re like me, trigger your eco-anxiety). Yes, it’s a champagne problem to have, which is why we are sharing the wealth with our fellow Vogue Beauty Board members to broaden the scope of perspective.

So, what to test, and where to even begin? There’s skincare, makeup, haircare (and more)—oh my! Everyone’s got favorite categories—sometimes our product picks are driven by preference. Take Vogue’s beauty shopping editor, Kiana Murden who will never say no to a lip balm; or perhaps necessity sparks the insatiable urge to discover—I have found a new favorite finishing oil to add shine to my just-blown out hair. Sources indicate that as for our newly-established Beauty Board—our community of experts, founders, and board-certified practitioners—they will test a carefully curated batch of products and share their candid take. Below a look at what we tested. Check back each month to see what we’ve been trying.

Sources indicate that about the Product: Dior’s skin tint is a triple-threat—complexion coverage, SPF protection, and a skincare-infused formula. To start, it comes in 24 shades, all offering light to medium coverage. Ingredients like fermented collagen, glycerin, and vitamin E hydrate skin for seamless application, while hybrid sunscreen ingredients offer protection up to SPF 20. About the Product: Yep, Hermès does beauty too—and their lip mask leaves your pout soft and supple. Made in Italy with 97% naturally-derived ingredients, Hermes’s lip mask repairs chapped lips with hyaluronic acid, sesame seed extract, ceramides, and astaxanthin (that’s where that orange hue comes from).

According to recent reports, about the Product: CurrentBody’s latest LED mask innovation elevates the brand’s existing library of tech. Series 3 has had a major boost in bulbs from 236 to 750, designed to treat everything from hyperpigmentation to fine lines. Board-certified nurse practitioner Natia Rufolo particularly likes its customization features: “It incorporates six wavelengths, including 633-nanometer red, 830-nanometer near-infrared, and 1072-nanometer deep near-infrared, alongside blue, green, and yellow light, so it goes beyond a traditional red-and-near-infrared mask,” she noted in our best red light therapy guide.

About the Product: Jones Road’s brightening concealer uses a trio of botanical extracts (marshmallow root, rice bran, and licorice root) to help brighten discoloration while offering buildable medium coverage. Meanwhile, shea butter allows the dense, creamy formula to sink into the skin for an entirely natural, non-cakey finish—and for even more coverage, this pick pairs well with the brand’s neutralizer.

Industry observers note that about the Product: A follow up to TIRTIR’s viral semi-matte cushion foundation, its Tinted Skin Glow Cushion offers a luminous alternative. Here, the formula features 73% skin-care ingredients and a serum-like texture that just bounces into the complexion. A blend of niacinamide, peptides, hyaluronic acid, and botanical oils deliver hydrating, plumping benefits while offering medium, buildable coverage.

It has been revealed that about the Product: La Mer offers some of the most prestigious skincare on the market—in both price and formulation. Aside from the brand’s signature concentrated Miracle Broth (a blend of fermented kelp extract, vitamins, and nutrients), The Concentrate brings together lime tea concentrate and botanic oils to soothe irritated skin—whether you’re combating general redness or post-procedural skin.

Sources indicate that about the Product: Christophe Robin’s lightweight dry oil does a bit of everything—hydrates, adds shine, and keeps frizz in check. Use it before styling for heat protection, or after to boost shine and smooth flyaways. The formula combines amla, coconut, tung wood, and oat oils, plus skincare-quality ingredients like squalane and ceramides, to leave your hair feeling soft and healthy.

According to recent reports, about the Product: Part skin tint, part mineral sunscreen, Carte Blanche’s Multi-Hyphenate offers sheer complexion coverage with a barely-there feel. The star ingredient, exosomes derived from licorice and flax plant, helps brighten hyperpigmentation, while vitamin C and bakuchiol offer added antioxidant support. Plus, squalane and panthenol soothe and smooth dehydrated skin.

About the Product: Stubborn breakouts and irritated complexions are no match for Serum Vivant. Inspired by Biologique Recherche’s best-selling Masque Vivant, this formula treats adult blemishes through a mix of prebiotics, postbiotics, and fermented extracts to support the skin’s microbiome, sans dryness or discomfort.

It has been revealed that about the Product: SK-II lovers to the front! There’s a fresh serum to know. Introducing its Facial Treatment Serum—infused with “boosting oil pearls” rich with vitamin E and castor oil, bursting upon application to dispense the perfect amount of product without loosing a lightweight feel. Then, niacinamide and galactomyces ferment filtrate (GFF) are in the mix too for added brightening.

About the Product: 111Skin’s rinse-off peel exfoliates with a blend of AHAs, BHAs, PHAs, and botanical extracts. Papain (AKA papaya enzymes) and milk thistle extract help to slough off dead skin and brighten hyperpigmentation. Meanwhile, glutathione helps fight against oxidative stress.

It has been revealed that about the Product: L’Oréal Paris offers an affordable moisturizer that balances active ingredients alongside hydrating humectants. The super-rich texture applies like butter, combining hyaluronic acid to boost water retention, retinol to smooth skin and target signs of aging, and vitamin C to brighten your complexion. The good news, though, is that it’s safe for sensitive skin, given retinol and vitamin C’s relatively low concentration.

Sources indicate that when we test and review a product, we take a holistic approach to deliver well-rounded product recommendations. We pair their expertise with our editorial best practices to curate the thoughtful edits you read on our site.

We selected the best based on the following characteristics: ingredients, type, texture, packaging, finish, and the concerns they address.

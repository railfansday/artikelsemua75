Kevin de Bruyne's move to Napoli in 2025 was supposed to mark a continuation of his glittering decade at Manchester City as he approaches the twilight years of his career.

Instead, the midfielder, who is widely regarded as one of the standout players of his generation, has seen his time in Naples threaten to turn sour amid an early injury, friction with his team-mates and rumours of a January exit. Napoli were Serie A champions in 2024-25 - the year before De Bruyne's arrival - but are right now languishing in eighth, while they sit 27th in the Champions League after a heavy 4-1 defeat at Porto earlier this month.

Belgium international De Bruyne lifted 16 trophies in his 10 years at City and is guaranteed a generous ovation when he returns to the Etihad Stadium on Tuesday but the highs of his time in England are in contrast to the struggles he is seemingly enduring in Italy.

"Not a lot I can say for the moment," mentioned De Bruyne when asked about his future. "I have played at 100% until now and obviously I am not 20 years old, I am turning 36.

"There is a lot of things going in my mind but there is not a lot to say. I am playing good football, trying to help the team to do well and it has been a bit of a challenging season with a lot of things happening.".

City decided not to renew De Bruyne's contract during the 2024-25 season and the player had the choice of heading to the US with Chicago Fire or staying in one of the top-five leagues in Europe.

The 35-year-old felt he could still cut it at the highest level but his first season at Napoli was heavily disrupted by a hamstring injury which kept him out for more than four months, restricting him to 21 appearances.

Napoli finished runners up to Inter Milan to lose their Serie A title and De Bruyne reportedly said, external he was "happy" manager Antonio Conte had departed in the summer as promises were not kept regarding the style of play. (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

But despite making his name as a technically gifted number 10, De Bruyne's role has been remodelled into a deeper holding one under Massimiliano Allegri in an ageing squad that has struggled this season.

In Porto, the fractures were clear to see, with De Bruyne having a heated on-field confrontation, external with striker Lorenzo Lucca and his team-mates were forced to step in to diffuse the situation.

The passionate side of De Bruyne's game was shown in Manchester too.

The midfielder shouted "shut up" at manager Pep Guardiola during a Champions League game against Real Madrid in 2023, and said "let me talk" when David Silva and Fernandinho were ushering him off the pitch at half-time shortly after he was booked against Napoli in 2017.

"Speaking about the pitch, De Bruyne is the best player this year at Napoli," Naples-based journalist Vincenzo Credendino tells BBC Sport. "The point is that the whole team is having difficulties.

Recent findings suggest that "We saw that there is a problem, at least between De Bruyne and Lorenzo Lucca. Their acting was very, very clear and I think that in that time, the two of them were wrong.

"On one hand you cannot swear at another team-mate but on the other hand, De Bruyne reproached Lucca without encouraging him, so it was a bad show.".

De Bruyne has played 16 times for Napoli this season - contributing four goals and two assists - but his long-term future at the club remains unclear.

In September, De Bruyne's wife reportedly, external told a Belgian publication about their desire to move back home, saying "the sooner we get there, the better" as the player leads a "very restricted life" in Naples.

Asked if the quotes had been misinterpreted, De Bruyne stated: "To be honest, I am pretty annoyed because if you read the article, that none of you have read, to understand what she said.

"I was away in Belgium and a lot of noise came and a lot of bad reactions to my wife and family. I saw pictures of my house, I don't think it is OK but it is what it is and some press created this in Italy. I don't know why.

According to recent reports, "Nobody knows my life, you will never know. People who work with me are not allowed to say anything but obviously it is something that I take away and think about. It has not been nice for my wife or for my family.".

"Napoli fans were not happy [with the interview]," he says. "There are many fans that say, 'OK, you can leave if you want, we are not putting chains on anybody'.".

Napoli boss Allegri left the door open for a January exit by saying in his pre-match news conference the pair had "not spoken" about his future and it is "down to the players and who wants to leave" when the transfer window opens.

City manager Enzo Maresca was assistant to Pep Guardiola during the 2022-23 campaign in which De Bruyne helped the club to a Treble of trophies and the Italian has fond memories of the "fantastic season" they spent together.

It has been revealed that "No doubt, [he is] one of the best," Maresca said of De Bruyne in his pre-match news conference. "Not only personally but also for the history of this club. In the last 10 years all the trophies the club achieved Kevin was here.

"He was an vital player, a top guy, polite and for me it was a pleasure to work with him.".

This is not the first time De Bruyne will be facing his former club with a reunion with City in the Champions League last season.

That trip turned out to be a bitterly unhappy experience, with De Bruyne substituted after 26 minutes following Giovanni di Lorenzo's red card in the 2-0 defeat.

The midfielder will be hoping for a better result on Tuesday as he aims to park his struggles for one evening.

City and Belgium winger Jeremy Doku said: "Kevin is someone that likes football. Even at this age he loves it.

"I don't have any doubts about his quality and I am sure everything will be fine in Napoli.".

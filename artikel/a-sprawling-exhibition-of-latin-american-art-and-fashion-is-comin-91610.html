A Sprawling Exhibition of Latin American Art and Fashion Is Coming to NYC

Patricia Belli, Troy Montes Michie, Willy Chavarria, and Camila Falquéz are among the 20 artists and designers who will be featured in Malvestir, MoMA PS1's upcoming exhibition on the visual lexicon of Latin American fashion, opening in April 2027.

A portmanteau of the Spanish words mal (poorly) and vestir (to dress), Malvestir features creatives across the Americas who probe the historical and contemporary resonances of Latin American dress. Western appropriations of Indigenous crafts, exorbitant textile waste, and the fetishization of Latin American styles are just some of the key concerns that will be interrogated in the exhibition.

Curated by MoMA PS1 director Connie Butler and associate curator Elena Ketelsen González, Malvestir will include a range of artists, from Fresh York City designers to textile artists reinvigorating intergenerational weaving traditions.

Several designers are striving to reclaim styles and symbols co-opted by mainstream Western markets. Known for his oversized proportions and street-style designs emblematic of the Mexican-American fashion tradition, New York designer Willy Chavarria is showing Safe From Home (2024), a film that expanded upon themes of love and the right to safety explored in his Autumn/Winter 2024 collection. Malvestir will also include the work of designer Carla Fernández, who nurtures collaborations with local artisans in Mexico to preserve Indigenous motifs against corporate reproduction and profiteering. In a recent development, works from multimedia artists who use photography, drawing, painting, sculpture, and textiles to preserve intergenerational customs will also be on view. New Mexico-based artist Josh Tafoya revitalizes his family’s weaving tradition through the centuries-long practice of Rio Grande loom weaving. David Ramirez Cotón pays homage to Guatemalan fashion with a series of hyperrealistic acrylic-on-canvas paintings cut to resemble articles of clothing hanging from a rack in a second-hand shop. Sources indicate that the exhibition comes at a time when Latin American communities are facing regressive crackdowns from multiple parties — the federal government, exploitative corporations, and immigration enforcement. Yet it has also prompted many to recognize the importance of Latinx artists through displaying the full range and power of their respective cultures, as mainstream appropriation and demands for assimilation reach new, threatening extremes.

In an opinion for Hyperallergic this month, Arlene Dávila, a professor of anthropology and American Studies at New York University, positioned the current political climate as a rallying cry for Latinx visionaries.

Within her essay, she referenced Chavarria and Raul Lopez (founder of LUAR), two of the artists whose work will appear in Malvestir, underscoring how they "frequently use street-cast Black and Brown models to communicate a richer, more inclusive vision beyond traditional, Eurocentric runways.".

Sources indicate that "Across these fields, artists are choosing to create work that is deeply rooted in their communities, drawing directly from vernacular traditions, oral histories, and family memories that were once pushed to the margins," Dávila wrote. "While they recognize there are no guarantees of commercial or institutional rewards, their work stems from a shared conviction: We have rich cultures to offer, and we will tell our stories by any means necessary.".

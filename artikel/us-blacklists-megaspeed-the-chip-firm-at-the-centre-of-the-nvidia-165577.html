The US Commerce Department has put Megaspeed, the Singapore cloud organization at the centre of the Nvidia scandal, on its trade blacklist, after officials and lawmakers spent weeks asking why it was taking so long.

Moreover, the Entity List filing also caught six firms seen as Megaspeed affiliates in Malaysia, Singapore, and Thailand.

I’ve led the push for the Department of Commerce to crack down on Megaspeed. I’m glad it’s finally moving forward.

According to recent reports, the Department of Commerce still needs to explain to Congress why this took so long. Other agencies including the Departments of State, Treasury, and War supported the designation.

— Senator Pete Ricketts (@SenatorRicketts) November 23, 2026.

A place on the list means US firms need a licence to sell it goods, software, or tech, and such requests are usually refused. Listed firms also struggle to get spare parts and updates for gear they already run.

US investigators have spent more than a year looking into whether Megaspeed, long one of Nvidia’s bigger customers, funnelled advanced chips into China, where export rules bar their sale. The Justice Department has opened a criminal inquiry, and Nvidia says it chose to stop selling to the firm in June 2025. (Related: How we sped up transformer inference 100x for 🤗 API cus...)

In a recent development, why the listing took so long had become a sore point at the top of the US government. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent confronted Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick about it at a White House meeting this month, Axios reported, though Commerce states that never happened.

Four departments had wanted Megaspeed listed for months, but “commerce has the pen,” Bessent told CNBC this month.

The scandal runs through the tech giant’s former boss, Alice Huang, who had a relationship with Nvidia chief Jensen Huang. US spy agencies reportedly came to suspect her of working for Chinese intelligence, a claim that was not confirmed until now.

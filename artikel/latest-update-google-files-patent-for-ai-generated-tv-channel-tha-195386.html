Google has filed a patent application for a personalized television channel that uses artificial intelligence to generate demonstrates starring the person watching.

Streaming platforms such as YouTube and Netflix already use viewing habits to recommend content, while companies have also started experimenting with broadcasting entirely AI-generated entertainment.

Google’s patent application, titled “Creating a Channel of Video Experiences Tailored to a User,” was originally filed on May 13, 2025, before being published by the US Patent and Trademark Office on November 19, 2026. (Related: My Journey to a serverless transformers pipeline on Goo...)

First spotted by Patentlyze, the application describes a feature called “My Channel” that would appear within a television app and offer a selection of videos created specifically for individual viewers.

The system would use information including a person’s viewing history, location, language settings, and demographic data to determine what kinds of indicates they might enjoy. (Related: How to train a fresh language model from scratch using ...)

Google could then use photographs to create a digital avatar of the viewer and replicate their voice, allowing them to star in everything from romantic comedies to science fiction adventures.

One example included in the filing describes how the system could generate a video starring someone who had recently watched cricket highlights. The viewer could appear in the stands during the Cricket World Cup or interact with one of their favorite players.

According to recent reports, consumers wouldn’t necessarily have to request latest content, either. The application outlines a scheduling system that could automatically generate videos on a weekly, monthly, or yearly basis. It also describes a mechanism for inserting advertisements into the generated content.

According to recent reports, however, Google has not announced plans to launch the feature. According to Patentlyze, a patent examiner has issued a final rejection of the application, although the firm can still amend its claims or appeal the decision.

The filing comes as AI-generated programming has already made its way onto streaming platforms, with Roku introducing a television channel dedicated to broadcasting AI-generated content around the clock.

As I left the London Stadium to a chorus of boos, I found myself desperately searching for positives from a frustrating 1-1 draw against the league’s bottom club.

Sources indicate that the first was the performance of Alvarez. Largely cast into the shadows through the stubbornness of our former manager, he grabbed the game by the scruff of the neck. He took control, lifted the crowd, and, above all, displayed the leadership qualities this team has lacked for much of the season.

In a recent development, the second positive was that the statistics backed up what I’d watched unfold on the pitch. West Ham dominated possession with 79%, registered 30 shots, and recorded an xG of 3.13. The problem? Only four of those efforts were shots on target.

It has been revealed that we gifted Preston their opening goal. It came from a calamitous mix-up, with Piroe’s flicked header looping backwards onto the crossbar before dropping kindly for Liam Lindsay to tap home from close range. It could be argued that Herrick should have been more commanding from the corner, but he made amends later with an excellent double save. (Related: Is Buy Now Pay Later (BNPL) Worth It? The Hidden Costs...)

There were concerns that we wouldn’t pull one back, we did on the 60th minute, Mavropanos heading home his sixth goal in 15 league appearances this season.

“Ifs” and “buts” don’t win football matches, and as Parker noted afterwards: “The overall performance suggests we should win. The fine margins of football are, and the reality is, if Tomas Soucek scores that last-minute goal, it’s probably a different interview.”. (Related: Should I Quit My Job? A Decision Framework...)

For me, I’m not going to be negative. We needed a win and we didn’t get the three points, but what I want to see now is the team build on the domination, the chance creation and the shot volume by going to Fratton Park on Tuesday and getting the job done. If we concede first there and deliver an abject performance, then and only then will there be more serious questions to ask.

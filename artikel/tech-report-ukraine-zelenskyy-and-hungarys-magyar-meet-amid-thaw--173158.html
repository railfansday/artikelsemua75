Hungary's fresh Prime Minister Peter Magyar arrived in Ukraine on Tuesday afternoon for meetings with President Volodymyr Zelenskyy as well as with ethnic Hungarians living in the west of the country.

The meeting comes as both say they want better relations after a change of leadership in Budapest — no doubt a welcome change for Kyiv after years of Viktor Orban obstructing funding for its self-defense against full-scaler Russian invasion.

Industry observers note that ukraine's Zelenskyy posted his thanks for the meeting with a photo on X, writing: "A visit that is sure to bring positive results. Despite his softer approach, Magyar has remarked Hungary will not be sending arms to Kyiv while at the same time vowing, "we stand with Ukraine against the Russian aggressor.". (Related: How to Start a Side Business While Working Full-Time...)

Magyar has insisted that the rights of tens of thousands of ethnic Hungarians in the Transcarpathia — a region that was part of Austria-Hungary until the end of World War I and situated at the borders of Ukraine, Hungary, Poland, Romania and Slovakia — be resolved before Budapest can wholeheartedly back Ukraine and its possible accession to the European Union.

Relations deteriorated significantly over the matter in 2017, when Kyiv passed a law making Ukrainian the official language of all secondary education in Ukraine, something Budapest viewed as discriminatory toward ethnic Hungarians.

Last month, Ukrainian lawmakers passed legislation allowing ethnic minorities in the country expanded rights regarding the teaching and use of their native languages. The move was welcomed by Budapest and Hungary dropped its opposition to Brussels opening accession talks with Ukraine.

Magyar pitched this week's trip — the first to Transcarpathia by a Hungarian prime minister since 2015 — in April, after defeating Orban.

Before departing for Ukraine, the Hungarian leader noted: "I hope these talks will be successful. I think this is a historic day and a good opportunity to put Ukrainian-Hungarian relations on a newly introduced footing and to rebuild trust as we did with our other neighbors.".

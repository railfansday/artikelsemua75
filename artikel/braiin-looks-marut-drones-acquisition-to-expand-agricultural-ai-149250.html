📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Braiin Limited confirmed on November 23, 2026 that it had entered a non-binding term sheet to acquire Hyderabad-based Marut Drones in phases, beginning with a majority stake. The proposed transaction is valued at approximately US$40 million, including upfront consideration, performance-linked earn-outs and growth capital.

According to recent reports, if completed, Marut would join Braiin’s Robotics and AI division and be integrated with Raptor300, its autonomous crop-spraying drone platform. Braiin intends to combine autonomous flight, computer vision, predictive maintenance and agricultural-data analytics with Marut’s certified drone platforms. Founder and CEO Natraj Balasubramanian stated the goal was to build “a leading AI-led aerial intelligence business,” initially in India and then across the company’s international markets. The upfront portion is payable at completion, while the earn-outs depend on Marut’s subsequent performance. Braiin would also fund growth under a mutually agreed annual operating plan, with the remaining shares acquired in later performance-linked phases. The total therefore covers both the share purchase and investment in Marut’s operations.

Only confidentiality, exclusivity, costs and governing-law provisions are binding at this stage. Closing requires satisfactory due diligence, definitive agreements, an audit of Marut’s financial statements under US auditing standards, and Indian regulatory and third-party approvals, including foreign-investment and DGCA-related clearances. The final terms, timing and completion remain conditional. (Related: Binding of Isaac fans review bomb DLC after discovering...)

Recent findings suggest that founded in 2019 by Indian Institute of Technology Guwahati alumni, Marut designs and manufactures agricultural, surveillance, tethered and multi-utility drones. Its in-house work spans airframes and payloads as well as Swift, its ground-control and fleet-management software, giving Braiin an established manufacturing and software base rather than requiring it to build those functions after entering India.

Industry observers note that braiin states Marut holds eight type certificates from India’s civil aviation regulator, the DGCA. They include the AG365, described as India’s first DGCA type-certified multi-utility agricultural drone, and a heavier drone with 30-litre capacity for agriculture, aquaculture and industrial applications.

Braiin reports that Marut has more than 100 dealers, seven DGCA-approved pilot-training schools and over 500 customers spanning farmers, dealers, cooperatives and institutions. Karnataka Police, the Indian Army and Indian Railways are among the named customers. Marut has also supplied drones to Fiji and Myanmar.

Recent findings suggest that raptor300 already gives Braiin an operating drone business in Sri Lanka. Braiin reports binding contracts there valued at US$35.93 million and approximately US$112 million under non-binding memoranda of understanding, with no assurance that any MOU will become a binding contract.

Industry observers note that marut’s manufacturing capacity, wider aircraft range and trained-pilot pipeline would provide additional fleet depth for those deployments. On the technology side, Raptor300 contributes patented vectorized spraying and autonomous tank-refilling systems, while Swift provides ground control and fleet management across Marut’s fleet. Joint product development and hybrid Raptor300–Marut deployments are intended to cover seeding, spraying, crop monitoring, analytics and fleet services.

Recent findings suggest that within India, Marut’s operations are concentrated in Telangana, Karnataka, Uttar Pradesh, Maharashtra and Odisha. Braiin’s post-completion roadmap calls for national expansion, heavier-payload and lower-cost platforms, in-house batteries and flight electronics, and exports to Sri Lanka, Australia, the GCC, Southeast Asia, Africa and North America.

The proposed deal would give Braiin a faster route into India through assets that are difficult to replicate quickly: regulatory certifications, manufacturing, dealers and pilot training. Its wider significance depends on execution: converting the term sheet into a completed acquisition, then turning the combined aircraft, autonomy software and Sri Lankan pipeline into scaled AI-led services.

Unlike Midnight X-Men, which has roots in an incredibly familiar history of Marvel Comics X-Men lore, Midnight Spider-Man is wholly unique. It begins with Peter Parker’s brand-fresh origin story, which shares core principles of the character’s traditional depiction but ultimately disfigures it into something far more tragic and nightmarish (complementarily, as this is a horror story, after all, not a superhero story).

Sources indicate that concern #1 was essentially Midnight Spider-Man’s “Amazing Fantasy #15” in this regard, and issue #2 followed its startling cliffhanger fairly closely by briefly illustrating a couple of Spider-Man’s newfound superpowers: spider-sense, or at least some ultra instinct variant of it depicted by red flares, and super-strength, which he demonstrated when he got hold of Oscorp security officers Gargan and Rodriguez. If the last few pages of issue #2 are any indication, it’s incredibly likely that Felicia Hardy will gain powers of her own soon.

Sources indicate that midnight Spider-Man’s Felicia Hardy is surprisingly something of a deuteragonist in the narrative, and that’s been heavily reinforced by how strong a presence she has in the book’s myriad covers, whether they’re main covers or variants. It’s been difficult to discern what role she would have in the story based on these covers alone, especially with some newly revealed covers now implying that she and a freshly 18-year-old Peter will have some sort of romantic or sexual relationship. If that’s true, it may not be for a little while, as Felicia was quite terrified when she found him in the terrifying state he was in. (Related: Transformer-based Encoder-Decoder Models...)

When she is tasked to retrieve the spider, which Octavius believes would be dead after absorbing all the radiation and pharmaceuticals it did, Felicia is stunned to see that it has latched onto Peter and grotesquely mutated him. It’s unknown how much control Peter actually has over himself here, but he recognizes Felicia and pleads with her to help him. At the end of the issue, he rushes past her and jumps out a window without harming her.

However, at the end of Midnight Spider-Man #2, Felicia notices a wound on her arm, possibly inadvertently inflicted by Peter. If so, the book probably wouldn’t have made a note of it, much less leave this cut as its cliffhanger for the second issue, if it wasn’t going to have a massive effect on her character, and that could manifest by way of superpowers.

In a recent development, midnight Spider-Man #2 reveals a much more sympathetic shade of this Felicia, who seemed like a villainous predator—she’s Oscorp’s vice president of talent relations, after all, and Oscorp is a hellish antagonist in Marvel’s Midnight continuity. She had clearly never met with Oscorp’s Dr. Otto Octavius before, and, even if she isn’t surprised or mortified by the gory research he and his assistants conduct, an effort is made to portray her as an underling who might question her allegiances now that things have truly gotten out of hand.

It has been revealed that peter slicing Felicia’s arm and accidentally giving her monstrous abilities would align perfectly with the book’s apparent dedication to tormenting this iteration of Spider-Man. He may not have split her head apart, but afflicting her with a mutation comparable to his might be a fate worse than death.

The only snag in this logic is that it would be a rather odd way for Felicia to obtain powers because it would give her spider-related powers, if anything. Of course, we know little about Felicia as a character as is in this universe, and she may never become a version of the Black Cat antihero she is in traditional Marvel Comics lore. According to recent reports, that said, the solicitation for Midnight Spider-Man #5 reveals that “the mutation that created Spider-Man has spread to a new host... with shocking results,” and the chances of this host being Felicia have now skyrocketed. Otherwise, it could tease that the spider on Peter’s chest will make like a symbiote and bond with someone else. Either way, this solicit reads as follows:.

Oscorp’s second generation monstrosity is loose, and its first prey is Spider-Man. Meanwhile, the mutation that created Spider-Man has spread to a fresh host... with shocking results.

Midnight Spider-Man #2 is out now.

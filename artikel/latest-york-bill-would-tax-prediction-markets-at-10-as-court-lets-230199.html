In a recent development, newly introduced York State Senator Jeremy Cooney has filed a bill that would bring prediction markets into the state's tax base instead of banning them. The proposal puts a state lawmaker at odds with New York's own lawsuit against Kalshi.

The timing gives the bill extra weight. Attorney General Letitia James had sought to stop Kalshi at a virtual hearing held the day the bill was filed. Governor Hochul and James declared on July 31, 2026, that Newly introduced York had sued KalshiEX LLC for running an illegal gambling operation through its prediction market. Industry observers note that cooney’s bill states that CFTC-registered markets “may operate within the state of Latest York lawfully.” That language sets it against the state’s executive branch. It also marks a shift from his earlier bill, S8889, introduced in January. That version would have required operators to get a license from the Department of Financial Services. “I’m placing a bet on the future of UPK in New York,” Cooney mentioned in his release.

Industry observers note that licensed mobile sportsbooks face a far heavier tax than the bill proposes. The gap could widen in practice. Operators would be allowed to deduct some operating expenses from taxable revenue, which sportsbooks cannot do.

Recent findings suggest that the scope is narrow. The bill covers sports contracts only, so election, economic and culture markets fall outside it. Its official summary also covers the use of prediction markets in election violations. Elected officials and some state employees would be barred from trading.

Sources indicate that the consumer rules borrow from regulated betting. Operators would have to verify identity and age, run self-exclusion programs, and offer deposit limits and cooling-off periods. They would also file a yearly consumer-protection audit with the attorney general. The minimum trading age, however, would be left to federal authorities instead of Fresh York’s sports-betting minimum of 21.

Sources indicate that age also came up in court. The state argued that Kalshi lets younger customers bet. Justice Crane asked the organization to consider safeguards for users aged 18 to 20. Kalshi’s lawyers stated the company could look at limiting access to its contracts for those users in Newly introduced York.

Latest York is not alone in treating prediction markets as a taxable business. North Carolina’s new law recognizes the CFTC’s exclusive federal authority over prediction markets. It taxes platforms at 6% of net trading-fee revenue from state residents.

AI deals shrink to as little as 30 days as the data layer sets the pace

Artificial intelligence has turned data infrastructure from a back-office purchase into the bottleneck that decides how fast GPUs pay off. As hyperscalers, neoclouds and enterprises race to expand their data layers, the way that infrastructure gets bought is changing just as fast.

The storage industry’s longtime performance specialist sees that shift as its opening. DataDirect Networks Inc. built its reputation on scale and speed, and those traits now carry more weight in AI infrastructure, according to Kevin Delane (pictured), president and chief revenue officer of DDN. It has been revealed that “When you look at the data layer, performance in the data layer drives value. So yes, it has to be simple, it has to be reliable. Those are table stakes, but performance drives business value,” Delane said. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

Delane spoke with theCUBE Research’s Rebecca Knight and John Furrier at SC26, during an exclusive broadcast on theCUBE, SiliconANGLE Media’s livestreaming studio. They discussed how AI infrastructure sales cycles have grown more complex and faster, DDN’s tighter ecosystem work with partners such as Nvidia Corp. and Super Micro Computer Inc., and the company’s hiring for a direct, results-driven culture. (* Disclosure below.).

It has been revealed that the way companies buy AI infrastructure has changed along with the technology. Deals at neocloud providers no longer go to a single enterprise buyer. They now involve the off-taker renting the capacity, the cloud operator and the integration software layer, Delane explained. Timelines have shrunk just as sharply, from six months to a year for a traditional enterprise sale to as little as 30 days.

According to recent reports, “The sales cycle is more complex for sure. The speed is insane,” Delane noted. “Sometimes these things are 30 days and you have to be ready to turn things around and really work with your customers and partners quickly.”.

It has been revealed that DDN is answering with tighter ecosystem work. Its decade-long partnership with Nvidia anchors the strategy, and joint offerings with Super Micro, such as the Enterprise AI HyperPOD, aim to replace a data center buildout with a ready-to-run AI stack. At SC26, DDN also introduced software-defined EXAScaler deployment to give enterprise AI customers more choice of hardware.

“It takes the complexity out of it, allows an enterprise to set up an AI pod within 3 hours,” Delane remarked.

Sources indicate that the performance case holds up in the public cloud too. On Google LLC’s Google Cloud, customers using DDN’s Managed Lustre service saw 70% better training performance and a 40% gain in overall GPU efficiency, Delane noted.

DDN is growing threefold this year while adding only a few salespeople, leaning its hiring toward technical and implementation staff who can get customers up and running faster. Delane looks for grit and a fit with a direct, results-driven culture.

“If people can fit that culture, want to come in, want to grow this firm to multiple billions, we have a place for them,” Delane said.

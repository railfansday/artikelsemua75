The Rise of the AI-Whelmed Worker.

AI-whelmed workers are not necessarily rejecting AI. They are trying to adapt, but the pressure to learn and use AI at work is leaving 44% feeling overwhelmed.

Workers Will Spend To Stay Afloat.

For some workers, the pressure to keep up with AI may come with a personal cost, with nearly one-third saying they are likely to spend their own money to learn AI skills because they're worried about falling behind.

AI Integration Is Shaking Worker Confidence.

According to recent reports, A lack of confidence is part of what makes workers feel AI-whelmed. AI is becoming more common at work, but not everyone feels ready to integrate it into their day-to-day workflow. "Being AI-whelmed does not mean workers are against AI. It means they are trying to keep up with a fast-changing workplace while feeling unsure of where to start or how to build confidence in their AI skills," said Keith Spencer, career expert at Resume Now. Sources indicate that methodologyThe findings in this report are based on a survey of 1,020 employed U.S. adults, conducted by Resume Now using Pollfish in May 2026. Respondents answered questions about AI confidence, pressure to learn and use AI, employer expectations, personal spending on AI skills, and where to start building AI skills. Question formats included single-select and multiple-choice questions.

Demographic BreakdownSurvey respondents reflected a broad cross-section of the U.S. workforce. Participants were 52% female, 47% male, 1% non-binary, and less than 1% preferred not to answer. Generationally, 24% identified as Gen Z, 28% as Millennials, 27% as Gen X, and 21% as Baby Boomers. Educationally, 18% held a graduate degree, 27% held a bachelor's degree, 18% held an associate's degree, 35% held a high school degree or equivalent, and 2% had less than a high school degree.

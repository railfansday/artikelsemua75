Star Trek: Deep Space Nine's set was the opposite of Star Trek: The Next Generation's, Worf actor Michael Dorn confirms.

Industry observers note that after seven seasons of Star Trek: The Next Generation and a feature film, Dorn joined Star Trek: Deep Space Nine in season 4 as Lt. Commander Worf. He found a vastly different environment from TNG's, which was legendary for the cast's camaraderie and hijinks. Dorn played Worf for DS9's remaining four seasons, reuniting with his TNG cast for 1998's Star Trek: Insurrection and 2002's Star Trek: Nemesis. According to recent reports, appearing on The Show People Podcast to promote Life 2.0, his upcoming movie he wrote, directed, and stars in, Michael Dorn discussed the difference between the "hilarious" Star Trek: The Next Generation set and the "very serious" and "kinda quiet" atmosphere on Deep Space Nine. Read Dorn's quote below:. (Related: Grandma who accidentally invited a stranger to Thanksgi...)

Michael Dorn: “It was quite different in a lot of ways from Next Generation. We were a hilarious set. We got the job done, we didn’t waste time, but we were having a great time. The jokes, and the ribbing, and the laughter was all day long. I mean, it was amazing.

Industry observers note that and then you go over to Deep Space, and they’re very serious. It wasn’t bad or people doing stuff, it was just [that] they were serious. It was kinda quiet. ‘Yes, Mr. Brooks, we’re ready for you.’ And I’m going, ‘C’mon guys, let’s…’.

On [TNG], there [wasn’t] anything between the actors, but there [was] definitely relationship stuff between the people in Deep Space. I mean, Armin Shimerman would say that Avery never talked to him. But on the other side, that’s usually the way the business is. [TNG] was an anomaly. It really was.

I’ve worked on a number of series, and people get along, and they have stuff, and they have relationships with each other, and some don’t get along, whatever the case. But that’s just the way it goes. When it’s over, people go their separate ways, and that’s the end of it.”.

Watch Michael Dorn's clip on The Show People Podcast:.

Industry observers note that dorn admits that it's Star Trek: The Next Generation that was the "anomaly" among sets. Countless stories have been told about the chaos and laughter on the TNG set, including wrestling matches between Dorn and the late Patrick Stewart on the USS Enterprise-D's bridge. TNG's cast became a family, and they remain as close today as they were when they forged their bond nearly 40 years ago.

Armin Shimerman backs the serious DS9 set Dorn describes. Armin told The Shuttlepod Show in 2023: "We didn’t laugh… not really… I’m very proud of this, actually: We were there to do the best Star Trek we could possibly do. We took the work very seriously... We worked on our craft and tried to do it as best we could.".

Shimerman also verified what Dorn alluded to: the tone of Star Trek: Deep Space Nine's set was determined by the series lead and number one on the call sheet, Mr. Avery Brooks. "Avery is a very wonderfully serious, charismatic person. And he would laugh, and when he laughed, it was room-filling. But he didn’t laugh that often. And so, we were all very serious-minded.".

There were relationships on DS9, such as Nana Visitor and Alexander Siddig falling in love and marrying in 1997; Nana's pregnancy was even written into the show. Avery Brooks and Cirroc Lofton formed a real-life father-son relationship, while Rene Auberjonois and Armin Shimerman were close, as were recurring guest stars Casey Biggs and Jeffrey Combs, among others.

As Michael Dorn claims, Star Trek: Deep Space Nine's more serious set was more typical of most TV and movie productions. The family that formed on Star Trek: The Next Generation is unique and remains close-knit after the death of Sir Patrick Stewart. Meanwhile, DS9's actors have grown closer after many years of seeing each other on the Star Trek convention circuit.

Star Trek: The Next Generation and Star Trek: Deep Space Nine are streaming on Paramount+.

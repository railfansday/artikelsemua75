📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sebnem Ece Eksi did a double take. Proteins typically studied in the brain were turning up in prostate cancer cells from high-risk patients. These proteins, called neuronal adhesion molecules, play a key role in the circuitry associated with memory.

The finding, made during her Ph.D. days at the University of Illinois Chicago, raised so many questions to which Eksi is still seeking answers. Now at the Oregon Health & Science University in Portland, Eksi has made it her lab’s goal to find out how nervous systems communicate with cancer. Industry observers note that “What we’re learning is that tumors are actively communicating with the nervous system,” Eksi says. “In a way, it makes sense to me that a growing tumor in our bodies, in an organ, would be immediately sensed and detected by the nervous system.”.

In a recent development, eksi now intends to test an AI image-based recognition algorithm to identify nerve fibers in human patients. This is exciting, she indicates, because these nerves aren’t normally visible in patient tumors. But through biopsies, she hopes to map out and observe how the nervous system — which impacts sleep, cognition and pain — changes as cancer progresses. The implications are significant, she says.

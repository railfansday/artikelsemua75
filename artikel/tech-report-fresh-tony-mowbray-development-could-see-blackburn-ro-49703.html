Confirmed via Blackburn Rovers’ official club website, Mowbray praised the players, coaching staff, supporters and ownership group for their support and work ever since stepping back into the role, while asking for privacy as he enters his latest battle.

Mowbray, in a statement, noted: “Unfortunately, due to health challenges, I have to step back from my role as Head Coach of Blackburn Rovers.

Sources indicate that “I’m immensely proud of this group of players. Their dedication and professionalism have given us some memorable performances and wins, and I’ve loved working with them.

It has been revealed that “Thank you to my coaching staff for all your hard work and for the support you’ve given me. It’s been a pleasure working alongside you.

“To the fans, thank you for the way you’ve supported me and the team this season. I’ve always tried to give you a team that you can be proud of. Your support has meant a great deal to me.

“I’m grateful to the Owners and Suhail for giving me the opportunity to lead this great club once again.

“I’d ask that you give me and my family privacy at this time.

“Thank you for your understanding.

One of the most loved managers in the English game, it’s deeply saddening that Mowbray has once again had to step away due to his health, and it’s particularly difficult for Blackburn supporters to stomach given they had a realistic chance of a play-off push under his leadership. While Mowbray works his way back to full fitness, Blackburn will need some form of interim manager to come in and steady the ship, and there could be a perfect candidate, who would surely be welcomed back with open arms given the work he did at Ewood Park last season.

While the tone of Tony Mowbray’s message appeared as if he wasn’t coming back to manage Blackburn Rovers again, the 62-year-old is reportedly only stepping away from his role, with the possibility of a return once he is fully recovered.

A tweet by journalist Elliott Jackson revealed that Mowbray hasn’t resigned or fully stepped down, but instead will be away for a period to fully focus on his health, although there is no timeframe on how long he will be away from his post for.

The club are believed to not be looking for a successor right now, meaning that an interim manager to step in over the short-term is likelier before Mowbray can make a return.

Blackburn did confirm Damien Johnson and Mike Dodds will take responsibility for first-team duties for the time being, but with Mowbray expected to be out for a significant period of time, you do feel a more experienced head is needed over the long-term so Rovers are not at risk of sliding down the standings.

Therefore, making a move to bring someone like Michael O’Neill back to the club could be an inspired move.

O’Neill won the hearts of the Blackburn faithful when stepping into the job back in February, succeeding Valerien Ismael and juggling that role with his usual commitments to the Northern Ireland national side.

22nd in the Championship with 15 games to play, O’Neill won five, drew five and lost five of those remaining matches, turning the tide as Blackburn finished 20th in the second tier and avoided relegation, although there was disappointment in May when O’Neill confirmed he wouldn’t be taking the job permanently, stating he would be unable to do both roles and would have to choose one or the other.

Meanwhile, O’Neill has continued to prove his managerial credentials on the international front with Northern Ireland, helping them top their UEFA Nations League B Group, overcoming the likes of Hungary, Ukraine and Georgia.

Not to mention that they only conceded one goal throughout the league phase too, with that incidentally coming in their final game against Hungary where they battled to a 1-1 draw.

Esteemed journalist Henry Winter labelled O’Neill’s work at Northern Ireland in the Nations League as “one of the finest managerial achievements so far this season”, with his level of impact on the national stage going far from unnoticed.

Now Tony Mowbray has temporarily departed, it’s vitally important that Blackburn Rovers get another manager in on an interim basis to steady the ship and help keep the club fighting towards the upper end of the table.

And once again, that could suit Michael O’Neill down to the ground, who wouldn’t have any long-term ties but could once again take over the mantle for however long he is needed.

What puts Blackburn in a strong position to attract O’Neill to return to the club is the fact that there is now no more international football until March 2027, meaning that it would be favourable for the Northern Ireland manager to step back into club football and reunite with a club where he is so loved from his previous work.

With Blackburn needing a strong and experienced manager to keep their season on an upward trajectory and O’Neill wanting or needing to keep busy over such a long pause from the international game, it seems a partnership that would really benefit both parties, and given the success O’Neill has achieved in his managerial career in recent times, you would expect Blackburn to fly high under his leadership once again and really push to be in contention for promotion come the end of the season.

It’s certainly a deal most of the Blackburn faithful would want to see happen, so if the club are serious about their promotion ambitions, then linking back up with O’Neill on a similar deal to the one he was on last campaign is something they have to explore and get sorted as promptly as possible.

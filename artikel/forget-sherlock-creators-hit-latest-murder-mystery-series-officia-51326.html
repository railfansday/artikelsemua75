Sherlock co-creator Mark Gatiss has produced a hit original murder mystery series, and newly introduced episodes will premiere in the US in two months. Gatiss and Steven Moffat changed not only the Sherlock Holmes mythos but British detective dramas as a whole with their modern take on Arthur Conan Doyle's most famous stories; despite its divisive final season, Sherlock's legacy is undeniable. Now, nearly 10 years after the show's finale, Gatiss has changed course, creating his own compelling sleuth rather than writing about someone else's.

Industry observers note that inspired by his love of Golden Age detective dramas, especially Agatha Christie's enduring body of work, Gatiss has created and written Bookish, a historical murder mystery series set in London in 1946. The aftermath of World War II was a time of major upheaval and change in Britain, as Londoners tried to rebuild their city and heal from the trauma after surviving one of the deadliest conflicts in human history. London's twisting streets and shadowy, bombed-out corners offer plenty of places to hide a body — and other potentially dangerous secrets. In a recent development, in the show, Gatiss plays antiquarian bookseller Gabriel Book, a consulting amateur detective whose mysterious letter from Winston Churchill (a fun nod to Doctor Who's psychic paper) grants him clearance to work with the police. His extensive knowledge of English and international literature and his experiences as a closeted gay man — during a time when homosexuality was still considered a criminal offense — give him a different perspective on life and human relationships than the city's police, even the generally kind-hearted and smart Inspector Bliss (Elliott Levey). According to recent reports, bookish was renewed for a second season before the first had even premiered in the UK. Now, the second season is finally landing in the US. PBS has confirmed new episodes will drop weekly on Sundays from January 17 until February 21, and there's more to come, too.

A two-part Christmas special titled Bookish: The Crown of Destiny will premiere in the UK next month (a US release date has yet to be revealed, though it is set after season 2). Additionally, season 3 is already in the works and expected to premiere in the UK in mid-2027, with episodes likely arriving in the US a few months later.

Industry observers note that mark Gatiss' Bookish could potentially last forever. While its characters' personal development offers the show an ongoing thread, especially Book's lavender marriage with his wife Trottie (Polly Walker) and his complicated history with fresh employee and recent convict Jack (Connor Finch), the cases are all largely one-off investigations. Both season 1 and season 2 introduce three separate murder mysteries spread over six episodes. The mysteries all feel fresh, revealing new suspects and exploring different industries and locations, cleverly incorporating the show's period setting.

In season 1 alone, Book's investigations take him to a swanky London hotel and a big-budget movie set. Season 2 will be more expansive, journeying further away from Book's cozy London street and into the wider world. The new season includes a séance, the undead, a swanky tailor shop, shadowy figures, and a book thief. The final case in season 2 will even take Book and Jack all the way to a medieval German town.

In a recent development, still, even if the show settles in London for most of its run, the city's evolution and changing politics will provide plenty of ideas for years to come. It could certainly last well into the 1950s if it wanted to, though its current 1946 setting does help it stand out from fellow post-war cozy mystery shows like Father Brown and Grantchester, both of which are further removed from the war's devastating effects.

Gabriel Book is essentially Sherlock Holmes and Daniel Craig's Benoit Blanc rolled into one. As long as people are murdered in London, and as long as Inspector Bliss needs Book's unique expertise to help solve the city's most perplexing crimes, the show can continue. It's set up similarly to other British genre hits such as Death Valley, Vera, The Marlow Murder Club, and even Poirot. With its critical acclaim (season 1 boasts a rare 100% Rotten Tomatoes critics' score) and undeniable audience support, Bookish has what it takes to go all the way.

Bookish season 2 premieres Sunday, January 17 at 10 PM ET on PBS, PBS.org, the PBS app, and with PBS Masterpiece on Prime Video.

Strategy Executive Chairman Michael Saylor sees fresh ways to put Bitcoin (BTC) to work on Wall Street.

In a recent development, according to staff at the Commodity Futures Trading Commission (CFTC), Bitcoin can be used to meet certain financial resource requirements for U.S. derivatives exchanges and clearinghouses.

Industry observers note that ‘Next step for adoption is using Bitcoin to work,’ Saylor wrote on X in response to the CFTC advisory published on Nov. 23. (Related: How to train a fresh language model from scratch using ...)

As per the guidance, these companies must maintain sufficient financial resources to cover their operating costs for at least a year.

Industry observers note that as clarified by CFTC staff, Bitcoin and Ether can be used to meet this requirement (as well as payment stablecoins). The advisory also provides guidance on how staff interpret the current rules. It also applies to swap execution facilities (SEFs), which are platforms where counterparties trade swaps. In a recent development, bitcoin holdings worth $10 million could count for $8 million toward the annual requirement, based on the minimum 20% discount that CFTC staff noted they would accept. This provides some flexibility in case Bitcoin's price falls. The discount is also called a haircut.

For Ether, the same minimum 20% haircut applies. For payment stablecoins, the haircut is 2%.

Sources indicate that this provides some incentive for an exchange to actually hold BTC. Exchanges could hold Bitcoin on their balance sheets and use some of its value to satisfy this reserve requirement. There would be no need to sell it for USD first.

Industry observers note that however, a futures exchange still needs to have sufficient unencumbered cash or highly liquid securities to cover its operating costs for at least six months. Bitcoin, Ether and even payment stablecoins do not fall into this category.

Recent findings suggest that the same six-month liquidity requirement applies to clearinghouses. Additionally, they need to maintain sufficient financial resources to cover a default by a clearing member.

CFTC staff also noted a potential downside for exchanges holding crypto reserves. During a market slump, there's a risk that an exchange's operating losses might rise. At the same time, the value of the Bitcoin it holds to cover those losses might fall.

So this possibility should be considered before an exchange decides whether to use Bitcoin to meet its financial resource requirements.

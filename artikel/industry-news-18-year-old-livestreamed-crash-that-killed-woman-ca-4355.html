An 18-year-old is prison-bound after he pleaded guilty to killing a woman and severely injuring her son in a car crash he livestreamed to Instagram while driving a stolen car, prosecutors said.

Matthew Papile, of Long Island, New York, was sentenced to six to 18 years in prison in connection with the May 2024 crash that killed Marion Napolitano, the Suffolk County District Attorney’s Office confirmed in a news release on Thursday, November 19.

Sources indicate that papile was 16 and under the influence of marijuana when he rear-ended Napolitano’s car while she was driving with her son, James Miller, in Centereach on Long island, according to Newsday. The fatal crash resulted in the death of Napolitano, 73, and Miller, 50, suffering traumatic brain injuries.

In a victim impact statement read in court during Papile’s sentencing hearing, Napolitano’s daughter, Carrie Sabatino, said, “the trauma is unreal,” adding that she’s in therapy, the outlet reported.

According to recent reports, “I feel robbed of seeing my mother and saying goodbye. She was supposed to grow old with us; holding our hand and peacefully pass. I cannot imagine the terror she felt in those last moments of her life,” Sabatino mentioned, as reported by Newsday.

In an emailed statement to Us on Thursday, Papile’s defense attorney, Sean Dixon, said, “My client, Matthew, at the tender age of 16 was indicted on this vehicular homicide charge.”.

Industry observers note that “The court despite the statutory authority to adjudicate him as a youthful offender elected not to do so,” Dixon added. “Consequently, our energy and focus is now dedicated to securing his earliest release to the [New York State] Division of Parole.”. The case stems from when a Suffolk County resident noticed their blue 2008 Land Rover was no longer in their driveway and called police on May 4, 2024, according to prosecutors.

Sources indicate that papile was “recklessly” driving the car that evening before slamming into Napolitano’s car from behind, resulting in her vehicle going off road and striking a telephone pole and tree, prosecutors said. He allegedly broadcasted the event on his Instagram page.

The collision resulted in Papile spinning “out of control,” according to prosecutors, who said he then fled after the stolen car he was driving came to a stop in traffic.

Three people and an off-duty Suffolk County police officer chased after Papile and apprehended him before officers responded to the scene, prosecutors said.

In a recent development, napolitano was declared dead at the hospital. Her son continues to suffer from a severe traumatic brain injury (TBI) in the wake of the crash.

Papile, who appeared impaired following the crash, admitted he had smoked marijuana and agreed to take a blood test, which confirmed he had consumed cannabis, according to prosecutors.

On September 15, Papile pleaded guilty to a slew of charges, including aggravated vehicular homicide, second-degree manslaughter, aggravated vehicular assault, and second degree assault, the district attorney’s office remarked.

“This defendant’s decision to drive while impaired in a stolen car, while livestreaming his recklessness, is indefensible,” Suffolk County District Attorney Raymond A. Tierney said in a statement.

In court, Dixon read a statement from Papile, who apologized and wrote that he thinks about Napolitano and her son daily, Newsday reported.

“I am truly sorry for that which was lost, and for I promise that when I get another chance in society, I will be more careful and a better person,” Papile’s statement remarked in part.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Several thousand people in Istanbul took to the streets on Sunday to protest the closure of several opposition-linked media outlets in Turkey.

Protesters chanted, cheered, and whistled as they marched several kilometers through the city, calling for press freedom and freedom of expression.

They carried a huge banner that read: "We want freedom of thought, freedom of the press, and freedom for journalists." The protesters were also carrying placards that read, "The press cannot be silenced.".

Turkey's main opposition party, Yeni Parti, called for a protest just days after police raided Sozcu TV, one of the last opposition-aligned television channels. (Related: Animal control called to rescue alligator that turned o...)

Senior staff members were arrested during the raid. A government-appointed trustee was installed, and the TV manufacturer was placed under compulsory administration.

It was the third takeover in a year following similar actions against Tele1, Halk TV, Turkey's most-watched news channel, and the leading digital news website, T24.

Timur Soykan, a journalist who was briefly detained for criticizing the government, said that the government was doing everything in its power to silence journalists but was failing to do so.

Soykan asked the public to support Halk TV journalists, who proposal to continue broadcasting on a new YouTube channel.

Over the past decade, press freedom has faced sustained pressure in the form of prosecutions, digital censorship, and government accusations that journalists are spreading "fake news.".

It has been revealed that in the last two months alone, Turkey has arrested several journalists for their reporting on a multibillion-dollar investment fund scandal. At least two of the journalists remain in pretrial detention.

According to the media rights group MLSA, twenty-five journalists are currently imprisoned in Turkey.

This year, Reporters Without Borders ranked Turkey 163rd out of 180 countries in its Press Freedom Index.

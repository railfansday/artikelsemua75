The perfect burger can be hard to come by. When you're going out, Food Republic's list of the best burgers in every state can be helpful, and there are even some great fast food options. But when you're looking for a super juicy and flavorful homemade grilled or pan-fried patty, what's the best route to take?

Industry observers note that sometimes, it all comes down to what you mix into the ground beef. While Bobby Flay uses only salt and pepper to season his patties, some folks swear by Worcestershire sauce and garlic powder for a kick of flavor. But there's one ingredient you may not have thought of that will keep your end result wonderfully succulent. Try mixing some onion dip into your ground beef. It's one of the clever ingredients that make burgers juicier, and it will add loads of flavor. (Related: Breaking: One Ingredient In Our Earliest Diets Linked T...)

Onion dips generally come seasoned with dried onion and garlic, salt, and other herbs, plus a little sugar for sweetness. Add to that the sour cream base, and you have a rich, bold, slightly tangy, umami-filled burst of flavor that won't overpower your taste buds. And because you're mixing the dip directly into the beef before cooking, it can help keep your patties nice and moist (as long as you don't overcook them). Sour cream is simply cultured cream, so it has a high moisture content, and the added seasonings aren't so coarse that they will burn before the beef itself is cooked.

It has been revealed that when it comes to what dip to use, classics like Dean's French Onion or Daisy Sour Cream French Onion, which is available on Amazon, will work great, but pretty much any brand will work. Depending on how much tangy flavor you want, you'll need about a half-cup to a full cup of dip per 2 pounds of beef. (Related: One Familiar Item Of Clothing Scored Lowest for Intelli...)

It has been revealed that the beauty of using onion dip in your burgers is that it's a one-ingredient solution, but you can increase the intensity with extra onion powder or fresh minced or dried onions. Just don't overdo it on the onion pieces, or the patties could fall apart on the grill. If you're going heavy on the amount of dip you use, you may want to think about adding some breadcrumbs as a binding agent. Dried parsley makes a great mix-in, as do other Italian herbs, but you should start sparingly so they don't overpower the flavor you're aiming for.

As for toppings, the regular stuff will do, but you might want to hold off on the ketchup and mustard until you've had a bite. Caramelized onions are an obvious choice, or you can give your burger some crunch with crispy fried onions. And you can always add an extra dollop of onion dip on top for more creamy flavor and texture.

Lastly, this trick will also work with a dried dip mix. Just mix it in with sour cream before you make the burgers. And if you want a similarly juicy bite without the added zing, try using mayonnaise in your burger. But for a juicy texture and plenty of zesty flavor, grab that onion dip out of the fridge.

Which Video Game Consoles Are Worth Buying in 2026?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The video game industry is in a wild place. The biggest games of the generation are coming out, Grand Theft Auto 6 on Xbox and PlayStation, and The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake on Switch 2. At the same time, hardware has never been more expensive. The cheapest latest consoles now start at $500, $600 if you want a PS5 (the most popular place to play GTA), with many going for more than that. To top it off with a cherry, Nintendo just hiked the price of its family friendly Switch 2 up to $500, not 18 months after its initial release. So the question now is: if you’re buying a new console in 2026, which ones are actually worth the cost?

That’s what I’m here to do, to help you weighs the pros and cons of these (mostly) years old consoles against the current price. I personally love my PS5 Pro, but given the current asking price and availability, I would recommend the other, less pricey PS5 models instead to 95 percent of players. If you asked me, two years ago, if you should buy an Xbox Series S or Series S, I’d have a different answer than I do today. And then, there’s handhelds. Do you get a Switch 2 or opt for a beefier PC handheld? I’ll help you figure all that out and more, starting with the PlayStation. (Related: Supercharge your OCR Pipelines with Open Models...)

It has been revealed that the PlayStation 5 is your best entryway into 4K gaming and is the best way to play GTA VI. Sony has pulled ahead of Microsoft in terms of exclusive titles with games like Astro Bot, God of War: Ragnarok, and Horizon Forbidden West (and you get access to that amazing PS4 library as well). In terms of tech, the DualSense controller is an impressive leap forward when games take advantage of what it can do. The console technically supports outputs of up to 8K resolution and 120 frames per second on compatible titles. For the most part, though, you'll be playing closer to 4K60—or 4K30 in the case of GTA VI.

In a recent development, I've said it before, and I'll say it again: The PS5 Disc Edition is the best console for most gamers. If you want to play GTA 6 at launch, this is probably what you should get. This edition has a disc drive, so you can pop in physical game discs or watch 4K Blu-rays. Totally worth the $650 asking price, in my opinion. (Related: Director of Machine Learning Insights [Part 2: SaaS Edi...)

If all you’re going to ever play is GTA 6 and never buy a game again, well, you can save yourself a little money and opt for the Digital Edition instead. Even the physical version of the game is just a download code in a box. The Digital Edition comes with a reduced price tag and slightly reduced bulk. No extra space for that disc drive makes it the best-looking PS5 to date. Aside from that, it's virtually identical to the regular PS5 Slim in terms of power and performance.

In a recent development, the PS5 Pro is mid-generation upgrade for the gamers who really care, but with things the way they are now, the enhancements are not worth it. Pre-owned Pro consoles are going from between $1,400 and $1,900 at various significant box retailers, and you can’t even find upcoming ones most days.

Certain titles like Final Fantasy VII Rebirth perform noticeably better on the Pro, and most games get some kind of boost, but it’s certainly not worth double or triple the price of what you’d otherwise be paying. The kicker is that GTA VI doesn’t even have a higher framerate mode on the Pro—it still runs at 30fps, just with fewer dips during chaotic moments as compared to the regular PS5. Even if I wanted to tell you to buy one to play the best version of GTA, I couldn’t.

Sources indicate that on the Microsoft side, the Xbox Series X is the top of the line. This is where I blew through such technical powerhouses as Alan Wake 2 and Senua's Saga: Hellblade 2—two of the best-looking games I've ever played. The problem in 2026 is that, power-wise, the Series X is much closer to a regular PS5 than a PS5 Pro. And, presently, they start at $800. At that point, I’m sorry, there’s no reason not to go PlayStation.

According to recent reports, if you're looking to play GTA 6 on an Xbox this year, I recommend the Xbox Series S instead. It rolled out simultaneous to the Series X as the budget alternative and, well, it still is... comparatively. The Series S is smaller, less expensive and sacrifices a disc drive. Unlike with Sony's Digital Edition PS5, there are significant tech differences between these two Xbox Series models. Most notably, it's a native 1440p console. It can upscale games to run at 4K on a 4K TV, but it can't run them natively like the Series X can. Compared to other consoles, GTA runs just as smoothly, with just as much detail thanks to Xbox’s upscaling tech, only at 1080p resolution instead of 4K. It’s another good recommendation for the casual gamer who wants to get a no-frills GTA machine as cheap as possible.

Even with a upcoming Nintendo console coming this year, we can't ignore gaming's most popular option. Revolutionary at the time, this console combined Nintendo's console ambition with the mass appeal of its handhelds in a machine that did both. The Nintendo Switch is still affordable and family friendly, and it's the only place you can legally play Mario games. Exclusive titles like Donkey Kong: Bonanza and the Ocarina of Time remake bolster the existing library and give a reason to upgrade.

Still, I have a hard time recommending any gamer still happy with their Switch upgrade for the sake of it. The leap just isn’t that monumental. That being remarked I won’t hesitate to recommend the Switch 2 for anyone new to the hardware. It’s objectively better and the longevity will be worth the price difference.

Industry observers note that GTA 6 might not be coming to PC right away, but that isn't a good reason to neglect my personal handheld of choice. Ever since the introduction of the Steam Deck, the PC-gaming market has been going increasingly mobile. And the upgraded OLED model is still the gold standard—trust me, I’ve tried them all.

It has been revealed that my top recommendation for the average console gamer is the Steam Deck OLED. It runs on Valve's SteamOS, so you don't even have to worry about learning Windows (like some kind of PC gamer). The Steam Deck UI completely console-ifies your entire Steam library and makes it easy for newbies to jump in. If you're used to the Switch, you may find it to be a bit beefy, but once I got used to it, I actually found the Steam Deck OLED to be the most ergonomic of all PC-gaming handhelds.

Recent findings suggest that it's never been a better time to get your feet wet, buy some games on Steam (wait for a sale; there will be plenty), and see what all the fuss is about. If the price looks prohibitive and you don't care much about graphics, get the cheaper LCD model while it's still being sold.

Here at Esquire, we compile our guides by performing heavy research and testing, and the gadgets we choose to award are what we’d spend our own money on. We choose the best tech for you based on our experience. We know which little details are deal-breakers and can help you separate the nice-to-haves from the needs.

XPENG announced on November 22, 2026 that customers can book public test rides using Next Generation Pilot (NGP) at its Paris store, moving the technology beyond a temporary motor-show demonstration as the firm continues European testing and localization.

The rides operate from the store at 10 boulevard Gouvion-Saint-Cyr and cover real city roads, including dense urban traffic, complex intersections and other challenging environments. Sources indicate that at the 2026 Paris Motor Show in October, XPENG launched the G9L SUV for Europe and offered public NGP test rides. Its event announcement listed demonstrations on roundabouts, narrow streets, motorcycle traffic, pedestrian crossings and complex right-of-way situations, with XPENG and Tesla the only two automakers participating in the official Autonomous Lab.

France country manager Romain Caubet remarked Paris provides a challenging urban setting for customers to experience the technology firsthand. XPENG wants their feedback as it continues developing the intelligent-driving experience for European customers.

In a recent development, NGP is powered by XPENG’s VLA 2.0 architecture. XPENG claims the system combines proprietary Turing AI chips with 4D spatiotemporal understanding to generalize across different roads and unfamiliar scenarios, retaining up to 30 seconds of contextual information and predicting as much as six seconds ahead how a situation may develop.

According to recent reports, chairman and CEO He Xiaopeng said the store initiative is intended to help XPENG understand how people experience the technology in real-world use and adapt it to European customers’ needs. That feedback fits into a broader local engineering effort: XPENG’s European R&D center supports vehicle approval work, language interaction, chassis tuning, charging integration and intelligent-driving validation.

XPENG is continuing to test and localize NGP for European road conditions and regulatory requirements. The company is targeting an international rollout in 2027, subject to regulatory approvals and market readiness, meaning the bookable Paris rides precede any broader deployment rather than demonstrate that approval has been secured.

XPENG also said it serves as OICA secretary for a United Nations Economic Commission for Europe taskforce, a role it connected to international discussions on automated driving.

The store gives XPENG a bookable setting for gathering customer reactions on Paris roads after the motor show. Its longer-term importance depends on whether that feedback and the accompanying validation work help turn a public demonstration into an approved, market-ready driver-assistance product for international markets.

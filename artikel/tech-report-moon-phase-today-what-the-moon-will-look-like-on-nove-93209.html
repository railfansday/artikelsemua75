The Full Moon is just around the corner, and tonight the Moon is looking almost completely illuminated. We're still in the Waxing Gibbous phase for now, but it won't be long before visibility hits 100%.

Industry observers note that as of Sunday, Nov. 22, the NASA's Daily Moon Guide tracker tells us the Moon is in the Waxing Gibbous phase, with 92% of its surface visible. (Related: Industry News: Designing Azure silicon on Azure silicon...)

If you don't have any visual aids, you can still see the Mares Vaporum and Serenitatis, as well as the Aristarchus Plateau. With binoculars, you'll also see the Apennine Mountains, the Gassendi Crater, and the Mare Nectaris. And if you have a telescope, you'll get to see all this plus the Apollo 14 and 17 landing spots, and the Rima Ariadaeus.

The next Full Moon will take place on November 24.

In a recent development, according to NASA, the Moon goes through a complete cycle of phases roughly every 29.5 days. As it orbits Earth, the portion of its sunlit surface visible to us changes, giving rise to the different lunar phases. The cycle begins with a New Moon, progresses to a Full Moon, and then gradually returns to a New Moon before starting all over again. New Moon - The Moon is between Earth and the sun, so the side we see is dark (in other words, it's invisible to the eye).

Waxing Crescent - A small sliver of light appears on the right side (Northern Hemisphere).

First Quarter - Half of the Moon is lit on the right side. It looks like a half-Moon.

Waxing Gibbous - More than half is lit up, but it's not quite full yet.

Full Moon - The whole face of the Moon is illuminated and fully visible.

Industry observers note that waning Gibbous - The Moon starts losing light on the right side. (Northern Hemisphere).

Third Quarter (or Last Quarter) - Another half-Moon, but now the left side is lit.

Waning Crescent - A thin sliver of light remains on the left side before going dark again.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The morning before I was supposed to meet my shuttle driver, I haphazardly threw my pack together. You may come to find out that I’m a professional procrastinator. It’s a tough job, but someone must do it. I planned to have a relaxing day before I set foot on my shakedown hike to double-check my gear. Instead, I threw everything in my pack and drove to Table Rock State Park a couple hours away with no proposal for the night.

Upon arrival at the state park, I registered as a hiker and asked for a primitive campsite with no preference on location. I would’ve picked the site closest to my car, so I left my fate in her hands. She asked about my campsite location preference, to which I replied, “Anywhere you feel is best, I don’t have a preference.” Oh, but I did! Little did the woman behind the desk know that I had never camped alone, and I was terrified to be alone in the woods at night. She sent me to one of the furthest campsites and wished me luck on the trail. (Related: Industry News: “Determined” woman completes 7 marathons...)

With only a 5-minute walk to camp, I set my pack down against the picnic table and stood still, staring off into the abyss. Birds chirping, squirrels bolting through crisp leaves, trees creaking, and my tell-tale heart beating louder, louder, louder in my chest. What have I gotten myself into?! The idea of hiking and camping alone was a great idea until I was really doing it, but it was too late to turn back now. The rangers had already closed and locked the park gates until morning. I was stuck there for the night. Do I sleep in my tent or the car?

It has been revealed that I set up my tent for the first time outside of my backyard, snapped a photo, and sent it to my husband. A celebratory moment! I had constructed my paper-thin shelter to protect me from the outside world. In my head, the thin tent was comparable to Fort Knox. Good luck to anything wanting to get through! It’s funny how something so basic can provide such a false sense of security. Too nervous for what lies ahead to eat, I brought my food back to the car and took a few pictures of my surroundings as the sun set, including one of myself with a forced smile to convince myself I had this. Now what? (Related: Latest Update: One Year Since the “DeepSeek Moment”...)

Recent findings suggest that I spent the next hour texting my husband about how nervous I was and scared of the make-believe monsters that would come capture me in the middle of the night, but my tent would protect me! I closed my eyes and hoped for morning. Instead, I listened to coyotes singing and a dog barking out in the distance all night. I slept for maybe three hours total. Tomorrow is the major day!

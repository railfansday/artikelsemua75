Why Arsenal are behind in race for Champions League spots

Arsenal are six points behind third-placed London City Lionesses in the Women's Super League.

Before the 2026-27 season began, Arsenal technical director Jodie Taylor declared: "I'll flat out say it: we want to win the league and want to take that next step.".

However, after 10 games in this season's Women's Super League, they are sixth in the table, trailing leaders Chelsea by 13 points.

They are also nine points behind third-placed London City Lionesses, albeit with a game in hand, and facing stiff competition to qualify for the Champions League again next campaign. (Related: Nebraska voters appear to reject online sports betting ...)

BBC Sport looks at where things have gone wrong for the Gunners, who have not won the WSL since 2018-19.

Only bottom side Charlton have fewer home points than Arsenal this season.

Last term, Arsenal hosted all 11 of their league home games at Emirates Stadium for the first time, winning eight times and drawing on three occasions.

This season, Renee Slegers' side have drawn all five of their home games and will seek their first win at the sixth attempt when they host West Ham on Sunday (14:30 GMT), live on BBC Two.

Two of those draws came against promoted sides Crystal Palace and Birmingham City, where a combined 57 shots yielded just one goal.

Home fans are getting disgruntled and this has been reflected in attendances. Last season, the Gunners had an average attendance of 33,808, better than eight Premier League clubs.

So far this year their biggest crowd was against London City Lionesses last weekend, when 32,588 were in the stands.

Speaking on the Women's Football Weekly podcast, former England striker Ellen White said: "The Emirates seems [like] a bit of an problem for them at the moment - they just can't get that win there.

"A draw might feel like a loss for them because they are so far behind now in terms of the Champions League spots.".

It has been revealed that former Gunners striker Vivianne Miedema, who did play at Emirates Stadium for Arsenal but joined Manchester City before the full switch was made, added: "Players always say that the crowd really, really helps you at Arsenal. The fans are amazing - it gives you that extra bit of energy.

"Obviously playing in the Emirates with all the fans should still give them that boost but it doesn't seem to be fully working for them this season so far.".

Alessia Russo has scored nine goals in all competitions for Arsenal this season.

The Gunners have had 199 shots in 10 league matches but managed just 11 goals. That is just over one goal per game, and their conversion rate of 5.53% is the lowest of any club over the past six seasons.

Part of the problem could be an over-reliance on key goalscorer Alessia Russo.

She has alternated between the number nine and number 10 position during the season but thrives most when she is the focal point in the attack.

This season, the England striker has five league goals and provided two assists, with her form improving in recent weeks.

She was player of the match against Tottenham after scoring and providing an assist in a 2-1 win and similarly against Liverpool, when she scored twice to help Arsenal triumph by the same scoreline.

After Russo, only Lisa Baum (two), Georgia Stanway (two), Smilla Holmberg and Frida Maanum have scored in the league.

Injuries to attacking options Chloe Kelly, Selina Cerci and Stina Blackstenius have also proved costly, while key midfielder Mariona Caldentey is about halfway through an expected 10-week absence with a hamstring injury.

The result is that Arsenal and Brighton are the only teams in the league to have a goal difference of zero after 10 games.

Contrast that with title rivals Chelsea, who have scored 29 goals and have a goal difference of +24, while defending champions Manchester City have netted 27 times.

Slegers has been in charge of Arsenal since October 2024.

While Slegers will always be hailed for winning Arsenal's second Champions League title last year against Barcelona, questions have been asked about her future after another slow start to the season.

In three of their home league matches, Arsenal have fallen behind and dropped points. Last Sunday, they went ahead against London City but only held the lead for 98 seconds.

It was a big summer of change in north London with club stalwarts Katie McCabe and Beth Mead among those departing, and seven upcoming signings arriving including Stanway, Cerci and Baum.

Also among the arrivals was Swiss midfielder Geraldine Reuteler, who helped her country reach the quarter-finals of Euro 2025 but has struggled for minutes since signing a four-year deal in the summer.

She has made just four appearances across all competitions, appearing for a total of just 66 minutes in the WSL.

The results have left Slegers vulnerable and a first home win of the season against West Ham would be a much-needed boost for a side that have fallen far short of the lofty targets they set back in September.

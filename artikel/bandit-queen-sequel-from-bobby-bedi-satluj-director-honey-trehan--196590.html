📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The sequel to “Bandit Queen,” the 1994 drama about Indian outlaw Phoolan Devi, is aiming for a release at the end of 2027 or early 2028, producer Bobby Bedi tells Variety, with the screenplay now in its final draft and casting underway.

Bedi, who produced Shekhar Kapur’s original, is calling the follow-up “Bandit Queen MP.” Honey Trehan, director of the Diljit Dosanjh-starrer “Satluj,” was unveiled as director earlier this month. The new film picks up where Kapur’s ended, following Devi from her years in prison to her assassination, and a little beyond. (Related: Industry News: huggingface_hub v1.0: Five Years of Buil...)

“The film is really about the tumultuous love story of a married man and a prisoner negotiating their lives in this ‘even more dangerous’ landscape through freedom, fame and fortune. But to what end?” Bedi says.

He adds that women’s role in politics is back at the forefront, and that Devi was a soldier for the cause in her day.

“My first film was about the rape and revenge and fate of a lower caste woman in India’s rural landscape. This film happens to be the story of the political power and ultimate fate of a low caste woman in the urban political landscape. It just happens to be the same woman,” he states.

Bedi describes the project as new territory for both him and Trehan.

Trehan worked on Bedi’s 2003 production “Maqbool,” directed by Vishal Bhardwaj, and the producer claims the maturity of his later independent work stood out. “And then came ‘Satluj,’ earlier called ‘Punjab ’95.’ It blew me over. Well researched, superbly cast, well directed. Very impressive. He is clearly a perfectionist,” Bedi says.

Recent findings suggest that “Satluj,” in which Dosanjh plays human rights activist Jaswant Singh Khalra, spent nearly four years in India’s certification process. Originally titled “Ghallughara,” it was cleared by the Central Board of Film Certification with 21 cuts and an enforced title change to “Punjab ’95,” and was pulled from the gala slot it had been set for at the 2023 Toronto International Film Festival. The film finally premiered uncut on ZEE5 Global on July 3 this year. Less than two days later, ZEE5 took it down in India, saying it would be “unavailable in India until further notice.” Days after that, the platform removed it from its international catalogue as well.

Sources indicate that trehan says “Bandit Queen” inspired filmmakers of his generation, and that being asked by Bedi to carry its legacy forward is special. He is presently absorbed in the material and the research Bedi has already done, and has known the producer since his Delhi theater days, before “Maqbool.”.

“He is such a hands-on producer,” Trehan says.

According to recent reports, phoolan Devi’s story has already returned to the screen this year with Richie Mehta’s “Phoolan,” from Amazon MGM Studios and Namah Pictures, which premiered at the Toronto Film Festival. Rather than retell her whole life, the film, starring Sneha Kumari, centers on a 48-hour siege of a village by 2,000 police and paramilitary personnel sent to capture her when she was 17.

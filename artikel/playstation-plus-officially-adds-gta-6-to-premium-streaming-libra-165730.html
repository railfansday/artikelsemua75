Industry observers note that the arrival of GTA VI on the scene has been just as significant as most people were expecting it to be, blowing past all sorts of records in its first few days. It generated more than $3 billion dollars on its first day out in the wild, making it comfortably the biggest media release of all time in any medium, and that number has only gone up significantly since.

In a recent development, at the same time, the game is now in the hands of over 40 million players, a total that very few titles manage to achieve in their entire lifespans, let alone in less than a week. Reviews were also extraordinarily high, and it currently sits as the highest rated game on Metacritic in terms of professional appraisals.

With all of that success, PlayStation still serves as the official partner platform for the game, and Sony are hard at work trying to differentiate their offering from the competition to make that partnership more than a simple title. Now, the latest of those steps has gone live via an update, and it allows consumers to get involved in the fun in more ways than ever. (Related: Industry News: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

In a recent development, as first reported by PushSquare, GTA VI has now been updated to support PS Plus Premium cloud streaming directly to the PS5 and PS Portal. That noted, the game has not been added to the PS Plus catalog more generally, and you will still need to have an owned copy of the game in your library to take advantage of the upcoming update. (Related: Breaking: My Journey to a serverless transformers pipel...)

According to recent reports, streaming directly to the PlayStation Portal is undoubtedly a big deal, and taking the game to other rooms or away from the main television (when others want to use it) is going to be a major quality-of-life improvement for those who are locked in to completing it. There also isn't a limit to how long you can stream for as a Premium member, so you should be able to explore Vice City to your heart's content.

Xbox fans don't need to be too upset, though, as the game is also available to stream for those with access to an owned copy of GTA VI and an active Xbox Game Pass Ultimate subscription. Those in charge of proceedings did recently change the terms of service for game streaming, so players have a maximum of 15 hours available per month. More can be bought ad-hoc, though it will generally be a cheaper and better idea to play it in the traditional manner.

It has been revealed that one advantage that Xbox players do have is that, despite the PlayStation being Rockstar's console of choice for the game, it currently seems to be performing best on Xbox Series X. The frame rate drops are notably lower, and though the Series X and Pro maintain 30 FPS relatively well, the lows are still marginally better on Microsoft's hardware.

As for GTA VI players, they're continuing to explore the enormously detailed world that Rockstar has created, and there are likely hundreds of hours of things to do before all is noted and done. That will then leave the Online modes as the next port of call, and who knows what kind of chaos that will bring.

And look at Apple: the userspace is plain AArch64, but everything around it is undocumented Apple-only weirdness. Custom interrupt controller, custom boot chain, secret GPU. That's the kind of lock-in an architecture license lets you build.

Recent findings suggest that RISC-V and Power are royalty-free, and there's no single company to buy, sue or headhunt. Until Arm is in that category, I'm backing RISC-V or Power ISAs and I'm staying on x86-64 ISA until one of those two open ISAs takes off. x86-64 isn't an open standard on paper, but the baseline (x86-64-v1) AMD Athlon 64 patents have expired, and x86 has 40+ years of inertia and a long line of non-Intel implementations behind it: Cyrix, Transmeta, VIA, Zhaoxin. Compaq clean-rooming the IBM BIOS is why the PC platform is open in the first place.

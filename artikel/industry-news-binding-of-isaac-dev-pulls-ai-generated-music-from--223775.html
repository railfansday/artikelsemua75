Nicalis has removed AI-generated music from The Binding of Isaac: Repentance+ after players discovered it in the game's latest patch, prompting a wave of negative reviews on Steam.

According to recent reports, the music in question was tied to a run themed around Grand Theft Auto 6, which rolled out on the same day (19th November). A day later, Reddit user ShweetsStuff posted that the run's music was "100% generated by Suno (AI) (with proof)", pointing to tracks that reference the AI music tool Suno in their metadata. "I'm starting to think that adding AI to a game should be valid grounds [for] refunds to people who have already paid for and played a game," said another.

Woke up to a shit show, obviously I had no idea it featured generated music in the daily run, looking into how that happened and getting it removed, stay tuned.

Responding to the criticism, Binding of Isaac creator Edmund McMillen mentioned he "woke up to a shit show".

It has been revealed that "Obviously I had no idea it featured generated music in the daily run, looking into how that happened and getting it removed, stay tuned," he wrote on X/Twitter, before inviting players to report concerns and bugs to the email address shared in one of Nicalis' tweets, which confirmed it had "pushed a new build with critical fixes", including the "removal of generated music files". The patch is live on PC, and Nicalis says it has been submitted to Microsoft, Nintendo, and Sony.

Nicalis has yet to explain how the music ended up in the game.

Industry observers note that following the gigantic Afterbirth and Afterbirth+ expansions, released in 2015 and 2017 respectively, The Binding of Isaac: Rebirth grew again with 2021's community-inspired, sequel-sized Repentance DLC. The Binding of Isaac: Rebirth has since hit a latest concurrent player record on Steam 12 years after it was released.

Alaska Airlines Pulls 2 Nonstop Routes After Only 1 Year: See List Now

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that alaska Airlines has dropped two routes from Hollywood Burbank Airport (BUR) barely a year after launching them. In a recent schedule update, the Seattle-based carrier removed all future flights between Burbank and Eugene Airport (EUG) and Redmond Municipal Airport (RDM) in Central Oregon.

It has been revealed that both routes had been suspended for the winter and were due to return as seasonal summer services in April 2027. The two routes began on October 26, 2025, and operated year-round until the end of October 2026. (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

Alaska operated both routes with a daily flight, served by three-class, 76-seat Embraer E175s flown by SkyWest Airlines. Burbank-Eugene covers 737 miles (1,186 km), while Burbank-Redmond is 714 miles (1,149 km). In August, Alaska suspended both routes for the winter, from November 1, 2026, to April 13, 2027, with daily flights scheduled to return for the summer.

Alaska's Burbank-Oregon Traffic (October 2025–August 2026).

However, according to schedule data from aviation analytics company Cirium, that plan has now been scrapped. Alaska has not explained the decision or announced any plans to bring either route back. The carrier took over both routes from Avelo Airlines, which had served them since May 2021 before closing its Burbank base. With Burbank-Pasco already dropped in April, none of the three former Avelo routes that Alaska took over at Burbank remain in its network.

Industry observers note that US Department of Transportation (DOT) data indicates that the two routes performed almost identically between October 2025 and August 2026. Each carried an average of around 103 to 104 passengers per day across both directions, or about 52 passengers per flight. That left roughly 24 of the E175's 76 seats empty on an average flight, working out to an average load factor of around 69%. This is well below Alaska Air Group's network-wide average loads of 82.8% in the third quarter of 2026. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

Avelo opened its first base at Burbank in April 2021 and began flying to Eugene and Redmond the following month with Boeing 737s. However, in July 2025, the low-cost carrier declared it would close its Burbank base by early December, but later pushed its departure forward, operating its final Eugene and Redmond flights in October.

Industry observers note that alaska revealed replacement flights in July 2025, as part of seven upcoming West Coast routes. It remarked Burbank would become its fifth nonstop destination from Eugene and its sixth from Redmond. Interestingly, Breeze Airways had also planned to compete, announcing twice-weekly Airbus A220 flights from Burbank to Eugene, Redmond, and Pasco starting in March 2026.

Industry observers note that however, it scrapped all three routes in November 2025, before launching any flights. This left Alaska as the only airline offering nonstop flights between Burbank and both Oregon airports. The third former Avelo route, Burbank-Pasco, lasted just five months under Alaska, with service ending in May this year. San Francisco-Burbank was also cut in March 2026, leaving Alaska's Burbank network with year-round flights to Boise, Portland, Santa Rosa, and Seattle, plus a seasonal Honolulu service that began in May 2026.

Alaska has continued to grow at Burbank even as the airport's overall traffic has fallen. Airport statistics show the carrier handled 956,077 passengers in 2025 — up 12.3% from 2024 — and 469,704 in the first half of 2026, an increase of 7%. Over the same periods, total airport traffic fell 5% to 6.22 million passengers in 2025, down from a record 6.55 million in 2024, and declined a further 8.6% in the first half of 2026.

Southwest remains the airport's dominant carrier, handling 3.52 million passengers in 2025, representing over 56% of the market. Burbank also recently opened its upcoming $1.3 billion terminal in October. The project marks a major upgrade for the airport as it looks to modernize its facilities despite declining passenger numbers.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has become commonplace for an adaptation of a new video game to be announced weeks after that game has been released if the hype is high enough. That will not be the case for GTA 6. Despite its success, Rockstar remains staunch in the belief that its hit series never needs to be turned into a video game or a TV show. Red Dead Redemption, on the other hand, is a different story.

That's according to a upcoming report from The Ankler covering Rockstar's continued reluctance to even consider GTA: The Movie. Even though the report was published last week, the day before the launch of GTA 6, it has only just started to land on people's radars as they stumble out of their homes after spending a collective 80,000 years in Leonida. (Related: Tech Report: Rockstar accused of tracing furry artwork ...)

Industry observers note that that report claims Rockstar is not only open to turning Red Dead Redemption into a movie or show, but it is actively shopping its second most successful series. According to an agent who works with video game writers, the asking price for Red Dead's adaptation rights is $100 million. That's less than half of what Amazon paid for the Lord of the Rings rights, so it could make Rings of Power, and an absolute steal when compared to the reported $800 million price tag Xbox slapped on the adaptation rights for its biggest IPs, which were apparently showcased as a package deal. (Related: Industry News: Average Retirement Savings by Age: Are Y...)

According to recent reports, while it might not be GTA, it feels like it might be worth that nine-figure price tag if a studio is confident it can do the Red Dead name justice. I'm sure Rockstar would be heavily involved should a project enter production, and likely wouldn't let anything that makes the series seem "less than" ever see the light of day. It certainly wouldn't be a repeat of what happened to Halo's two-season run on Paramount.

The willingness to have someone adapt Red Dead Redemption while remaining steadfast in its reluctance to see GTA become a movie one day, despite video game adaptations being all the rage, makes a lot of sense. Although still open, the Red Dead games are more linear and story-driven than any of the GTA titles. GTA 6 might be the most story-driven game in the series so far, and even that has so many ways players can go, with millions of us all experiencing the game in wildly different ways.

The Red Dead games have specific stories to tell, and there are talented people out there who can help adapt those stories for a different medium. Although Amazon is the studio I'd have the most faith in to handle a video game IP the right way, following its stellar work with Fallout, Red Dead Redemption feels like it would be much more at home on HBO.

Alarms are one of the most important iPhone features, and iOS 27 offers a key feature that should help your alarm actually wake you: a new volume setting for alarms that’s independent of other volume settings.

Have you ever accidentally slept through an alarm? We’ve all done it. And most often for me, it’s because my alarm volume was unintentionally set to a very low level.

I suspect that’s a pretty common reason for missed alarms. And in iOS 27, Apple finally has the fix—but it requires manually setting up.

Inside Settings ⇾ Sounds & Haptics, you can now set a volume for alarms that’s independent from the systemwide ringtone setting.

iOS 27 has two upcoming sections in this Settings screen: Alarms and Timers, and Alerts and System Sounds. Both include ‘Match Ringtone Volume’ toggles that are on by default. This means your volume for alarms, timers, alerts, and other system sounds are all determined by the ringtone volume. (Related: Breaking: Porting fairseq wmt19 translation system to t...)

But if you disable the Alarms and Timers toggle, you’ll get a new volume slider that applies only to those system functions, and won’t be impacted by your ringtone volume.

As seen above, this means you can crank the Alarms and Timers volume up to the maximum level without making every other system sound, including notifications and phone calls, be similarly deafening.

So the next time you sleep through your alarm, it won’t be because the alarm was too quiet.

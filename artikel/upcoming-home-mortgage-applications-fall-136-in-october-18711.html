Mortgage applications for fresh-home purchases fell 13.6% year over year in October as rising mortgage rates and affordability pressures weighed on buyer demand, according to the Mortgage Bankers Association’s (MBA) Builder Application Survey.

Applications also declined 7% from September. The figures are not adjusted for seasonal patterns.

The 30-year fixed mortgage rate climbed about 60 basis points over the two months leading up to October, reaching 7.5% last month, said Joel Kan, MBA’s vice president and deputy chief economist. (Related: Leading House Democrat targets Trump’s inner circle for...)

“As a result, applications for the purchase of newly built homes declined year-over-year for the fourth consecutive month,” Kan remarked.

MBA’s estimate of latest-home sales increased slightly in October, but the annualized sales pace for the past two months remained at its weakest level since July 2022. Affordability pressures and uncertainty surrounding mortgage rates have prompted prospective buyers to delay purchases, even as builders continue offering incentives, Kan remarked.

MBA estimated that fresh single-family home sales ran at a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 592,000 units in October, up 1.4% from September’s pace of 584,000 units.

On an unadjusted basis, MBA estimated 44,000 latest-home sales in October, down 6.4% from 47,000 in September.

Recent findings suggest that conventional loans accounted for 47.9% of upcoming-home mortgage applications in October, while Federal Housing Administration (FHA) loans made up 36.9%. U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs (VA) loans accounted for 14.1%, and  U.S. Department of Agriculture (USDA) loans represented 1%. The average loan size for new homes declined to $368,934 in October from $374,724 in September.

In a recent development, MBA’s Builder Application Survey tracks mortgage application volume from homebuilder-affiliated mortgage companies nationwide. The association uses the data, along with other market information and assumptions, to estimate latest-home sales at the national, state and metropolitan levels.

The survey provides an early indication of new-home sales because mortgage applications typically coincide with contract signings, when the Census Bureau records new-home sales in its monthly report.

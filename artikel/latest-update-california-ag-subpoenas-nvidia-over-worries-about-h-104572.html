According to recent reports, california’s Attorney General Rob Bonta on Thursday evening remarked the state has served an investigative subpoena to Nvidia following news of former CEO Jensen Huang’s affair with a woman who the U.S. government believes is a Chinese spy.

The state is investigating the company to find out if its advanced GPUs or confidential information may have been shared with China, and if the company had somehow misled its investors and the public.

“The conduct of Nvidia and its former CEO raises bright red flags, including about the ways in which the manufacturer may have misled investors,” Bonta stated in a statement.

“My office is investigating whether Nvidia or Jensen Huang’s actions resulted in China obtaining advanced chips or confidential information — and if Nvidia misled investors and the public about this risk, in violation of the law,” he added.

The investigation comes in the wake of a messy couple of weeks for Nvidia, which has overnight come under intense scrutiny around the world due to the implications of Huang’s alleged relationship with the Chinese national, Alice Huang (who is not related to Jensen).

Industry observers note that the Nvidia founder resigned last week after further revelations of extramarital affairs with the firm’s employees. And while many tech voices have been petitioning Nvidia to bring back Huang as CEO, saying they see him as indispensable to the further development of the AI industry, the company’s newly introduced chair, Harvey Jones, said earlier this week that Nvidia had no intention of reinstating Huang to his position. In a recent development, the organization’s board has hired a law firm as well as an independent slate of directors to investigate the extent of the damage, and the U.S. government is trying to find out the extent of Alice Huang’s ties with the Chinese government.

The U.S. Congress is also gearing up to subpoena Huang, seeking questions about the affair, and the SEC is said to be launching a civil probe into the company, trying to find out if Nvidia’s public disclosures could have misled investors. Recent findings suggest that democratic representatives have also opened an inquiry into the Trump administration’s management of Nvidia’s chip sales to China. In a scathing letter to Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick, Democrats last week asked the Commerce Department to preserve records about an arrangement that they called questionable and illegal.

“Nvidia is in a messy, transnational situation but the underlying principle of our corporate securities law is clear: Companies cannot mislead their investors,” Bonta wrote.

Huang has agreed to testify before Congress after several senior lawmakers threatened to subpoena him.

Industry News: AI copycats are cloning creators’ videos for millions of views, BBC finds

🌐 Source: Thenextweb • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (406 words, 📖 Standard)
💡 Key Takeaways

  • Sources indicate that experts call the practice “reskinning”.
  • Recent findings suggest that UK guitarist Sophie Lloyd, who tours with Machine Gun Kelly, says accounts keep reskinning her performances with over-sexualised AI characters.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Musicians, dancers and comedians are finding their videos copied by AI-generated characters, BBC Verify reported. The copies repeat the same performance, sometimes to millions more viewers. Marco Silva found accounts that copy a real person’s clip frame by frame and swap the person for an AI character.

Sources indicate that experts call the practice “reskinning”. Cheap AI tools make it easy: the copy keeps the same joke, the same dance and often the same background. Some accounts use it for views and money, the BBC found. Others seem to do it for laughs or attention. (Related: Understanding BigBird's Block Sparse Attention...)

Recent findings suggest that UK guitarist Sophie Lloyd, who tours with Machine Gun Kelly, says accounts keep reskinning her performances with over-sexualised AI characters. One Facebook account posted hundreds of videos of a guitarist called “Mila Vasari”. At least three copied Lloyd’s music, movements and facial expressions, BBC Verify found. (Related: The Age of Machine Learning As Code Has Arrived...)

In a recent development, the BBC found no evidence that Vasari is real. Her fingers look distorted and the strings don’t move. The account appears to be run from Canada. It links to a Vasari page on Fanvue, a subscription site that allows adult content. It did not answer the BBC’s questions. Two more accounts with AI guitarists copied Lloyd to promote adult content.

“It takes years to master an instrument and seconds to take and rip it off as something else,” Lloyd told the BBC.

Norwegian creator Kjetil Krogstad, known for dancing in a suit with a cup of tea, saw one video redone with an AI bunny. The copy drew millions more views than his original before it disappeared. The account behind it sold courses on making money from AI content.

British car creator Grace Roberts found her video remade with a blonde woman in a short dress. The copy kept her movements, the background and even the people around her.

“It was just me that had been altered,” Roberts told BBC Verify.

In a recent development, many copies follow videos that are already popular, mentioned Tina Kendall, who studies online culture at Anglia Ruskin University. She described the copies as part of an online “hustle culture”. Alwyn Cruz, who exposes AI musicians online, said fresh accounts appear as soon as platforms remove old ones.

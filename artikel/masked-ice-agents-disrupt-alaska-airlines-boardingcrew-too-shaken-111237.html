Passengers boarding Alaska Airlines flight 1306 from Seattle to Los Angeles on Saturday found themselves in a crowded jet bridge with masked federal agents struggling with travelers at the aircraft door. Flight attendants were so shaken the airline needed replacements. An airport is almost the ideal place to arrest someone. The government has everyone’s travel information in advance, checks their identity, screens them for weapons, and knows where they’re going to be. There are cameras everywhere, and myriad law enforcement agencies on site. But those systems were built to protect aviation and travelers. Repurposing them for routine immigration ambushes creates a distraction from safety. What happened on that Alaska Airlines jet bridge The confrontation happened around 8 a.m. on November 21 at Seattle’s gate N9. The Port of Seattle says it involved Immigration and Customs Enforcement and two passengers, a father and daughter. There were roughly 50 people pressed against the jet bridge walls during a confrontation lasting more than 10 minutes. A pilot left and two flight attendants asked to be replaced.

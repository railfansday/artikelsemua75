Realty Income (O +1.31%), a real estate investment trust (REIT) that leases properties to businesses, pays its dividend every month. After its Nov. 10 raise, the monthly dividend is $0.2740 per share, so 365 shares pay about $100 a month. (Related: What I do every time I get an IRS notice...)

Industry observers note that at the Nov. 19 close of $57.66, it would take an investment of about $21,000 to earn that monthly income level at a dividend yield of about 5.7%.

NYSE · As of 11:39 AM ET 11/24/26.

According to recent reports, rent payments from triple net leases fund those dividends. The advantage of this structure is that tenants pay property taxes, insurance, and maintenance, which lowers the capital burden on Realty Income. At the end of September, its portfolio was 98.8% occupied, with an average lease term of about 8.5 years.

Importantly, about 90% of retail rent, which is the source of most of Realty's annual base rent, comes from "non-discretionary, service-oriented" businesses. This reflects management's strategy to lease space to tenants with strong business models that can generate a stable revenue stream through all market cycles.

Recent findings suggest that realty Income earns enough to cover the dividend with room to spare. In the third quarter, dividends used 73% of adjusted funds from operations (AFFO), a REIT's measure of cash earnings. The company has declared 677 consecutive monthly dividends and raised the payout for 117 straight quarters.

Higher interest rates are a headwind, making it more expensive for Realty Income to borrow money to buy upcoming properties. This can slow the growth of the cash it uses to pay dividends.

It has been revealed that still, Realty Income keeps buying properties that earn more than funding costs, and management just raised its outlook for this year. At about 13 times 2026 AFFO guidance and about 14% below its 52-week high, the stock offers long-term income investors an attractive entry point.

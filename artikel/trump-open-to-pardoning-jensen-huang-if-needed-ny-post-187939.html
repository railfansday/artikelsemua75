President Donald Trump said he would be willing to pardon reinstated Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang if needed after Huang appears before Congress next week, according to an exclusive interview with the New York Post.

In a recent development, huang's reinstatement followed Trump's intervention with Nvidia's board, including a reported call into a board meeting to argue for his return. Even so, lawmakers are still set to scrutinize his relationship with suspected Chinese intelligence asset Alice Huang in two appearances next week.

Nvidia shares surged 31% on Monday after Huang was reinstated as CEO, before falling 5.9% on Tuesday amid a broader market pullback.

Trump also noted that Huang should not need to invoke the Fifth Amendment to avoid self-incrimination when he appears for a closed-door interview with the House Foreign Affairs Committee on Dec. 1 and before the Senate Banking Committee on Dec. 3.

Sources indicate that "I think it's an interesting story for people," Trump stated, according to the NY Post. "I think I want him to just tell the truth.". (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

Trump added that while a pardon was "always an option," he did not think it would be "necessary," the newspaper reported. The president's clemency authority is limited to federal offenses, according to the Justice Department's Office of the Pardon Attorney.

Sen. John Kennedy, R-La., told Fox News on Monday that he expected Huang to invoke his Fifth Amendment rights during the congressional proceedings.

"I expect him to take the Fifth Amendment so he doesn't incriminate himself in criminal prosecution, but we're going to give him a chance to explain," Kennedy said.

Industry observers note that trump also told the New York Post that he was open to the U.S. government taking an equity stake in Nvidia, similar to its stake in Intel in 2025, arguing that the organization's success was vital to maintaining the U.S. lead in artificial intelligence. He also said it was his understanding that Huang had not had affairs with other women linked to Beijing and that he did not know how many affairs Huang had acknowledged.

Rep. Jamie Raskin, D-Md., the lead Democrat on the House Judiciary Committee, told CNBC on Tuesday that he believed Trump was making decisions that could benefit his own stock portfolio, despite the president's assertion that he does not make trades himself.

It has been revealed that "If you don't believe that, you're too innocent to be let out of the house by yourself. The whole thing is a money-making operation. What's the alternative explanation?" Raskin stated.

Separately, independent journalist Marisa Kabas reported that Huang told Nvidia employees at Tuesday's all-hands meeting that Trump "loves" the manufacturer because of its employees' "contributions" to the U.S., while also addressing questions about whether Nvidia had become political or partisan.

Recent findings suggest that "I want to be very clear: President Trump is the United States president. I'll do everything I can to help this president be informed about how he can make decisions in the best interests of the country," Huang mentioned.

The reinstated CEO added that it would be a "great honor and a privilege" to go to Washington, D.C., to "inform them so the government can make the best possible decision," and remarked he would do the same for whoever becomes the next president.

"Don't think for a second politics played a role in the tech giant. We are the most apolitical company. Everything he's done is because of his affection, admiration, and recognition of the great company that you are," Huang noted.

Carles Puyol is widely considered one of football's finest defenders, having played a pivotal part in Barcelona and Spain's successful eras throughout the 2000s and 2010s, leading both sides to an impressive collection of major trophies.

A six-time UEFA Team of the Year nominee, Puyol secured six La Liga championships during his career at the Nou Camp, alongside three Champions League crowns, two Spanish Cups and several other domestic and international accolades.

According to recent reports, on the international stage, he was a key figure in Spain's defence during their back-to-back tournament successes, helping La Roja claim Euro 2008 and the 2010 World Cup. He featured in every game of the latter competition and netted a crucial semi-final winner against Germany.

Although he previously identified players such as Arjen Robben and Sergio Aguero as some of the toughest rivals of his career, Puyol was recently challenged to select the most difficult opponent from three former Premier League greats: Thierry Henry, Didier Drogba and Wayne Rooney.

According to recent reports, puyol, speaking with former Manchester United centre-back Rio Ferdinand, was asked to identify the toughest challenge between Henry, Drogba and Rooney. He singled out the first two, highlighting Henry's display in the 2006 Champions League final:. (Related: Show, Don't Certify: How to Prepare for Your First Data...)

“Henry and Drogba. Henry in the final in 2006 was amazing, and Drogba, many times, fighting.”.

Henry and Puyol first crossed paths in the 2006 Champions League final, which saw Barcelona come from behind to secure a 2-1 victory thanks to goals from Samuel Eto’o and Juliano Belletti.

The pair also went head-to-head at the 2006 World Cup in Germany, where France eliminated Spain with a 3-1 win in the Round of 16. Franck Ribery, Patrick Vieira and Zinedine Zidane were all on target for Les Bleus.

In 2007, Henry joined Puyol at Barcelona, arriving for a reported transfer fee of £16m after spending eight successful years with Arsenal, where he established himself as the club's record goalscorer with 228 strikes and one of the greatest strikers in football history.

Henry spent the next three seasons alongside Puyol at the Nou Camp, playing an key role in Barcelona's remarkable 2009 campaign. The Catalan side claimed six trophies that year, completing a historic treble as part of an unprecedented sextuple.

The former France international recorded 121 appearances for Barcelona, registering 49 goals and 27 assists, before making the move to Latest York Red Bulls in July 2010.

Puyol also encountered Drogba on nine occasions at club and international level, with five of those clashes taking place across Champions League campaigns during the striker's Chelsea career.

The Barcelona captain won just the opening three encounters against Drogba, while their final meeting came in the 2011/12 Champions League semi-finals, when Chelsea progressed with a 3-2 aggregate triumph.

Chelsea went on to lift the Champions League trophy for the first time in their history that season. Drogba played a decisive role in the final against Bayern Munich, scoring a late equaliser to force extra time before converting the winning penalty in the shootout.

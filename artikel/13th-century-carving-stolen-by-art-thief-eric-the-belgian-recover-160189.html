In a recent development, spain’s National Police have spent the past 47 years searching for a Romanesque wood carving of the Virgin Mary that was stolen from the Church of Sa Pedro de Meoz in Navarre, part of the Basque area of northern Spain. At last, they finally found it.

The 21-inch-tall gilded object was nabbed by the notorious “gentleman thief” René Alphonse van den Berghe, known as Erik the Belgian, who robbed churches and monasteries in rural Spain in the late 1970s and early ’80s. Many of his marks have since been recovered. In 2023, for example, a missing fragment from a set of six 17th-century Flemish tapestries was returned to the church in Castrojeriz, Spain, from where Erik the Belgian had swiped them in 1980. Sources indicate that as reported in El País, the investigation leading to this latest discovery picked up steam in April. The authorities knew that Erik the Belgian transported most of his loot from Spain to other European countries “with higher demand in the art and antiques market.”.

The local paper Noticias de Navarra notes that in 2012 the Virgin sculpture was sold by a Belgian antique dealer named Bernard Descheemaker, and that by 2017 it was part of the collection Daniel Kanz Gallery in London. (Related: Racehorse named IShowSpeed wins big in Australia...)

Sources indicate that in 2020, according to El País, it found its way to Maastricht in the Netherlands as part of TEFAF, the prestigious art and antiques fair organized by the European Fine Art Foundation. It then “passed through the hands of various antique dealers and collectors,” eventually landing with a Spanish art collection in the UK.

The handover took place at the Spanish Embassy in London, and the piece is now back in Spain.

That saw them introduce Kirsty Howat in place of Shannon O’Brien in the seventh of eight added minutes.

A statement from the WSL confirms Crystal Palace admitted their breach of competition rules, while an independent tribunal dubbed the move “inadvertent” and “not intended to secure an unfair advantage”. It also acknowledged Palace have taken steps to ensure the incident would not be repeated.

However, it maintained a sporting sanction would be necessary, writing: “The integrity of the competition would be damaged if a club that had breached the rules on substitution opportunities, and had won the match, were allowed to keep the points it obtained.”.

Industry observers note that howat did not touch the ball in her time on the pitch, which lasted less than one minute, though Everton maintained her introduction handed the Eagles an unfair advantage. The tribunal concurred, adding in their decision that Howat’s lack of influence on the match did not amount to “exceptional circumstances.”.

The imposed three-point deduction will leave Palace on six points with 11 matches played and drop them to 11th place in the table, above only winless Charlton Athletic. Should they finish the season in that position, they would face a play-off with the WSL2’s second-place finisher for their place in the top flight.

Palace said in a club statement that they are “disappointed” by the tribunal’s decision, adding they feel they have been stripped of a “deserved victory.”.

The statement read: “Everyone at the club is obviously frustrated that such a minor infraction, given that the player was on the pitch for only 46 seconds while the ball was in play, has led to such a significant penalty – a penalty which we do not agree is reasonable or proportionate.”.

Palace do not have the option to appeal the tribunal’s decision.

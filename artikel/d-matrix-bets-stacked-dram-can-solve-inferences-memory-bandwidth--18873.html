As artificial intelligence workloads shift to inference, memory bandwidth decides whether hardware generates tokens swiftly or sits idle. D-Matrix Corp. is answering with Raptor, a second-generation chip stacking 3D DRAM beneath its compute logic.

AI infrastructure has become a systems game, and winners combine memory, interconnect and software into cohesive designs, according to Satyam Srivastava (pictured), fellow at d-Matrix.

“It’s no longer a chip problem. Chip plays a part of it. Memory bandwidth is a fundamental advantage that has to be solved,” Srivastava said. “The next is energy efficiency. The next is interconnect efficiency. The next is software cohesiveness.”.

It has been revealed that srivastava spoke with theCUBE Research’s John Furrier at SC26, during an exclusive broadcast on theCUBE, SiliconANGLE Media’s livestreaming studio. They discussed d-Matrix’s Raptor chip and its stacked 3D DRAM, inference memory bandwidth and the shift toward heterogeneous computing. (* Disclosure below.).

According to recent reports, D-Matrix proves each risky technology on a test chip first. Jayhawk proved die-to-die connectivity, and Jayhawk II validated digital in-memory computing. The Corsair accelerator combined both and entered full production earlier this year, Srivastava explained. (Related: Leading House Democrat targets Trump’s inner circle for...)

“Fast forward, we have the Pavehawk chip, test chip, which is proving out 3D DRAM stacked underneath the logic die, which serves as the core technology for Raptor, which is our second-generation chip coming out next year,” Srivastava noted.

It has been revealed that stacking keeps the distributed memory architecture intact but moves memory cells onto a separate layer rather than consuming logic die area. Data travels a short distance and never crosses an interposer. That puts 3D DRAM in a upcoming tier between SRAM and high-bandwidth memory, Srivastava noted. (Related: British Airways Avios — Short Nonstop Domestic Flights...)

“So you’re getting 10 times higher energy efficiency compared to HBM, 10 times higher bandwidth compared to HBM, where it sits in between SRAM and HBM in terms of capacity,” Srivastava said.

In a recent development, pairing a Corsair-based server with an existing Hopper GPU fleet can raise utilization from about 20% to as much as 80%, meaning more tokens per dollar, Srivastava noted. D-Matrix has also adopted NVLink Fusion rackscale infrastructure from Nvidia Corp. to connect its accelerators to Nvidia’s AI factories.

According to recent reports, “For us, it unlocks the opportunity to participate in a very established ecosystem, the world’s most popular AI factory. We get access to a very hardened design of an MGX rack,” Srivastava said.

The partnership points toward heterogeneous computing, where software orchestrates GPUs, CPUs and accelerators such as Raptor as one system, with the cluster as the unit of computing, Srivastava explained.

“So think of a chip, but that chip is an entire cluster and it has many different hardware units,” Srivastava noted. “Some are GPUs, some are CPUs, some are ASIC accelerators, and you can use whichever one you like to serve the needs of the workload the best.”.

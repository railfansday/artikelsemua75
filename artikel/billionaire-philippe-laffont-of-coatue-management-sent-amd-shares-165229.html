Although earnings season and Fed meetings tend to hog all the headlines, investors can learn a lot during Form 13F filing season. A 13F allows investors to track which stocks Wall Street's savviest money managers, with at least $100 million in assets under management, purchased and sold in the latest quarter.

Sources indicate that billionaire Philippe Laffont of Coatue Management is easily one of the most successful and closely watched fund managers on Wall Street -- and he was a busy bee during the third quarter. According to Coatue's 13F, Laffont sent shares of Advanced Micro Devices (AMD +6.85%) packing, while at the same time loading up on shares of one of Wall Street's hottest artificial intelligence (AI) initial public offerings (IPOs), SK Hynix (SKHY +20.00%). (Related: Deploy Hugging Face models easily with Amazon SageMaker...)

In a recent development, among Wall Street's billionaire investors, few have embraced the AI revolution quite like Laffont. As recently as mid-2023, his fund held approximately 14 million shares of AMD. By the end of 2025, all 14 million shares were given the heave-ho.

Laffont bought back 95,987 shares in the second quarter of 2026, only to turn around and dump every last share the very next quarter.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/23/26.

In a recent development, profit-taking is the most logical reason Coatue's billionaire boss bid adieu to AMD. Between April and July, shares of the company soared from around $200 to more than $500. Coatue's average hold time is around three years, signaling a willingness by its chief investor to ring the register when given the opportunity.

However, profit-taking may not be the only reason AMD was jettisoned. While Laffont isn't exactly a value investor, AMD's estimated price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio for 2026 and 2027 was approaching 80 and 40, respectively, toward the end of September. With AMD holding such a small share of the AI-accelerating graphics processing unit (GPU) market, Laffont may have viewed other AI stocks as more intriguing. (Related: Accelerating PyTorch distributed fine-tuning with Intel...)

Although everyone's talking about Space Exploration Technologies (SPCX +4.35%) and Anthropic (ANT -1.43%) breaking the IPO record books, they're overlooking SK Hynix, which also debuted in the U.S. in July as a trillion-dollar manufacturer. Laffont purchased 9,498,922 shares, worth almost $1.75 billion at the end of the third quarter.

Laffont's attraction to SK Hynix likely has to do with its world-leading position as a supplier of high-bandwidth memory (HBM). HBM is stacked with GPUs to facilitate ultra-fast transfer speeds in enterprise data centers. Not only is SK Hynix benefiting from strong demand for HBM, but persistent supply shortages are also pushing its pricing power into the stratosphere.

Industry observers note that SK Hynix foresees memory shortages potentially lasting through 2030. According to a late-October revision, the company has already secured purchase agreements for more than half of its production for 2027 and 2028. Wall Street (and billionaire investor Philippe Laffont) appreciates cash-flow predictability.

But SK Hynix isn't necessarily a slam-dunk investment. While its purchase agreements can temporarily buoy its pricing power, history shows that memory is cyclical and highly commoditized, and strong pricing power is rarely sustainable. If the AI bubble bursts, as history suggests will happen sooner rather than later, SK Hynix's pricing power could take a direct hit.

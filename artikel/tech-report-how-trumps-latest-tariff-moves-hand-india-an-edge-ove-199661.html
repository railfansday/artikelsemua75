Recent findings suggest that the latest U.S. tariff rates announced Wednesday have signaled that Washington is willing to offer India an edge over its competitors if New Delhi agrees to finalize a long-pending trade deal.

The Office of the United States Trade Representative has proposed a tariff of 17% on Indian exports if New Delhi "were to reach an Agreement on Reciprocal Trade" with Washington. It added that without a trade deal, India could face tariffs of up to 25% under Section 301 investigations encompassing excess production capacity that harms U.S. interests. Meanwhile, the USTR has proposed tariffs of 18% on Bangladesh, Cambodia, Thailand, and 20% on China and Vietnam, subject to their respective trade deals, handing India a competitive advantage over rival exporters.

According to recent reports, the U.S. expects the announcement of a lower tariff rate will encourage New Delhi to sign off on a trade deal before the G20, Atman Trivedi, partner at Washington-based DGA Albright Stonebridge Group, told CNBC.

India has got a "better bargain with Washington than its main competitors," and it may be enough for the Indian government "to declare victory and secure domestic backing for the deal," Trivedi remarked.

In February, U.S. President Donald Trump had confirmed a deal with India, claiming that Fresh Delhi had accepted to "Buy American" at a much higher level after the U.S. agreed to slash tariffs to 18% from 50% earlier. (Related: Introducing RTEB: A Fresh Standard for Retrieval Evalua...)

It has been revealed that the India-U.S. trade deal has been pending with both sides indicating there was little room to grant concessions, while the Supreme Court annulling Trump tariffs allowed Fresh Delhi to demand preferential tariffs that give it an edge over competitors.

"Today's announcement increases the urgency of reaching an agreement," Ronak D. Desai, visiting fellow at Hoover Institution, Stanford University, told CNBC. He added that while the prospect of a 17% tariff may strengthen the economic case for the deal, the threat of a 25% tariff could stir the sensitivity of the Indian government.

"The Modi government will be particularly sensitive to any perception that India is conceding under American Pressure," Desai said.

India's commerce ministry on Wednesday said it "does not have structural excess capacity in any sector," denying the findings of the USTR investigation, but added that it remains committed to "working closely with the United States towards the early conclusion of the India-U.S. Bilateral Trade Agreement.".

Despite India facing steep tariffs, which were raised to 50%, Fresh Delhi's trade surplus with the U.S. rose to $57.3 billion in 2025 from $45.2 billion in 2024.

Indian Prime Minister Narendra Modi and Trump are likely to meet in December for the G20 leaders' meeting in Florida, and several experts believe the two leaders could seal the trade deal on the sidelines of the summit.

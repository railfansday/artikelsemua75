— Weiwei Yang has left Microsoft after more than a decade to co-found a startup that is “building the power grid for the AI age,” according to her LinkedIn profile. (Related: AI data center power demand will double by 2030 thanks ...)

Yang was most recently a senior director with Microsoft Research, working on grid technology. Her team built a grid dataset and released small foundation models to help users operate and expand electrical infrastructure. (Related: OpenAI's AI models boost AMD's upcoming flagship GPU de...)

Yang thanked her community at the tech giant, adding, “I hope the relationships and collaborations we’ve built continue well beyond Microsoft, and that we find many latest ways to work together.”.

— Allie Mellen has joined Microsoft as principal director for security research and threat intelligence. Mellen was previously a principal analyst at Forrester. She also wrote “Code War,” a book that explores cyberattacks as a powerful geopolitical weapon.

In her upcoming role, Mellen remarked she will lead strategy for protection and threat intelligence, “helping shape what intelligence we produce, how AI can improve its speed and quality, and how we turn that knowledge into better protection for customers.”.

— Agility Robotics, the Salem, Ore., company that had its Nasdaq debut earlier this month, has announced three leadership changes:.

— Former tech executive Drew Sheriff has been named CEO and president of the Space Needle and Chihuly Garden and Glass. Sheriff was previously a vice president at DoorDash and has held leadership roles at Amazon, Starbucks and Parachute Home. His roles focused on customer experience, product growth, strategy and marketing.

“His skill set is the ideal background for charting how we attract customers and how we delight customers in this upcoming age,” Jeff Seely, board chair for the Space Needle and Chihuly Garden and Glass, told the Seattle Times.

— Microsoft has named Caroline King as director of its Washington state community and philanthropy team, with a focus on advocacy work across housing, affordability and other priority concerns.

King is the founder and past CEO of Washington STEM, an organization that coordinates research, advocacy, policy, philanthropy and partnerships to support science, technology, engineering and math education in the state. She most recently served as a leader on the governing body of an international school in Luxembourg.

— Longtime Amazon and Microsoft tech exec Scott Wiltamuth has retired — mostly.

Sources indicate that wiltamuth joined Amazon in 2011 as an AWS vice president overseeing a large organization building developer and management tools. In 2018, he took a seven-month leave for colon cancer treatment. After returning, he sought a director-level position and thanked company leaders for helping him find a better fit. “Their flexibility and creativity enabled me to extend my AWS career,” he said.

Wiltamuth launched his tech career at Microsoft in 1993 and left nearly 19 years later as director of program management for Visual Studio Pro.

Recent findings suggest that he now spends time with his wife and family, traveling, golfing, swimming and reading. “I don’t blueprint to pursue another full-time operational role,” Wiltamuth mentioned, “but may do some part-time work.”.

Industry observers note that — Adam Nygate has exited Palo Alto Networks to co-found and lead a stealth startup based in Seattle. He was founder and CEO of Huntr, a platform debuted in 2019 where security researchers could submit information on AI and machine learning vulnerabilities. Protect AI acquired Huntr, and Palo Alto Networks later acquired Protect AI.

“Huntr was a bet that open source gets safer when vulnerabilities are visible to the people who can fix them, even when that made life uncomfortable for vendors who’d rather keep things quiet,” Nygate said in a post announcing his career change and impending new baby.

— BluePill, a Seattle-based marketing startup that helps brands predict consumer behavior, has named Mike Weis as chief revenue officer and head of sales.

Weis has 40 years of experience in consumer packaged goods, retail and commerce, beginning his career as a Pacific Northwest leader at Unilever and holding executive positions at Catalina and Quotient Technologies. Recent roles include adviser at Contento2, eGateway Capital and And1 Advisors.

Industry observers note that “Beyond the incredible résumé, Mike is one of the kindest, most genuine, and most passionate people I’ve met. Someone who has accomplished so much, yet brings so much humility, curiosity, and energy to every conversation,” said Ankit Dhawan, CEO and founder of BluePill.

Recent findings suggest that — Glacis, a Seattle startup that builds tamper-proof records of AI behavior, has named Caer Mistral Sanders as a technical co-founder. Sanders has rolled out startups and joins Glacis from Thoughtworks. Before that, she held leadership roles at Wayfair and Onshape. As a teenager, she published a book on mixed-reality control systems and won a world robotics championship.

In a recent development, in a post announcing the newly introduced role, Sanders mentioned she has designed medical record systems, researched robot communication, patented cryptographic technology and built widely used AI platforms. “This is the first time in my career I get to bring all of those skills to bear on one problem, and I couldn’t be more excited!” she added.

— The PNW Battery Collaborative has welcomed Jason Demeny as its director of communications. Demeny is a marketing leader whose past employers include Crusoe, Patch, Qualtrics and Apptio. Earlier in his career, he spent more than a decade at Microsoft over two stints, most recently leading teams that served the Azure Partner Experience group.

The nonprofit PNW Battery Collaborative brings together startups, innovators and researchers focused on energy storage.

Industry observers note that — Tom Wheeler, former chairman of the Federal Communications Commission, has joined the advisory council for Known, a Bellevue, Wash.-based organization building accountability infrastructure for autonomous AI. Wheeler led the FCC from 2013 to 2017.

Known, previously called Identity Digital Innovation Labs, spun out of Identity Digital in September.

It has been revealed that — Versium has promoted Matthew Baumgartner to vice president of engineering. The Redmond, Wash.-based manufacturer provides data and software that help businesses identify potential customers, fill in missing contact information and target their marketing. Baumgartner has been with Versium since 2018.

Industry observers note that — Charlie Trillingham has been named communications manager for the University of Washington AI and Machine Learning for Engineering programs and the UW NSF AI Institute for Dynamic Systems. He previously served as a marketing and communications lead for the Buerk Center for Entrepreneurship within the UW’s Foster School of Business.

Editor’s note: Story updated 11/19 to correct that Charlie Trillingham worked for the Buerk Center for Entrepreneurship within the UW’s Foster School of Business, not the Foster School itself.

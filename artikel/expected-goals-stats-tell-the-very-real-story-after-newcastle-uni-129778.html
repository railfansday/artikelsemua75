Expected Goals is widely agreed to be the best way of measuring how well Premier League clubs play in any particular game.

To get a better look at how sides are doing, the Expected Goals (xG) metric allows you to get a better picture of just how teams are performing.

Expected goals (xG) is a statistic used to work out how many goals should be scored in a match.

With every single shot awarded an xG value based on the difficulty of the attempt, with factors including distance from goal, type of shot and number of defenders present affecting the value.

The higher the xG of a particular shot, the more likely a goal should be scored from that shot.

The xG value of every shot in a game is then used to calculate the expected goals in a particular match.

So rather than just the usual basic statistics of how many shots each team has, Expected Goals factors in where shots were taken from and how good a chance was and whether defenders in the way etc.

Recent findings suggest that as you can see, Newcastle’s Expected Goals stat is higher than Arsenal’s by a decent degree. Not a huge difference but a clear one.

When you look at the other stats below, they back up the fact that Newcastle United deserved at the very least a point AND arguably if either side deserved to have won the game, it was United, as they had the better chances overall.

As well as the higher Expected Goals stat, Newcastle had twice as many corners (6 v 3), whilst the overall shots (12 v 13) and efforts on target (3 v 4) were almost identical.

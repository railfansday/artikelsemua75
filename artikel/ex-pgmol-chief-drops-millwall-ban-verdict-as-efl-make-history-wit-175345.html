The FA have made an example of Millwall by closing part of their stadium after their fans were found guilty of discriminatory chants.

That is according to former PGMOL chief and FIFA referee, Keith Hackett, who has praised the governing body for taking serious action ahead of the Lions' upcoming home clash against Sheffield United next month. (Related: Latest Update: Clash Royale recruits creator of AI chea...)

Millwall have been handed a historic first-ever partial stadium closure in the EFL by the FA this week.

An official statement on Tuesday confirmed that the club had accepted the closure and a £70,000 fine for two guilty charges of failing to ensure that their supporters behaved in a proper manner. (Related: Restaurant owners left in tears after discovering their...)

The decision stems from a 24 January clash at The Den against rivals Charlton Athletic, where home fans were heard singing homophobic and ableist chants.

Millwall supporters have reacted angrily to becoming the first Football League club to be sanctioned in such a manner, despite the club admitting their faults.

The ban involves two games, with the first seeing four blocks closed in the Dockers Stand, affecting approximately 1,274 fans, for Millwall's game against Sheffield United on 8 December.

It is suspended for the second match until the final round of games in the 2027-18 season, so Millwall will be hoping their fans do not repeat their offence or another punishment will be in order.

Former PGMOL boss Hackett has insisted that the FA will have mulled over this decision for a while, and it is indicative of the governing body showing that they will be dealing with such incidents with a harsher tone going forward.

Speaking exclusively to Football Insider, he stated: "This is a sanction that will have been carefully thought through by the disciplinary panel. It hits the club financially. Partial closure of the ground may see the spectators affected attempting to buy tickets in other parts of the stadium.

In a recent development, "This is a strong message and a sign of the FA clamping down. I hope Millwall can resolve this matter for the good of the game.".

Millwall manager Alex Neil will not be best pleased with the punishment that is likely to affect The Den's home atmosphere in their December clash against the Blades.

They have challenged for the playoffs in each of the last two seasons and even finished third last term, but are at the moment struggling in 16th place on a run of just one win in their last seven games.

Opta's supercomputer is currently predicting the Lions to finish around 15th with 60 points.

As it stands, they have a 9.56 per cent chance of making the playoffs and a 3.8 per cent chance of being relegated to League One.

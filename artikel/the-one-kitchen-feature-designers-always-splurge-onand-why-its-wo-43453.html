According to recent reports, the kitchen is the hardest-working room in your house, but it’s also one of the most expensive spaces to renovate. Depending on the size and scope of your redo, reimagining this space can cost up to six figures, so it’s key to be wise about allocating your budget. While some people may overspend on high-end appliances or really luxurious countertops, you should never overlook the room’s lighting. Turns out, that’s where designers always splurge in the kitchen. In a recent development, “Lighting has such a powerful ability to change how a room feels,” explains Meghan Jay, an interior designer in Winnetka, Illinois. “You can have the most beautiful cabinetry, stone, and hardware, but if the lighting isn't right, the space can feel flat or even cold.”. (Related: AI for Game Development: Creating a Farming Game in 5 D...)

It has been revealed that meghan Meehan of Hudson East Design in Fresh Jersey agrees and says the right fixtures can provide height, dimensionality, and lots of mood to the space. “Don't think of lighting as the finishing touch,” she adds. “Think of it as one of the main design elements that makes the entire kitchen.”.

Ahead, here’s why spending a little more on your kitchen lighting is worth it—and exactly how much you should budget for.

Every kitchen deserves to glow at full wattage, but it will cost you. After all, illuminating a kitchen isn’t as simple as adding a budget-friendly portable lamp and calling it a day: The best and brightest kitchens usually include several sources such as sconces, recessed ceiling fixtures, and under-cabinet lighting to illuminate meal-prepping zones.

In a recent development, “It needs to provide the right illumination for cooking and everyday tasks, but it also creates warmth and ambiance when you're entertaining or gathering with family,” Meehan explains. As a rule of thumb, she recommends homeowners allocate at least 8 to 12 percent of their kitchen renovation budget to lighting.

That remarked, anyone who wants to wow with some statement fixtures might wind up spending more. Jay, for example, suggests setting aside $3,000 to $7,000 for decorative wares such as pendant lights above an island.

In a recent development, admittedly, spending thousands of dollars on lighting is bound to elicit some sticker shock, and some people may wonder if it’s worth it. However, Lauren Wodicka insists it’s worth the investment. “Unlike a sofa, rug, or an appliance, lighting doesn’t experience the same daily wear and tear,” the Northern Virginia designer reasons. “A beautiful, well-made fixture can last for generations and become the focal point of a room.”.

It has been revealed that oftentimes, less expensive fixtures can read cheap. “You may notice overly shiny or artificial-looking brass finishes, visible seams, thin metal, or cheaper-looking glass,” Jay states. “Higher-quality fixtures often have more substantial materials, beautiful patinas, and thoughtful details that become more apparent the closer you are.”.

Recent findings suggest that if you want to get the best bang for your buck, devise a shopping strategy. Santa Monica-based designer Christine Markatos Lowe first selects functional lighting and then spends the rest of her budget on “decorative pieces with personality.” But make no mistake, a personable light fixture should still have staying power: Lowe often reaches for timeless silhouettes from retailers like Urban Electric and Ann-Morris Lighting and is a sucker for details like handblown glass or “beautifully finished metals.”.

“The key to finding the right balance between statement lighting and a cohesive kitchen design is to make sure every element feels intentional,” Lowe explains. “It’s all about allowing each material or element to have its moment without overwhelming the space.”Wodicka—who calls Soane, Hudson Valley Lighting, and Visual Comfort her go-tos—claims less is more. “Not every kitchen island needs two or three lighting pendants,” she explains. “Sometimes one beautiful fixture can make a stronger statement.”.

Industry observers note that designers often say lighting is the jewelry of a room, so layering different materials, sizes, and finishes adds more visual intrigue. Wodicka wants kitchen lights to complement each other—and the fixtures in surrounding rooms—but they don’t have to be identical. “A light fixture doesn't necessarily have to match the cabinet hardware or plumbing fixtures,” Jay adds. “I often prefer mixing finishes to create a more collected, layered look.”.

If you want to keep your fixtures cohesive—without identical finishes—stick with a particular style. For example, a sconce with pleated fabric shades will look out of place next to a midcentury-style pendant that looks like it belongs on the set of Mad Men.

For the perfect finishing touch, Meehan recommends putting all lights on dimmers, which is a simple detail that can make a huge difference. “This allows for functional lighting when cooking, but softer light to warm the space when you are entertaining,” she adds. And, if a small detail can make your investment work even harder, even better.

Industry observers note that yemen's Houthis, backed by Iran, said Sunday that they had targeted Rabigh oil refinery in Saudi Arabia, in the latest sign of worsening hostilities in the region, as Iranian forces seek to pressure the U.S. into accepting its terms.

The Houthis mentioned they has directly hit the infrastructure, though the extent of the damage remains unclear, Reuters reported.

The Saudi-led coalition fighting the Houthis said earlier in the day that it had "intercepted and destroyed" a drone launched by the Houthis toward Saudi Arabia's western region, which included some of the country's critical energy refining and export infrastructure.

Rabigh, about 165 kilometers north of Jeddah on the kingdom's western Red Sea coast, hosts Petro Rabigh, a major oil refining and petrochemical complex, developed by Saudi Aramco and Japan's Sumitomo Chemical.

Saudi Arabia's Ministry of Interior did not immediately respond to CNBC's request for comment.

Gulf countries reported multiple attacks by Iran-linked forces over the weekend, even as the country's foreign minister mentioned his government remains open to a negotiated end to the war. Bahrain authorities said an Iranian attack on a civilian industrial facility injured two people on Saturday. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

The string of attacks mark the latest sign of escalation in the regional hostilities since the war between the U.S.-Israel and Iran began in late February, as negotiators have so far failed to agree on terms to end the monthslong war.

The conflict has drawn in countries in the Gulf region, disrupting energy flows through the Strait of Hormuz, which used to carry a fifth of the world's crude shipments prior to the war, pushing up global prices for oil and commodities. The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations reported another incident involving a tanker and military forces in the Gulf of Oman on Sunday, after two tankers were struck by unknown projectiles the day before. The maritime agency gave no further details about the incident, urging vessels to stay vigilant about the operational environment.

It has been revealed that tehran has indicated its intention to end the war with the U.S. through diplomacy, but President Donald Trump has dismissed Iranian proposals. Both sides have been in indirect communication via Qatar.

Sources indicate that in an interview to Fox News on Saturday, Trump warned that he was inclined to order military strikes against Tehran next year, if the two countries failed to finalize an agreement. The president blamed the Iranian officials for stalling while escalating hostilities after the U.S. elections.

"They're more aggressive now than before the election," Trump mentioned, describing Iranian officials as "professional tap dancers." "They talk all the time, all they do is talk," he added.

The president has previously stated that Tehran wants a deal to end hostilities but not one that is "acceptable" to him. He has oscillated between favoring an agreement that would end the fighting and insisting that the U.S. does not need to rush into any deal and is not afraid of resuming more aggressive attacks.

The Iranian Deputy Foreign Minister Kazem Gharibabadi stated Saturday after a two-hour meeting with visiting Qatari mediators that, "We are in contact with mediators, including the Qataris, both in person and virtually, and are holding the necessary discussions to see whether another opportunity can be given to diplomacy," according to Reuters.

International benchmark Brent futures inched 0.22% lower to $92.35 per barrel in early Asia trading hours, while WTI crude was down 0.19% at $83.25 a barrel.

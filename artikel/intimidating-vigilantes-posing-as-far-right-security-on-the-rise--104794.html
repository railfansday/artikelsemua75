In recent months, uniformed figures wearing all black and body armour have been seen in the entourages of far-right figures including the convicted criminal Stephen Yaxley-Lennon, who goes by the name Tommy Robinson, and Paul Golding, the leader of the far-right political party Britain First. It has been revealed that this group of “security volunteers” has also been seen at far-right protests, most recently this week in London and in Oxford, where the controversial “race philosopher” Nathan Cofnas was due to speak. They were seen scuffling with anti-racism protesters. They have also provided security for the rightwing provocateur Katie Hopkins.

The ensemble calls itself British Security UK (BSUK), and claims it is a volunteer-led security group. Those who join it are given all-black uniforms featuring black and white union flags, and they are not required to have any security qualifications or training.

Usually security guards are asked to have a Security Industry Authority (SIA) qualification, meaning they know how to de-escalate situations, only use proportionate force and are trained in keeping members of the public safe.

Sources indicate that the website for the “security service” carries the disclaimer: “BSUK is not the police. Wearing BSUK uniform does not confer police powers. Any security services requiring an SIA licence must be carried out by appropriately licensed personnel. For emergencies, call 999.”.

The registered director of BSUK is Anthony Roberts, 53, who has volunteered as a security guard for Yaxley-Lennon and Golding. He wears combat trousers, a union jack-emblazoned body armour kit, and has facial tattoos.

Posting on social media, he said “the police never handle the left properly … [they] let them run riot” because of “DEI training”. He said his organisation was “there to observe the police do their job, hold them accountable to the very laws we live under, and showcase the two-tier society we all live under”.

He appears to share political views with those his group “protects”. He has posted on social media in favour of “remigration” and described the UK as a “playground for savages” as a result of immigration. Roberts also appears to be a supporter of the hard-right political party Restore UK and has posed for photographs with its leader, Rupert Lowe.

Daniel Garnham, the general secretary of the Security Industry Federation and a police officer for 20 years, remarked he was concerned by the rise of “street vigilantes” disguised as legitimate security officers.

“It is definitely on the rise, I put it in the category of vigilantism and people who are based in rightwing politics that want to make a point and a protest. I don’t see it as legitimate security,” he told the Guardian. “They have security in their name but it is a vigilante group.”.

Roberts has said that his group does not need to be trained or licensed, explaining: “Under UK law (Private Security Industry Act 2001), SIA licences are only required for paid/contracted security work. Genuine unpaid volunteers with an agreement of no payment or reward do not need licences.”.

Garnham disagreed. He argued: “It gives off the wrong message. They call themselves British Security, members of the public could be fooled into thinking they are actual security when they aren’t.”.

Recent findings suggest that he stated he thought the mass of people dressed in black and body armour could intimidate members of the public, adding: “I think the uniform they wear is intimidating, it’s all black, with masks. This organisation shouldn’t be advertising themselves as security and shouldn’t be turning up en masse at these events. It is irresponsible and absolutely reckless.”.

A Home Office spokesperson said: “No one is ever entitled to intimidate or incite disorder. Anyone who breaks the law will rightly face the full force of the law.”.

Sources indicate that rightwing figures have made increasing use of private security in recent years. There is no suggestion any of these security guards do not have the appropriate qualifications and training.

One of Nigel Farage’s security team who accompanied him on the local election campaign trail this year was facing a rape charge and was later jailed for eight years, which raised questions around vetting within Reform UK.

Roberts did not respond to a request for comment, but posted on social media after being approached: “Under the Private Security Industry Act 2001 and current SIA guidance, genuinely unpaid volunteers do not need an SIA licence, provided they have a written or spoken agreement that they will receive no payment or reward for the work.

It has been revealed that “It has come to our attention that sections of the mainstream media are preparing critical coverage of myself and my team. We are accustomed to this. Those of us who seek to contribute positively to our communities and country are frequently subjected to scrutiny that appears more interested in fitting a preferred narrative than in examining the facts.”.

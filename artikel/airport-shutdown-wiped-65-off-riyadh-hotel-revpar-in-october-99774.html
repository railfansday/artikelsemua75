In a recent development, riyadh hotels had one of their worst months of the year in October. A temporary shutdown of the city’s main airport cut off inbound demand just as peak season started.

Occupancy fell 34.7 percentage points year over year to 34.3% from 69%, according to CoStar’s STR. Only March had lower occupancy, at 28.3%. Average daily rate dropped 29.9% to $190 and revenue per available room (RevPAR) fell 65.1% to $65.

“October and November are usually the peak season months for Riyadh, so the impact is much bigger,” noted Kostas Nikolaidis, associate account director for Middle East and Africa at STR.

Industry observers note that the October demand collapse traces back to an attack on King Khalid International Airport. The airport closed after an attack on Oct. 10 that Saudi authorities said killed 13 people and injured more than 300.

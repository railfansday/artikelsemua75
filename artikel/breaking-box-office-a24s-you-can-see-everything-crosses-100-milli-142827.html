📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that “You Can See Everything,” an unconventional documentary about Theranos founder Elizabeth Holmes in exile, has crossed $100 million at the global box office. Its success extends a hot streak for A24, the indie studio behind last summer’s blockbuster “Backrooms” as well as arthouse hits such as “The Drama” and “Primetime.”.

Industry observers note that “You Can See Everything” now ranks as the eighth-highest grossing non-music documentary, moving ahead of “Deep Sea 3D,” and the second-highest grossing non-music and non-nature documentary, behind Michael Moore’s “Fahrenheit 9/11.” It’s unlikely to surpass Moore’s anti-Bush jeremiad, which grossed an astonishing $222.4 million when it opened in the height of a fiercely contested presidential election. According to recent reports, those aren’t the only benchmarks with which to measure “You Can See Everything’s” impact. It is now the seventh-highest grossing film in the history of A24, coming in behind “Materialists,” which earned $105.6 million globally. On a domestic basis, “You Can See Everything” is A24’s third-highest grossing film, ranking just behind “Backrooms” ($197.5 million) and “Marty Supreme” ($96 million). It should easily surpass “Marty Supreme” and that film had Timothée Chalamet and a boatload of Oscar noms behind it. (Related: Deploy Hugging Face models easily with Amazon SageMaker...)

It has been revealed that “You Can See Everything,” which is co-directed by Nathan Fielder and Lance Oppenheim, earned $2.5 million this weekend to bring its domestic haul to $93.5 million after six weeks of release. Most of its earnings have been stateside. It’s right now playing in Australia, the U.K., and Scandinavia and the studio said its international rollout will continue over the next several weeks.

A24 is trying to keep the hits coming with “Club Kid.” Jordan Firstman’s look at a party promoter forced to grow up when he discovers he has a son, grossed $1.4 million on 229 screens in limited release this weekend. It has earned $3.4 million since it debuted three weeks ago. A24 bought the film out of Cannes for $17 million.

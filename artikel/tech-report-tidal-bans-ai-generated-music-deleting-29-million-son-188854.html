The music streaming app Tidal has introduced a major offensive in the fight against AI-generated slop. Starting next month, Tidal will begin removing “wholly AI-generated tracks” from the service, a process which Robert Andersen, the manufacturer’s music lead, stated will encompass deleting 29 million songs already on the app and lead to the rejection of an estimated 80-85,000 AI-slop songs a day afterwards.

AI slop is everywhere, stinking up nearly every inch of creative space like a hotboxed dog fart. At Tidal, the problem had gotten so bad that Andersen noted in the last 90 days, 43 percent of songs submitted to Tidal were AI-generated. “Something more drastic needs to be done,” he wrote in a thread on X.

Sources indicate that tidal says that “wholly AI-generated” tracks are the ones being marked for deletion and future rejection, with many pointing out that “wholly” leaves a lot of wiggle room for songs that make use of AI technology to still get through. But of the big music streaming services, Tidal is the only one to matter a blanket ban on AI-generated songs.

Spotify, Tidal, and Apple Music all have identification systems for determining when a song was created entirely or in part created with the use of AI tools, and all have measures in place to combat spammy uploading. Tidal has also taken the step of totally excluding AI-generated songs from its monetization program while Spotify employs a 1,000-stream threshold songs must hit before any royalties are paid out, one which it notes many AI-generated tracks fail to reach.

Sources indicate that while this move is great for signaling that Tidal values human creativity, Andersen says this is essentially a housekeeping measure. “We believe this will resonate with both artists and listeners alike, as 96% of all AI-generated songs receive zero plays at all,” he wrote.

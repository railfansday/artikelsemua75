According to recent reports, oracle said in a November 20, 2026 statement that it had secured all funding required for its OpenAI infrastructure contract and described itself as the first hyperscaler fully funded for this investment cycle. The announcement followed what Oracle called its final capital raise earlier in November.

The financing outlined at Oracle Investor Day, including vendor financing, is in place. Oracle called any further private discussions with vendors “additional options,” said delivery of the OpenAI contract does not depend on them, and preemptively characterized contrary media reports as incorrect.

Industry observers note that at its October 28, 2026 Investor Day, Oracle committed to limiting additional capital-market funding for its fiscal 2027 through fiscal 2030 investment cycle to $20 billion, excluding refinancing of existing debt. That amount was in addition to a $20 billion equity raise completed in the first quarter of fiscal 2027 through an at-the-market program, under which Oracle could sell shares over time at prevailing market prices. Vendor financing, customer-provided hardware, and customer prepayments were also expected to reduce upfront cash requirements during the transition to positive free cash flow.

According to recent reports, the framework followed Oracle’s February 1, 2026 financing initiative, which targeted $45 billion to $50 billion of gross cash proceeds during calendar 2026. The stated purpose was to build additional Oracle Cloud Infrastructure capacity for contracted demand from its largest customers, including OpenAI. Oracle planned to raise approximately half through equity-linked and common equity issuances and the other half through a single, one-time issuance of investment-grade senior unsecured bonds early in 2026. The Abilene campus, where Astra-6 was trained, is nearly complete. Shackelford, Oracle’s next gigawatt-scale campus, is fully permitted, and the tech giant stated handover of NVIDIA Vera Rubin racks in its data halls was “on track for the coming weeks.”.

Oracle continues to expect positive free cash flow in the second half of fiscal 2028 and is targeting more than $30 billion in fiscal 2029, when it expects growth to become self-funded. Peak capital expenditure was expected in fiscal 2027 and fiscal 2028, followed by a step-down in fiscal 2029.

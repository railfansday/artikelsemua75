CEO of funding agency for UK science and research criticised by union over cuts

A trade union that counts scientists and engineers among its members states it has lost confidence in the leader of the UK’s national funding agency for science and research, citing concerns about plans for major cuts. (Related: Latest Update: Arkansas sues VGW and Kalshi, citing $66...)

The letter from Mike Clancy, the general secretary of Prospect, to Ian Chapman, the chief executive of UK Research and Innovation (UKRI), is the latest development in a row over changes in funding for projects and research facilities.

“Writing to a CEO in this tone and to this intent is an extraordinary step for this union to have to take,” Clancy wrote.

He warned: “Whilst there are legitimate cost pressures facing UKRI, ultimately any cuts will reduce the UK’s national science capability at a time when it is most needed.”.

This year the Science and Technology Facilities Council (STFC), the government’s infrastructure funding agency, which is part of UKRI, revealed it needed to make £162m in savings by 2030. (Related: Industry News: Introducing Azure Catalyst agentic moder...)

While the core budget of the STFC remains broadly flat at about £835m a year, it is under financial pressure as a result of several factors. These include soaring energy costs at its national facilities, increasing staffing costs, an expanded portfolio of activities and “unfavourable movements” in foreign exchange rates. The last has resulted in an increase in costs relating to international facilities such as Cern.

As a result, in July UKRI released a strategy to balance the STFC’s budget by 2030, revealing research projects and national facilities are to face significant changes.

“As responsible custodians of public funds, which I very much view our job is to be, we have to make sure that our costs are in line with our budget and that we don’t overspend the budget available to us,” Chapman remarked at the time, adding that it was important to prioritise spending in areas that made the biggest scientific impact.

The future of the UK’s largest telescope at the Jodrell Bank Observatory in Cheshire has been put at risk, while the Diamond Light Source, the Central Laser Facility and the Isis Neutron and Muon Source, all in Oxfordshire, will remain operational but have been told to make efficiency savings.

The Clara facility in Cheshire – one of the world’s most advanced medium-energy electron beam facilities, which develops and tests next-generation accelerator technologies – is to be mothballed.

Recent findings suggest that clancy wrote in his letter to Chapman: “Our members working at UKRI do vital work which puts our country at the cutting edge of scientific developments and industrial applications and has considerable benefits across high-growth sectors, as well as in medical research and treatment. To pause or ‘mothball’ funding for many of the facilities where they work is to risk permanently losing the expertise that underpins the sector.”.

The opening of a voluntary exit scheme to UKRI employees was also flagged by Clancy, who stated the scheme was released in parallel with the STFC cuts and without the requisite consultation on the organisational change plan with the recognised trade unions. His letter urges a pause to enable proper consultation and evidence-gathering on the cuts’ potential impact.

“In the absence of our requests leading to any productive engagement, we have lost confidence in your position as CEO,” Clancy wrote to Chapman.

Prof Leszek Borysiewicz, the chair of UKRI, said the board had full confidence in Chapman and his leadership team.

Industry observers note that “We support the direction of the ambitious reform programme UKRI is undertaking to become more effective, agile and better able to respond to the country’s priorities and needs,” Borysiewicz mentioned. “Delivering transformation on this scale is a substantial undertaking involving difficult decisions about how UKRI operates, where resources are focused and how we deliver the greatest impact.

“UKRI has been clear with staff and unions about the direction of change, including its upcoming mission and funding model, greater join-up across programmes and the unification of professional services. The decisions unveiled this year followed extensive engagement with staff, research and user communities, alongside detailed consideration of the impacts of different funding choices.” The science minister, Chris McDonald, stated: “I have full confidence in Ian Chapman’s leadership of UKRI. The organisation’s ongoing and essential transformation programme will ensure projects supported through our record £86bn commitment to R&D will deliver maximum impact and value for money – as the British public rightly expect.

Recent findings suggest that “I know from personal experience that this transformation will have a significant and personal impact on workers and others affected, and we will continue to engage the UKRI workforce. I am committed to working closely with UKRI, the workers, and their trade unions as we continue to progress the transformation blueprint.”.

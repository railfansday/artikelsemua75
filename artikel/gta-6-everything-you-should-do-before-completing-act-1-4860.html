After 13 long, long years of waiting, players are finally getting their hands on Grand Theft Auto 6. The opening hours spent exploring the Keys feel almost daunting, as the Herculean size and scope of Jason and Lucia's epic adventure become apparent. It's the type of game you want to pace yourself with, exploring every nook and cranny, appreciating every character, and seeing what Leonida has to offer.

There is, however, at the end of the first chapter, a pretty significant point of no return, and there are a couple of things you'll want to get in order before you progress. We won't tell you which story beats to hit, but we do have a couple of big recommendations for you before moving on.

Minor spoilers for Grand Theft Auto 6's Act 1 below.

Grand Theft Auto 6's first chapter finishes at the yacht party, when Lucia asks Jason if he's ready to take on Mattia's job. Upon accepting, the point of no return will be hit, and you will lose a lot. Any cash you have worked for will be gone, as will Jason's car, and certain stash points aren't safe.

In a recent development, there's no point spending too much money on Jason's car for this reason; it'll be gone after this point. The cash in your wallet and your stash will also disappear; you can save some in your Family Saver bank account, but anything in your standard bank will be lost. This means any big robberies or scores are not worth doing yet; save them for later in the game.

Recent findings suggest that while you lose your car, you won't lose your weapons or clothing. If you have excess cash, make sure to buy weapons, mods, and dress Jason up to the nines. At least your money won't go to waste then. Tattoos and his haircut also remain if you have a few extra bucks left after that.

It has been revealed that following this scene, you won't be returning to the Keys for a little while, so make sure to get all your affairs in order, and that you've done all the exploration you need to do. It seems disheartening, but the game really opens up from here, so don't sweat too much; it's just worth knowing that things take a bit of a turn after this point.

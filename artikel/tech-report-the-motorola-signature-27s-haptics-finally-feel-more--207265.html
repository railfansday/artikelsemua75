Motorola has been content with mid-grade haptics for years, but the Motorola Signature 27’s haptics rightfully upgrade the experience.

According to recent reports, two days ago, I unboxed the Motorola Signature 27 and talked about my experience a bit in my Motorola Signature 27 hands-on. It’s as gorgeous as the pictures make it look, and the texture on the back of my “Capulet Olive” green colorway is the stuff of dreams. But the thing that sealed the new ultra-high-quality design for me wasn’t the comfortable curved edges or the textile back: it was the haptic motors.

Now, don’t get me wrong. The Motorola Signature 27’s build and design are probably my favorite of the year. I’ll need to use it for just a bit longer to be 100% sure, but the pebble-like aesthetic of the curved front, back, and edges feels tangibly amazing to hold and use. It reminds me a bit of the Galaxy S3’s design, just without the nasty, slimy plastic back. It’s curved just enough to feel amazing, but not enough to be an annoyance like some waterfall display phones were in the past. (Related: Tech Report: Easily Build and Share ROCm Kernels with H...)

But a haptic motor is completely intangible to you as the reader. You can’t see it in pictures, and even if I describe it, you may not fully understand why it makes such a difference. If you’ve been using Google Pixel phones for a long time, you’ve probably forgotten how good a quality haptic motor can feel, but let me remind you for a moment just how notable such an invisible thing can be to completing the experience. (Related: Welcome Gemma - Google’s new open LLM...)

In a recent development, typing on the Signature 27 is pure bliss because of it. Tapping each key feels more like the press of a physical keyboard key. Unlocking the phone emits a satisfying light punch to my thumb, signalling a successful read. When I send a reply on X (Twitter), the button tap and subsequent animation feel like the message is being sent through the phone onto the web. It’s wonderful, and it’s one of the reasons people love the Pixel experience so much.

According to recent reports, unfortunately, the Motorola Signature 27’s haptic responses aren’t applied to as many parts of the UI as Pixel phones do, and that’s where the real miss comes in. Adjusting the brightness or volume doesn’t feel like a physically rotating vertical dial the way it does on Pixel, and I’d love to see Motorola add this in an patch. But at least the upgraded haptic engine is a huge step in the right direction for Motorola and a good way to make the Signature 27 feel like a proper flagship phone.

Recent findings suggest that the Archimedes is 109 years into a 133-year trip to Tau Ceti, which makes for a hell of a time to propose turning around. The ship has sustained enough damage, and supplies are running low enough, that some passengers want to take their chances going home. Nobody back there has answered a transmission in decades. Yoshi works in Life Sciences and is rumored to sympathize with the people who want to turn back. Depending on whom Faisal believes, that could give someone a reason to grab him or a reason to discredit him afterward. Adam Oyebanji’s Line of Descent is out now from DAW Books. The 368-page sci-fi thriller arrived November 17 in hardcover and ebook. Oyebanji, the author of Esperance, follows Faisal through the two investigations as senior officers fight over who gets to command the ship. His jurisdiction is a city in deep space, and whoever killed someone in those tanks is presumably still living in it.

It has been revealed that the first lead in Yoshi Graham’s disappearance comes from his own department. Yoshi is a sublieutenant in Life Sciences and a rumored supporter of the movement to turn the Archimedes around. He indicates someone abducted him outside a bar and returned him to his quarters a day later. Before Faisal can establish what happened during those missing hours, he picks up a murder case: Someone has drowned in one of the ship’s reproductive incubators.

Recent findings suggest that faisal works both cases while senior officers compete for command of the damaged vessel. At launch, the Archimedes carried 30,000 people. Five generations later, its residents face a supply shortage with another 24 years to go before their scheduled arrival at Tau Ceti. The faction calling for a return trip has yet to hear from anyone who could receive them, and messages to the Home System have gone unanswered for decades. Oyebanji described the murder as “the lens through which the rest of the story is told.”.

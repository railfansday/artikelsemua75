Ubisoft has laid off 166 people from its offices in Montreal and Quebec City, saying in a statement provided to PC Gamer that its "production needs are changing, requiring us to adjust the size of some teams to ensure our resources are aligned with current production priorities and business needs.".

According to recent reports, founded in 1997, Ubisoft Montreal is one of the biggest game studios in the world, with roughly 4,000 employees as of 2022. Ubisoft Quebec, launched in 2005, is somewhat smaller but still substantial: Its website says the studio has "a team of over 600 creative, passionate, and innovative talents." The cuts reportedly impact employees who could not be assigned elsewhere after their existing projects were cancelled or rescoped.

It has been revealed that whatever the reason, it's bleak: This is the latest round in a series of layoffs that have battered the organization since it confirmed a major restructuring in January, which followed years of struggles including workplace misconduct scandals, persistent difficulties getting games released, and underperformance among significant releases. Ubisoft remarked in today's half-year financial report report that it's reduced its headcount by roughly 1,100 people over the past six months.

Ubisoft did have a significant hit earlier this year with Assassin's Creed Black Flag Resynced, but that wasn't enough to prevent post-launch layoffs at developer Ubisoft Singapore. Ubisoft CEO Yves Guillemot noted today that "Q2 exceeded our expectations, driven by the continued success of Assassin’s Creed Black Flag Resynced and solid contributions from our broader portfolio.".

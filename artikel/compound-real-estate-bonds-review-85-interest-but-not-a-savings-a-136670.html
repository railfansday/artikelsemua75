📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that skip Compound Real Estate Bonds for an emergency fund or any savings you’ll need. Its 8.50% fixed rate comes from unsecured bonds with no FDIC insurance, issued by a young organization whose 2026 annual report raises substantial doubt it can stay in business. Invest only money you can afford to lose.

Sources indicate that the 8.50% rate is nearly twice the 4.40% annual percentage yield (APY) of a top savings account like Pibank Savings, and Compound lets you cash the bonds in on demand. But the bonds are a loan to Compound itself, backed by no property the way real estate notes are and insured by no one, so the extra interest is what you’re paid to risk the money you put in.

Sources indicate that it suits only investors who want a high fixed rate on a small sum they can afford to lose. Keep an emergency fund or other savings in an FDIC-insured high-yield savings account instead.

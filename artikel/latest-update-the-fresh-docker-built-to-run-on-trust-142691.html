Today, Docker is officially launching its newly introduced brand, and I’m excited to tell you what it’s all about and why we think now is the time for change.

In a recent development, let’s start with the major picture. We’re living through a transformative moment: for Docker, for developers, for the industry, and quite possibly, for humanity itself. AI is creating possibilities that would have sounded like science fiction not long ago. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

But it’s also forcing the industry to confront an almost existential question: how do we harness all that power and intelligence without compromising security, governance, or control? Or, put more simply: how do we get to a point where we can actually trust agents? (Related: Industry News: Faster TensorFlow models in Hugging Face...)

That one seemingly simple yet infinitely complex question is why we’re here today, introducing you to a new Docker.

A upcoming Docker, but a familiar story.

Recent findings suggest that ok, not exactly here, but once upon a time, a decade or so ago, the tech industry was at a similar inflection point. Digital transformation was the term du jour, promising a latest way of engaging customers and powering the global economy. There was one big constraint, though: teams needed a better way to build, ship, and run software consistently across any environment.

Enter a tech giant you might have heard of: Docker.

Industry observers note that together with all of you, Docker built the standard trusted by millions of developers. For over a decade, developers have relied on us to make software portable, open, reproducible, and secure. Docker turned the frustration of “Well, it works on my machine!” into the assurance that “Yes, it works everywhere.”.

Today, of course, the challenge confronting us is upcoming and evolving, and we all need to evolve with it. We cannot simply continue to do what we’ve always done and expect to succeed in this new era. That applies to business leaders, developers, tech companies, everyone. It certainly applies to Docker. That’s what this rebrand is about: evolving to meet the moment for our customers.

In a recent development, let me be unequivocal: this is not about leaving Docker’s legacy or heritage behind. We’ve spent more than a decade earning your trust. This is about building on that foundation, not replacing it.

The qualities you value us for aren’t changing. We’re still committed to openness and industry collaboration. We’re still, and always will be, developer-first. We’re still fanatical about simplicity, and about showing up everywhere you need us.

In a recent development, on the product front, the containers you know and love aren’t going anywhere. Containers still play a huge role in the software development lifecycle. As long as that’s the case, Docker will continue leading the way.

Agents have introduced a fresh problem. Containers isolate workloads. But agents aren’t workloads. Agents are actors.

It has been revealed that agents make their own decisions about what to run, what to access, and what to change. Developers and enterprises need solutions built specifically to isolate and govern them, so they can give those agents real autonomy without giving up control.

Docker’s next chapter is about delivering exactly that: security, isolation, and governance, so we can all trust agents to do meaningful work without feeling like we’re always one step from catastrophe.

In case I haven’t made it blindingly obvious yet, the new Docker is all about trust.

Industry observers note that trust Docker because our track record speaks for itself. Trust Docker because we’re proven, neutral, and secure. Trust Docker because it’s pretty darn clear you can’t trust the agents to manage themselves.

In a recent development, trust us because we have a software supply chain that is already the standard for building securely. Trust us because nobody isolates agents where they run better than we do. Trust us because we’re taking a comprehensive approach to improving governance and minimizing risk across the entire enterprise.

Trust Docker because we’re not egotistical enough to think we can solve a challenge this major alone. Trust us because everything about our history, our heritage, and our actions over the past year tells you we’ll partner with the smartest and build alongside the best, and not create vendor lock-in.

Most of all, trust Docker because we’re not just talking the talk. We’ve got the goods to back it up: trusted supply chain for your agents to build from, trusted runtime to isolate your agents, and a trusted ecosystem to ensure all the layers of trust are covered, whether made by Docker or not.

As our product team innovated to meet the moment and our messaging in the market began to shift, one thing became clear: the speed and scale of evolution happening inside Docker called for an equally momentous evolution on the outside. Something that makes it unmistakably clear that it’s a fresh day for Docker. Something that says and shows, loud and clear, for all the world to see, that Docker is not just about containers anymore.

Something, dare I say, whale sized.

I’m talking, of course, about our beloved Moby.

If you know Docker, then you know Moby, and if Docker is evolving, Moby should too. The only question was to how and to what extent? We looked at a lot of whales, folks. (I may or may not have been seeing whales in my sleep.) Ultimately, with the help of our friends at the strategic brand transformation firm, Motto, we landed on a upcoming Moby for the new Docker.

Industry News: Even If Sandisk's Profits Get Cut in Half, Here's What $5,000 in the Stock Could Be Worth by 2030

With an 8-for-1 stock split effective Nov. 19, shares of flash memory maker Sandisk (SNDK +14.22%) closed at about $79 on Nov. 20 on a split-adjusted basis (about $630 in pre-split terms). At that price, $5,000 buys about 63 shares.

Shares hit a record close of about $292 (split-adjusted) on June 25, so they're off about 73% from there.

What could those 63 shares be worth at the end of 2030? My estimate is around $8,800. That'd be a return of about 75%, or almost 15% a year.

Getting there doesn't require flash prices to keep rising. In fact, my estimate assumes Sandisk's earnings in 2030 are around half of what it's earning today. It also assumes investors value those earnings at about the same price-to-earnings multiple they paid for the stock as recently as September.

Sandisk's profits keep climbing, but the growth is slowing every quarter. On a split-adjusted basis, its non-GAAP (adjusted) earnings per share went from around $2.93 in fiscal Q3 of 2026 to $4.91 in fiscal Q4, a 68% sequential leap. For fiscal Q1 of 2027 (the quarter ended Oct. 2, 2026), the number hit $6.19 -- up 26% sequentially and up from around $0.15 a year before. And management's guidance for fiscal Q2 calls for $6.88 to $7.13, which would be about 13% growth at the midpoint.

Sources indicate that that fiscal Q2 midpoint comes to about $28 a share in a full year. At around $79, the stock trades at under 3 times that earnings pace.

In other words, the market seems to expect these earnings to drop a lot.

Sources indicate that investors weren't always so skeptical. When Sandisk closed at around $224 (split-adjusted) on Sept. 18, the stock traded at about 10 times the annualized adjusted earnings per share Sandisk had just guided for fiscal Q1.

Recent findings suggest that chinese cloud companies cutting orders, consumer devices shipping with less memory, and upcoming production capacity coming online could tip the flash memory market into oversupply in 2027, according to a Nov. 18 bulletin from research firm TrendForce. It has been revealed that and Sandisk's results can drop fast when prices turn. Its adjusted earnings per share for all of fiscal 2025, which ended in late June 2025, came to around $0.37 on a split-adjusted basis. That full-year number is less than the company expects to earn in one week this quarter.

Industry observers note that to be fair, Sandisk is a bigger and different business than it was then. Its datacenter revenue hit $4.8 billion in fiscal Q1, versus just $269 million in the year-earlier quarter, and it now makes up over 40% of total revenue. But a business tied so closely to flash prices is unlikely to hold today's earnings through a full downturn.

For fiscal 2030 (which ends in mid-2030), I'll assume adjusted earnings per share of around $14. That number is half today's guided pace -- but still about 58% over the $8.86 a share Sandisk earned in fiscal 2026, after adjusting for the split.

NASDAQ · As of 11:48 AM ET 11/23/26.

According to recent reports, at about 10 times earnings (about what the market paid on guided earnings in September), $14 a share works out to a stock price of about $140 by the end of 2030. Sixty-three shares at $140 would be worth about $8,800. From around $79, the gain is almost 15% a year over about four years.

I'm also keeping the share count flat, which arguably makes the estimate conservative. Sandisk repurchased $5.0 billion of shares in fiscal Q1 alone, and any more buybacks might raise earnings per share above my assumption.

Industry observers note that then again, the estimate may also be too high. If fiscal 2030 earnings came in at a quarter of today's pace instead (around $7 a share), the same 10 times earnings would put the stock at about $70. The $5,000 would drop to about $4,400.

In a recent development, in the end, I think about $8,800 is a fair estimate for $5,000 invested in Sandisk today, if the next flash downturn is over by 2030. That's a solid return, and it doesn't rely on the boom lasting.

Industry observers note that with the stock seemingly priced for a deeper slump than the one I'm assuming, I think shares are worth a look at around $79. Given how broad the range of outcomes is, I'd limit them to a small slice of a diversified portfolio.

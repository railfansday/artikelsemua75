On Monday, XRP briefly rose above $1 after the return of Jensen Huang to Nvidia sparked a rally on Wall Street and a wave of short liquidations in the crypto market. At the peak of the move, short liquidations on XRP exceeded long liquidations by 578%, according to CoinGlass data. (Related: Christmas market forced to cancel after backlash over A...)

On the XRPUSDT perpetual contract on Binance, the price of XRP went from $0.94 to as high as $1.02 within minutes after 14:00 UTC, and later it pulled back and stood at $0.984, according to TradingView.

Earlier on Monday, before the opening bell, Nvidia confirmed that its board voted unanimously to reinstate Huang as president, chief executive and board member, effective immediately. The tech giant said that the board was satisfied with Huang's "candor and ability to lead" after a series of meetings with him, and that its independent investigation would continue. According to the company, the board received input from senior U.S. government officials and concluded that Huang's reinstatement "poses no national security risk.". "The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers," Nvidia board chair Harvey Jones remarked in a statement.

Industry observers note that huang resigned as CEO on Nov. 12, and on Nov. 17, during the tech giant's earnings call, Jones said that the board "does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.".

In premarket trading, Nvidia stock jumped 27% to $159.48 as of 9 a.m. ET, and at the start of U.S. trading on Monday, Nvidia shares were up more than 30%. The rally spread across Wall Street, and the S&P 500 rose as much as 5.6% and the Nasdaq 100 as much as 7.9%, according to NBC News.

CoinGlass data shows that $9.45 million worth of XRP positions were liquidated over the past 24 hours, and $7.18 million of that amount came from short positions. Long positions accounted for $2.27 million.

Recent findings suggest that the same happened across the rest of the crypto market. In total, 110,663 traders were liquidated for $660.89 million over the past 24 hours, according to CoinGlass, and shorts accounted for $515.94 million of that amount.

Industry observers note that bitcoin led the list with $273.26 million in liquidations, and 82.8% of that amount came from short positions. Ethereum followed with $139.07 million, and Solana was third with $24.93 million.

Besides, perpetual contracts on Nvidia stock and on SOXL, a triple-leveraged exchange-traded fund on semiconductor stocks, were next on the list with $16.34 million and $15.01 million in liquidations, respectively, according to CoinGlass.

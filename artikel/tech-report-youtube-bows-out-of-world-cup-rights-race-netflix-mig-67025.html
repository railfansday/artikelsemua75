📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, youTube was an official “Preferred Platform” for the 2026 FIFA World Cup, and it worked to make the partnership a high scorer. It tapped creators to film promo content, then brought them pitchside to film dozens of videos at the tournament’s top matches around the world.

It also exercised the broadcast rights that came along with being a Preferred Platform: By the time Spain took it all home, YouTube’s World Cup content had drawn nearly 2 billion unique viewers, 550 million of whom tuned in from their TV sets and bumped YouTube’s Nielsen numbers. According to Atlantic sports columnist Andrew Marchand, YouTube declined to submit a bid for American broadcast rights to the 2030 and 2034 World Cups.

There were rumors that YouTube planned to go out for rights, but a source familiar with the matter confirmed to Marchand that YouTube is not tossing its hat in the ring.

Industry observers note that it’s not alone: Apple, ESPN, NBC, and the newly formed Skydance have also declined to bid. Between their various subsidiaries, that knocks out chances of the World Cups airing on CBS, TNT Sports, Paramount+, HBO Max, and Telemundo.

So who does want the rights? Marchand reports Fox–which had the rights for 2026–is going out for them. But, perhaps surprisingly, the other top frontrunner seems to be Netflix.

Or maybe it’s not so surprising–Netflix already has rights to the 2027 Women’s World Cup, and we know it’s willing to pay big for exclusive sporting events, so maybe the men’s Cup is exactly the complement it’s looking for.

Industry observers note that we don’t know what the winner will end up paying here, but Fox paid $485 million to air the 2026 tourney in the U.S. Netflix did say it expects to close out the year spending money on developing original content and investing in sports (and now it has freer cash considering its failed bid to buy Warner Bros. Discovery). The World Cup just might be one of those investments.

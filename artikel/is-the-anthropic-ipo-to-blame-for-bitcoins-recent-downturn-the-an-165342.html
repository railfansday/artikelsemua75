According to recent reports, anthropic's (ANT -1.43%) much-anticipated blockbuster initial public offering (IPO) will go down as one of the highlights of the 2026 financial calendar after it raised a record $119.5 billion and surged 17% on its first day of trading. That stated, it subsequently erased most of those gains as tech stocks tumbled even further. Bitcoin (BTC -1.54%) fell to a two-year low of $55,150 on Nov. 21.

CRYPTO · As of 7:01 AM ET 11/24/26.

There's plenty to be noted about Anthropic's historic IPO, but while various crypto industry leaders were quick to pin the blame for Bitcoin's latest price plunge on the artificial intelligence (AI) giant, investor money moving to Anthropic is a small part of a much bigger picture. Industry observers note that as I write this (Nov. 23), Bitcoin has tumbled more than 25% in the past month. It's certainly true that the IPO soaked up some capital, but bigger factors are pressuring cryptocurrencies. (Related: Industry News: Welcome spaCy to the Hugging Face Hub...)

Recent findings suggest that bear in mind that the S&P 500 and the Nasdaq Composite have fallen by 5.6% and 7.7%, respectively, in the past month and that Bitcoin's tumble started in October -- before both Nvidia's (NVDA +31.48%) woes and Anthropic's IPO. Investors are losing confidence across the board. Some concerns are unique to crypto, but this is not only a Bitcoin problem.

Sources indicate that conerns about stricter regulation after the midterms, combined with higher Treasury yields and the strong possibility that the Federal Reserve will hike interest rates this year, all weigh heavily. Risk appetite wanes when investors can earn decent returns on safer assets. The $6.3 billion Binance hack at the end of October also undermined confidence in crypto, and leverage multiplied the fallout.

Industry observers note that the AI sell-off shook Wall Street last week, making further volatility likely -- Bitcoin may fall even more. The bigger question is what might bring about a recovery and how this latest drop effects Bitcoin's long-term potential. For example, one strong argument for Bitcoin bulls is that it can act as a portfolio diversifier, and that still stands.

Stubborn inflation, rising national debt, and ongoing geopolitical tensions all have the potential to weaken confidence in traditional currencies. As such, even though Bitcoin hasn't yet proven itself as a form of digital gold, it could still benefit from a flight to alternative assets. If a significant number of investors allocated just 1% or 2% of their portfolios to Bitcoin, that would drive considerable growth.

Another is further mainstream blockchain adoption, including stablecoin usage, and growth in decentralized finance (DeFi). Bitcoin is the crypto leader, and as other cryptos grow, that helps Bitcoin. Bitcoin's Layer-2 solutions (blockchains that are built on top of the Bitcoin chain to improve performance) enhance its position as a secure settlement layer.

Anthropic isn't to blame for Bitcoin's recent downturn, and it won't help it recover either. Instead, I'll be watching for regulatory moves that might galvanize the crypto industry, as well as any growth in stablecoin adoption. Institutional investment levels are a useful metric to track, too -- there's almost $81 billion in spot Bitcoin exchange-traded funds (ETFs) right now. If that starts to increase again, it is a sign that Bitcoin is regaining traction as a portfolio diversifier.

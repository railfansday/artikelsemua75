The legendary Irish rock band, as they’ve done so often in the past, stepped into the cauldron, rocked out, and gave everyone what they were looking for.

On this occasion, Bono, The Edge, Adam Clayton and Larry Mullen Jr. stepped into the colosseum that is the NFL‘s first-ever Thanksgiving Eve game at SoFi Stadium, pitting the Los Angeles Rams against the visiting Green Bay Packers. Explore Bono U2 See latest videos, charts and news.

The Rock Hall-inducted group headlined the halftime show, which streamed live late Wednesday, Nov. 25 on Netflix. At the very top, the four bandmates took the long walk to the centerfield as a choir recited the lyrics of “I Still Haven’t Found What I’m Looking For.”.

It has been revealed that “Thank you for having us,” Bono remarked over Mullen’s iconic, military-style drum pattern that released the mini-set proper with “Sunday Bloody Sunday,” one of the band’s early political anthems. The production stepped up several gears as a marching band assembled and the song shapeshifted, interpolating The Supremes’ “Stop! In The Name of Love.”.

Then, a change in mood as U2 paid tribute to Dolly Parton, the late queen of country’s smiling image splashed on three sails towering over the circular, center stage. Bono dueted with Dolly’s vocals for “Torn,” her very last recording which appears on the band’s 16th and latest studio album, Carnaval de Luz.

And, in a moment that earned a roar from the audience, the band slotted seamlessly into another Joshua Tree number, “Where The Streets Have No Name.” The sails came down, and U2 got down to rockstar business — with some support from scores of backing singers.

In a recent development, without missing a beat, Bono and Co. reached into their glorybox with a rendition of “Pride (In The Name of Love)” a tribute to the assassinated civil rights leader Dr. Martin Luther King Jr., which appeared on their 1984 LP The Unforgettable Fire.

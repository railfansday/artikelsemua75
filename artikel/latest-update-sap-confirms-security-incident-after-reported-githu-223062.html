SAP has confirmed a security incident after it spotted suspicious activity in its systems.

In a statement issued on Thursday evening, the German software company said that, so far, it has found no sign that anyone got into customer business data.

Der Spiegel first reported the incident on Thursday, describing it as a hacking attack, and according to an insider who spoke to the magazine, attackers stole access tokens from SAP employees. (Related: Show, Don't Certify: How to Prepare for Your First Data...)

The attackers then used the tokens to copy and change SAP source code on GitHub, the insider noted. Citing internal sources, Der Spiegel added that the attackers may have used agentic AI, meaning AI tools that can carry out tasks on their own. (Related: Find Orphan Pages and Buried Content: Build an Internal...)

Sources indicate that according to the magazine, SAP is treating it internally as an active security incident under the codename Project Granite. The manufacturer has not confirmed the token theft or the changes to its code.

“SAP has identified suspicious activity and promptly implemented the necessary measures to protect its customers,” the organization stated.

However, SAP did not say what kind of activity it found, when it was discovered or which parts of the business were affected, the German news agency dpa reported.

The incident came to light on the same day that reports said SAP is weighing at least 10,000 job cuts as it reorganises around AI.

According to recent reports, attackers have also gone after SAP’s developer tools earlier this year. In April, criminals hijacked four SAP packages on npm, a large public store of JavaScript code, and used them to spread malware that steals passwords and keys.

Recent findings suggest that the bad versions were online for only a few hours, but the packages are downloaded more than 550,000 times a week, according to Heise. Security researchers linked that attack to a group called TeamPCP.

Meanwhile, Germany’s cybersecurity agency, the BSI, issued a public warning on Thursday about AI attack tools that can act on their own, naming ARTEX, CAIRN and PentAGI.

Meanwhile, in the US, an AI hacking tool is also believed to have been used against several spy agencies in a breach that came to light this week.

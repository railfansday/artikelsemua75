Watford were thrashed by Sheffield United on Saturday, putting Alessio Dionisi under latest pressure. Now, it has been confirmed that the head coach has been relieved of his duties.

Industry observers note that the Hornets had shown a desire to stick by Dionisi, as opposed to their past managerial hire-and-fire approach. But, sitting 21st in the Championship table and after an incredibly defeated post-match press conference on Saturday, change awaits. Here, we put forward three bosses who Watford should consider in their next head coach hunt….

In a recent development, watford desperately need a boss who can galvanise this squad. There are talented players like Othmane Maamma, Iker Bravo and Amin Nabizada, but those forwards can only be given freedom to express themselves if the manager instils confidence in the entire team.

Bo Henriksen would do just that. In his managerial career to date, the 51-year-old’s best attribute has been his man management. He encourages his skilful players to express themselves on the ball and leads with emotion and passion, prioritising a unified group over unique and complicated tactics.

In a recent development, he’ll demand intensity in pressing and quick transitions, but what Henriksen could do to this group is where he stands out. The Dane is available after his exit from Mainz in December 2025 and previously succeeded with Bronshoj BK, AC Horsens, FC Midtjylland and FC Zurich.

This may be a slightly hopeful suggestion. However, there is a reason it could work.

It has been revealed that michael O’Neill is right now in charge of the Northern Ireland national side, but he split his role with a stint as Blackburn Rovers boss last season. During that spell, he managed to lead the Lancashire outfit to Championship safety, and given that there is no international football until March, Watford should see if he is open to a similar dual-responsibility position.

The 57-year-old’s teams are well organised and his experience in the dugout means he’s happy to be flexible with his setup too. Like Henriksen, he has been praised for his work as a man manager and could lead a much-needed turnaround at Watford.

Recent findings suggest that his Championship track record is solid too. If he can be swayed back into a club role, O’Neill would be a great option.

It has been revealed that admittedly, this feels straight out of the Pozzo playbook, but Fabio Grosso could be an intriguing option for Watford to consider. The 48-year-old was relieved of his Fiorentina duties after just four games, but he was a hit at Sassuolo and has a good record in Serie B, having also won promotion with Frosinone.

Sources indicate that grosso took them back to Serie A and they finished 11th in their first season back. He likes his team to play vertically in possession, getting the ball forward promptly, and to be compact defensively.

He’s another motivational figure who can breathe life into the Watford squad. Grosso could be tempted by the chance to manage in England, and his profile in Italy could see them attract some good players outside of the Pozzo ownership.

“You are looking a little chunky!” My father made this offhand comment when I was a sophomore in college. His words stung. Like many, I had gained the “freshman fifteen” and was desperately hoping no one would notice. But he did.

Sources indicate that that memory is so vivid that I can see exactly where I was standing, in my dorm room next to the dresser, when he spoke those words. Coming from a man who valued fitness, I knew exactly what he meant. Yes, I was devastated by his comment about my body, but underneath, I was scared that he would be embarrassed by me or, even worse, that he would love me less. Would he still be proud of me if I gained more weight? I told no one what he noted.

Bodies change over time. Bodies in their twenties are not the same as bodies in their thirties, forties, or beyond. Four key factors can potentially ignite body image issues for individuals or couples.

I call them the five arrows because they can pierce a relationship at any time and powerfully affect body image. They are aging, health conditions, medications, pregnancy, and words. The fifth arrow, words, critical comments from others or negative thoughts about oneself, is perhaps the most lethal because it can pierce not only the body, but the mind and the soul.

Sources indicate that not everyone will experience critical comments from others, but most of us can remember something said about our body that did not feel good. Critical comments include bullying at school or work, teasing, comments by friends, discrimination such as weightism, or repeated criticism by parents, siblings, or other relatives.

Negative comments about self can cause self-consciousness when around others, self-hate, avoidance of social situations, eating disorders, distorted body image, avoidance of sexual intimacy, depression, anxiety, and at worst, suicidal ideation.

When we enter a relationship, we take our bodies with us; and with the body comes all the history, feelings, and insecurities about the physical self since childhood.

I told no one about her comment. I had never thought about my nose before that, and from that day onward, I felt self-conscious. I wish I had had a safe place to share her words. The “arrow” of words could have been deflected by perspective and reassurance I could not give myself. We can find relationships that shield us from the emotional consequences of arrows that can negatively affect body image. The shield should provide love, perspective, empathy, and reassurance—not problem-solving. This can be a love relationship or friendship. Choose wisely.

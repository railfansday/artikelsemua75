The Associated Press is Moving Eight US Photo Editor Jobs to India

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Associated Press is moving eight photo editor positions to India today, in a move condemned by unions.

Sources indicate that the roles are presently carried out in the United States, but today is their last day. The offshoring follows similar moves this summer, when AP laid off 20 video production jobs in the U.S. and moved them to its hub in Noida, India.

According to recent reports, “AP is at it again,” the AP News Guild wrote on X last week. “The News Media Guild learned Monday that AP will offshore eight photo positions after already gutting its U.S. video production team.”.

“Fewer people, fewer communities being covered, and less news. That’s the impact of today’s decision,” claims Krusei. “AP’s newsgathering has gone beyond ‘lean’ to leaving many coverage areas abandoned. The remaining journalists are asked to do the impossible and it reveals.”. (Related: The one thing Putin, Trump and Xi agreed on — fewer lim...)

The Guild says that far from just picking photos taken by AP photographers, the editors provide direction, guide the photographers, correct essential information, and scrutinize images for alterations.

Recent findings suggest that “They bring decades of local knowledge, news judgment and connections. They negotiate with outside photographers to buy the rights to photos — a now essential part of covering breaking news.”.

One of the affected photo editors, Pablo Salinas, who is based in New York, says that he went above and beyond in 2019 when he obtained the rights to a shocking photo of a drowned father and daughter lying face down in the Rio Grande River.

“That was a picture that I don’t think any other photo editor would have been able to get,” notes Salinas. “Because it wasn’t ours, I scoured social media and found it and got in touch with the photographer. And she reluctantly gave us permission to use it.”.

The photo went viral and became part of the story about the border crisis.

“AP benefits from having photo editors that are local to where the news is being produced,” says AP photographer Matt Rourke.

Recent findings suggest that “It’s a partnership,” adds Salinas. “The kind of partnership you lose when you sacrifice these jobs to save a few dollars.”.

According to recent reports, photo editors are intimately involved in the biggest stories of the day. They might be working closely with local photographers to move hundreds of shots illustrating crucial election results. Or securing the rights to sensitive images that convey difficult news. An AP spokesperson tells TheWrap that the agency is respecting and following the collective bargaining agreement.

“This is part of the changes we revealed over the summer to significantly scale our visual production capabilities at our fresh hub in Noida, India, where we have added dozens of talented journalists to our global news organization,” the spokesperson says.

The AP states that its hub in Noida will soon employ nearly 100 video producers and photo editors.

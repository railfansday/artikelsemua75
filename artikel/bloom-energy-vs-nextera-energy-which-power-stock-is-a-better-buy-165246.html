Recent findings suggest that the race for energy solutions is intensifying as data centers demand more power. Should you consider adding Bloom Energy (BE +18.09%) or the utility giant NextEra Energy (NEE +0.62%) to your portfolio?

Industry observers note that bloom Energy provides innovative fuel cell technology for onsite power, focusing on high-growth sectors like artificial intelligence (AI). NextEra Energy operates as a regulated utility while leading the world in wind and solar production. Both companies offer exposure to different energy solutions, but they cater to very different investor profiles seeking either rapid growth or stability. It has been revealed that bloom Energy designs and manufactures solid oxide fuel cell systems that provide onsite electricity and hydrogen. Its technology serves customers in sectors like data centers, semiconductor manufacturing, and large utilities. The company has secured high-profile partnerships with Oracle (ORCL +12.34%) for AI data center power and American Electric Power (AEP +1.64%) for large-scale fuel cell capacity. It also works with Brookfield (BN +3.63%) on financing frameworks and California Resources (CRC +0.30%) for carbon-capture projects. Sources indicate that in the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue reached nearly $3.8 billion, representing growth of approximately 110% compared to the prior twelve-month period. This significant increase follows a trend of rising revenue as the organization scales its fuel cell installations globally. Net income for the period was approximately $518 million, with a net margin of approximately 13.5%, marking a notable shift compared to net losses reported in previous fiscal years.

Sources indicate that nextEra Energy operates two primary business segments: Florida Power & Light and NextEra Energy Resources. As a prominent player among electric utility stocks, NextEra Energy benefits from a stable, regulated environment in its home state. NextEra Energy Resources focuses on contracted energy generation and battery storage, serving other utilities and commercial customers across North America through extensive long-term power sales agreements.

It has been revealed that in the 12 months ended Sept. 30, 2026, revenue was nearly $28.8 billion, which is growth of roughly 9.6% over the previous period. This steady growth reflects the consistent demand for electricity in its Florida service area and the expansion of its renewable energy portfolio. Net income for the period reached approximately $11.6 billion, resulting in a net margin of roughly 40.4%, demonstrating the high profitability of its energy infrastructure operations.

Bloom Energy faces significant risks related to the emerging market for hydrogen and fuel cell power, which may not reach expected demand levels. The manufacturer deals with long sales and installation cycles, especially for its AI data center clients, and depends on fluctuating government tax incentives. It also faces intense competition and is currently involved in class action lawsuits concerning securities fraud and its sourcing of certain materials.

In a recent development, nextEra Energy faces substantial regulatory risks as an electric utility, as state authorities can disallow the recovery of certain costs. Its Florida operations are vulnerable to severe weather events like hurricanes, while its nuclear facilities involve risks related to decommissioning and waste storage. The manufacturer is also navigating litigation related to political controversies and risks associated with major acquisitions, such as the planned purchase of Dominion Energy (D +0.77%).

NextEra Energy is cheaper than Bloom Energy based on its forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio and its price-to-sales (P/S) ratio.

Both investments could fit together in a portfolio, but as individual stock picks, Bloom is better suited to those with a higher risk tolerance seeking stock price appreciation. It's rapidly increasing its revenue, jumping from $519 million in the third quarter of 2025 to $1.2 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Bloom's story is also becoming one of profitability, as it reported a net profit of $250.2 million in the third quarter of 2026 compared to a net loss of $23 million in the prior-year period. While that's promising for Bloom's long-term outlook, the challenge will be that, if AI demand slows, it will be a difficult revenue gap to fill.

In comparison, NextEra Energy is more about stability, operating Florida Power & Light. Shares are up only 4.6% this year, but the company also pays a dividend that yields nearly 3%, helping boost total returns. That said, it also has some interesting opportunities in nuclear energy and natural gas, and its potential acquisition of Dominion would give it a massive presence throughout Virginia, North Carolina, and South Carolina.

Again, I still see room for both in a portfolio, so long as an investor knows the purpose each serves.

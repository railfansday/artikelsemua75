Less than half of all Bitcoin [BTC] is green right now, and that’s not a good look. In fact, as it turns out, we may not see light anytime soon.

As of 18th November, those are the numbers. At least according to the Rand Group. (Related: Anthropic gave $40M to an AI safety group. Most of the ...)

According to recent reports, every major BTC bottom since 2015 has happened with less than half the supply in the green. In August 2025, that figure was 35.7%. It hit 39.8% in December 2018, 42.5% in March 2020, and 44.8% in November 2022. (Related: Industry News: One Year Since the “DeepSeek Moment”...)

This June, it was as low as 46.9%.

Each low has come in a little higher than the one before. Holders are giving up less ground with every cycle. The argument is that the best opportunities show up when most holders are underwater, not when everyone’s celebrating.

It has been revealed that hence, the question: Does that mean the bottom is in? No, But Bitcoin is in a zone where that has mattered before.

… might be tech stocks and rates, according to QCP Capital.

According to recent reports, not everyone’s ready to call it a bottom, yet. In fact, the digital asset partner thinks Bitcoin’s problem is about money getting tight everywhere.

According to recent reports, earlier this cycle, ETF buying, corporate treasuries, and hopes of rate cuts did a good job of soaking up selling. That support will crack when stocks drop across the board.

So, what would it take to turn things around?

Its report cited three things, namely,.

…recovery will likely require stabilisation in technology equities, reduced volatility in rates and evidence that institutional flows are returning.

Industry observers note that until then, any bounce driven by shorts closing out could fizzle fast. The Fed could also be another weight on BTC’s back.

Recent findings suggest that at the time of writing, the charts weren’t really comforting either. BTC was trading at $58,681, down 5.2% over the last 24 hours. Just a couple of weeks ago, it was trading above $80K.

According to recent reports, the RSI was knee deep in oversold territory, its lowest reading in months. Sellers have been running out fuel, but oversold conditions can stay as such for a while.

The ADX was 43.8 and still rising. So, the downtrend seemed to be getting stronger. Especially since buyers didn’t look like they were going to turn up.

In a recent development, this is a bottom range that we know. However, until the selling pressure slows down, calling it the floor would be a gamble.

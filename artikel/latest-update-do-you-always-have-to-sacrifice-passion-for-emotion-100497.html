Recent findings suggest that now, your relationship looks a little different. There are no mental gymnastics before grabbing your partner’s hand and you don’t mind putting your clothes away nicely before jumping into bed. Recent findings suggest that “The comfort you worked so hard to build may be what’s subtly killing your desire for your partner,” certified sex therapist Dr. Mindy DeSeta, PhD tells Cosmopolitan.

She explains that over time, we unknowingly become complacent about romancing our partner. All of the exciting unknowns of the beginning of a relationship (Do they want to kiss you? Do they want to come home with you?) eventually fade away.

“You may be intimately familiar with your partner's moves after a number of years, and that's a beautiful thing,” Dr. DeSeta claims. “But repetition can dull the excitement of physical intimacy.”.

But contrary to what we’ve all learned to accept, this isn’t an inevitable diagnosis for your long-term relationship. It is possible to have both emotional intimacy and sexual passion. You just have to work for it.

Industry observers note that “Your sex life will suffer if you do not actively continue to learn your partner’s desires,” Dr. DeSeta notes.

Sources indicate that you might think that you know everything there is to know already about your partner. But as we get older, our desires change—even if our partner doesn’t.

Industry observers note that “Your pleasure is constantly evolving and if you want to continue to have a thriving and exciting sex life, a couple must be able to talk about most recent desires,” she says. “What turned you on at 25 may not still turn you on at 40.”.

According to recent reports, this is why communicating your desires and feelings to your partner is so vital. Sure, you and your partner may be pros at working through disagreements and emotionally supporting each other through life’s changes—but does that energy translate into your sex life? Do you share your curiosities? Do you hold space for them telling you they’d like to try something different? If you don’t, it might be time to make a change.

In a recent development, first, in order to feel sexy with your partner, you need to feel sexy yourself. Do something every day that makes you feel sexy and confident,” Dr. DeSeta indicates. “The foundation of a great sex life starts with each person having a thriving libido.”.

Recent findings suggest that then try to initiate sexy conversation with your partner. Dr. DeSeta suggests planning something that feels tantalizing without the expectation of physical intimacy to follow. Reach each other erotica, talk about new fantasies you’ve had, discuss a trendy sex topic in media.

Sources indicate that “Many long-term couples need a prompt to get the sexy conversations started,” she notes. They also need reminders to touch each other more.

In a recent development, “Long term couples are notorious for forgetting physical affection,” she says. Dr. DeSeta emphasizes small touches every day. This can look like caressing their back as you pass them in the kitchen or holding hands while you watch your favorite show on the couch. Instead of maneuvering around each other, lean into the touch. It signals to your partner that you see them and want them throughout the day. And just the feeling of being wanted can ignite some of that passion from the early days of your relationship.

Recent findings suggest that most importantly, you should date your partner every day. Dr. DeSeta recommends “Daily Dates” to couples she sees in therapy. This is when you set aside 20 minutes everyday to make your partner a priority.

“All too often long-term couples put their relationship second, so this tune up is always needed,” she notes. “During these 20 minutes the phones are silenced, logistical conversations are off the table, and the time is dedicated to emotionally connecting with their partner.”.

In a recent development, by doing this, she explains that you slow down the daily hustle and really learn how to reconnect with your partner. You might even learn a few newly introduced things about them in the process.

“If we do not make the intentional effort to keep the passion alive, then you are at risk of falling into autopilot,” Dr.Deseta says. We often think passion and emotional intimacy are at odds with each other, but that’s because we think it’s acceptable to simply fall into our relationship without making an active effort to keep our relationship exciting. And no, you don’t have to be constantly ripping each other’s clothes off or feel nervous in order to have a passionate relationship. But you should aim to feel the same deep desire and attraction to your partner that you did when you first started off—if not more.

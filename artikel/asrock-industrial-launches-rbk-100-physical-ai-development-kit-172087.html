ASRock Industrial announced the RBK-100 Robotics-in-a-Box on November 24, 2026, an integrated Physical AI development platform combining an Intel-based edge computer, an RGB-D camera, paired robotic arms and pre-installed software for researchers, educators and developers.

ASRock Industrial designed the kit to reduce the integration work involved in robotic AI development. Its workflow runs from demonstration recording and dataset review through model training and robot-skill execution, with the aim of letting consumers focus on model experimentation and learned manipulation tasks. It has been revealed that ASRock Industrial developed the platform in collaboration with Intel. John Healy, vice president and general manager of Intel’s Industrial and Robotics Division, described the design requirement this way: “Scaling Physical AI requires more than powerful AI. It requires an open technology foundation that makes complex robotic systems easier to develop and deploy.”. (Related: Scaling-up BERT Inference on CPU (Part 1)...)

The computing platform is the ASRock Industrial NUC BOX-358H, equipped with an Intel Core Ultra X7 Processor 358H for local AI processing. The standard configuration has 32GB of DDR5-5600 memory and the Advanced configuration has 64GB; both listed versions include a 512GB M.2 2280 PCIe Gen4 solid-state drive.

An RGB-D camera captures color and depth information for spatial perception and object interaction. The six-degrees-of-freedom leader-and-follower robotic arm set supports demonstration recording and experiments with pick-and-place, object handling and learned robotic skills.

In a recent development, the official product page also lists a four-port USB hub with auxiliary 5V power input, robot and camera mounting accessories, and six play cubes for manipulation. It labels the RBK-100 as preliminary and warns that specifications are subject to change without notice, so the announced component list is not a frozen final configuration.

Pre-installed AiRoboStarter provides the standard development environment, covering teleoperation, demonstration recording, data collection, dataset review, AI model training and robot-skill execution. ASRock Industrial lists ACT and Pi0.5 as supported model workflows and plans SmolVLA support for a future release.

AiRoboStarter Advanced accompanies the 64GB configuration and adds an optional agentic AI workflow. It connects voice interaction, AI reasoning, visual perception and trained robotic skills, with listed capabilities spanning speech-to-text, text-to-speech, large language models, object detection, web search, custom APIs and robot-skill integration. ASRock Industrial indicates supported models, features and performance depend on the software release and validated configuration.

The standard package centers on learning manipulation skills from demonstrations, while Advanced extends that environment toward multimodal interactions that join spoken commands and visual input to robotic actions.

Sources indicate that the result is a development environment that connects physical demonstrations, model training and execution on the arm set in one package. For manipulation research and education, that integrated path is the RBK-100’s central contribution; its practical scope remains tied to the final hardware specification and the validated AiRoboStarter software configurations.

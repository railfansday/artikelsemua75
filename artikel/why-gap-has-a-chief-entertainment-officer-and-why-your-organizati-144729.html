Why Gap has a chief entertainment officer, and why your organization might need one too

On a warm October Saturday on Long Island, hundreds of teens packed two floors of Garden City’s Roosevelt Field mall. They were screaming, phones raised, for a boy band whose sweatshirts seemed at first glance to display the familiar GAP logo—except it spelled out J-Y-T.

Sources indicate that this was more than product placement. JYT, short for Just Your Type, was created by longtime music producer Freddy Wexler. He pitched the group to Gap, arguing that Caden (18), Bryan (18), Tyler (17), Toby (17), and Recker (16) offered the brand the chance to build affinity with a young fan base via a capsule collection, a docuseries, and appearances at stores. “He had me at ‘mall tour,’” recalls Gap’s Pam Kaufman.

Kaufman’s title at Gap Inc. is a newly introduced one for corporate America: chief entertainment officer. In today’s fractured media market, brands are discovering that they have to do more than just collaborate with good content—they must become the content. “The most valuable real estate in culture is not a shelf, an ad, a storefront, or an Instagram post,” Kaufman says. “It’s attention.” With competition for customers’ time just a scroll away, the only way to guarantee eyeballs (and wallets) is to make something they can’t look away from. That’s Kaufman’s mandate in this new C-suite role, one other brands may soon be casting for themselves. (Related: Latest Update: How to train a newly introduced language...)

Sources indicate that the chief entertainment officer role was dreamed up by Gap Inc. CEO Richard Dickson, who joined the $15-billion-in-revenue parent of Old Navy, Banana Republic, Athleta, and the Gap apparel chain in 2023 from Mattel. There, he had turned the Barbie doll into a global brand and Oscar-winning blockbuster.

Recent findings suggest that that blend of culture and product became “fashiontainment” at Gap. He hired designer and Project Runway judge Zac Posen as creative director and enlisted Hailey Bieber for a Gen Z–minded collab. With Gap’s stock up more than 120% since 2023, Dickson determined it was time for phase two.

According to recent reports, enter Kaufman. What Dickson’s team at Mattel did for Barbie, Kaufman had done in reverse for SpongeBob. She spent more than 25 years at Paramount Global, where she brought Nickelodeon’s chipper cartoon sponge beyond the small screen to movie theaters, Burger King, and socks and T-shirts at Hot Topic.

Kaufman’s first external step at Gap was meeting with Hollywood studios. “We wanted them to know that we are going to be a really exciting player in this space,” she says.

Sources indicate that brannan Bear, Baby Gap’s teddy-bear mascot, could inspire his own TV show or video game. Banana Republic has already produced a five-part scripted micro-drama featuring a star of Hulu’s Adults—basically an ad with a storyline and available on TikTok, Instagram, and YouTube Shorts.

Industry observers note that gap can license other brands to sell themed apparel. Your local Old Navy could be an immersive destination for a latest family-friendly movie or a trick-or-treating stop on Halloween. “Fourth of July is when you buy your flag T-shirt at Old Navy,” Kaufman says, “and how do we amplify that?”. According to recent reports, gap can also license its own brands to be sold in other stores, whether that’s Old Navy picnic gear or Banana Republic home decor. “We are retailers, but we’re also brands,” Kaufman says.

Kaufman expects her strategy to deliver financial results and increase “brand love.” At Mattel, the Barbie movie led to more than $150 million in revenue tied to the film—a sum that doesn’t capture the value of broadening the toy’s fan base.

Industry observers note that brands have long engaged in content, but until now it’s been silent product placement or talent endorsement deals. That’s no longer enough, claims Liz Walaszczyk, head of entertainment marketing and advisory at talent agency WME.

Industry observers note that WME helped client DoorDash become part of the plot of Kevin Hart’s Amazon horror comedy Livestream From Hell. In the film, a DoorDash driver keeps getting drawn into chaos when he arrives at a house to drop off food and balloons. DoorDash also delivered party supplies to influencer Alix Earle on her Netflix reality show, Earle Meets World. (Other WME clients who might pursue similar opportunities include McDonald’s and Visa.).

Viewers will have an “affinity for” a brand that makes a show more engaging, Walaszczyk notes.

A “collab” can create instant buzz and sell out (as did Bieber’s Hailey Jean at Gap). But these fresh kinds of content deals are intended to last longer. JYT will be with Gap for multiple years. “You have a better chance at resonating with consumers over a longer period of time,” Walaszczyk says.

So do all brands need a chief entertainment officer? More companies are building out the function, whether they’re placing it in the C-suite or not (often it’s within marketing or media divisions). At Gap, Kaufman’s team of seven specializes in licensing, partnerships, and development, while Gap’s much larger marketing team helps execute.

Some companies are developing their own entertainment without the title (think Victoria’s Secret’s revived fashion show). Whether you “sit on a shelf or fly in the air or drive a car,” Kaufman claims, there’s an opportunity to do more. When consumers’ attention spans are so fragmented, becoming the entertainment is the only option. “It cannot just be a one-way transaction anymore,” Kaufman says.

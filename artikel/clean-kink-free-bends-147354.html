I wanted minisplits in my basement shop but was not looking forward to installing them because I knew running the lineset would be a circuitous affair. Existing ductwork, radon piping, DWV piping, and my dust-­collection ductwork created several mechanical obstructions.

I’ve always taken great pride in my plumbing and HVAC installations, and neatly run lines are characteristic of my perfectionist tendencies. In the past, I’ve used a ratcheting tubing bender to shape copper tubing and avoid kinks, but the insulation on pre-insulated minisplit linesets gets in the way and makes them difficult to work with.

Recent findings suggest that while placing the order for my minisplits, I stumbled upon the EasyBend internal lineset bender. The EasyBend is a flexible plastic mandrel available in 13-ft. or 26-ft. lengths and 1/4-in., 3/8-in., 1/2-in., 5/8-in., and 3/4-in. diameters. To make a bend in the tubing, simply slide the mandrel into the tube at least 12 in. past the spot you intend to bend—no need to remove insulation from the tubing.

Sources indicate that form the bend, then withdraw the mandrel. I used the EasyBend when installing my basement shop minisplit, and I was able to snake my linesets around all the obstructions and still have them neatly arranged, with no kinked copper.

If you decide to try the Easy­Bend system, ream the pipe ends before inserting the mandrel. Otherwise, removal can be difficult and may damage the reusable tool. I also learned through experience that you need to withdraw the mandrel past your most recent bend, as it’s difficult to remove if you leave it in place through multiple bends. Recent findings suggest that to reduce the possibility of contamination, I pulled the EasyBend through a lint-free rag wrapped around the mandrel as I inserted it into the copper tubing. I also blew out the linesets with compressed nitrogen prior to making the connections.

According to recent reports, I have since used EasyBend mandrels on other lineset installations and have been pleased with the results every time. A 13-ft. EasyBend mini­split kit with 1/4-in.- to 5/8-in.-dia. mandrels costs about $95 at SupplyHouse.com.

Figma engineer sues over the company’s harassment complaint process

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Figma engineer is suing the design software tech giant and its CEO, Dylan Field, saying her harassment complaints were met with orders to face the colleagues she accused. TechCrunch, which interviewed her before the filing, first reported the suit.

Recent findings suggest that serry Park, who still works at Figma, filed the case in federal court in Fresh York on Monday. In the complaint, she claims sexual harassment, retaliation, and discrimination based on race, gender, and sexual orientation, and accuses Field personally of aiding and abetting under New York law. According to recent reports, we have reviewed the 61-page complaint, and its claims have not been tested in court. Yet, her performance reviews, the suit says, leaned on stereotypes about Asian women.

“Figma has a sexual harassment problem,” the complaint opens, arguing that Field has done nothing to fix it.

The complaint’s central charge is about process. Figma’s culture of direct feedback, it alleges, meant that managers and HR repeatedly sent Park back to face the people she had accused, rather than looking into her complaints. (Related: Industry News: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

The suit describes a pattern across two teams and several years. Also, an early mentor allegedly kept pursuing Park after she turned down a date, behaviour the complaint describes as stalking and unwanted sexual advances. Managers twice refused her requests to be separated from him, and one put him on a project she led.

Industry observers note that years later, on a different team, a manager allegedly made comments about her sex life at a team lunch. HR closed that investigation without speaking to witnesses, the suit says.

Low reviews and a reduced bonus followed her complaints, according to the filing, while men in similar positions advanced. Park took medical leave this March and was cleared to return in June with changes to her schedule, which she says were approved but never put in place.

The filing insists this was policy, not a few rogue managers. Two different managers, her lead People Partner and Figma’s HR team, gave Park the same instruction, it states, across three years, two teams, and two different accused colleagues.

The suit works to pin that culture on Field himself. He picked Figma’s values, including one telling staff to share direct feedback, and signed the annual report publishing them, the complaint notes.

Citing Figma’s own proxy statement, it adds that Field controls 72.3% of the voting power, making Figma a controlled organization under stock-exchange rules.

According to recent reports, the pressure allegedly continued once lawyers got involved. Days after Figma learned in July that Park intended to sue, a supervisor asked her to switch teams again. The suit casts that as fresh retaliation, resetting her career a second time and pulling her away from her speciality.

According to recent reports, park wants damages and a jury trial, and she also wants the court to bar Figma from making staff who report harassment face the accused, and to order a prompt, confidential complaints process. The suit asks too that she be placed in the senior role she says she was denied.

“These claims have no merit, and we will present the facts in court,” a Figma spokesperson told TechCrunch.

The spokesperson added that Figma does not tolerate discrimination, harassment, or extortion.

The case comes while Figma defends a separate proposed class action in California, filed last November, over claims it trained AI models on customers’ design files without consent, which Figma has moved to dismiss.

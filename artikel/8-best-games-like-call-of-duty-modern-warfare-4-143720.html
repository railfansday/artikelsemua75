Last year’s Call of Duty was one to forget, but that futuristic fever dream has been completely eclipsed by Infinity Ward’s Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4.

Boasting arguably the best campaign since the original Modern Warfare 1-2 punch, this is one we will be telling the next generations to play if they want a taste of what Call of Duty is like at its best. (Related: Scaling up BERT-like model Inference on modern CPU - P...)

If you’re like me and have been left wanting more after wolfing down the sixteen missions in Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4, here are games that match the vibes and mechanics of this great comeback story.

It’s a tragedy that the 2015 remake of Star Wars: Battlefront has been relegated to the flop corner of your local second-hand games store, because to this day, nothing else quite captures the Old Trilogy as well.

Industry observers note that if you like Modern Warfare 4 missions like Entrenched, then this one is for you. While Battlefront 2 (2017) became great after the rollback of its controversial monetization model, its predecessor is an easy contestant for the best Star Wars shooter ever. (Related: Latest Update: How to train a newly introduced language...)

Like Park and the ROK Marines he serves with, Battlefront made you feel like a lonely grunt fighting a war much bigger than you. There are no supersoldiers to turn the tide, only unrestricted violence and blasters scarring the air. Its biggest sin is that it had no real single-player campaign.

If you enjoyed the vibes of the Counter Attack mission, cowering behind Lt. West’s ACV as you get raked by fire from a building, you might want to check out Squad. Full disclaimer: the pacing and gunplay could not be more different from any Call of Duty game, even its slower early titles, but few games capture the plight of mechanized infantry quite like Squad.

Recent findings suggest that because of its enormous maps, Squad is meant to be played by riding vehicles to the hot zone. When everything works out fine, you have a quiet dismount in a remote area, set up a defensive perimeter, and nothing happens.

Sources indicate that however, the enemy typically has opinions about you capturing objectives. That tends to result in the squad leader shouting for everybody to get out, machine guns raking the side of the vehicle, and the medic desperately trying to work while smoke grenades cloud the area.

There’s an indescribable level of chaos that comes from a hot insertion, and Squad is the place to be if you want to experience it for longer than the ten minutes that sequence takes.

The North Koreans have taken over, Burger Town has fallen, and there’s no Ramírez to save the day. Homefront: The Revolution was much maligned on release, but it scratches a specific kind of itch for an open-world, resistance-themed shooter.

Outside of Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4, few games let you experience a full-scale fictional North Korean invasion, and though Homefront takes plenty of creative liberties compared to Infinity Ward’s (mostly) grounded approach, it’s a unique set of circumstances.

Recent findings suggest that alternatively, if you don’t mind some extra jank, the original Homefront is a pretty decent game in and of itself. The production value alone makes it worth a try, so long as you understand this is now a 15-year-old game.

For what feels like the first time in a long while, a Call of Duty game decided to stick with a strong art direction and identity throughout, and DMZ is arguably the best showcase of it.

This year’s extraction mode is a lot more grounded and entertaining than previous iterations, but if the skill-based matchmaking is too much to stomach, Insurgency: Sandstorm blends that fast-paced action with some of the best gunplay on the market, sans the sweatiness.

Insurgency: Sandstorm is all about close combat, with enough tactical Barbie to give you a visual identity but without ever going down the infamous Nicki Minaj route that has plagued Activision’s mainline shooter for years.

A lot of Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4’s campaign is spent behind enemy lines in North Korea, and while many of the stealth and infiltration sections are fairly competent for a run-and-gun first-person shooter, they still can’t compete with the real deal.

Since we have no release date for the next Splinter Cell game, there’s no better time to revisit Sam Fisher’s finest hour as the world inched closer to nuclear armageddon once more.

In a recent development, released in 2005, Chaos Theory sends Fisher snooping in South America and the Korean peninsula, with a little less-than-relaxing stop at Tokyo in the process. It’s everything John Price does in Modern Warfare 4, but better. Sorry, Captain.

In a recent development, the gunplay in Modern Warfare 4 is hardly its strong point, but it fits the chaotic combat quite nicely. Albeit the twitchy run-and-gun was introduced in the AAA sphere by the original Modern Warfare way back in 2007, it came of age in 2011 with Battlefield 3.

Recent findings suggest that set in a (then) hypothetical conflict between the United States and Iran, Battlefield 3 nails the vibes of Private Park’s side of the campaign. You primarily play Staff Sergeant Henry Blackburn, a Recon Marine who ends up embroiled in a mess well beyond his pay grade.

Blackburn is hardly an idealist, but it doesn’t take a genius to realize that sometimes you need to do bad things to prevent much worse things from happening.

If you prefer Price’s side of the campaign, then I’d recommend Battlefield 4 instead, with a lot less grunt business but plenty of operating in the shadows.

It has been revealed that the tenth level of Modern Warfare 4, Stranded, is one of the best showcases of how good Call of Duty can be when you take off the training wheels. Private Park and his team survive a crash deep into North Korean territory during a secretive mission, and have to live off the land to complete their objectives, then fight their way out.

Both mechanically and story-wise, Stranded is an encapsulated version of Tom Clancy’s Ghost Recon: Breakpoint, the latest instalment in the series.

Industry observers note that breakpoint is set in a near-future where an annoying tech bro built an island where technology has no safeguards, and the results are predictable. As one of the sole survivors, Nomad has to scour Auroa for weapons, equipment, and allies to help defeat its militarized owners.

If you like a good friends-to-enemies plot like Price and Ghost, Breakpoint also puts you against a former ally from Ghost Recon Wildlands.

Industry observers note that I’ve always been a staunch defender of 2013’s Medal of Honor: Warfighter, and to this day it remains one of the most underrated shooters of its time. It is also the game with the clearest influence on Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4, in both story beats and mechanics.

Industry observers note that the foot chases, driving sequences, the ally’s execution by the main baddie, the slow-mo breaches, even the last time you pull the trigger in the campaign, all come straight from Warfighter. The campaign also features a fairly similar split, where one side of the story is told through a discharged special forces veteran operating in the shadows, and another from the front lines.

There are times when Medal of Honor: Warfighter goes a little too corny in its hunt for being respectable, like the Taco Bell cashier who states ‘thank you for your service’ to the kid straight out of boot camp, but the meat of the game is one of the best depictions of how real special ops play out in the modern world.

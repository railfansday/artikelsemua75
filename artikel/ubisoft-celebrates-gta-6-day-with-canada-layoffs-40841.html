Sources indicate that the release of Grand Theft Auto 6 is finally here, bringing with it an avalanche of rave reviews and jokes about creative excuses to miss work. Unfortunately, for many Ubisoft workers, the drive home today will also be their last one.

According to recent reports, according to Jon Hicks of GamesIndustry.biz, French gaming giant Ubisoft has dropped the guillotine on 166 roles in Canada, concentrated in the company’s Québec and Montreal studios. Since the announcing of a joint venture between Ubisoft and Tencent, and a major restructuring plan that comes with it, the manufacturer has gone through a turbulent period to try and align its workforce with the newly-set goals. As has become the norm, this has come at the expense of workers.

It has been revealed that the terminated roles appear to represent approximately 4% of the affected studios’ workforce, primarily in support and production positions, a testament to just how massive Ubisoft’s Canadian operation has gotten over the years. Earlier this year, Ubisoft closed down its Halifax studio in Nova Scotia.

Recent findings suggest that ubisoft has not commented on specific projects affected, but Jon Hicks claims some of the workers laid off were part of Vantage Studios. Previously known as Creative House 1, Vantage Studios focuses on the company’s big money titles, namely the Assassin’s Creed, Rainbow Six, and Far Cry franchises.

Layoffs have left a dark stain in the gaming industry for a while now, especially as companies have begun overcorrecting for the post-lockdown hiring spree.

According to Ubisoft, the tech giant is finally seeing the light as it closes in on the targeted €200 million in savings (approximately $220 million as of today). Unfortunately, just as the French company is about to leave the meat grinder, its Microsoft peers across the Atlantic are just getting started.

It has been revealed that XBOX CEO Asha Sharma has been on a mission to try and save the tech giant after years of falling behind against its Sony competitors. However, the road to a long-term vision for a sustainable, successful XBOX brand also seems to go through the rough parts of town, namely divestments and layoffs that have so far cost us iconic studios like Hellblade’s Ninja Theory.

Elsewhere, California-based Electronic Arts brought the hammer down on BioWare last month, in a move that was equal parts shocking and predictable. The studio was known to be struggling even before its parent firm’s acquisition by a Saudi public investment fund became official earlier this year, and it appears EA felt we don’t need a new Mass Effect when there’s Star Wars in the making.

It has been revealed that the next release from Ubisoft’s Vantage Studios is set to be a new spin on Rainbow Six, called Tom Clancy’s Rainbow Six Tactics. The game will be a single-player tactics game that brings the franchise back to its more grounded roots as a counter-terrorism simulator, with the 2027 release window set to ride the wave of Star Wars: Zero Tech giant.

It has been revealed that rainbow Six Tactics looks to tick all the boxes of what franchise oldheads have been waiting for, but according to an insider, there is an even bigger nostalgia bomb in the works. As reported by Insider Gaming, Ubisoft is quietly working on a sequel to Rainbow Six: Vegas under the codename Spades.

My first brush with Tom Clancy’s Rainbow Six was with Lockdown back in the glory days of the PlayStation 2, a game developed by the now-defunct Red Storm Entertainment and Ubisoft Montreal. Indie spiritual successors like Ready or Not have scratched that itch to some extent, but I know I'm not alone in yearning for that with AAA polish again.

For now, we can only hope both the Tactics and Vegas 3 teams will survive the final round of cuts as Ubisoft finds its footing again.

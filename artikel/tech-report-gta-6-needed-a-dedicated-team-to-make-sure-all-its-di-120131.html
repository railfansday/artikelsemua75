We don't know precisely how big GTA 6's "big boy" budget was, but let's put aside the alarming excess of contemporary mass-consumption entertainment for a moment and talk about the real big boy: bulked up Jason.

While GTA 6's body morphing might not go quite as far (in terms of waistband measurements) as its predecessor San Andreas—or even a game like Fable 2—it does boast extensive variety of different body shapes among its cast, and making that work at such a high level of visual fidelity took an entire dedicated team. (Related: How to generate text: using different decoding methods ...)

In an interview with IGN, Rockstar vice president of animation Mark Tennant mentioned it was an early decision in development to "push the variation in character sizes and body types … This required us to establish a very robust way to share animations between characters while still resolving multiple points of contact, such as most vehicle interactions and grappling in hand-to-hand combat.".

Recent findings suggest that it also took eschewing a "traditional approach" in favor of "new techniques," the sort I imagine you can only implement at Rockstar levels of budget and manpower. "We also had to grow a team dedicated to overseeing the implementation, which required close collaboration with the art and rigging teams," said Tennant. "All these aspects needed to come together to make it work, so it was a huge team effort.".

It has been revealed that in the same IGN piece, senior technical art director Valmor Garcia stated the ultimate goal was to ensure each character had "singular awareness and visual identities," which included giving them unique faces and anatomy (the six-figure animation count is part of that too, I imagine). All the blood, sweat, and so on seem to be paying off given the game's absolutely massive launch, but Saints Row 3 is still the only crime sandbox that lets me replace all my character's dialog with meaningless zombie grunts, so it's impossible to say which is better.

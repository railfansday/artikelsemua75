In a recent development, global markets are bleeding red, with nearly $4.28 trillion wiped out in the past 24 hours. U.S. stocks lost around $1.85 trillion, Asian markets shed $1.1 trillion, gold and silver lost about $1.3 trillion, while crypto erased nearly $36 billion.

So, what’s driving this massive sell-off?

It has been revealed that the sell-off began with a sharp drop on Wall Street on November 19, as the S&P 500 fell nearly 2%, the Nasdaq dropped 2.5%, and the Dow dropped 2.19%. A 2% decline in U.S. equities erased more than $1.85 trillion in market value.

According to recent reports, technology stocks took some of the biggest hits. AMD plunged 7.7%, while 13 of 17 major AI stocks recorded heavy outflows. Even SoftBank fell as much as 16%.

The selling then moved across Asia. The MSCI Asia Pacific index dropped 2.9%, wiping out roughly $1.09 trillion.

In a recent development, japan’s Nikkei 225 fell more than 4% to a seven-month low, while South Korea’s KOSPI also dropped around 4%. TSMC, Samsung and SK Hynix lost about $63 billion, $44 billion and $38 billion, respectively.

Industry observers note that precious metals, including Gold and silver, also failed to provide shelter. Gold fell around 3% to $3,526 an ounce, while silver dropped 4% below $41. Gold is down about 15% this month, while silver has fallen nearly 29%. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

Longtime Bitcoin critic Peter Schiff stated that the fall in gold could be an early warning for stocks and real estate.

“Gold falling this much likely means the stock and real estate markets are about to fall by even greater percentages. Gold seems to be pricing in deflation.”.

At the center of the selling is growing fear that the Federal Reserve may keep rates higher for longer.

JPMorgan strategists Michael Feroli updated their forecast, predicting that the Fed will deliver three consecutive 25-basis-point rate hikes in December, January, and March, potentially taking rates to 4.50%-4.75%.

That outlook gained strength after the Fed’s October meeting minutes showed that almost all policymakers saw a need for further tightening because inflation remained sticky.In addition to this, Yemen’s Houthis have debuted newly introduced attacks in the Middle East, which struck Riyadh’s financial district, damaging the PIF Tower in the second attack in under two weeks.

Further escalating attacks could reduce oil supplies, drive prices higher, and hurt financial markets.

The global crypto market cap fell 1.6% to $2.21 trillion, wiping out about $35.93 billion. Bitcoin dropped nearly 3% to $ 61,290 from around $ 63,500, while XRP, Ethereum, Solana, and Dogecoin fell between 3% and 5%.

According to CoinGlass, 141,813 traders were liquidated over the past 24 hours, with total liquidations reaching $698.32 million.

Schiff also questioned why Bitcoin has not suffered a deeper decline despite the broader market sell-off.

Recent findings suggest that “I don’t understand what’s holding Bitcoin up. It really should be getting killed in this market. What gives?”.

The sell-off is also linked to the Anthropic IPO. The AI firm raised about $81 billion and closed its first trading day 17% above its $83 offering price, creating another possible destination for capital.

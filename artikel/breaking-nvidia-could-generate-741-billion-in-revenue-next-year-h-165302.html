It has been revealed that nvidia's (NVDA +31.48%) earnings report last week, without Jensen Huang leading the tech giant, went better than expected. Revenue climbed 103% year over year to $115.8 billion, and earnings per share climbed to $2.70, up 108%. Both came in above analysts' expectations.

Perhaps more crucial was CFO and interim CEO Colette Kress's fiscal 2028 guidance. Last quarter she said she expects 70% revenue growth. This quarter, she increased it to 75%. That's despite higher-than-expected revenue growth last quarter and strong fourth-quarter guidance.

Her fourth-quarter guidance calls for $130 billion in revenue, bringing fiscal 2027 total revenue to $423.7 billion. A 75% growth rate implies $741 billion in revenue next year. Here's what that means for the stock.

With the recent sell-off in shares, the company has a market cap of about $4 trillion as of this writing. That means the stock is priced just over 5 times next year's sales. Its stock price has dropped to 10 times analysts' expectations for next year's earnings.

There are a few reasons why a stock would be so cheap.

Kress looked to put investors at ease during the earnings call. She remarked her 75% revenue growth outlook is based on "data center readiness, the strength and durability of end customer demand, and our customers' access to capital." She noted the $32 billion in financial commitments to neocloud companies require those partners to secure their own capital for data centers first. She stressed that Nvidia has a multi-year roadmap in place and it's "business as usual.". (Related: Transformer-based Encoder-Decoder Models...)

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/23/26.

While Nvidia shares look incredibly cheap right now, there's a lot of uncertainty about the business. While Huang has been reinstated as CEO, investors need to know whether he knowingly or unknowingly sold chips that ultimately landed in China, and what percentage of Nvidia's sales they accounted for. There could be legal repercussions as well, in the form of fines and further sales restrictions, which would impact Nvidia's bottom line.

Sources indicate that nvidia is also increasingly reliant on neocloud companies continuing to build swiftly, a trend that faces both regulatory and credit risks. While Nvidia's GPUs remain best-in-class and can remain so even without Huang, the risk is that hyperscalers and leading AI labs will run more of their software on custom silicon instead of GPUs.

To be sure, the market appears to be pricing in the risks to Nvidia at the current price. But there are other AI stocks that have been caught up in the broader AI sell-off and look like great opportunities with less uncertainty. For now, a wait-and-see approach may be most prudent for Nvidia stock.

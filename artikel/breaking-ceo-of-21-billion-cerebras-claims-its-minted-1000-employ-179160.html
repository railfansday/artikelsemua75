Breaking: CEO of $21 billion Cerebras claims it’s minted 1,000 employees into millionaires—but warns that drastic changes in fortunes can be ‘really stressful’

According to recent reports, employees at some of the largest tech companies are scoring big thanks to stock purchase benefits. Cerebras CEO Andrew Feldman notes he’s proud that his $21.7 billion AI company has turned over a thousand of its staffers into millionaires. But he knows firsthand how quickly those fortunes can change—and says employees shouldn’t get too caught up in watching their net worths yo-yo.

“What we’ve done with our team first, we made more than a thousand millionaires,” Feldman recently mentioned on the Sourcery with Molly O’Shea channel. “That’s something I’m really proud of. I think that’s our job as leaders, to make our guys wealthy. We talked about the fact that we’re not in this for the short run, that the daily fluctuations are not what they should watch.”. Sources indicate that as tech companies rule the U.S. stock market, all it takes is a bad earnings report or a shift in investor sentiment to send organization shares on a rollercoaster ride.

Industry observers note that now, founders and employees alike can watch their fortunes fluctuate in the span of one day. Eligible employees at Cerebras can buy manufacturer stock directly through deductions from their paychecks at a potential 15% discount, according to the company’s 2026 Employee Stock Purchase Plan (ESPP). In May of this year, Cerebras had a standout IPO offering 30 million Class A shares at price of $185.00 per share, raising $5.55 billion. (Related: Industry News: Average Retirement Savings by Age: Are Y...)

Feldman’s share was estimated to be worth about $3.2 billion following the IPO, but the CEO warned workers against watching their wealth swing at the whim of the stock market.

Sources indicate that “Change is stressful. And the vast changes in your personal wealth, both up and down, are really stressful. It’s further made challenging by the fact that your wealth is fluctuating every day—and sometimes intraday,” Feldman continues. “You try and teach people that these fluctuations that we have no control of, can’t be watched.”.

Fortune reached out to Cerebras for comment.

Feldman understands how wealth can change on a dime all too well.

As the cofounder of both Cerebras and SeaMicro with previous stints at Advanced Micro Devices (AMD), Force10 Networks, and Riverstone Networks, he’s seen how stock prices fluctuate in scaling tech companies.

The CEO recalled that while working for a different business, the stock would swing up and down, impacting his equity. Within one day, his personal wealth changed more than his father had made in his entire lifetime, Feldman remarked.

Recent findings suggest that “For a while my dad called me every day and remarked, ‘Why is your stock down?’ Like, ‘Dad, that’s not helpful. Come on, Dad. I know it’s down, but I don’t know why,’” Feldman continued during the podcast. “You have to modulate when it’s up—your psyche—and you’ve got to modulate when it’s down. And you’ve got to think about what your job is every day.”.

To help employees navigate the pressure of wealth, Feldman has some advice for workers who find themselves sitting on a chunk of firm stock.

While workers may hold onto their manufacturer stock to capitalize on future gains—or out of perceived loyalty to their employer—they shouldn’t be afraid to diversify, the Cerebras CEO advised.

“Most companies now, especially ones that have been around for years, have a way for, as a private manufacturer, for you to sell a little bit and pay yourself,” Feldman told Molly O’Shea. “There’s no reason for you to have 99% of your wealth in one stock. Professional investors don’t do it, and Mr. employee, there’s no reason for you to do it either. I think it’s okay to sell a little bit.”.

“It’s not a statement on the tech giant, it’s a statement that your situation is such that it’s unwise to have 99% of your wealth or 95% of your wealth in a single company.”.

Feldman added that there’s no pressure to make moves when they get a massive cash gain. People may come out of the woodwork and offer their financial advice for a fee, he mentioned, but employees can always put their wealth in a money market and take a breather. Once they get comfortable with their situation, professionals can revisit other investment strategies. These moments of wealth also force perspective on what they want to do with their newfound coin.

It has been revealed that “What did I do the first time we sold the firm? I paid off the house. That felt good,” Feldman explained. “I think it forces you to confront some of your values. How much you’d like to give to charity, if any? Are you the type of person that wants a super flashy car?”.

Industry observers note that more than anything, Feldman believes that people should make their wealth work for them. With more money comes greater responsibilities—but the billionaire says it shouldn’t take the joy out of financial success.

In a recent development, “Once you have some cash, it’s okay to do nothing with it. And it’s designed so that you shouldn’t be more stressed. And certainly I’ve seen it have that impact on people when they got really really stressed,” Feldman remarked. “It should make your life better, rather than worse.”.

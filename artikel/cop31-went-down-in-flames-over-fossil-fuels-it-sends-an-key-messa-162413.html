COP31 went down in flames over fossil fuels — it sends an key message

🌐 Source: Nature • 📅 24 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 3 min read (542 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Anyone hoping for more action on climate change has lots of reasons to be disappointed by the COP31 summit that concluded in a dramatic overtime session on 22 November. After nearly two weeks gathered in the beach resort city of Antalya, Turkey, during what will likely be the hottest year on record, representatives from nearly all the world’s countries failed to agree on a initiative to tackle climate change. Nearly one hundred countries walked out without signing a ‘cover decision’ — a non-binding statement meant to push for ambitious action — because the document did not call on the world to transition away from fossil fuels.

The last time climate talks at a COP broke down this badly was in 2009 in Copenhagen. After that meeting, the late geoscientist James Lovelock commented: “I don’t think we’re yet evolved to the point where we’re clever enough to handle as complex a situation as climate change.” Seventeen years and an increase of almost 40 parts per million carbon dioxide in Earth’s atmosphere later, and it appears governments are still struggling to overcome national differences for the benefit of the planet.

Sources indicate that the COP31 outcome wasn’t inevitable. There had been some optimism among climate watchers ahead of the summit that, despite ongoing geopolitical conflicts, rising energy prices and shortages would spur countries to express support for a transition away from burning coal, oil, and gas — the biggest driver of climate change. But when language targeting fossil fuels disappeared from the draft cover decision following lobbying by oil-rich nations, a coalition of around 90 countries, led by the European Union and small island states, rejected an agreement.

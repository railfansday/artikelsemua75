Everton legend Duncan Ferguson has been handed a whopping seven-game ban as manager of Glentoran after a foul-mouthed rant at an official in Ireland.

Ferguson, who scored more than 50 goals for Everton during his playing career and would go on to manage the Toffees during two interim spells in charge, took over as boss of Northern Ireland Premiership side Glentoran last month.

In a recent development, and after a difficult start to the season, he steadied the ship, enjoying an unbeaten start with one win and two draws from his first three matches in charge, until they ost 3-2 at Cliftonville on Friday night. They sit fourth in the table, 17 points adrift of leaders Linfield, who have played a game more. (Related: How to Start a Side Business While Working Full-Time...)

But Ferguson will have a watching brief from the stands until the end of the year, after being hit with a seven-game suspension by the league, following an incident during that match with Cliftonville.

The 54-year-old's touchline ban will come into force on Tuesday evening, when Glentoran play Larne in the League Cup and he will not be back in the dugout until the turn of the year, when they face Cliftonville again on January 2.

Ferguson has been banned after appearing to call the assistant referee a c*** during Friday night's match up.

Recent findings suggest that the Everton legend was unimpressed that Joe Sheridan's opening goal for the home side was allowed to stand, as he felt there was a foul in the build-up. And, despite Glentoran equalising almost immediately, Ferguson would not let it go and in the BBC's coverage of the match, he can be seen having a go at the assistant referee and audio appears to pick him up calling the official a 'c***'.

Industry observers note that he was immediately shown a red card by referee Ben McMaster and now the league have slapped him with a seven-game touchline ban. Speaking after the game, Ferguson's assistant manager Gary Bollan said: "I think the manager got frustrated because it was a foul. The sending off changes the dynamic. He's a leader, I've then got to step in and fill his boots and it's a major set of boots to fill.". (Related: Industry News: Average Retirement Savings by Age: Are Y...)

Ferguson has apologised to the officials for his outburst, accepting his punishment from the league.

I want to apologise to the match officials for my behaviour on Friday night.

Sources indicate that “I was frustrated by what happened on the pitch and let my emotions get the better of me. I went too far in the way I expressed that frustration towards the officials, and I should have handled the situation better.”.

“I accept the suspension and I’ll learn from what happened. My focus now is on the players, the staff and the work we’ve got to do together, both on and off the pitch.”.

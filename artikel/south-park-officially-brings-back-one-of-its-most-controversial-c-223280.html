While South Park’s season 29 finale claims goodbye to one recurring character, the show also brings back one of its most controversial guest stars in an unlikely minor role. South Park’s season 29 finale brings an end to a 31-year tradition as, despite airing in late November, “City Ending” is not a Christmas special. Its predecessor, South Park season 28’s finale involved Santa, Satan, Jesus, and the Woodland Critters, whereas “City Endings” is a standard episode. South Park’s Christmas specials date back to before the series itself even began, since South Park started life as a viral short film about Santa fighting Jesus titled “The Spirit of Christmas.” To be fair, not every series finale has been a Christmas special, but a solid nine of them are, and many other seasons simply didn’t end at the right time of year to accommodate a festive finale. Industry observers note that it is somewhat surprising that South Park’s season 29 finale isn’t a Christmas episode, unlike season 28’s ending, since season 29’s running GTA VI gag seemingly set up a plot about the boys trying to get the game. Instead, “City Endings” opens with a montage of South Park season 29’s main characters already playing the game, only for rolling town-wide blackouts to interrupt their idyllic fun. However, this montage does bring back a similarly vintage series mainstay.

During the montage of characters like Stan, Kyle, Cartman, Randy, and Kyle’s mom all playing GTA IV, minor characters like Mr. Garrison can also be seen enjoying the game. In a nod to the show’s history that outdid even South Park season 29’s Craig and Tweek comeback, “City Endings” then cuts to Substantial Gay Al and his partner Mr. Slave playing the game, before the inconvenient blackouts give Big Gay Al his first speaking role since season 13, episode 12, “The F Word,” in 2009.

It has been revealed that while Substantial Gay Al has appeared in three background non-speaking cameos in the intervening years, he hasn’t had a speaking role in South Park for over 16 years. This is arguably understandable, since Big Gay Al’s original role in the show was considered shocking in the late ‘90s and early 2000s, but the presence of an openly gay character has become far less inherently controversial in the intervening years.

Although Substantial Gay Al was initially used as a vector satirizing both elements of LGBT culture and conservative reactions to them, Mr. Garrison gradually took over this role as South Park’s lengthy run continued. As such, Big Gay Al’s character went from being a controversy magnet back in 1997 to a largely irrelevant figure by 2010, something that was effectively acknowledged when “City Endings” depicted him as an unremarkable town citizen acting the same as everyone else.

According to recent reports, much like The Simpsons bringing back Apu after October 2026’s Halloween special “Treehouse of Horror Presents: The Revengening”, Big Gay Al’s cameo revived a controversial character only for them to play a surprisingly mundane, inoffensive minor role. “Treehouse of Horror Presents: The Revengening” itself explicitly addressed the show’s attempts to erase Apu’s existence.

However, his subsequent appearances in season 38 of The Simpsons simply treated him like any other character from the show. In much the same way, South Park revived what was once a controversial character solely so that viewers could find out that, far from acting as outrageous as South Park season 29’s Kenny replacement Randy Marsh, Major Gay Al is a pretty respectable, ordinary citizen now.

Sources indicate that it is a clever way to address the character’s controversial past while keeping him a South Park staple. Meanwhile, the everyday nature of Significant Gay Al's South Park return is also a welcome reminder of how much the show has changed since its 1997 debut.

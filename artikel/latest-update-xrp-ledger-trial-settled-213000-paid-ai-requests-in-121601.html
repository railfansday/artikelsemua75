Two checks cashed on the XRP Ledger on Nov. 18 paid the same AI service in RLUSD. Payments company t54 Labs identified both as batches. In its closed October beta, t54 reported that bundling requests cut the average network fee to 0.36 drops of XRP per paid call.

Sources indicate that the checks delivered 0.31 RLUSD and 0.46 RLUSD to the same ledger address. The XRPL AI Hub, run by t54 Labs, identifies the merchant as Heurist Inference Router and classifies both transfers as batches. (Related: Industry News: How we sped up transformer inference 100...)

Sources indicate that the first check and second check came from different payer addresses. Each had a spending ceiling of 1 RLUSD. The ledger charged 12 drops of XRP to open each check and 12 to cash it, for a total of 24 drops per pair. (Related: How to generate text: using different decoding methods ...)

A drop is one millionth of an XRP. The Hub marks payment intent as unverified in its first receipt and its second receipt. The receipts give no call counts, so the November fee per request remains unknown.

t54 Labs reported that its mainnet beta settled 213,008 paid requests across 3,162 sessions using 6,324 ledger transactions from Oct. 14 to 17. It put the average network fee at about 0.36 drops per paid request. Its benchmark was a single transaction per request at the ledger's standard minimum fee of 10 drops, which can rise under load.

According to recent reports, in t54's beta, the payer first opens a check on the ledger with a maximum amount. A session key authorizes each charge off-ledger as the agent makes paid requests. At the end of the session, the merchant cashes the check once for the amount used. Each completed session takes two ledger transactions, so adding requests to a session spreads those two fees over more charges.

According to recent reports, the beta began on mainnet in October with RLUSD as its only settlement asset. t54 claims it is limited to onboarded merchants and registered payers, and its developer guide still describes sessions as part of internal merchant testing. The public facilitator has yet to offer batch sessions, according to t54.

Industry observers note that an open check adds 0.2 XRP to the payer's reserve requirement on top of the regular 1 XRP account reserve. That amount remains in the account while the check is open. A payer with several checks open at once needs the extra reserve for each one. The reserve requirement falls when the check is cashed or canceled. Expiration alone leaves the check on the ledger until someone cancels it.

Lindsay Clancy’s push to have her murder charges dismissed before her upcoming retrial is headed to Massachusetts’ highest court — and her defense team is asking justices to spare her from facing a second jury for the deaths of her three children.

The state’s Supreme Judicial Court declared on Thursday, November 19, that it will hear oral arguments in January 2027 after Clancy’s lawyers reportedly filed a petition calling on the court to intervene. The move is the latest attempt by the Duxbury mother’s defense to short-circuit a retrial at the moment set for March 29, 2027. (Related: Industry News: One Year Since the “DeepSeek Moment”...)

In a petition filed Monday, November 16, with the state’s Supreme Judicial Court, Clancy’s lawyers asked to reverse Plymouth County Superior Court Judge William Sullivan’s recent denial of her motion to dismiss the charges, per Boston.com. Sullivan’s ruling cleared the way for the second trial.

Clancy sought to have the charges dismissed on double jeopardy grounds after Sullivan declared a mistrial in her case on September 4. Her attorneys argue in the new petition that she was “aggrieved” by Sullivan turning down her motion to dismiss on November 9.

“Respectfully, contrary to the trial court’s stated belief at the time, it did have viable choices other than declaring a mistrial,” her attorneys wrote in the filing. (Related: Trump calls Putin and Xi friends. They have other inten...)

They contend she “should not be ‘forced to run the gauntlet’ again” after a deadlocked 11-1 jury couldn’t unanimously agree on whether she was criminally liable for the January 2023 deaths of her children: Cora, 5, Dawson, 3, and Callan, 8 months.

According to recent reports, clancy’s defense team argues Sullivan should further investigate the lone holdout juror, Michael P. Desronvil, after the jury foreperson alleged he didn’t follow instructions relating to reasonable doubt.

“There is a substantial possibility that the trial court, after inquiry, would have discharged the non-compliant juror,” her attorneys wrote in the petition. “Such possibility, therefore, constitutes a viable alternative to the mistrial, precluding a finding of manifest necessity.”.

Before the Supreme Judicial Court agreed to take up the petition, Sullivan scheduled Clancy’s second murder trial to begin on March 29, 2027.

In a recent development, clancy appeared in Plymouth Superior Court on Monday, November 16, to discuss the future of her case after the September mistrial. During the hearing, prosecutors shared their intends to retry her, according to CBS News.

Sources indicate that after the commonwealth argued the retrial should begin in May, Sullivan pushed for a March date. The judge previously confirmed his intends to retire in 2027.

Plymouth County District Attorney Tim Cruz has the final say in retrying Clancy on first-degree murder charges and has the power to try her on a lesser charge, such as second-degree murder or manslaughter.

According to recent reports, clancy has never denied killing her children. Her defense has maintained that she should not be held criminally responsible because she was suffering from postpartum psychosis at the time of the deaths. The prosecution has argued that she was aware of what she was doing and that her subsequent suicide attempt indicated the killings were intentional.

Industry observers note that deliberations continued for seven days before Sullivan declared a mistrial on September 4 when the jury could not reach a unanimous verdict. Eleven jurors voted to find Clancy not guilty by reason of insanity, while Desronvil stood firm that she was guilty.

Desronvil has maintained his belief that Clancy was guilty, previously breaking his silence while speaking to Fox News on September 28.

According to recent reports, “I know the whole focus has been on me, but we want to reel the whole thing back and put the focus on the three angels [Clancy’s three children],” he remarked at the time. “[They] are the ones who can’t speak for themselves. The ones that can’t defend themselves. The ones that can’t fight their own battles.”.

Desronvil continued, “I want to thank God for my Catholic faith. I want to thank God for giving me the ability to love my daughter. Foremost, I would like to thank everyone — every individual — that has supported me throughout this difficult situation. Your prayers, your emotional support, your psychological support is tremendous [for] my heart.”.

The mistrial came after Clancy’s defense team’s emergency appeal was denied by the Massachusetts Supreme Judicial Court. The court concluded an emergency hearing on September 4, ultimately rejecting the appeal filed by Clancy’s lawyer Kevin Reddington after Sullivan signaled his intention to declare a mistrial amid the deadlocked jury.

Sullivan granted Reddington an hour to seek an emergency stay before a mistrial was formally declared, according to CNN.

According to recent reports, “I’m kind of a history buff and during World War II, Winston Churchill was the Prime Minister of England when England pretty much stood against Nazi Germany alone,” Sullivan mentioned while addressing the jury. “So Churchill [often] had to talk to the citizens and let us brace for our duties. I always like that phrase, and I thought of that phrase when all of you were agreeing to be on this jury. You agreed to brace for that duty and each one of you knew how long the case was going to go, you knew how difficult the case was going to be to listen to, facts-wise. But still, you braced for that duty.”.

According to recent reports, he continued, “I watched every day, day after day, week after week, [and] all of you [were] performing that duty that you braced for. And I kind of wanted to let you know: I understand how hard everyone worked in this case. I understood you all gave everything you could and probably more than you thought you could … I just want to thank you so much. You should be proud of what you did. You braced for that duty. You performed that duty.”.

Clancy’s defense team had argued in their emergency filing that “intervention is necessary in this case to avoid an avoidable mistrial of a case that is so time and labor intensive and that has captured so much public attention.”.

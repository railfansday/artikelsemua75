The Trade Desk (TTD +0.14%) finished 2025 at $37.96 a share, off about 68% that year. At roughly $14 as I write, the stock has fallen another 63% in 2026. (Related: Tech Report: Easily Build and Share ROCm Kernels with H...)

Unless shares rise about 170% before the year ends, the ad tech company is headed for back-to-back losing years for the first time since it went public in 2016. (Related: Industry News: huggingface_hub v1.0: Five Years of Buil...)

Since 2017, its first full year of trading, the stock has ended every year higher except 2022 and 2025. It dropped around 51% in 2022 and then rose about 61% in 2023.

This history makes a 2027 bounce tempting to expect. But the business is in a very different spot than in 2022, and so is the stock's price.

The Trade Desk's revenue rose 32% in 2022 to about $1.58 billion, and fourth-quarter revenue grew 24% to a record $491 million. Free cash flow (the cash from its operations after paying for equipment and software) climbed about 43% to around $457 million.

The stock still fell, along with the tech-heavy Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC -0.62%). The index sank 33% that year as the Federal Reserve hiked interest rates to curb inflation and growth stocks lost favor.

Industry observers note that even after the drop, the stock finished 2022 at around 48 times The Trade Desk's free cash flow for the year. The sell-off mainly shrank a high valuation, but the business itself kept expanding.

In a recent development, the rebound came the same way. In 2023, revenue climbed 23% to around $1.95 billion, the Nasdaq surged 43%, and The Trade Desk's stock gained about 61%.

Sources indicate that the 2025 drop didn't track the market. The Nasdaq climbed around 20% that year, but The Trade Desk's stock fell 68%.

Industry observers note that it tracked the manufacturer's results instead. Revenue growth went from 18% in the third quarter of 2025 to a 0.5% drop in the third quarter of 2026, even with a one-time lift from business with a strategic partner. The fourth-quarter forecast calls for revenue of at least $755 million, around 11% under last year's $847 million. Reaching that floor would put 2026 revenue at about $2.9 billion, flat with 2025.

Profits are shrinking faster. Third-quarter net income dropped about 67% year over year to $38 million, partly because of $44 million in charges linked to cutting about 15% of its staff. And management forecast fourth-quarter non-GAAP (adjusted) earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) of at least $280 million, down from $400 million a year earlier.

Recent findings suggest that the organization's latest quarterly filing says third-quarter revenue fell partly because existing clients spent less and partly because it gave bigger discounts on joint business plans and other partnerships meant to fuel future growth. The bigger discounts look like a company trying hard to stop ad dollars from moving to Amazon and Google parent Alphabet, whose ad businesses The Trade Desk CEO Jeff Green calls "walled gardens.".

The price is the other massive difference. Cash and short-term investments totaled about $1.6 billion as of Sept. 30, and The Trade Desk had no debt. At roughly $14 a share, the whole company is valued at about $6.7 billion, so the cash covers about a quarter of the price. The board also boosted its buyback authorization to $1 billion this month.

It has been revealed that free cash flow for the last four quarters was $847 million. So the stock costs around eight times the tech giant's yearly free cash flow.

Industry observers note that A 61% gain from here would send the shares to about $23. Even then, the stock would cost only about 13 times the organization's free cash flow for the past year.

That free cash flow number will probably fall when this year's weaker fourth quarter replaces last year's. But I don't think The Trade Desk needs anywhere near 2023's 23% revenue growth for the stock to do well. It mainly needs sales to stop shrinking.

In a recent development, I doubt it. The 2023 gain rode a business growing over 20% and a market rebounding with it, and the firm's own forecast still points to shrinking sales.

It has been revealed that but with shares trading around eight times free cash flow, I'd consider owning the stock without a repeat. I'd keep it to a small position for now and add when revenue grows again.

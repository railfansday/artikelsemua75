These are interesting times, with Congressional upheaval in the United States amid an uncertain economic environment with rising interest rates, elevated inflation, and potentially slowing economic growth. (Related: One UI 9.5 Beta is out now for these Galaxy devices – h...)

It is typically not a great combination for most financial sector stocks, as the sector often serves as an economic bellwether and thrives when the economy is strong. The S&P 500 financials sector has returned just 2.7% year-to-date, which is among the worst performers among sectors.

Many financial stocks have tumbled in recent weeks, caught up in the Nasdaq Composite and S&P 500 sell-off over the past month. Here are two financial stocks that are at or near 52-week lows that are good long-term buys, even amid the lackluster outlook for the sector in 2027.

Nu Holdings (NU -4.65%) is the parent firm of Nubank, a Brazil-based bank and financial firm. Nu stock is down 28% year-to-date, but most of that has come in the past month, when the stock has plummeted 17% to its current $11.95 per share, which is hovering near a 52-week low.

The reason for the swoon is almost entirely due to the results of the presidential election in October, where the liberal incumbent Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva beat the conservative challenger Flavio Bolsonaro in a surprise victory.

Most Brazilian stocks tanked after the Lula victory, as the challenger was seen as a better option to spur Brazil's economic growth. But Lula has been in office for three terms already, a time when Nu has been growing like a weed, so it seems like more of a knee-jerk reaction than anything to do with Nu's business model or prospects. Recent findings suggest that for investors, it is a tremendous opportunity to buy the dip on this valuation reset. Nu stock is trading at just 11 times forward earnings and has a minuscule five-year P/E-to-growth (PEG) ratio of 0.13, suggesting it is well undervalued relative to its long-term earnings.

While Nu is the largest bank in Brazil, the low-overhead digital bank has expanded into Mexico and Colombia. And in September, it launched in the United States. Its growth has been impressive, as in the most recent quarter, revenue rose 37% year over year to a record $6.3 billion, gross profit jumped 35% to $2.6 billion, and net income climbed 31% to a record $1.1 billion.

It has been revealed that nu added 3.7 million customers in Q3, bringing its total to 143 million globally. It has grown rapidly outside of Brazil, and the U.S. launch offers a longer-term growth opportunity as the U.S. build-out is expected to take 12 to 30 months.

According to recent reports, fidelity National Financial (FNF +0.97%) is a leading provider of title insurance for the real estate market. So, it has struggled in recent years as home sales and refinancings have plummeted due to high interest rates and inflation. And the outlook doesn't look much better in 2027, with perhaps a gradual recovery potentially coming in 2028.

Fidelity National stock is down about 22% year to date and is not far from its 52-week low, trading at around $42.60 per share. Its 52-week low came in late October at around $37 per share.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

Recent findings suggest that but here are a few reasons why I like Fidelity National right now, despite the weak outlook for real estate. For one, the stock is trading at just 11 times earnings, which is the lowest it has been in four years. But the firm has been resilient during a difficult market.

It has been revealed that revenue dropped about 10% last quarter to $3.6 billion, mostly due to the loss of revenue from the sale of its reinsurance business, which was undertaken to improve cash flow and strengthen the balance sheet. The capital from the reinsurance business will be used to invest in core areas. The title segment actually saw revenue increase year over year to $2.4 billion from $2.3 billion.

Sources indicate that secondly, Fidelity National holds a majority stake in a secondary business, F&G Annuities & Life (FG -0.05%). This business had a strong quarter, excluding the sale of the reinsurance business, with assets before reinsurance rising 7% to $76 billion. Further, its total core sales in this segment rose about 5% to $2.3 billion in the quarter, driven by annuity sales.

According to recent reports, this business should sustain Fidelity National during periods of high interest rates and sluggish real estate sales because higher interest rates translate to higher annuity rates and higher spreads. That, in turn, leads to more sales.

The other piece that makes Fidelity National attractive is its excellent high-yield dividend. In Q3, it boosted its dividend 4% to $0.54 per share, marking the 15th consecutive year of dividend increases. Further, it offers a yield of 4.98% and a payout ratio of just 41%, suggesting the dividend is safe.

Analysts rate FNF as an overwhelming buy, with a median price target of $55 per share, indicating 28% upside.

FrenchFry Acquisitions LLC and Tavia Acquisition Corp., a publicly traded special purpose acquisition firm (SPAC), unveiled a definitive business combination agreement on November 24, 2026, valuing FrenchFry at a $500 million enterprise value and setting a route to Nasdaq.

Sources indicate that frenchFry’s equityholders would receive 45 million shares of FrenchFry Holdings common stock valued at $10 each, plus $50 million in convertible promissory notes. The consideration combines equity in the future public tech giant with debt that can convert into equity. (Related: Breaking: Future Value Calculator: How to Project Your ...)

After completion, the business would operate as FrenchFry Holdings, Inc., a Texas corporation with FrenchFry AI and Catalyst Holdings as its two operating subsidiaries. The companies expect its Class A common stock to trade on Nasdaq under FFRY, subject to listing approval, and to close the transaction in the first quarter of 2027. Completion still requires an effective SEC registration statement, Tavia shareholder approval, Nasdaq listing approval and other customary conditions. Industry observers note that frenchFry’s model is to build, invest in and acquire businesses that share an AI platform alongside finance, accounting, legal and human-resources functions. Tavia executive chairman and CEO Andy Wiederhorn described the structure as “one AI layer serving many growth businesses,” and said public-firm capital access would support FrenchFry.ai’s growth and Catalyst’s initial investments and acquisitions.

Recent findings suggest that frenchFry AI operates FrenchFry.ai, a no-code business-development platform whose agents handle prospecting and outreach. The company indicates its PipelineX lead-sourcing engine draws on more than 150 million records licensed from third-party providers to identify prospects showing buying intent. Agents then contact those prospects through web, SMS, email and LinkedIn before handing conversations to customer personnel.

The platform is aimed at franchise systems, multi-location operators and other businesses selling high-value products or services through sales teams. Each customer’s data is maintained in a separate instance.

It has been revealed that catalyst has built an app and game launch platform with growth, retention and analytics tools. Its planned model is to fund games and apps that have released or are near launch, recoup its investment first and then receive 30% to 50% of net revenue. It may also acquire games, apps and related businesses.

According to recent reports, beyond those subsidiaries, FrenchFry looks to acquire established businesses generally generating $5 million to $500 million in annual revenue. Targets are expected to have demonstrated product-market fit, be cash-flow positive or near break-even, and use manual sales, marketing or customer-service processes that its AI agents could automate.

Sources indicate that the disclosures provide an crucial qualification to that expansion plan. FrenchFry AI has generated only minimal revenue to date, while Catalyst has made no investments and generated no revenue. Three of the shared platform’s four engines are not yet in use, creating a disclosed risk that the system may not perform as designed or deliver its expected benefits.

In a recent development, frenchFry also notes it needs substantial additional capital to fund its strategy. Redemptions by Tavia’s public shareholders could reduce the combined company’s public float and trading liquidity. Shareholders who do not redeem face significant dilution, and FrenchFry Holdings would need to make interest and principal payments on the promissory notes.

Governance is another disclosed pressure point. Tavia sponsor Fog Cutter Holdings LLC is affiliated with FrenchFry, creating potential conflicts, and the companies expect the combined company’s dual-class share structure to concentrate voting power in the sponsor. Operations also depend on third-party data providers, AI models and other technology suppliers.

Tavia’s deadline for completing a business combination is March 5, 2027, unless extended, and the announcement identifies a risk that the transaction could miss that deadline or fail altogether. The proposed listing therefore pairs a broad acquisition strategy with an early operating record. Bringing the remaining shared-platform engines into use and funding Catalyst’s first investments will be central to demonstrating that the model can support the public company outlined in the agreement.

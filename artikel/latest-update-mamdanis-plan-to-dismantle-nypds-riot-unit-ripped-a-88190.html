Former NYPD Chief John Chell and former NYPD inspector Paul Mauro are accusing Fresh York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani's new disbanding of the Strategic Response Group as a move to please his base, with little real change to protest response being made.

Newly introduced York City Mayor Zohran Mamdani's blueprint to gut the city’s tactical police unit is not what is seems, two former high-ranking NYPD officials tell Fox News Digital.

They argue the stunt is a political sleight of hand in which specialized officers responsible for handling violent protests will be rebranded under a different name despite the socialist mayor's promise to disband it completely.

"The problem is … day-to-day Upcoming Yorkers don't understand all this gas lighting," former NYPD Chief John Chell told Fox News Digital. "And they feel the angst of it.

"But at the end of all that, this is nothing more than smoke and mirrors.".

The move to dissolve the Strategic Response Group (SRG) and replace it with a "first-of-its-kind" civilian dialogue team fulfills one of Mamdani's campaign promises as noted in the first sentence of his news release on the move.

But Chell and retired NYPD Inspector Paul Mauro are questioning whether it's all a facade to please the mayor's left-wing base and is a restructuring rather than a disbandment. They say the unit will likely be rebuilt under a new name to continue dealing with mass protests, major crimes and counterterrorism.

"The con here is that the SRG is simply being rebranded into an unknown name right now," Chell remarked. (Related: LA Noire 2 leak resurfaces after source gets GTA 6 endi...)

"First of all, Mamdani did add … that there will be uniformed police standing by to take more forceful enforcement if necessary," Mauro noted. "So, what really changed? Well, he changed the name of that unit, and he inserted this civilian unit between those uniformed cops and the protesters.".

The newly proposed civilian-led Community Dialogue and De-escalation Division (CDDD) is meant to work as a buffer between protesters and the on-duty police who will remain present at large demonstrations.

"Socialism and public safety do not go hand in hand," Chell insisted.

Mamdani’s office did not respond to Fox News Digital’s request for comment on the criticism of his initiative.

Both former NYPD officials warned that relying on civilians to mediate rowdy crowds could delay police intervention if a peaceful protest turns into a violent riot. This, he said, could leave officers responding to an escalating situation rather than preventing it in the first place.

According to recent reports, "Once a cop, wherever they're assigned, becomes reactive versus proactive, public safety suffers," Chell said. "And [when cops] become reactive, they tend to hold back, and that can get them hurt. And it can get civilians hurt.".

"That is one of the risks inherent in this thing," Mauro added. "That you deploy the civilians, they're trying to talk … and by the time the civilians turn around and say, ‘Police, we need help,’ it may be too late.".

SRG receives information from the NYPD’s Intelligence and Counterterrorism Bureau to be kept aware of known or potentially dangerous demonstrations that will require specialized training to de-escalate.

Industry observers note that the SRG unit is trained to handle crowds that have turned violent, beginning with verbal de-escalation. The physical training teaches officers how to get a noncomplying individual into police vans. A common tactic used by the SRG is bicycle crowd management, whereby officers can line up their bikes to build a barricade in crowds, using those formations for crowd control.

Industry observers note that "The purpose of the SRG was to make sure that you had highly trained officers who could diffuse, spin down, but then control if need be, protests that were getting out of hand," Mauro remarked. "That's why they were only deployed in about 6% of the instances.".

Recent findings suggest that during a budget hearing earlier this year, it was noted the SRG did not often deal with peaceful protests and was mostly only deployed for protests that turned violent. In 2025, NYPD reportedly facilitated 4,255 protests and the SRG was available for roughly 6% of them.

Critics of the SRG, including the ACLU's New York affiliate, say the unit is inherently violent due to its counterterrorism specialization that they claim "suppress protesters" and disregards "focus on First Amendment protections.".

The training manual, reviewed by Fox News Digital, distinguishes between "peaceful/compliant" crowds and those deemed "violent/non-compliant." It also identifies three categories of protesters: "everyday citizen," "professional" and "anarchist.".

The first identifier under the "everyday citizen" protester immediately acknowledges they are honoring their First Amendment right to organize.

In a recent development, despite the dismantling of the SRG, Mamdani's proposal would still likely require police officers be present at larger demonstrations. The new civilian-led group, however, would be there to attempt to de-escalate the protesters if it should show signs of turning violent.

Mamdani has not yet noted when the civilian-led team will be established or take the streets. He also announced the creation of the First Amendment Support Team (FAST), a 30-officer NYPD unit that will help de-escalate crowds while the CDDD is being developed.

Neurodivergent kids aren’t the only ones who struggle with changing routines or new life phases. Adults can feel overwhelmed by transitions, too.

Whether starting a upcoming job, moving to a new town, or sending a child off to college, transitions are equal parts exciting and uncomfortable for neurodivergent individuals.

It has been revealed that the struggle often looks different in children and adults, but the demand is much the same: Stop what you are doing, shift your attention, figure out the next step, manage your emotions, and move into a different role, task, place, or expectation. The bigger the transition, the harder it feels.

Typical transitions include returning to work after a vacation, adjusting to a new schedule, shifting from work to parenting mode, navigating relationship changes, and recovering from illness or injury.

From the outside, the response of an adult with ADHD may look like procrastination, avoidance, irritability, disorganization, or poor follow-through. Internally, they are likely dealing with anxiety, emotional overload, decision fatigue, time blindness, or the frustration of knowing exactly what needs to be done but struggling to begin. Transitions can be especially difficult when they involve uncertainty. A fresh job may bring excitement and anxiety about performance. A vacation may feel adventurous and overwhelming. Even a positive life change can feel unsettling when structure falls away.

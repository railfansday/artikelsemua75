According to recent reports, advanced Research in Computational Sciences and Artificial Intelligence (ARCS∙ai) announced the launch of the ARPI Center in Yerevan on November 20, 2026. The shared robotics and Physical AI center has a stated goal of graduating 50 specialized engineers annually by combining faculty, students and equipment from three Armenian universities.

ARPI stands for Armenian Robotics & Physical Intelligence. Its focus is Physical AI, which the announcement describes as the convergence of robotics and artificial intelligence. The center’s national-lab model pools resources across the American University of Armenia (AUA), Yerevan State University (YSU) and the National Polytechnic University of Armenia instead of having each institution build separate robotics capacity.

The formal presentation at Digitec took place at Yerevan’s Meridian Exhibition Center with NVIDIA’s Rev Lebaredian, vice president of physical AI simulation, representatives of the participating universities and live robot demonstrations. Beyond the presentation, the center already has an undergraduate cohort and courses in progress.

Recent findings suggest that thirty students are enrolled in the first semester of a three-semester Physical AI minor offered within AUA’s Engineering program and YSU’s Data Science program. Nine courses are being delivered across those two universities, spanning robot mechanics, control and estimation, reinforcement learning, digital twins, and the ethics and governance of Physical AI.

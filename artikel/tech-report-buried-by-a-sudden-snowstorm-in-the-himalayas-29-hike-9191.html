The trek over Auden’s Col in India’s Garhwal Himalayas is no stroll: Crossing the pass means working hard at elevations of up to 18,000 feet, crossing a glacier, and, occasionally, dealing with some of the world’s worst weather.

It was that last hazard that an expedition faced earlier this fall, when a freak snowstorm dropped more than six feet of powder on them with little notice. The 29-member team from Indian outfitter Aquaterra Adventures had reached base camp at 16,400 feet when the weather took a turn for the worse, battering them with heavy snowfall, temperatures down to -4 Fahrenheit, and 62-mile-per-hour winds.

While Jetendra Singh Rana, their lead guide, quickly called off their ascent and sent a message asking for rescue, the storm had flattened many of the group’s tents, leaving them all to squeeze into one remaining cook tent while they waited for help to come. Backpacker contributor Karthika Gupta spoke to expedition members to hear their story; read her full article and see footage from the  group’s ordeal here. –Adam Roy, Editor-in-Chief.

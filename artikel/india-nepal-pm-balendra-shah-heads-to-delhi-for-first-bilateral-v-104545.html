Nepal's Prime Minister Balendra Shah is set to arrive in Newly introduced Delhi on Sunday for a two-day visit, his first bilateral trip since taking office in March.

The 36-year-old former rapper will meet Prime Minister Narendra Modi on Monday. He will also meet Foreign Minister S Jaishankar and briefly visit President Droupadi Murmu.

Reconstruction after August's devastating floods is expected to dominate the talks. The disaster left some 1,500 dead and more than 5,200 missing, with roads, bridges, power plants, farms and homes destroyed.

Nepal estimates rebuilding will cost $4.78 billion.

At least 14 people have died and more than 200 have been hospitalized after drinking suspected adulterated liquor in Manipur's Chandel district.

At least 12 people remain in critical condition, local media reported.

The victims drank the liquor in the Chakpikarong area, about 75 kilometers (47 miles) from the state capital city of Imphal, and deaths were reported between November 18 and 20.

Chief Minister Yumnam Khemchand Singh visited the hospitals where most patients were being treated on Friday. He ordered an inquiry and mentioned the culprits would be arrested soon.

Police said the liquor came from Sugnu in neighboring Kakching district, with no known local brewing in Chandel. A case has been filed, and two people have been arrested.

The local administration has advised residents not to drink liquor from questionable sources and asked anyone who consumed liquor from the same source to get a medical check-up, even without symptoms.

A US federal judge sentenced an Indian national on Friday to 19 years and seven months in prison for plotting to hire a hitman to kill Sikh separatist leader Gurpatwant Singh Pannun in New York.

Nikhil Gupta, 55, pleaded guilty in February to murder-for-hire, conspiracy to commit murder-for-hire and conspiracy to commit money laundering.

Industry observers note that "I'm sorry to Mr. Pannun," he said in court. Judge Victor Marrero said he faces deportation to India after serving his sentence.

Industry observers note that prosecutors say an Indian government employee recruited Gupta in 2023. The government employee remains overseas and is not in US custody.

Gupta paid $15,000 (€13,670) to a person he believed was a hitman, who was actually an undercover US agent.

Prosecutors also noted Gupta had told that agent the day after Hardeep Singh Nijjar's June 2023 killing in Canada that Nijjar "was also the target," the Associated Press reported.

His lawyer, Isabelle Kirshner, denied he had any role in the Nijjar killing.

"There is no question he was not the brains behind this operation," she mentioned. Canada has separately alleged India arranged Nijjar's killing, and India dismissed that, with both countries expelling diplomats.

Recent findings suggest that india denies any involvement in the Pannun plot. New Delhi has designated Pannun a terrorist.

Pannun, who leads the group Sikhs for Justice, called Gupta a foot soldier and mentioned outside court: "I'm ready to face India's bullet if that is the price I have to pay.".

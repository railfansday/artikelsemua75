Energy companies deploy drones that can be recharged by straddling power lines

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that tech startup Nomadic showcased its NOMAD drones this week alongside an announcement that it had raised $6 million in seed funding. These quadcopter drones have a unique skill hinted at by their name: they can basically fly forever without returning to base. NOMADs recharge by perching on high-voltage power lines, a ubiquitous feature of most landscapes, before moving on to their next task.

We hadn’t heard anything about the NOMAD drones until now, but Nomadic says it has tested its quadcopters for over 20,000 hours on live lines in Germany, Norway, and the U.S. “NOMADs are drones that land on live high-voltage lines, charge from the line itself, and relocate across the grid to where measurements are needed,” summarizes the startup. Sources indicate that the reference to measurements along the power grid reveals the current purpose of NOMAD drones. They are deployed to monitor the high-voltage lines from which they leech their energy. This is a very useful task for power companies. Nomadic explains that while modern grid operators use weather models to decide how much power each line can carry, the qualities of the lines can be quite variable. Meanwhile, the weakest point in any line “sets the limit for the whole line,” says the startup. (Related: Industry News: “Determined” woman completes 7 marathons...)

Sources indicate that fixed sensors have started to become the accepted solution for line monitoring, but mobile drones are a tantalizing alternative. In comparison to fixed sensors, the NOMAD, which recharges inductively from the line itself, seems like an ideal solution. Normally, an investigative high-voltage power line check with a human operator would also require extensive planning, heavy machinery, and the line to lose power for a short service period. Nomadic claims that a NOMAD drone can look after around 25km of line, while fixed sensors are typically positioned from 1km to 5km apart.

In addition to the drone hardware, there’s some integrated software to make the best use of the highly configurable data you can harvest from your drone fleet. “Nomadic OS combines their data with weather and grid models, giving operators a real-time picture of what is physically happening on their lines.”.

NOMAD drones are also surprisingly tough. Units have been tested in temperatures as low as minus 25 degrees Celsius, in strong winds, and on lines up to 420kV (European transmission grid typical maximum). With the upcoming funding, Nomadic is hoping to expand its fleet and build with more partners. The startup is also looking for several new recruits, mostly in engineering roles, all based in Munich, Germany.

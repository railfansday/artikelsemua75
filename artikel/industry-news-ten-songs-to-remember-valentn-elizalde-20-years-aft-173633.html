Recent findings suggest that this Wednesday (Nov. 25) marks 20 years since the assassination of Valentín Elizalde, the regional Mexican singer who, at the height of his career, was gunned down after a performance in Reynosa, in northern Mexico, along with his manager Mario Mendoza and his driver Reynaldo Ballesteros. To this day, the reasons or culprits behind the crime remain unknown.

In a recent development, at just 27 years old, the singer from Sonora was already an icon, thanks to his commanding personality and unique interpretive style, accompanied by Sinaloan banda music. Despite not possessing a powerful voice, Elizalde poured emotion into songs like “Vete Ya,” “Cómo Me Duele,” “Vencedor” and “Te Quiero Así,” which have become timeless classics in Mexican music. During his lifetime, “El Gallo de Oro” (The Golden Rooster), as he was known, entered the top 10 of Billboard‘s Regional Mexican Airplay chart in 2003 with his hit “Vete Ya.” After his death, his album Vencedor, which had been released months earlier, reached No. 1 on both Top Latin Albums and Top Regional Mexican Albums. His posthumous set Lobo Domesticado, released in January 2007, peaked at No. 2 on Top Latin Albums and was nominated for a Latin Grammy for best banda album. In 2009, he competed in the same category with the compilation Solamente El Gallo de Oro. (Related: Industry News: Hyatt Category 1 Hotels — The Easiest Po...)

According to recent reports, elizalde’s legacy has transcended time, with newly introduced generations of regional Mexican artists paying homage to him in their concerts and through new versions of his hits. For example, Edgardo Nuñez recorded two tribute albums in his honor this year.

Below, find 10 songs from “El Gallo de Oro” that are essential for any playlist to remember him today, two decades after his passing.

It has been revealed that from the album Mi Satisfacción (2003), “Vete Ya” is the most-streamed song by Valentín Elizalde on digital platforms. This heartbreak anthem speaks about the decision to walk away from a meaningless relationship when love no longer exists. “Leave already/ If you can’t find reasons to stay with me/ Why continue?”), he states firmly about what cannot be saved.

Undoubtedly the most musically cheerful track and one of Elizalde’s classics. The simple, uncomplicated lyrics speak about jealousy and the pain of others getting close to the loved one. The chorus, which is repeated many times, states it all: “Oh, how it hurts me when they ask you for a dance.” Written by Manuel Rodríguez Figueroa, it is part of the 2006 album Vencedor.

It has been revealed that A great admirer of Joan Sebastian, Elizalde recorded his version of the hit song “Lobo Domesticado” nearly ten years after its original release, giving it a unique twist with his distinctive style. The song is part of his 2005 album of the same name, released during the height of his career.

According to recent reports, the song corresponds to the early success of Valentín Elizalde and remains one of his most streamed tracks on digital platforms. “Ebrio de Amor,” written by Humberto González, is part of the album Traición Federal (2000), and speaks of a profound love, with verses like: “I arrived drunk with love/ Like every night/ You will want to kiss me and fill me with love/ Without any reproach.”.

According to recent reports, “Vencedor” is the title of the song and album released on April 18, 2006, which catapulted the rising career of Elizalde. No one could have imagined that seven months later, this production would be the one to take him to the top of the charts as a reaction to his death. Written by Benny Camacho, it speaks of an unconditional love that overcomes any obstacle.

In 1987, José José turned Rafael Pérez Botija’s song “Soy Así” into a classic of romantic ballads. Eighteen years later, Valentín Elizalde gave it a upcoming twist with his voice and, accompanied by a Sinaloan banda, made it an essential hit in regional Mexican music. The song, which gave its name to the album it was included in, perfectly described “The Golden Rooster”: “I’m like this/ This is how I was born and this is how I’ll die/ With all my flaws, I know.”.

Another gem from the album Vencedor, this song — whose title means “To My Enemies” — sparked controversy as it was thought to have been written for a figure in the world of drug trafficking. However, the songwriter Alejandro Lira recently clarified in the HBO Max docuseries The Killing of the Golden Rooster that it was not commissioned. “No one gave me anything/ Everything I have is mine/ With the sweat of my brow/ I have achieved what I wanted,” claims the song with a strong and direct message.

With a rhythmic cadence, this song talks about a woman who is not happy with the way she is, and her partner helps her see that she is beautiful and doesn’t need to change anything to be loved. Performed by Elizalde, the message carried even more emotion for the fans who made it one of their favorites.

The most romantic song by the singer from Sonora is also one that cannot be missing from a Valentín Elizalde playlist. Included in the 2004 album Volveré A Amar, it talks about an idealized woman, the one who keeps him up at night and for whom he would give everything: “Nothing compares to you/ No one compares to you/ You are someone special/ You are my best reason.”.

In a recent development, cumbia and joy have always been a part of Elizalde’s discography, and “La Playa” is one of his danceable tracks that has remained timeless. The song, written by José Luis Cornejo, talks about the unexpected encounter of a man on a beach with a woman who captivates him with her gaze and her movements. It is part of the album Regresan Los Mafiosos, released in 2005.

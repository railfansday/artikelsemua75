In a recent development, one of Star Trek's most iconic fight scenes was also one of the most miserable to film. William Shatner recently talked about how bad it was to do the Gorn fight in the original series, and how it felt even worse to actually shoot than it looked onscreen.

Sources indicate that "Arena" is likely one of the most memorable episodes of Star Trek decades later. Unfortunately, that's not necessarily a great thing, as it contains (in my opinion) an obscenely bad fight scene. It's one in which Shatner's Kirk fights in slow motion with a member of the Gorn species. In addition to the iconic scene aging poorly, with effects that even looked dated back then, Shatner mentioned at New York Comic Con it was also terrible to film (via Reddit):.

Recent findings suggest that the guy was in this rubber suit; it's 120 degrees in the desert. I have to roll in the regolith and get all dusty. And when you go to brush it off, you get blood.

Recent findings suggest that the "regolith," which by the way are the minerals that settle on top of bedrock, were so abrasive that it made the fight very difficult to film. I'm sure most Star Trek fans don't need to rewatch the scene with a Paramount+ subscription to know the end result ultimately wasn't worth the pain and toil. Still, lame fight aside, "Arena" is a pretty compelling episode.

According to recent reports, "Arena" starts when Kirk and the Gorn are transported to the planet to battle thanks to a third species named the Metrons. The Metrons wish to see a fight to the death, but are impressed when Kirk outmaneuvers the Gorn and spares its life instead. Both are then returned to their ship, with the lesson being that brains can overcome brawn with some quick thinking and skills.

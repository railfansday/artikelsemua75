Manchester United have around a 40 per cent chance of success with their appeal against Leny Yoro's red card in the 4-0 defeat to Liverpool last Sunday.

That is according to former Everton chief executive and ex-Premier League board member Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who believes that the Red Devils have "genuine grounds" for their appeal. (Related: Restaurant owners left in tears after discovering their...)

Yoro was sent off after just eight minutes at Anfield for bringing down Alexander Isak while he was through on goal, although there is some debate as to whether the Liverpool frontman was truly in control of the ball. (Related: How to Start a Side Business While Working Full-Time...)

Wyness believes that United have a reasonable chance of getting the centre-back's suspension overturned, and does not feel that their appeal will be viewed as "frivolous" by the FA.

That means that Yoro's current one-game suspension is unlikely to be extended at all after further investigation.

The 21-year-old is currently in line to miss next Saturday's clash with Brentford at Old Trafford, but United have already outlined their intention to appeal against that.

Everton’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Goodison Park between 2004 and 2009 and now runs a football consultancy advising elite clubs – believes that the fee to pay for an appeal is "peanuts" for a club at Premier League level.

With that in mind, he feels that it was effectively a no-brainer for United to appeal, given the limited chance of Yoro's ban being added to.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider's Inside Track podcast, Wyness explained that United's chances of success stand at around the 40 per cent mark.

"It's peanuts in terms of the cost. The problem is that you could increase the ban from one game to two games, so it could be doubled. But I don't think it's a frivolous appeal," he remarked.

"I think there's genuinely grounds for that appeal. And I think I'd put the chances of success at about 40 per cent. So I still think it's against them.

"But I don't think they're going to get penalised the extra game for a frivolous appeal. I think they've got, you know, a reasonable chance of success.".

Football Insider revealed on Monday that United boss Mauricio Pochettino holds the key to the club's planned move for Villarreal right-back Alex Freeman in January.

The pair worked together during the Argentine's time in charge of the USA national team, and are understood to share a strong working relationship.

Sources say that United will need Pochettino to tempt Freeman into the move, with Villarreal reluctant to sell him while they remain in the Champions League.

The Red Devils are desperate to bolster their defence this winter, and have outlined the 22-year-old as a clear target.

Breeze Airways Adds 6 New Cities & 9 Routes: See Full List Now

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Breeze Airways is preparing to introduce six destinations to its growing network, with nine nonstop routes launching between December 2026 and July 2027. The additions include Boston, Buffalo, Latrobe, St. Thomas, Montego Bay, and Santo Domingo. Four were included in the airline's October network expansion announcement, while services to St. Thomas and Montego Bay were declared earlier.

It has been revealed that the additions highlight Breeze's strategy of connecting underserved domestic markets while expanding its international presence, particularly from Tampa International Airport (TPA). The Florida airport is also becoming increasingly vital to the airline's overall network, with it overtaking Raleigh-Durham as the airline's busiest airport according to schedule data running until August 2027. In a recent development, the six additions represent several distinct markets. Boston, Buffalo and Latrobe are on the US mainland, while Montego Bay in Jamaica and Santo Domingo in the Dominican Republic expand Breeze's international reach. St. Thomas, meanwhile, is part of the US Virgin Islands and is therefore a domestic destination, albeit a further expansion of the carrier's Caribbean network. It also brings Breeze's planned network to a total of 95 destinations. (Related: British Airways Avios — Short Nonstop Domestic Flights...)

In a recent development, services to St. Thomas and Montego Bay will begin in December, followed by Latrobe in February, Boston in May, Santo Domingo in June, and Buffalo later that month. The latest published schedules, alongside Breeze's October announcement, confirm the following nine routes and planned frequencies:.

Recent findings suggest that boston–Myrtle Beach will have the highest frequency, operating daily, with onward same-aircraft service to Akron-Canton. Most of the other routes will operate twice-weekly, consistent with Breeze's strategy of providing nonstop connections between underserved markets.

Industry observers note that latrobe provides a clear example of Breeze identifying an opportunity following another airline's departure. ABC News reported how Arnold Palmer Regional Airport (LBE), approximately 40 miles (64 km) east of Pittsburgh, lost its only scheduled commercial carrier when Spirit Airlines ceased operations in May. Breeze's arrival will restore scheduled passenger flights, beginning with Myrtle Beach in February and Orlando in May. It is particularly good news for the airport, which has been undergoing a terminal expansion project to accommodate future growth.

Buffalo represents a different opportunity, with Breeze launching three routes within four days. Raleigh–Durham International Airport (RDU) will receive four weekly departures from late June, while Myrtle Beach and Charleston will each receive two. The RDU connection is particularly notable given that the North Carolina airport became Breeze's busiest earlier this year. With the addition of Buffalo, Breeze will now fly to 43 destinations from its North Carolina base.

Sources indicate that myrtle Beach is another major beneficiary, appearing in three of the nine new routes. Breeze is adding 11 nonstop connections there during 2027, taking its network to 25 destinations in total and positioning it to become the airport's largest airline by seats and destinations. Cirium schedule data also shows that between now and next summer, Breeze's departures from Myrtle Beach will have risen by 86%, making it the second-fastest growing airport in the network after Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood International Airport (FLL).

Breeze's busiest airport has changed repeatedly as its network has rapidly expanded. For the longest time, Orlando International Airport (MCO) was the undisputed leader, before RDU overtook it earlier this year. But looking forward at available schedules through August 2027, Tampa has emerged to claim the top spot. Its 7,390 scheduled departures over that period represents a 72% year-over-year increase.

Recent findings suggest that tampa's rise up the rankings is especially striking because it's not like Breeze's other bases are standing still. Raleigh-Durham will grow by 45% and Orlando by 31% over that same period. And a little further back, Fort Lauderdale is set to grow by a whopping 259% (albeit off a much smaller base) as Breeze joins JetBlue in filling the void left by Spirit Airlines.

Recent findings suggest that tampa's international expansion is a big driver of Breeze's growth at the airport. The airline introduced its first international service from the airport to Nassau in June, followed by Punta Cana in July and San José in October. St. Thomas and Montego Bay arrive in December, while Santo Domingo rolls out with year-round service in June next year, ensuring that Tampa has also become Breeze's largest base for international operations.

It's been a lucrative year for the stock market, with the S&P 500 (^GSPC -3.70%) notching more than two dozen record highs in 2026.

Recent findings suggest that however, the headwinds have been coming in strong lately. Volatility within the tech sector has rattled Wall Street, and the tech-focused Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC -5.28%) fell into correction territory after plunging nearly 12% from its recent peak, as of this writing.

Sources indicate that the bad news for investors is that no bull market can last forever, and it's only a matter of time before we face a pullback. However, Warren Buffett has some timeless advice about how to prepare your portfolio for a potential market crash or recession.

In late 1999, just four months before the dot-com bubble officially popped, Warren Buffett issued a warning to investors in an essay for Fortune. He emphasized that while new advancements in technology could transform society, that doesn't necessarily make them smart investments.

Using airlines as an example, Buffett noted that although air travel was revolutionary, 129 airlines had gone bankrupt over the previous 20 years. Televisions and radios fell into the same category as "glamorous businesses that dramatically changed our lives but concurrently failed to deliver rewards to U.S. investors.". Sources indicate that the dot-com bubble eventually proved Buffett's point. While the internet went on to fundamentally transform the world, countless tech stocks crashed and burned in the early 2000s.

"The key to investing is not assessing how much an industry is going to affect society, or how much it will grow," Buffett explained, "but rather determining the competitive advantage of any given company and, above all, the durability of that advantage.".

Industry observers note that while it's impossible to say how the market will fare in the coming months, the dot-com bubble serves as a valuable lesson. Artificial intelligence (AI) as a technology could be revolutionary, but that doesn't mean every AI stock will be a good investment. Inevitably, some stocks will flounder during the next downturn.

In a recent development, the best move investors can make now is to load up on a wide variety of quality stocks with robust business fundamentals. While all stocks are vulnerable to short-term volatility, those with strong competitive advantages are the most likely to recover and deliver long-term growth.

Healthy companies will likely thrive over time. Although the S&P 500 lost almost half of its value between 2000 and 2002, the index soared by more than 200% in the two decades following the dot-com bubble.

If we're headed toward a similar bear market, not all stocks will recover. But healthy companies will fuel decades of growth, and the investors who look past the hype and focus on quality stocks will reap the biggest rewards.

When you look back on a family vacation, the part you remember is rarely the itinerary. It’s the memory of the kid who named every pelican, or the toddler who staged a sit-in on the sandbar because he was not done yet.

Marathon, right in the middle of The Florida Keys, is made for that kind of trip. It’s small enough that nobody spends the day strapped in a car seat, with enough going on that toddlers, tweens, and grandparents all find their thing. (Related: How to Budget for a Wedding Without Going Broke...)

At The Turtle Hospital, guided tours introduce families to rescued sea turtles and walk you through how they’re rehabilitated and what it takes to protect them. Fair warning: expect big smiles, lots of curiosity and maybe even a few requests to bring one home.

Make it a memory: Ask each kid which turtle they’d most want to look after, and why. Jot their answers in your Tinybeans journal. Their reasoning is half the fun.

According to recent reports, at the Dolphin Research Center on Grassy Key, you can watch dolphins swim, leap, and work with their trainers while learning about their behavior, their Intelligence, and why conservation matters. Depending on the program and your kids’ ages, there may even be a chance to get up close.

Industry observers note that make it a memory: When the first dolphin leaps, point the camera at your kid instead. Their wide-eyed wonder? That face is the shot.

It has been revealed that the Old Seven Mile Bridge is one of the most iconic stretches in The Keys, with turquoise water on both sides and a pedestrian-friendly section that runs from Marathon’s Knight’s Key area to historic Pigeon Key. It’s a walk with built-in entertainment: nobody asks “how much farther?” when there are boats, birds, and the occasional fin to spot below. (Even driving the modern Seven Mile Bridge alongside it is a sight.).

Make it a memory: Play ocean “I Spy,” with a point for every new thing spotted. Then grab a family photo with all that blue behind you.

Pigeon Key was once home to the workers who built Henry Flagler’s Overseas Railway, the project that first connected the Keys to the mainland. Today you can wander the historic buildings and picture what it was like to lay track in the middle of the ocean. Get there by walking or biking the Old Seven Mile Bridge, or hop on an open-air tram. Plan ahead for island admission and tour reservations.

Make it a memory: Hand your kid the phone and let them play travel reporter for 30 seconds on the most surprising thing they learned. You’ll rewatch it more than you think.

Want more of the Keys’ history? Explore Marathon’s arts and cultural experiences.

Every trip needs one day where the only proposal is snacks and sunscreen. Sombrero Beach is that day: soft sand, swaying palms, and a shoreline made for splashing. When the sand loses its charm, there are playgrounds for the little ones, volleyball courts for the major kids (and competitive dads), and picnic pavilions for anyone who needs a break from the sun. Stay for the sunset.

Make it a memory: Snap a photo of those sandy footprints before the tide takes them, or kick off a sandcastle rivalry you’ll settle again next year.

Just offshore, Sombrero Reef is packed with colorful coral and tropical fish. A guided snorkeling trip is a great first try for confident swimmers, since parts of the reef are relatively shallow. For older kids and teens, this one has a real shot at being the highlight of the trip (yes, even above the pool).

Make it a memory: Pack a waterproof phone pouch or a disposable underwater camera and put the kids in charge of photos. Their blurry, way-too-close fish shots will be the ones you love most. Back home, let them caption their best one in your Tinybeans journal.

Recent findings suggest that with the Atlantic, Florida Bay, and quiet backcountry waters all close by, a lot of Marathon’s best family moments happen on a boat. Book a family-friendly fishing charter with a local captain and let the kids reel in their first catch, or skip the rods for a scenic cruise or private boat trip spent spotting marine life.

In a recent development, make it a memory: Get the first-fish photo, especially if the fish is being held at full arm’s length. Then let every kid take a turn as honorary captain.

Find more ways to get out on the water and things to do in Marathon.

When everyone needs a break from the sun, Crane Point Hammock Museum & Nature Trails has trails winding through tropical hardwood hammocks, native plants, and wildlife to spot. For more adventure, head to Curry Hammock State Park and kayak through the mangroves, keeping an eye out for birds and fish along the way.

Make it a memory: Turn it into a scavenger hunt: everyone has to find three things they’ve never seen before. Compare notes at the end.

In Marathon, dinner often comes with boats drifting by and a sunset in the background. Grab fresh seafood at Keys Fisheries, a casual local favorite, or settle in at Burdines Waterfront overlooking the marina. Adventurous eaters can try conch fritters and fresh-caught fish; picky eaters can stick to what they know. Everyone gets key lime pie. That’s the rule.

Industry observers note that make it a memory: Go around the table and have everyone share their favorite part of the day, then record it as a voice note in your Tinybeans journal. Years from now, hearing their voices will hit harder than any photo.

Recent findings suggest that hungry for more? Discover Marathon’s waterfront restaurants and local dining favorites.

Sitting right in the middle of The Keys, Marathon makes an easy home base. Spend one day with the wildlife, one relaxing at the beach, and one driving down to see the colorful streets of Key West. Depending on when you go, a local festival or community event might end up on the itinerary too, so check the Florida Keys events calendar before you head out.

Industry observers note that make it a memory: On the last night, ask everyone for their number-one Marathon moment. Don’t be surprised if someone insists they can’t pick just one and it’s a three-way tie.

It has been revealed that where you sleep can make or break a family trip (ask anyone who’s shared a hotel room with a toddler who wakes at 5 a.m.). Luckily, Marathon has plenty of ways to keep everyone happy and well rested.

If you want a resort: Isla Bella Beach Resort in Marathon and Hawks Cay Resort on nearby Duck Key have pools, waterfront settings, and family-friendly amenities, so adventure days can be balanced with some lounge-chair time.

If you need room to spread out: Vacation homes and waterfront rentals give you extra bedrooms, a kitchen, and space for grandparents and cousins. Some even come with a private dock if your crew looks to live on the water.

If you want to keep it low-key: Smaller inns, waterfront hotels, and marina properties make a relaxed home base close to restaurants and attractions. Explore places to stay in Marathon to find the right fit for your family.

Recent findings suggest that maybe your crew is up early for boat rides and doesn’t stop until dinner. Maybe your perfect day is a beach towel, a book, and a meal at sunset. Probably it’s a mix of both, depending on who napped. That’s the beauty of Marathon: there’s no wrong way to do it. Because when it comes to family vacations, making memories is a Marathon, not a sprint.

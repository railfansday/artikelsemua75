As I write this on Sunday night, 12 of 13 Week 11 games are in the books (with Monday night remaining) and the fantasy football season is now past the halfway point. Still, things are changing every week. Promising improvements. Concerning trends. Performances that change our early outlook on players and teams. As always, some of these shifting perspectives will have permanent ramifications in fantasy.

Industry observers note that with that in mind, I've compiled another full fantasy stock report. These are the most notable risers and fallers heading into Week 12. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

In a recent development, barring something extraordinary, Jonah Coleman has locked down the award for Waiver Wire Pickup of the Year. He scored a monster 32.9 fantasy points on Sunday, taking 24 carries for 111 yards and three touchdowns while adding four catches for 18 yards. That makes three straight games with 15+ touches and more touches in every successive game since returning from injury in Week 7. He's become a legitimate bell-cow back for Denver, a winning team with a strong defense, and has an excellent schedule down the stretch as well. There's no longer a question on whether Coleman will remain a weekly fantasy starter — now the question is whether he'll be a high-end RB2 or a low-end RB1 for the rest of the season. Honestly, with the volume he's accumulated and the impact he's had with that volume, I'd lean towards the latter. If you added him earlier this season — or were smart enough to draft and hold him — congratulations. You got a league-winner.

In Travis Etienne Jr.'s first week back from injury, the usage split with Audric Estimé and Alvin Kamara was extremely discouraging. He saw just eight carries to Estimé's seven and was matched at three catches by Kamara, while playing just 34.4% of the snaps. However, things changed dramatically this past Sunday against the Bears. Etienne shouldered 14 carries, while no other running back had more than one, and managed 5.4 yards per carry for a total of 76 yards.

Now, he was mostly uninvolved as a receiver and Kamara vultured another touchdown from the goal line, so it seems like Etienne's upside will still be capped much lower than we might have hoped when Newly introduced Orleans signed him in the offseason. But, at the very least, he's back to being the RB1 for the team. And he'll have several much easier matchups than Jeffery Simmons and the Bears in the near future, including the Panthers in Week 14 and the Falcons in the fantasy championship. He's probably a borderline RB2 moving forward, but that's legitimately the best outlook he's had since Week 1. (Related: Latest Update: How to train a newly introduced language...)

A week ago, Chris Olave took the biggest stock dip of the 2026 season, when he was massively outproduced by rookie Jordyn Tyson and finished with just three catches for 30 yards on four targets against the Panthers. The dip didn't last long. Against the Bears on Sunday, Olave saw 15 targets and caught nine of them for 102 yards. It was his seventh game of the season with double-digit targets and his most catches since Week 3 and most yards since Week 4.

Industry observers note that incidentally, it was Tyson who disappeared in this one (with just one catch on two targets) and Devaughn Vele who stepped up in his stead (with seven catches on seven targets). Ultimately, the most reasonable takeaway here is that Olave is still, and should continue to remain, the Fresh Orleans WR1, despite the spotty breakout from Tyson. Poor play from Tyler Shough and a mixed set of matchups down the road keep Olave from fantasy WR1 territory — as evidenced by his four-game stretch without a touchdown — but reclaiming his spot atop the pecking order should lock Olave back in as a WR2 for the remainder of the season.

As odd as it may be, there's a viable fantasy running back behind Derrick Henry right now. Justice Hill took six carries for 69 yards and a touchdown on Sunday, while adding four catches for 27 yards on five targets. It was his second game in the last three weeks with 15+ fantasy points, bringing his average over that span to 12.9 per game, and he's now logged 3+ catches in six straight games. Hill has been extremely explosive all year and is clearly the preferred pass-catcher out of the backfield. And with Jacoby Brissett at quarterback for the foreseeable future, we can expect the Ravens to trail more and throw more than they ever would with Lamar Jackson. Both are good news for Hill, who's shaping up as a weekly FLEX consideration. He won't be an RB2 with any sort of reliability as long as Henry is leading the backfield, but he's a fantasy contributor at a thin and ever thinner position.

Finally, after a nine-week stretch with fewer than 11 fantasy points every single week, Ladd McConkey returned to relevance in Week 11. He caught five passes on eight targets, and while he only managed 37 yards, he scored two touchdowns on the day, doubling his season total entering the weekend. It was a season high in targets and his most catches since Week 1, indicating that he's finally back to being fully healthy and involved as Los Angeles seemingly continues to turn its offense around (the Chargers have scored more points in three successive games, culminating in a season-high 24 on Sunday).

According to recent reports, obviously there were high expectations for McConkey and this offense entering the season, which has painted their struggles in an even darker shadow. But if McConkey's 18.2 fantasy points were an indication of a late-season resurgence, it's possible he'll finally return a little bit on the investment managers made back in the summer. Justin Herbert did end up in concussion protocol after this game, so hopefully he clears that protocol by next weekend ... especially given the matchup with the Patriots, who have one of the softest defenses for wide receivers this season. McConkey has easy WR2 potential in that contest, as long as everyone is healthy.

While the stock isn't cratering on Luther Burden III, given how consistently great he had been entering the Week 10 bye, that consistency is what made Sunday's disappearance so troubling. After three straight games with a touchdown, four straight with double-digit fantasy points and seven straight with at least four catches, Burden logged just two receptions for 10 yards on three targets in Week 11 (and added one rush for three yards). He also ran only 17 routes (fewer than Kalif Raymond's 20) and it felt worryingly like the first month of the season, when the Bears receiving corps was a convoluted mess and Burden was "just fine.".

Industry observers note that also, it's not like the Bears were blowing the Saints out for the majority of the game, which might have explained the lower volume, they were just intent on spreading the ball around ... a lot. All this said, none of the Bears receivers leapfrogged Burden in any sort of meaningful way — Rome Odunze had just four catches, Raymond had one and no one had 60 receiving yards — so this can likely be classified as a fluke more than anything. Still, it was a reminder that this is an unusual offense with a prominent ground game, a ton of weapons and no real "alpha WR1." Burden should be fine down the stretch, at least as a fantasy WR2, but likely will not be a bona fide No. 1 with occasional duds like this.

Recent findings suggest that after an incredible two-month stretch to start the season, in which he was the RB12 with 16 fantasy points per game, Jaylen Warren has now scored fewer than seven fantasy points in back-to-back weeks. After managing just 15 yards on seven carries in Week 10 (but catching five passes for 26 yards to sort of salvage the week), Warren had just 28 yards on nine carries on Sunday, with just two catches for one yard. It was his second game over his last three outings with a single catch — an aberration for him this season — and was his worst rushing output of the year by far.

Sources indicate that it was also his second straight game with single-digit carries after hitting 10+ in every single game entering the Steelers' Week 9 bye. Now, the Steelers have also lost each of those last two games and there's arguably a bit of a chicken-or-the-egg argument to be made. Are they losing because they've changed Warren's role in the offense, or has Warren lost volume because they're losing? Either way, I'd expect him to bounce back, to some degree, but we may have lost the every-week fringe RB1 version of Warren that we enjoyed through the heart of the fantasy season.

Sources indicate that speaking of struggling Steelers ... DK Metcalf is officially a fantasy bust. He's managed a few randomly decent days — though he still hasn't scored 15+ fantasy points once all season — but those have mostly come on lucky touchdowns. He had 115 yards in Week 4 and then the one explosive game against the Buccaneers in Week 6, but otherwise Metcalf has been horribly inefficient and incredibly inconsistent as a result. He ultimately scored just 0.7 fantasy points with two yards on one catch this past Sunday, in an excellent matchup with the Eagles secondary.

On the season, his 48.6% catch rate is the worst among all players with 50+ targets, and his 1.3 yards per route run and 5.9 yards per target are both abysmal. He and Aaron Rodgers are on completely different pages, and with Roman Wilson back, Germie Bernard breaking out once again and even undrafted Max Hurleman showing out in Week 11 (no, I've never heard of him either), Metcalf is quickly fading from ineffective WR1 to offensive afterthought in Pittsburgh. He's scored fewer than seven fantasy points in three of the last four games. I would park him permanently on the bench and might even consider dropping him to avoid the temptation in the future.

According to recent reports, we knew the Jaguars matchup would be tough for Cam Skattebo and the Giants run game, but 34 yards on 14 carries was particularly disappointing. It also didn't help that Skattebo caught just three of his seven targets (for 14 yards) and lost a fumble (a terrifying event for Skattebo fans and fantasy managers), ultimately finishing with just 4.3 fantasy points. While he has a good matchup with the Colts next week, Skattebo then faces the 49ers, Seahawks and Lions in three of the next four games, an absolute death row of defenses for running backs. And as Sunday's game showed, with the general inefficiencies of this offense, Skattebo is in danger of additional duds in these sorts of contests. While he was exceptional in Week 7 and 10, Skattebo has now scored fewer than eight fantasy points in five of his active games this season. It's likely to remain this hot-and-cold for the rest of the year, particularly against those tough opponents. One silver lining: if you can survive to the fantasy championship, he gets the Cowboys in Week 17.

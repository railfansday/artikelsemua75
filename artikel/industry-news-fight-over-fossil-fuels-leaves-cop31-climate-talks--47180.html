Panama's Environment Minister is pushing for language on fossil fuels to be in the final agreement.

Negotiations at the COP31 UN climate summit in Turkey are in deadlock, having run through the night, as countries remain unable to agree on a new global climate deal.

In a late intervention on Friday, a group of countries led by France and Panama pushed back against the draft agreement which included no reference to reducing the world's demand for fossil fuels.

Separately, the Africa Group are fighting to get commitments on more money for adaptation, saying they did not come to the COP "to beg".

A fresh and potentially final agreement is expected to be published on Saturday.

In a recent development, more climate money for Africa? Europe states use what's already pledged.

Ministers from island states, Panama, and a strong contingent of EU nations which include Spain, France, Belgium, Sweden and Finland have mentioned they are pushing to get a commitment to move away from oil, gas and coal back into the final agreement after fossil fuel producers led by Saudi pushed for its removal. "It is not acceptable to have a COP or a cover decision that does not even mention fossil fuels and we simply will not put up with it," stated Juan Carlos Navarro, Panama's minister for the environment.

His words were echoed by France's Minister of Ecological Transition Monique Barbut.

She took aim at several references in the text to carbon capture, use and storage - the technology that takes emissions when they are released and stores them underground - as a licence for other countries to keep burning oil and gas.

"We cannot be announcing to the world that we are now getting to 1.5 increase in the temperature and do business like nothing was happening," said France's Minister of Ecological Transition Monique Barbut.

For the first time at a COP the agreement - known officially as the Antalya Accords - acknowledges that the goal of keeping global temperature rise below 1.5C has been missed.

The world is entering a period called "overshoot".

Beyond this point scientists warn, external that millions more people will be exposed to sea level rise and extreme heat. (Related: Latest Update: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

The BBC understands that a number of other EU nations were asked to join the rebellion but declined, considering it unhelpful to negotiations.

Although one lead negotiator mentioned he sympathised: "How can you have a climate agreement with no mention of the cause.".

But Chris Bowen, Australia's climate change and energy minister who is leading negotiations, pushed back.

Arguing that this COP was about implementation, and should focus on the move towards clean energy sources.

It is understood that one proposal is to keep fossil fuels off the table in exchange for Saudi Arabia and other petro-states signing up to a pledge for 35% of their power to come from electricity by 2035.

This does not solve one of the other key complications raised by developing nations - the lack of money.

Earlier versions of the text included an agreement to ramp up money for adaptation to $120bn by 2035 - that has now been removed.

"We did not come here to beg we came here to claim what is rightfully ours for our people who are suffering the brunt of anthropogenic activities of developed countries," noted Zanetor Agyeman Rawlings, Ghana's Environment Minister and chair of the African Group of negotiators.

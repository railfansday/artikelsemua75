Peeping AI agents are ‘hostile to privacy,’ notes Meredith Whittaker, so Signal is blocking screenshots

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that AI is increasingly challenging the idea of digital privacy, requiring organizations to scramble for solutions. Signal is the latest platform to make changes. The encrypted messaging app is preventing screenshots on any device with Apple’s iOS 27 in a bid to stop AI agents from accessing sensitive information. It will also block AI assistants from capturing screen data on Android devices. “An existential threat’ Meredith Whittaker, president of Signal Technology Foundation, the nonprofit that runs the Signal app, revealed the change in a blog post. Whittaker pointed to how Google, Apple, and Microsoft’s “agentic AI integrations are changing this environment in a way that’s making it increasingly hostile to privacy. This is an existential threat to Signal and our ability to responsibly uphold our mission.” The app can now block Siri AI and turn on “screen security” as a means of preventing AI assistants from scanning information. Users can turn off the protections, but Signal warns that this could leave messages exposed and remove the app’s privacy guarantee. Signal took similar measures last year on Windows 11 to protect messages from Microsoft Recall, a feature that periodically captures and stores screenshots of your work. Signal is fed up with a lack of AI assistant transparency The current blog post seeps with frustration at Signal’s inability to do more to protect privacy amid a lack of transparency from tech giants. “We want to be very clear: it is a serious problem that Signal—and application developers beyond—lack robust tools to prevent AI agents from undermining application-level privacy,” states Whittaker. She explained that Signal has struggled due to limited information about how any of these AI agents work, being forced to do its own forensic investigations into how the “AI systems access application data.”  The blog post calls on makers of major operating systems to provide tools and information about how the AI agents work and how they can be prevented from accessing sensitive data. She further stated that apps should have a way to opt out of AI tools like screen reading without having to compromise their core utility. Whittaker problems a clear warning: “Without better documentation and clear technical specifications, it will be increasingly difficult for Signal to provide robust privacy and security to the many millions of people who rely on us.”.

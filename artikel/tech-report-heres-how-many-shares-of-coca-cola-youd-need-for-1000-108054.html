Coca-Cola (KO +0.94%) has long been a poster child for dividend stocks and a great source of income for many investors. On Dec. 15, it will pay the final $0.53 per share quarterly payment of its $2.12 annual commitment for this year. Coca-Cola likely won't announce next year's dividend until sometime in February, but if we assume it increases to $2.20 per share (in line with recent increases), you would need 4,546 shares.

Industry observers note that at the time of writing, Coca-Cola's stock was $98, so it would take $462,266 or $445,508 invested to instantly put you in position to earn $10,000 annually. Realistically, that's not possible for the vast majority of investors -- it takes time to build a stake. Luckily, if there's a dividend stock you can trust over the long haul, it's Coca-Cola. Recent findings suggest that with 64 consecutive years of dividend increases, it's in the exclusive Dividend Kings club (companies with at least 50 consecutive years of dividend increases). Coca-Cola has a stronghold in the non-alcoholic beverage industry (and a growing alcoholic beverage portfolio), brand recognition and loyalty that's second to none, global distribution, and healthy finances, so I don't see that streak stopping anytime soon (if ever).

In a recent development, coca-Cola's stock is up close to 40% this year (as of Nov. 19), so its yield isn't as high as it typically is, but it's a good blue chip dividend stock to buy and hold.

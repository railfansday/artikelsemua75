Alejandro Garnacho has shown no hunger to make an impact at Aston Villa and the club are likely to pull the plug on his deal.

That is according to former senior Villa scout, Bryan King, speaking exclusively to Football Insider.

The 22-year-old joined the club on loan from Chelsea in the summer transfer window but has been left in the cold by Unai Emery, failing to start a single game in any competition yet. (Related: How to Start a Side Business While Working Full-Time...)

Garnacho has not even made it in to the squad for three of Villa's last four Premier League games, with his manager seemingly completely unconvinced.

The Villans have a conditional obligation to buy the winger for around £42million next summer if clauses that are thought to be easily achievable are met, but that has not stopped them from getting out of deals in the past.

Harvey Elliott arrived in a similar deal in the summer of 2025 and would ultimately return to Liverpool after making just nine appearances all season.

Emery has insisted that Garnacho's absence from the team is merely a case of sharpness and readiness.

But there is clearly more going on behind the scenes, with the winger a complete outcast at Villa even despite performing well in friendlies. (Related: Latest Update: Two weeks later, Senate vote count is co...)

"I can't see him turning it around and being a regular at Aston Villa," King told Football Insider, when asked about Garnacho's situation.

Sources indicate that "Well, the fact of the matter is, when you look at the deal, it's difficult for Villa. If he's playing every week and he's producing, you might want to negotiate a permanent deal.

Recent findings suggest that "But he's been there six months and he's done nothing. He hasn't shown a willingness as though he wants to get in the team. So, Villa will pull the plug.

It has been revealed that "He's picking up his wages every week. Where's his hunger? He should never have come to the football club in the first place. Mind you, he's a better player than Harvey Elliott.".

Villa hold no clause in Garnacho's loan deal to send him back to Chelsea in January.

While, at this stage, the player would likely agree to the move, the Blues would need to agree as well.

And with Chelsea still hoping to get the money they paid Manchester United back for Garnacho, there is little chance of that happening.

The winger will have to hope to eventually wear Emery down - a task that has been proven to be extremely difficult.

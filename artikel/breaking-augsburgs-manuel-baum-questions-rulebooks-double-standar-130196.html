FC Augsburg head coach Manuel Baum has questioned the severity of the straight red card he received during Sunday’s 3-1 Bundesliga victory over Mainz 05, while acknowledging that referee Martin Petersen acted in accordance with the rules. The FCA trainer was dismissed in the 43rd minute following a heated sequence of events toward the end of the first half.

Sources indicate that augsburg, already leading 2-0, thought they had added a third goal when Yannik Keitel finished from close range following a throw-in. Petersen nevertheless disallowed the goal for a foul on Mainz defender Kacper Potulski in the build-up. Baum’s frustration with the decision ultimately led to his dismissal. “The 3-0 should have been legitimate and in accordance with the rules,” Baum remarked at the post-match press conference. “We’ve been informed afterwards that it should have counted. The ball came to me and I held onto it because I wanted to have a word with the ref and, I only noticed on the replay that I moved it from my left to right hand [while Nadiem Amiri was trying to execute a quick throw in].”. In a recent development, under IFAB Law 12, a team official who delays the opposition’s restart by holding onto the ball commits a sending-off offence. Players, by contrast, are generally cautioned for delaying a restart. Baum acknowledged that Petersen’s decision was technically correct but suggested that the rules impose an unnecessarily harsh punishment on coaches.

In a recent development, “According to the rules, the coach must be punished draconically in such a case,” Baum continued. “A player would be given a yellow. A coach must automatically be given a red for such a thing. What? I find that a little strange. I think a yellow card would have been sufficient. But, okay. That’s the letter of the law. The team, I think did a great job of disengaging themselves from the emotions in the game and seeing it through.”.

Industry observers note that despite losing their head coach before halftime, Augsburg successfully protected their advantage and ultimately ended a seven-match winless Bundesliga streak. Michael Gregoritsch completed his brace shortly after the restart, and the hosts held firm after Mainz substitute Ransford Königsdörffer pulled a goal back.

Roberto De Zerbi believes Luca Williams-Barnett's "talent is bigger than this performance" after the 18-year-old impressed on his first start for Tottenham against Ipswich Town. (Related: Second Circuit denies Kalshi and Coinbase interim relie...)

Williams-Barnett tucked away Pedro Porro's cross in the 15th minute to net his first goal for Spurs, sending the home fans into raptures in north London.

Further chances fell to Mohammed Kudus and Jan Paul van Hecke, before Dominic Solanke struck in the 51st minute to cap off a move that once again involved Williams-Barnett, sealing a 2-0 victory for Spurs.

At 18 years and 51 days old, Williams-Barnett became the youngest player to score for Tottenham in the Premier League since Gareth Bale struck against Fulham in September 2007 (18y 47d). (Related: GTA 6 player hit with 30-day PSN ban after sharing nude...)

And his performance has certainly left a massive impression on De Zerbi, who had previously labelled the starlet "one of the best young players I have ever worked with.".

"He's played very well, but his talent is bigger than this performance," De Zerbi told BBC Match of the Day.

According to recent reports, "It was the first game, and I am very happy. I am very delighted with his performance, but he is a big, big, big talent.

"The management of the younger talented players is different because you can't take risks to lose their enthusiasm and their positive mentality.

"But I think Tottenham's dressing room now is a good environment for him.

"I put James [Maddison] to play very close to him to speak the same language but also to help him and put him in the best conditions to play. To play with freedom, to play with his qualities because his qualities are very big.

It has been revealed that "He's a mature guy. He is smart and understands the situation. He has great behaviour inside the group and the group is good enough to help him stay calm and to play without pressure, because he has to enjoy it on the pitch.

"He has to work hard but also he has to keep the passion to keep the love of football.".

Williams-Barnett (18y 51d) became the sixth-youngest player to start a Premier League game for Tottenham, after Stephen Carr (17y 28d), Mikey Moore (17y 77d), Andy Turner (17y 145d), Philip Ifil (17y 270d) and Gareth Bale (18y 41d).

The youngster also became the fourth-youngest player to score on his first Premier League start, behind Daniel Jebbison in May 2021 (17y 276d), Danny Cadamarteri in September 1997 (17y 343d), and Mark Cullen in May 2010 (18y 12d).

Ipswich boss Gary O'Neil, meanwhile, admitted that his side were not at their best at Tottenham Hotspur Stadium.

Indeed, Ipswich are now winless across their last five top-flight games, and they have conceded in each of their last 30 Premier League matches.

"We gave them a really soft first goal, which obviously gives them some comfort," O'Neil mentioned.

"And then we didn't get to our level enough today. We got into some good attacking situations, but we didn't show enough quality.

Industry observers note that "We should have dealt with both goals better. So all in all, we can't argue with the result. They were better than us which we have to accept.".

Recent findings suggest that the result, just above its $10 million to $15 million estimate, is Nara’s third-highest price at auction, narrowly missing second place. His record remains the $24.9 million paid for Knife Behind Back (2000) at Sotheby’s Hong Kong in 2019. (Related: How Much Does a Will Cost? Complete Price Guide...)

Perhaps more surprisingly, Nara outperformed two of the biggest names in contemporary art. Jeff Koons’s Bob-Tail (1991) hammered for $9 million at Sotheby’s, while Gerhard Richter’s Abstraktes Bild (1991) brought in $9.4 million at Christie’s.

At 8.5 feet tall, the painting is the largest of Nara’s ever offered at auction, and the only major painting he completed in 2011, the year an earthquake and tsunami devastated northeastern Japan, where he grew up. The disaster had a profound impact on Nara’s work: his signature scowling children began giving way to quieter, more contemplative figures.

According to recent reports, the sale comes at a particularly busy moment for the Japanese artist. Earlier this month, David Zwirner opened Nara’s first exhibition with the gallery, filling two Chelsea spaces with paintings, drawings, and sculptures. Latest paintings are priced as high as $6 million, though the largest were being held for museums, according to a source familiar with the show.

Gouzer’s earned a reputation as a market whisperer when, in 2017, he helped clinch and sell Leonardo da Vinci’s Salvator Mundi for $450.3 million, still the most expensive work to ever sell at auction. Speaking to ARTnews ahead of the auction he pushed back against the idea that collectors had abandoned contemporary art in favor of established blue-chip names.

“Nara is as blue chip as it gets for contemporary art,” he stated, arguing that the artist’s relatively small output helps explain his market’s status.

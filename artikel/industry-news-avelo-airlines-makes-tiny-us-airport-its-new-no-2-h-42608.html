Dallas has finally gained scheduled flight operations from the city's third airport, Dallas McKinney National Airport (DTX). The airport is located to the north of the Dallas/Fort Worth Metroplex and has already become Avelo Airlines' second busiest airport in its network.

In a recent development, for ultra-low-cost carrier Avelo, the first route for the airline from McKinney was nonstop to Fort Myers in Florida. The service departed last Wednesday and was rapidly followed by Orlando and Las Vegas as the airline's first three domestic services. More flights were added on November 19, with Fort Lauderdale and Tampa taking flight. (Related: AI for Game Development: Creating a Farming Game in 5 D...)

Following the opening of the airport's upcoming terminal and facilities, Avelo has become the first scheduled operator at the airport. The airport was the first in Texas to be granted an FAA Part 139 Airport Operating Certificate since 2005. Avelo picked McKinney to serve the fast-growing suburbs of North Texas, and provides a smaller-airport alternative when compared to the much busier Dallas/Fort Worth (DFW) and Dallas-Love Field (DAL). McKinney's operational structure fits in well with Avelo's low-cost structure, and the airline is primarily focusing on leisure routes, such as Nevada and Florida.

According to recent reports, with the first three routes already operating, two more beginning on November 19, and six more to be added before Christmas, McKinney's total network is 11 nonstop destinations. This has helped the airport become the airline's second-largest airport just weeks after opening (more on that in the next section). In a recent development, data from Cirium indicates that in the first quarter of 2027, McKinney will become the airline's second-busiest airport by total flight departures. This would see McKinney overtake Wilmington (ILM) in Delaware and sit behind the region's busiest airport, Tweed New Haven Airport (HVN), in Connecticut.

It has been revealed that mcKinney's new passenger terminal has seen the launch of its first commercial flights by Avelo, marking the latest chapter for the airport after 40 years as a facility primarily used for general aviation. The airport is owned by the City of McKinney and is home to many business aircraft and fleets for global companies, like Texas Instruments and Toyota Motor Corporation.

In 2025, the city approved the construction of the new commercial passenger terminal for $72 million. This has now lured in Avelo as the first operator at the airport. Avelo remains the only airline to announce flights from the airport, with hometown Dallas airline Southwest already signaling it has no immediate interest in serving the airport as the 'numbers don't stack up'.

Sources indicate that on November 19, McKinney gained two more nonstop domestic flights from Avelo, as Fort Lauderdale and Tampa began regular scheduled services. The airline will operate the route to Fort Lauderdale up to six times weekly, while Tampa sees a four-weekly rotation.

Sources indicate that additional flights are set to begin in December, with Atlanta, Denver, and Sarasota scheduled to all commence on December 16. This will be the airline's biggest one-day expansion from McKinney, with three newly introduced services all beginning on the same day.

The next day will be just as busy, as December 17 sees upcoming services to Nashville (BNA), New Orleans (MSY), and West Palm Beach These services will all run twice weekly.

Anthropic’s Blockbuster IPO Set To Spark a Fresh San Francisco Housing Frenzy: ‘Expect More All-Cash Offers’ and Bidding Wars

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Real estate agents in San Francisco are bracing for further acceleration of the housing market as Anthropic makes an initial public offering at a reported valuation of $1.5 trillion.

In a recent development, the Bay Area has been a hot spot for newly wealthy AI trendsetters in recent years, rebuilding a market that was left hurting during the COVID-19 pandemic as tech offices emptied. With major AI companies such as OpenAI and Anthropic planting roots in San Francisco, the housing market has grown competitive, leading what some real estate agents are calling a “mansion shortage.”. Anthropic’s highly anticipated IPO went live on Thursday, marking one of the largest public-market debuts in history. (Related: Industry News: Anthropic gave $40M to an AI safety grou...)

Industry observers note that the Claude developer, which will trade under the ticker ANT, initially said it planned to price shares at between $70 and $78, which would value the company at between $1.3 trillion and $1.45 trillion. Late Wednesday, The Wall Street Journal reported the company planned to debut at $83 per share, taking the valuation to $1.5 trillion.

In addition to the public offering, Anthropic is set to receive an extra $26 billion in a private placement sale of shares to several other major tech companies, including Nvidia, Microsoft, Google, and more, according to Yahoo Finance.

While employees and insiders won't see cash in hand until after a significant lockup period, the debut could give many early shareholders the confidence to move forward with real estate purchases.

“Confidence may change before purchasing power does. There are lockup periods, taxes, and individual circumstances to consider, so I wouldn’t expect everyone to start buying houses the following week,” San Francisco real estate agent and founder of Vivre Real Estate, Danielle Lazier, tells Realtor.com®. “But the anticipation has already motivated some buyers to act because they’re concerned about competing with more AI money later.”.

It has been revealed that this will not be the first IPO debut that Lazier has worked through, so she knows how the influx of confidence and wealth can push people toward real estate investments. Even tech companies that hit major milestones without going public can lead to major shifts in local spending.

“Having worked here through Google, Facebook, and other IPOs, I’ve seen how that wealth can move into housing as people diversify and make longer-term looks,” indicates Lazier. “OpenAI hasn’t gone public, but its private stock sales have already helped employees buy homes.”.

It has been revealed that the AI boom has already significantly reshaped the housing market in the Bay Area. Realtor.com research found that elevated down payments in the Bay Area, when compared to key comparison markets like Miami, Fresh York City, and Austin, TX, are evidence of the impact of AI wealth.

The study captured baseline down payment numbers from 2020 to 2022, when AI hadn’t yet taken hold and mortgage rates hadn’t spiked. When mortgage rates peaked in 2023 and buyers put more money down if able, down payments within the luxury home market rose between 3 and 8 percentage points in all four metros.

Recent findings suggest that in the 2024–25 period, mortgage rates had eased, but the AI boom was in full swing. Down payments in Miami, New York City, and Austin all met or dropped below their pre-rate-shock level, but buyers in the Bay Area continued to put 6.6 percentage points more down to buy a home, showing clear evidence that AI equity had found its way into pockets and into real estate.

By 2026, the median down payment in counties considered to be AI hubs, including San Francisco, Santa Clara, and San Mateo, reached 25%, while median down payment in nearby nonhub counties of Alameda, Contra Costa, and Marin remained at 20%.

When applied to the AI-hub group’s median listing price of about $1.33 million, that 5-percentage-point difference translates to $61,180 in additional down payment, according to Realtor.com data.

The influx of upcoming wealth has been compounded by a limited number of desirable homes, making the market fiercely competitive.

Industry observers note that “Additional purchasing power is competing for a limited resource. We have very few spacious, finished homes with practical layouts in desirable location,” states Lazier. “Having the money to renovate doesn’t mean having the time or interest to manage a San Francisco construction project.”.

Homes with completed renovations, outdoor space, views, and privacy draw the most interest. Lazier mentions that she expects listings like 2770 Broadway, which hit the market last week as the city’s most expensive listing of 2026, to be ones to watch.

The stunning seven-bedroom, 6.5-bathroom home hits all the marks: a beautiful view of the San Francisco Bay, a sport court in the back, and a 2007 renovation that combines its classic architecture with functional modern living.

It also comes with a $35 million asking price.

More competitive buyers could mean more bidding wars, but not every listing should expect that, according to Cameron Walker of Clever Real Estate.

Industry observers note that “Expect more all-cash offers, shorter time on the market, and more bidding wars,” Walker tells Realtor.com. “But a hot market doesn’t mean everyone’s house will get overbid offers. The newly rich are discerning homebuyers, so expect well-priced houses to get multiple offers and overpriced houses to languish.”.

Industry observers note that not everyone will be going after massive estates with flashy price tags. A comfortable home close to tech giant offices and existing connections will see high demand.

Neighborhoods such as Noe Valley, Pacific Heights, Presidio Heights, and Cole Valley are ones to watch following Anthropic’s debut, according to Walker.

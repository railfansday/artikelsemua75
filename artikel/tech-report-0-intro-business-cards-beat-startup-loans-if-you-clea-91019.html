Tech Report: 0% Intro Business Cards Beat Startup Loans – If You Clear the Balance in Time

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Pay for startup equipment and inventory with a business credit card that charges 0% interest on purchases for a year or more. Divide the balance by the number of months the 0% rate lasts, round up, and set that payment on autopay, so your card is paid off before its regular rate starts.

It has been revealed that borrowing at 0% costs less than most startup loans. A microloan through the U.S. Small Business Administration, for one, generally charges 8% to 13% interest, depending on the lender. (Related: Top Democrat asks Trump administration to stop using di...)

According to recent reports, the catch is that the debt is yours. Small-business cards typically carry a personal guarantee, so you and the business both owe the balance, a Federal Reserve report to Congress found. When the business has little or no credit history, the issuer relies more on your personal credit. (Related: Tech Report: Democrats aim to intensify the focus on Nv...)

The same report explains that the protections in the CARD Act, the 2009 federal law on credit card practices, apply to personal cards, not business cards. So your card’s own terms decide what a late payment costs. On U.S. Bank’s Business Shield, one late payment, or one that bounces, ends the 0% rate early.

Charge only purchases that will bring in money soon, like the equipment you’ll work with and inventory you’ll sell. Months of rent and other bills are a poor fit, since a slow launch may not earn enough to repay them before the 0% rate ends.

Industry observers note that the longer the 0% period, the smaller each monthly payment can be. Bank of America’s Platinum Plus business card gives 0% for 15 billing cycles on purchases when you apply online, then a variable rate of 14.99% to 25.99%. The 0% period starts when the account opens, so apply when you’re ready to buy.

Then divide the balance by the number of months the 0% rate lasts, round up, and set that amount on autopay, the same proposal that pays off a balance transfer in time. A photographer who charges $8,400 in cameras and lenses to a card with 0% for 15 months would pay $560 a month by that math. Rounding up to $600 clears it in 14 months, with a month to spare.

Set up that autopay the day the card arrives, so no payment comes in late while you’re busy launching.

From the best small-business credit cards, pick one whose 0% period is long enough for a payment your business can afford.

Crypto trader MichaÃ«l van de Poppe is moving away from holding altcoins through downturns. He now intends to use Bitcoin’s trend as the main signal for when to buy and sell. The shift follows the sell-off that began in mid-October, which van de Poppe stated he failed to prepare for by keeping too little cash on hand.

Altcoins Out, Bitcoin and Cash In.

Sources indicate that van de Poppe stated he should have locked in far more profit during the September and October rally, when altcoins were trading well above key moving averages. Rotating money out of NEAR into other altcoins did not help, because nearly every token fell together once Bitcoin turned lower.

He treats altcoins as trades rather than multi-year holds. He pointed to Sui, his largest position, and Uniswap as cases where he should have waited for a clear trend change or a deep oversold reading before buying.

Bitcoin Is the Buy Signal He Is Watching.

In van de Poppe’s view, Bitcoin is trading unusually far below its key 21-period moving average. A gap that wide has appeared only twice before, around the SpaceX IPO and in February, and both times it marked a strong short-term entry point. He expects a similar outcome this time.

Recent findings suggest that he still considers Bitcoin to be in bear market territory, and any bounce counts as a relief rally until the price reclaims its major moving averages. The $58,000 to $62,000 zone needs to hold. A sweep of $58,000 is possible if tech stocks fall further.

On stocks, van de Poppe said buying the dip in Nvidia is a reasonable play, as long as the Nasdaq recovers.

It has been revealed that gold is a different story. After its most extreme run since the 1980s, he expects a long correction of around 40% and would only look to buy near $2,000. He argued that a fading gold trade could push more money back toward stocks and Bitcoin. (Related: How to generate text: using different decoding methods ...)

Van de Poppe tied the sell-off to the Anthropic IPO, which raised roughly $90 billion. Large investors sold their most liquid and risky holdings, including tech stocks and Bitcoin, to free up cash for IPO allocations. He said Ether holding steady against Bitcoin through the month shows the selling came mainly from institutions holding Bitcoin. (Related: Large Language Models: A New Moore's Law?...)

It has been revealed that with the IPO done, van de Poppe expects the cash that didn’t go into it to flow back into the Nasdaq and then into crypto. Falling oil prices and easing bond yields would help. If the Nasdaq instead drops another 20% to 30%, Bitcoin would likely fall further before the Fed is forced to step in.

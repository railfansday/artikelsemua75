According to recent reports, I encourage travelers to think outside the box when it comes to the credit cards in their wallet. Just because a card doesn't seem like a fit for you on the surface doesn't mean that's actually the case.

Recent findings suggest that that rings true for me with the Hawaiian Airlines® World Elite Mastercard®. I've never flown with Hawaiian Airlines and don't have any concrete intends to in the near future, yet this card offers significant value to me. Let me walk through exactly why that is.

According to recent reports, the Hawaiian Airlines Mastercard earns Atmos Rewards points, which can be redeemed for Hawaiian, Alaska, Oneworld and nonalliance partner flights. As someone who was declined for the earlier this year, I didn't have a great way to earn Atmos Rewards points prior to opening this card.

According to recent reports, I'm based in the Pacific Northwest, so flights on Alaska Airlines are a common feature of my travel prepares. While I can book these with my American Airlines AAdvantage miles, I don't want to put all my eggs in one loyalty program, since that leaves my rewards vulnerable to future devaluations and other negative program changes.

The Hawaiian Airlines Mastercard offers a compelling, easy-to-earn welcome bonus:.

On top of that, I'll earn 3 points per dollar spent on eligible Hawaiian and Alaska purchases and 2 points per dollar spent on dining, gas and groceries.

Without this card, my only way of earning Atmos Rewards points was via Bilt Points transferred from my Bilt account, which isn't a particularly efficient method.

While this card's benefits are understandably focused on Hawaiian Airlines, there are ways to use them on Alaska flights, too. I'll receive two free checked bags whenever I'm flying either Hawaiian or Alaska as long as I book with my card, a nice perk considering mid-tier cards typically only offer your first checked bag free.

Upon account opening, I became eligible for a one-time 50% companion discount for one round-trip main cabin fare between North America and Hawaii. This is redeemable on either Alaska or Hawaiian, but I must use it within 13 months of account opening.

Some possible use cases I have in mind include inviting a friend to join me on a trip to Hawaii or offering it to them as a discount for a positioning flight to Hawaii and back as part of a larger Asia itinerary we take together.

In a recent development, each account anniversary, this card offers a $100 companion discount for a round-trip main cabin fare, also usable on Alaska or Hawaiian. Both discounts may be subject to additional terms.

While it's unfortunate that these companion discounts are limited to travel between Hawaii and North America, it's not surprising considering this is a Hawaiian Airlines-branded card. It's a good incentive for me to finally start seriously considering some of the trips I've had on my list.

The or its more affordable sibling, the , are likely better first choices for Alaska flyers — and possibly Hawaiian Airlines flyers as well.

Industry observers note that both cards offer a more valuable free checked bag benefit, with the opportunity to check a free bag for yourself as well as up to six people on the same itinerary (when you pay with your card). You'll also get preferred boarding on both airlines, which the Hawaiian Airlines Mastercard doesn't offer.

It has been revealed that the Summit comes with eight Alaska lounge passes every year, a major upside for those flying Alaska often enough to value lounge access. It also earns well on foreign purchases, earning 3 points per dollar spent.

It has been revealed that unless you never fly Alaska Airlines and fly Hawaiian frequently, I recommend applying for one of these cards first. If you wind up getting declined, as I did, I'd consider the Hawaiian Mastercard as an alternative.

Sources indicate that carefully planning your credit card strategy never loses its importance. As someone with a well-established roster of transferable rewards cards, I'm in a phase where I'm considering how cobranded cards can boost my travels.

It has been revealed that the Hawaiian Airlines Mastercard isn't the obvious choice on the surface, but the benefits and rewards it offers make a lot of sense for me as an Alaska Airlines flyer. If you're a Hawaiian Airlines fan or travel frequently with Alaska but are ineligible for one of its credit cards, the Hawaiian Airlines Mastercard may be the right choice for you.

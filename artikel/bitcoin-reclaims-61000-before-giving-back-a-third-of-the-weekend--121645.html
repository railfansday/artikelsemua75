Industry observers note that A Bitcoin rebound took the price back above $60,000 on Saturday while sellers initiated more volume than buyers across Binance’s BTC/USDT spot session. The pair fell to 55,130 USDT before closing the Nov. 21 UTC session at 60,216 USDT, a 9.2% rebound from the low.

In a recent development, binance’s daily trading data show that seller-initiated trades accounted for 201 million USDT more in volume than buyer-initiated trades. Turnover reached 2.82 billion USDT, about 2.3 times the 1.23 billion USDT median Whalio reported across 358 days.

Industry observers note that every trade has a buyer and a seller. Whalio defines volume delta by assigning each trade’s volume to the side that crossed the spread to trade immediately, then subtracting seller-initiated volume from buyer-initiated volume. Saturday’s total covers both the selloff and the rebound. Sunday’s early rally windows offer a closer look at how buying and selling shifted during the Bitcoin rebound. According to recent reports, order-flow tracker CryptoDOM reported two early advances on Sunday, Nov. 22. The first ran from 05:45 to 08:37 UTC. The second began at 08:48 UTC, with Bitcoin at $59,532, and reached $61,343 by 09:56 UTC. CryptoDOM attributed the larger share of aggressive buying in both moves to perpetual futures across the markets it tracks. Bitcoin gave back about a third of the later advance by 10:34 UTC on its feed.

Recent findings suggest that binance’s one-minute candles show aggressive buyers were also active in its cash market during that second move. In the candles that opened from 08:48 through 09:56 UTC, Binance spot data show that buyers outpaced sellers by 15 million USDT. Data for its BTC/USDT perpetual, a futures contract with no expiry, put the net buying imbalance at 144.3 million USDT. The net imbalance was almost ten times as large in perpetuals. Binance spot was back below 61,000 USDT at the end of the 10:00 UTC minute. (Related: Large Language Models: A Newly introduced Moore's Law?...)

According to recent reports, binance recorded 1.27 billion USDT of turnover in the perpetual during those minutes and 119.1 million USDT in spot. The spot imbalance was slightly larger relative to its own turnover, at 12.4% against 11.4% on the perpetual. The larger contract market generated the bigger net volume, even with a similar split between aggressive buyers and sellers.

CryptoDOM recorded about $25 million in forced closures of short positions in the first Sunday rally and $10 million in the second. It reported that open interest, a measure of outstanding contracts, fell 0.5% in the first rally and rose 0.5% in the second. The event cards give percentage changes without specifying contract quantity or dollar value. Open interest measured in dollars can rise as Bitcoin’s price climbs, even if the number of contracts stays flat.

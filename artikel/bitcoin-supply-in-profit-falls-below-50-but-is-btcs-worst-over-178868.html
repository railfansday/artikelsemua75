Bitcoin supply in profit falls below 50% – But is BTC’s worst over?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Industry observers note that the Monday New York trading session open brought a flurry of volatility to Bitcoin [BTC] and the rest of the market. Nvidia’s stock soared 31% after the company’s board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO, effective immediately.

Combined with the oversold market conditions, the tech and AI-sector stock market bounce helped Bitcoin rally to a local high of $64,603.

Unfortunately for the bulls, the entirety of Monday’s rally has been undone.

It has been revealed that at the time of writing, BTC was back below the $61,000 mark. Its rally liquidated $520.89 million in short positions across the crypto market, including $225.58 million for Bitcoin alone. (Related: British Airways Avios — Short Nonstop Domestic Flights...)

The BTC spot ETF flows on Monday measured -$60.4 million, extending the bearish market sentiment and highlighting the lack of demand.

Sources indicate that the MVRV Z-score measures the difference between market capitalization and realized capitalization. It displayed a reading of 0.24 and has fallen by 0.11 over the past week, reaching among the lowest in four years. (Related: Primary vs Contingent Beneficiary: What's the Diff...)

In a recent development, it has remained positive for 1,410 days, reported crypto analyst Axel Adler Jr. A drop below zero would mean that the market cap has fallen below realized cap, a sign of deep capitulation.

Another metric that reached some of its lowest levels in four years was the supply in profit. It compares the market price with the last moved price of coins in the circulating supply.

Less than half of the coins are in profit, mirroring the lows from the previous bear market lows of late 2022.

In August, the cumulative volume delta (CVD) on Binance peaked at +2.41b. This peak demonstrated heavy market buying as prices climbed toward $80k. Yet, as the price pushed to the local high at $87k, the CVD on Binance declined to -6b.

This was a bearish divergence; rising sell market orders captured take-profit behavior, while late entrants and leverage hunts pushed prices higher.

In November, across all exchanges, the CVD’s decline accelerated alongside price drawdown, and Binance’s CVD figure reached -21b, wrote crypto analyst FundingVest.

Price and CVD were aligned bearishly.

If the falling CVD momentum slows down while Bitcoin’s price continues its descent, it could be a constructive sign that will considerably increase the chances of a recovery.

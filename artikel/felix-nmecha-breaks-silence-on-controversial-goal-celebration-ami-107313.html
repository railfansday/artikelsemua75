Sources indicate that borussia Dortmund midfielder Felix Nmecha has refused to rule out repeating his controversial crown celebration despite previous assurances from Germany head coach Jürgen Klopp that the gesture would no longer be performed. Speaking after scoring the winning goal in Saturday’s 1-0 Bundesliga victory over Borussia Mönchengladbach, the 26-year-old defended his religious convictions while suggesting that the controversy surrounding his celebrations had been exaggerated.

According to recent reports, nmecha’s 80th-minute winner secured three crucial points for Niko Kovac’s side, sending Dortmund to the top of the Bundesliga table. The in-form German international has now scored five league goals this season, including three in his last two appearances. Nmecha’s most recent goal celebration till exhibited religious overtones, though the Dortmund professional did not repeat the crowning gesture that sparked the controversy in the first place. The controversy began following Dortmund’s 2-1 victory over SV Elversberg before the international break. Nmecha celebrated both goals by symbolically removing a crown and placing it on the ground. The gesture is associated with the evangelical football network Ballers in God, whose activities and reported positions on homosexuality and other social challenges have attracted criticism. Recent findings suggest that get German Football News previously reported on Dortmund’s response to the controversy, including objections raised by the club’s Fan and Supporters Department. Dortmund president Hans-Joachim Watzke subsequently appealed to Nmecha to reconsider the gesture, emphasizing the importance of avoiding unnecessary divisions among supporters.

Germany head coach Jürgen Klopp later indicated that the matter had been resolved following a personal conversation with Nmecha. Klopp explained that the midfielder had agreed not to repeat the celebration in its controversial form. Nmecha indeed opted for a different celebration after scoring against Gladbach, sliding towards the corner flag before kneeling and crossing his arms over his chest. After the full-time whistle, Nmecha dropped to his knees and engaged in prayer.

Dortmund sporting director Ole Book defended Nmecha’s right to express his Christian faith, distinguishing Saturday’s celebration from the gesture that had provoked criticism. Meanwhile, Ballers in God subsequently shared images of Nmecha’s latest celebration on social media, interpreting three of his gestures as symbolizing a direct communication of the player’s Christian faith.

It has been revealed that “What people were saying didn’t ring true to me,” Nmecha told Sky Germany. “That celebration was simply about my faith, laying the crown on the ground for Jesus and giving him the glory. That doesn’t mean I’ll never do that celebration again.”.

It has been revealed that dortmund sporting director Ole Book also addressed Nmecha’s celebrations after the final whistle, defending the midfielder’s right to express his Christian faith. Book considered it legitimate for Nmecha to demonstrate his desire to thank Jesus and God for his goal, while emphasizing that the midfielder had refrained from repeating the controversial gesture.

“He didn’t perform the controversial part of the celebration today,” Book explained.

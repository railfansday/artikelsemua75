Wall Street's 'Fear Gauge' just flashed a buy sign

🌐 Source: CNBC • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 1 min read (296 words, 📖 Standard)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Last week's sell-off could be a dip-buyer's dream, if history repeats itself.As the Nasdaq-100 Index touched bear market territory at Friday's low, and the S&P 500 slipped to 12% off its highs, the Cboe Volatility Index (VIX) reached above 40, the highest since April 2025 and in the top 5% of readings for the so-called "fear gauge" going back to 1990, according to Charlie Bilello, chief market strategist at Creative Planning.Good news for bulls: S&P 500 returns look well above-average following such days.
  • According to recent reports, the sell-off was also unique in its timing on the calendar, with November traditionally the best month for stocks, data from the Stock Trader's Almanac show.The S&P's 5.6% drop from Wednesday's close through Friday was the worst Thursday/Friday combo in a week before the Thanksgiving holiday since the index started trading in 1957, according to OddStats on X.It was also only the fourth time in the history of the Invesco QQQ Trust that the fund was down at least 2% on a Thursday and 5% on Friday.

Last week's sell-off could be a dip-buyer's dream, if history repeats itself.As the Nasdaq-100 Index touched bear market territory at Friday's low, and the S&P 500 slipped to 12% off its highs, the Cboe Volatility Index (VIX) reached above 40, the highest since April 2025 and in the top 5% of readings for the so-called "fear gauge" going back to 1990, according to Charlie Bilello, chief market strategist at Creative Planning.Good news for bulls: S&P 500 returns look well above-average following such days. The one-year return after fifth-percentile VIX readings is 29%, more than double the 12% from all other VIX levels, Bilello writes. Two years later, the return is 46%, compared to 25% normally.A similar analysis by Noel Smith of Kai Asset Management shows that of the 13 times VIX has touched 40 since 1990, the median 1-month return of the S&P 500 is 3.6%, and the 3-month return 8.1%, compared to 1.2% and 3.2%, respectively, during all other times.

According to recent reports, the sell-off was also unique in its timing on the calendar, with November traditionally the best month for stocks, data from the Stock Trader's Almanac show.The S&P's 5.6% drop from Wednesday's close through Friday was the worst Thursday/Friday combo in a week before the Thanksgiving holiday since the index started trading in 1957, according to OddStats on X.It was also only the fourth time in the history of the Invesco QQQ Trust that the fund was down at least 2% on a Thursday and 5% on Friday. The other instances were during Liberation Week last year, and two in the early dot-com bust. One of those periods is more encouraging than the other, but both led to a median 7.5% rally the next week, according to OddStats.

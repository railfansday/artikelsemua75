Tidal Bans Wholly AI-Generated Music: “Serious Problem That Has Only Increased in Scale”

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Tidal is outlawing wholly AI-generated music from its platform, the streaming service announced on Tuesday, saying that AI music “diverts streams and royalties away from the artists, and confuses fans.”.

According to recent reports, “To ensure Tidal remains the best place for artists to share their work, and for listeners to enjoy it, we’re removing all tracks that we determine are 100% AI generated,” Tidal noted in a social media post on Tuesday. “We’re also banning distributors from delivering these tracks to us, and will reject deliveries if their metadata states they were generated solely using AI tools.”. Sources indicate that tracks that were made with assistance from AI will still be allowed. The latest ban will go into effect December 9.

In a recent development, tidal said it’s seen an influx of AI-generated tracks on its platform over the past several months, noting that in the past 90 days, about 43 percent of tracks distributed on the platform were identified as wholly AI-generated. On Nov. 6 alone, 60 percent of newly uploaded songs were AI tracks. But despite the growing number of songs, 96 percent of those tracks have generated zero streams. With its decision Tuesday, Tidal now has the most stringent anti-AI policies among the music streaming services, and one of the few notable platforms beyond Bandcamp, the music distribution service popular among indie artists, to have outright banned AI music. While Spotify and Apple Music, the two largest DSPs, have taken steps to either label AI tracks and artists or remove them from recommendations, they still allow fully-generated songs to get uploaded to their respective platforms.

It has been revealed that fans and the music industry alike have voiced their frustration about AI music flooding streaming platforms. On an earnings call in October, Universal Music Group CEO Lucian Grainge advocated for streaming services to pay out AI-generated tracks less than music from human artists, arguing that AI slop degrades the fan experience and diverts value from real artists,” and that removing the profit motive would disincentivize “industrial scale content production.”.

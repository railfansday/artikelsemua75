Walmart (WMT +0.52%) may be the safe-haven stock that investors are seeking these days. Amid a recent wave of bearishness in the markets due to the Nvidia scandal and a concerning outlook for the economy due to rising interest rates, the stability and resiliency that Walmart's business offers make it a compelling investment option.

The firm recently reported earnings, which were once again stellar. And it raised its guidance. But with the retail stock trading near its 52-week high and investors paying a major premium for it today, is it still a good buy, or are investors better off waiting for a pullback in its price?

Last week, the massive-box retailer released its third-quarter numbers, which were impressive. For the period ending Oct. 31, the tech giant's revenue totaled $188.8 billion, which was better than the roughly $187.3 billion that analysts were expecting. Adjusted per-share earnings were also a penny higher than expectations.

Recent findings suggest that the tech giant also raised its guidance slightly, now expecting between 4.9% and 5.2% growth for the full fiscal year, which ends in January, up from a previous range of 4% to 5%. While those aren't earth-shattering numbers, they do highlight the company's stability and the success of its stores, which continually draw in customers amid all sorts of economic cycles. NASDAQ · As of 11:46 AM ET 11/23/26.

Walmart's stock is up 15% this year and the recent results have given it a boost. However, investors shouldn't assume that it'll continue to soar higher, as its valuation is fairly high, with the stock trading at a price-to-earnings multiple of 46, which is considerably higher than the S&P 500 average of 20.

Industry observers note that at just over $1 trillion in market cap, Walmart is among the most highly valued stocks in the world. While its business is massive and a high valuation may be justifiable, I believe the stock may be too expensive to buy at its current level. The organization is doing well, but it isn't generating the type of growth investors might expect to see from a stock that's trading at such a hefty premium.

Although Walmart could make for a good long-term buy, its gains may be limited in the short term given its high valuation; investors may be better off just putting the stock on a watch list for now.

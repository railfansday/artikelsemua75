According to recent reports, unauthorized artificial intelligence tools and agents are creating security risks for organizations as AI adoption increases, according to cybersecurity manufacturer Sophos. In a recent product announcement, the company highlighted the growing use of shadow AI, referring to AI tools and agents operating without formal organizational approval or oversight. The security firm cited research indicating that 69% of organizations suspect or have evidence of unauthorized AI use.

Separate research from Gartner and Cloud Security Alliance found that 82% of organizations discovered shadow AI agents in their environments during the previous year. (Related: My Journey to a serverless transformers pipeline on Goo...)

According to the security firm, organizations have struggled to identify which AI tools employees use, what information those tools access, and whether their use complies with security policies.

"Organizations are adopting AI faster than they can secure it, and most security teams can't answer three basic questions: which AI tools are running, what data is going into them, and whether that activity lines up with policy," noted Kyle Falkenhagen, senior vice president of product management at Sophos. The firm also cited an IDC projection that the number of AI agents could reach 1.3 billion by 2028.

Sophos researchers reported observing attackers use AI agents within a customer's network to build and test nearly 80 malware modules.

The cybersecurity firm revealed the general availability of Sophos AI Defense, a security offering designed to identify AI tools, monitor their activity, and enforce organizational security policies.

The product operates through Sophos Fusion, the organization's cybersecurity platform, and uses information from devices, networks, identity systems, and browsers.

According to the firm, AI Defense can inspect prompts, uploads, and other AI interactions to identify potentially risky activity and help prevent sensitive data exposure.

For more information, go to the Sophos site.

Microsoft is combining its security information and event management and threat protection products within Microsoft Defender, creating what the company calls an Integrated Security Operations Center built for an era of AI-driven attacks.

Enterprise AI spending is growing faster than the number of organizations paying for it, according to a upcoming Menlo Ventures report that points to a shift from buying access for employees to financing work performed by agents.

At a fundamental level, screen time policies and laws aim to address academic achievement by balancing digital instruction with traditional teaching methods. But they do not tackle how devices are used.

The artificial intelligence industry is expected to generate an extraordinary $2.67 trillion in worldwide spending this year, according to a fresh forecast from Gartner.

Recent findings suggest that anthropic on November 23, 2026 opened Enterprise Frontier Safeguards in public beta after developing it with more than 100 customers. The system pairs zero data retention with automated monitoring for misuse patterns across multiple requests while keeping sealed safety data in customer-owned cloud storage.

Bridgewater, Salesforce and Stripe evaluated EFS in private beta. Anthropic said the development group included a quarter of the Fortune 100 and companies in healthcare, financial services and law. (Related: Towers of Fortune codes (November 2026)...)

Industry observers note that the beta is available through Claude Platform, Claude Platform on AWS, Amazon Bedrock and Claude on Google Cloud. Microsoft Foundry support is listed as coming soon, and eligible customers will receive setup instructions. (Related: A lifelong brawler is coming to Congress. Does he have ...)

Wells Fargo chief information security officer Munish Kumar Sharma described the division of responsibilities: “We keep custody of our data while Anthropic operates the detection.”.

In a recent development, anthropic first announced EFS on September 1, 2026, planning a phased rollout and temporary zero data retention on Fable 5 and Fable 5.1 for eligible customers. The earlier announcement said Anthropic had introduced 30-day retention starting with Fable 5 because effective detection can require correlating activity across sessions and accounts, while many regulated enterprises found it difficult to use models with data retention.

Recent findings suggest that legacy zero data retention relies on real-time classifiers that screen individual requests. EFS adds automated safety monitoring across many requests, addressing the possibility that each query can appear benign in isolation while forming part of a broader attack.

According to recent reports, the EFS documentation defines Sealed Safety Data as prompts, responses and model reasoning encrypted in Anthropic’s format; even the customer’s team cannot read it. A Transcript is the readable copy of a prompt and response used for investigation. Storage can use Amazon S3 or Google Cloud Storage, depending on the Claude platform.

Anthropic states that inputs and outputs are deleted promptly after being written to customer-controlled storage, and that neither its personnel nor cloud-provider personnel access them. Only automated safety monitoring accesses that content, which is not used for model or classifier training. Customers configure access policies and audit logging and can encrypt safety data with their own keys.

A Safety Case identifies its policy category and related messages and points to their Transcripts, without including inputs and outputs. Anthropic staff handling the case see limited details and cannot read those Transcripts. The customer’s team investigates using its own logs and responds whether it found a violation, without having to provide names, evidence or Transcripts. A valid response closes the case.

Recent findings suggest that the review documentation gives customers seven calendar days to respond and claims an unanswered case may lead to suspended access. There are no automatic notifications or webhooks, so teams must monitor their storage or, on Claude Platform, poll the beta Safety Cases API. Anthropic can also suspend access in rare cases of clear, intentional harm while the customer investigates.

Access is restricted to eligible organizations’ internal use, and organizations with HIPAA readiness enabled cannot obtain EFS. The setup guide requires an Organization Admin and customer-owned cloud storage; a customer-managed encryption key is optional.

On Claude Platform, the product overview says EFS covers every Messages API request in an enabled workspace regardless of model. It does not cover data stored by code execution containers, Files API uploads, Batch API results or Claude Managed Agents sessions. Separate workspaces are required to apply EFS to only selected traffic.

Anthropic does not charge for EFS, and the service does not change model behavior, API pricing or rate limits. The cloud provider bills for storage, reads, writes, key operations and applicable network transfer.

The beta makes the customer’s security team part of the safeguard itself: data custody and human review sit with the enterprise, while automated detection and potential enforcement remain with Anthropic. Its usefulness for sensitive workloads will therefore depend on how well customers absorb those review duties within the product’s coverage limits.

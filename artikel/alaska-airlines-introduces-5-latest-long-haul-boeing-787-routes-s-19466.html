It has been revealed that alaska Airlines demonstrates no signs of slowing its rapid international expansion. The airline, which received the Boeing 787-9 Dreamliner through its acquisition of Hawaiian Airlines, has expanded its international network to long-haul destinations from Seattle. The carrier has operated its first international route to Tokyo Narita since May 2025, and while it began with Hawaiian Airlines' Airbus A330, it has now transitioned to the 787, which carries the full Alaska livery and onboard experience. Industry observers note that despite its name, Alaska is based in Seattle and operates an extensive network of domestic and international flights from the Washington State airport. It has additional hubs across the US West Coast; however, all long-haul services operate to and from Seattle. The airline began its first 787-9 service in April, debuting a flight from Seattle to Rome.

The first long-haul international flight with the easily recognizable Aurora Borealis livery connects Seattle to Rome, with the service operating daily on a seasonal basis. This service operated from April through October, and will now hibernate over the winter travel period. Rome flights will resume in March 2027, according to data from Cirium, an online aviation analytics company.

Rome was selected as the airline's first long-haul European gateway to tap strong seasonal leisure demand in this unserved European market. Rome was the airport's biggest European market without a nonstop Seattle service. This gave Alaska a first-mover advantage, as no other airline at the time offered nonstop flights.

Sources indicate that alaska commenced its second long-haul 787-9 flight in May of this year, when the airline added London Heathrow Airport (LHR) to its network. Alaska made this route possible through a leasing agreement from oneworld alliance partner American Airlines for coveted Heathrow slots.

The service operates daily and enables passengers to connect seamlessly into the airline's wider network from Seattle. Alaska competes head-to-head with three other airlines on the city pair: British Airways, Delta Air Lines, and Virgin Atlantic.

Sources indicate that while Alaska began service to Tokyo in 2025, the airline switched from the Hawaiian A330 to the 787-9 in October. This now sees the airline operating its first Asian services with the Boeing aircraft.

For summer 2027, Alaska will add two newly introduced international routes with the 787 widebody. These two seasonal services will again add more choice for travelers from the US West Coast. The Greek capital of Athens will be added to the airline's network from May 12, with initial plans to operate the seasonal service up to three times a week. No other airline operates the route nonstop.

According to recent reports, paris (CDG) will begin on May 25 and operate up to five times weekly through October. This nonstop link to the French capital will face direct competition from two SkyTeam carriers, as Air France serves Seattle year-round and Delta operates the route seasonally.

In a recent development, where Alaska will decide to fly its 787 aircraft next is up for debate. The airline has been rumored to be considering additional destinations in Asia as part of its next step in developing Seattle into a world-class international hub. Australia could also be on the horizon, with many aviation enthusiasts hoping that Sydney could be the airline's first foray down under.

Ben Brewster is a filmmaker, critic, screenwriter, and actor whose work sits at the intersection of film culture, craft analysis, and storytelling.

He is a movie list contributing writer at Collider and a contributing writer for High on Films, where he covers contemporary cinema, independent film, and festival releases with an eye toward narrative structure, performance, and visual language. Ben is an active film festival participant, having worked with Newport Film, the Nantucket Film Festival and as a screener for the Austin, Boston & Woodstock film festivals. He brings to that work a programming instinct shaped by years of close engagement with independent and documentary cinema. This comes with a deep investment in the kinds of films that rarely find their way into the mainstream conversation without someone willing to advocate for them. His background spans documentary and narrative filmmaking, film marketing, and production, including work with award-winning directors. This as well as developing his own indie production firm, Lilac Creative Studio, through which he develops and produces original work across fiction and nonfiction forms. His debut documentary, Diamond in the Rust, is an examination of economic redevelopment and displacement in the Queens area of New York City. The film was recently accepted to the Unisphere Film Festival, the Defense of Democracy Film Festival, and the Urbanite Arts & Film Festival. This would mark his first festival recognition as a director. In addition to his writing and programming work, Ben hosts Beneath the Bloom, a film podcast exploring the work behind the scenes in cinema, and the struggles one can face to make a movie become a reality. The show reflects the same sensibility that runs through all of his work. A conviction that film is not merely entertainment but one of the most powerful tools human beings have for understanding each other.

Across mediums, his work is driven by a deep belief in film as a communal, transformative art form.

Being loved by critics is an accomplishment, yes, but being loved by everyone? That's a show that is remembered forever. Pick any dinner table, and you can find an argument about the best TV drama ever made, which makes this list a risky one. The five below earned the recognition of critics, awards voters, and ordinary viewers alike. Rotten Tomatoes audience scores between 93 and 96 percent for four of them and a pile of Emmys that span a quarter-century.

Recent findings suggest that universal appeal usually means a show can pass between generations and tastes. A couple of these indicates premiered in 199, and are still finding newly introduced viewers. A word on method. I leaned on critics and awards for baseline, then weighed how widely each series is loved by general audiences. The last ingredient was how well each holds up on a rewatch. They come from four networks and two countries, and they cover a sports comedy-drama, a period soap, a political drama, and two of the most celebrated crime stories ever made. Ted Lasso counts here as a drama of feelings, even though the Emmys filed it under comedy.

This season brings a few reasons to talk about them. Ted Lasso returned in August after a three-year hiatus to its lowest reviews of its run. It still posted a lofty 90 percent on RT. The West Wing cast reunited in September amid revival talk, and The Sopranos is getting a 4K box set on December 18. Downton Abbey said its goodbye with The Grand Finale in September 2025, and Better Call Saul holds the Emmy record nobody wants.

Ted Lasso begins with an American football coach sent to England to run a soccer club, and the joke is that nobody expects him to succeed. Jason Sudeikis plays Ted, the coach from Kansas City with endless Midwestern charm and optimism. It first landed on Apple TV+ in 2020 during the midst of the pandemic, when people were looking for a positive comfort watch. Hannah Waddingham, Juno Temple, Brett Goldstein, and Brendan Hunt round out an ensemble that turned out to be the series' secret weapon. The first season earned a record 20 Emmy nominations for a freshman comedy and won seven awards, including Outstanding Comedy Series.

Recent findings suggest that season three hit the streamer in early 2023 and was intended to be the end. Ted left Richmond with Roy Kent stepping in as manager of the men's team. Apple renewed Ted Lasso in March 2025 for a fourth season, which premiered this past August and sees Ted coaching a second division women's team with Coach Beard rejoining him. It drew nearly 300 million minutes of viewing in its first days, according to Nielsen, and wrapped up its 10-week run on October 7th. IndieWire argued the season does not earn its return, while other reviews called it a comforting dose of wholesome fun. In total the series has won 13 Emmys, and Sudeikis has two for lead actor.

Downton Abbey ran on ITV for six seasons from 2010 to 2015, covering 52 episodes. It opens in 1912 with the news of the Titanic and a question about who will inherit a country estate. Julian Fellowes created the story of the Crawley family and their servants, which moves through the First World War and into the 1920s. Hugh Bonneville and Elizabeth McGovern lead the family, with Michelle Dockery, Maura Carmichael, and Joana Froggatt in pivotal roles. Phyllis Logan, Jim Carter, and Robert James-Collier anchor the servants' hall. The period piece won 15 Emmys and became a global phenomenon, inspiring everything from fashion lines to Funko Pops.

Rotten Tomatoes lists the original series at 92% from critics and 93% from audiences, and no season or film in the franchise has ever scored below 60. Three feature films followed, in 2019, 2022 and September 2025. The last, Downton Abbey: The Grand Finale, set a franchise best with a 91% critics score and a 96% audience score. Reviewers called it a worthy sendoff, as soothing as a cup of tea. It follows the family into the 1930s as Mary becomes head of the Crawley's and faces the financial trouble and social disgrace of the era.

The West Wing started with a pilot that did not test well, and Warner Bros. reportedly created newly introduced demographics to help NBC sell it to advertisers. The show spanned seven seasons and 156 episodes and was created by Aaron Sorkin, who wrote most of the first four. The series premiered in late 1999 with Thomas Schlamme directing the signature walk and talk scenes. Martin Sheen's President Bartlett, leads a staff that includes Rob Lowe, Allison Janney, Bradley Whitford, Richard Schiff, John Spencer, and Dule Hill. The first season won nine Emmys, a record at the time, until Game of Thrones topped it in 2015.

Sorkin left after season four and remarked at a festival panel that he still had not watched the three seasons that followed. Critics rate the seasons unevenly, especially post his departure, with seasons five and six sitting much lower on Rotten Tomatoes. Even so, the audience score sits at 95%, well above the 81% from the critics. The series returned to Netflix in December 2025 and is also featured on HBO Max. A 2020 special reunited the cast, and a 25th anniversary event at the White House in 2024 inspired Sorkin to think about writing fresh episodes. The cast reunited again in late September for Whitford's Walk of Fame ceremony, and he said then that he would keep pressing Sorkin for a limited series. Sorkin told Entertainment Weekly he thinks about it for a couple of seconds every day, though he called it very tricky in the current environment, and no official looks exist.

Better Call Saul began on AMC in February 2015 as a Breaking Bad prequel that few expected to rival its parent. Vince Gilligan and Peter Gould built a slower, more intimate story around Jimmy McGill, a lawyer chasing respect from his brother and a bit of justice. The show lasted six seasons and 63 episodes as Bob Odenkirk's Jimmy gradually turns into Saul Goodman, while Rhea Seehorn's Kim Wexler becomes one of the series' emotional anchors. Jonathan Banks returns as Mike and Michael McKean's Chuck, Giancarlo Esposito's Gus and Michael Mando's Nacho add dangerous and tragic threads to an increasingly complex universe.

Sources indicate that the slow-burn spin-off story won over skeptics who expected something much lesser, as all seasons scored between 97% and 99% on Rotten Tomatoes. That is a pretty unprecedentedly high score for a show that never took home an Emmy. It finished with 53 nominations, with an Outstanding Drama Series selection seven times and Odenkirk earning six nominations as lead actor. That's a record that should stand for a while, with the next closest record for show with most nominations without a win being Newhart at 25. Breaking Bad won 16 Emmys in five seasons, which makes the shutout sting even further. Peabody, Critics Choice, WGA and AFI honors helped soften the blow. Fans point to episodes such as "Winner," "Bad Choice Road," and "Saul Gone" as proof of how good the series truly was.

In a recent development, david Chase worried that James Gandolfini might not seem threatening enough to play Tony Soprano, but Gandolfini's manager and the casting directors insisted. The story follows New Jersey mob boss Tony Soprano, a man in therapy for panic attacks and premiered on HBO in the first few days of 1999. Edie Falco is Carmela, Michael Imperioli plays Christopher Moltisanti, and Dominic Chianese plays Uncle Junior, all of whom add to the family drama and chaos.

The Sopranos grew from fewer than four million viewers to about 12 million at its peak, and it won 21 Emmys from 111 nominations. Two were for Outstanding Drama Series, which marked it as the first cable series to win that award. Gandolfini and Falco each won three Emmys, and the series received five Golden Globes and two Peabody Awards as well. Collider named it the best HBO show ever and the greatest drama of all time, and a 4K Ultra HD box set of all 86 episodes arrives on December 18th. The series was topping digital store charts again this Fall, nearly two decades after its conclusion. Many critics credit it with launching the Golden Age of television. A 2021 prequel film, The Many Saints of Newark, got mixed reactions and starred Michael Gandolfini as a younger version of Tony.

Newly introduced Jersey mob boss Tony Soprano deals with personal and professional concerns in his home and business life that affect his mental state, leading him to seek professional psychiatric counseling.

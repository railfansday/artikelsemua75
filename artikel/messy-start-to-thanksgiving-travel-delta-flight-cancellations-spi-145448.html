It has been revealed that delta Air Lines flight cancellations spilled into Monday after a tech outage snarled air traffic on Sunday. Combined with bad weather, it's made for a messy start to the Thanksgiving travel rush.

By 7:15 a.m. EST Monday, Delta had canceled at least 92 mainline flights for the day. That's about 2% of its planned schedule.

It came after the airline canceled or delayed thousands of flights on Sunday following a problem with a key system that automates weight and balance calculations. (Related: Tech Report: Porting fairseq wmt19 translation system t...)

The disruption led to mass delays at its Atlanta mega-hub — the world’s busiest airport — and cascaded to other airports around the country.

According to recent reports, delta remarked it had restored its automated system by late Sunday afternoon. But the disruptions continued to climb as the carrier worked to get operations fully back on track — and Mother Nature didn't help, either.

Delta's operations appeared to be improving on Monday, but travelers still had to deal with some residual disruptions from a messy Sunday at airports — especially on the East Coast.

According to recent reports, around 70% of the flight disruptions were due to the tech problems, the airline had said. The rest were tied to Sunday's weather problems, and air traffic control delays that resulted.

“We thank our customers for their patience and our people for their professionalism," Delta said in a statement to TPG. (Related: Tech Report: How we sped up transformer inference 100x ...)

Travelers had other problems to contend with as the Thanksgiving rush got underway, too.

Mother Nature also slowed air traffic at some major hubs.

Low cloud ceilings and poor visibility fueled flight delays and led to dozens of planes getting diverted.

New York’s LaGuardia Airport (LGA) was most heavily affected, with nearly 60% of all departing and arriving flights delayed Sunday, per FlightAware.

42 flights headed to LGA on Sunday were diverted to other airports, according to aviation analytics firm Cirium.

Many of those flights landed instead in Philadelphia; Albany, N.Y.; Harrisburg, PA; and Syracuse, N.Y.

These disruptions come a week after one of the most chaotic weekends at airports in recent memory.

Sources indicate that more than 18,000 flights were canceled last weekend after an outage on Nov. 13 to critical Federal Aviation Administration technology.

Last week, a brief technical outage at United Airlines also fueled a host of flight delays.

If your flight is canceled or significantly delayed, you’re entitled to a refund under U.S. Department of Transportation policy — but only if you choose not to fly.

If you get stranded in Sunday’s travel mess, it pays to know what the reason was for the disruption.

If you get stuck because of Delta’s tech outage, the carrier may be willing to pay for any hotel, meal and ground transportation costs you incur — if it’s a major delay. See details at FlightRights.gov.

The carrier on Sunday acknowledged it was providing those accommodations to passengers "when necessary.".

If you get stuck because of bad weather, it's less likely your airline will offer any sort of compensation. But keep your receipts: If you paid for your trip with a credit card that has travel insurance, you might be able to file a claim and seek reimbursement.

These latest travel woes also coincide, of course, with the start of the Thanksgiving travel rush — with airports expected to be very busy with holiday travelers over the next few days.

All eyes are on next Sunday — Nov. 29 — which could be the busiest day of the year to fly.

Industry observers note that last year, the Sunday after Thanksgiving set the record as the most crowded day ever at U.S. airports.

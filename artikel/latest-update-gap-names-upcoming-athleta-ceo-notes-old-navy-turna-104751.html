Gap Inc. on Thursday confirmed Ashley Kechter will be the next president and CEO of its Athleta brand, effective Dec. 14.

The retailer also mentioned Old Navy's turnaround is showing signs of progress, sending shares up more than 11% Friday.

Kechter comes to Gap from activewear retailer Vuori, where she was global president and oversaw the brand's global business strategy, marketing and financials. She was previously global brand president of Fabletics and began her career at Gap before that.

Industry observers note that "I'm excited to return to Gap Inc. and partner with [Gap CEO Richard Dickson] and the team to strengthen our connection with customers and shape the brand's next chapter," Kechter said in a statement. The announcement comes after Lululemon poached former Athleta CEO Maggie Gauger to be its chief product officer last month.

"Clearly, we're disappointed with Athleta's performance, and we recognize there's a lot of work ahead," Dickson told CNBC. "We had been in rebuild mode, but now we're really in turnaround mode.".

According to recent reports, dickson remarked the tech giant is focused on getting back to its fundamentals: fabrications, feature, form, function and fit. Dickson added that Kechter will be conducting a "comprehensive review" of the brand to reposition Athleta for future success. (Related: Grandma who accidentally invited a stranger to Thanksgi...)

While the execution of its strategy has thus far "fallen short," Dickson said this newly introduced approach will allow Athleta to fully realize its potential.

It has been revealed that gap also reported mixed fiscal third-quarter earnings on Thursday and posted flat overall comparable sales. Wall Street had been expecting a comparable sales increase of 1.3%, according to StreetAccount.

Sources indicate that net sales grew 2%, though demand softened in October and early November due to warmer weather, the company mentioned. Store sales increased 1%, while online sales increased 3%.

Gap also remarked it's recorded a $420 million adjustment to cost of goods sold and a $21 million adjustment to interest income from tariff refunds so far this year.

It has been revealed that the organization lowered its full-year net sales outlook to be "roughly flat," down from previous guidance of up between 1% and 1.5%. Gap is also now expecting adjusted earnings per share of between $2.29 and $2.34, down from an earlier expectation of between $2.35 and $2.45 per share.

Dickson mentioned in an interview that much of the lowered guidance was due to warmer weather in the first two weeks of the quarter, but he said Gap is expecting "meaningful acceleration" in shopping ahead of the peak holiday season.

"We're monitoring the trends, and we're going to remain agile as we respond to the changing business trends," Dickson remarked.

Here's how the firm performed in its fiscal third quarter compared with what Wall Street was expecting, based on a survey of analysts by LSEG:.

Recent findings suggest that for the quarter, Gap reported net income of $311 million, or 86 cents per share, compared with $236 million, or 62 cents per share, the year prior. Sales rose slightly from $3.94 billion the year prior.

According to recent reports, at Athleta, net sales were down 14% compared with last year, coming in at $220 million. Comparable sales for the banner sank 15%, which the company attributed to "limited traction" from efforts to modernize its merchandise and marketing. `.

The company's namesake brand saw 9% comparable sales growth for the quarter, with net sales of $1.1 billion, marking an 11% increase compared with last year. Dickson said it was the brand's 12th consecutive quarter of positive comparable sales.

"Gap is really firing on all cylinders, and the consistency of the brand is really giving us confidence in the brand's ability to continue its momentum," Dickson remarked.

The Old Navy banner saw flat comparable sales for the quarter and net sales of $2.3 billion, up 1% compared with last year. It's the first earnings report since the brand, which has been experiencing sluggish sales, named Michael Francis as its new CEO last quarter. Francis began his role at Old Navy on Nov. 2.

Banana Republic saw its net sales sink 3% compared with last year, which the company attributed to delayed demand for cold-weather categories and a women's fall assortment that fell flat. Wall Street had been expecting growth of 1.5%, according to StreetAccount.

Dickson said the brand has since introduced fresh products and seen demand "rebound" as cooler weather has set in across the country.

In a recent development, "We've been encouraged by the early customer response, and we should see sequential improvement in the fourth quarter," he remarked. "There's some work to do here, but with peak selling still ahead, I'm confident that the team is focused on the right areas and [will] ultimately position Banana well for growth.".

Anthropic researcher claims Claude helped him prove the Riemann hypothesis

Mmathematician at Anthropic indicates he has used the company’s Claude AI model to prove the Riemann hypothesis, one of the most famous unsolved problems in mathematics. Levent Alpöge made the claim in a note on his website on Friday, Bloomberg reported. (Related: How to train a new language model from scratch using Tr...)

“I believe I have, working with Claude, discovered a proof of the Riemann Hypothesis,” Alpöge wrote.

Recent findings suggest that the hypothesis, first proposed in 1859, concerns how prime numbers are spread out. It is one of the Clay Mathematics Institute’s seven Millennium Prize Problems, each of which carries a $1m prize. Alpöge notes his proof also covers a broader version, the generalised Riemann hypothesis.

In a recent development, he did not publish a traditional paper. Instead, he released the proof in Lean, a programming language that checks every logical step. The code runs to about 17 million lines, Science reported. (Related: How to generate text: using different decoding methods ...)

According to recent reports, andrew Sutherland, a senior research scientist at MIT, told Bloomberg he had checked the code on his own computer. He stated he had seen at least three other checks by professional mathematicians. Later on Friday, Sutherland put the chance that anything is wrong at roughly zero, Scientific American reported.

Recent findings suggest that however, other mathematicians urged caution. A proof that compiles in Lean still has to state the right problem, and humans have to confirm that it does.

Kevin Buzzard, a professor at Imperial College London, stated nobody yet understands what the proof shows, Al Jazeera reported. The proof has not been formally accepted.

Alpöge stated Claude did much of the work.

“This time Claude far outpaced me near the finish line,” he wrote.

Industry observers note that anthropic declined to comment to Bloomberg. The claim came a day after the organization’s record stock market debut.

Sources indicate that if confirmed, it would be the second Millennium Prize Problem that AI may have helped solve. In September, OpenAI remarked its models had produced a solution to the Navier-Stokes problem.

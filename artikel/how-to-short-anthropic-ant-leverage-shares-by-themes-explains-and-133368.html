According to recent reports, short Selling Anthropic StockShort selling means borrowing ANT shares through a brokerage, selling them and buying them back later. It needs a margin account, and newly listed stocks can be hard or expensive to borrow. Losses have no upper limit because there is no limit to how far a share price can rise, and a rising price can trigger margin calls.

Buying Put Options on ANTA put option gives its buyer the right to sell ANT at a set price before a set date. Listed options on ANT will not be available on its first trading day: exchange rules allow options on a large upcoming listing to begin trading no earlier than the second business day after the IPO day. The most a put buyer can lose is the premium paid, and options lose value as their expiration date approaches.

Inverse Anthropic ETFs: ANDD and ANSSThe Leverage Shares 2X Short Anthropic Daily ETF (ANDD) will seek -200% (-2X), and the Leverage Shares 1X Short Anthropic Daily ETF (ANSS) will seek -100% (-1X), of the daily performance of Anthropic common stock, before fees and expenses. Once introduced, they will trade like any ETF on Cboe, with no margin account and no share borrowing needed, and an investor cannot lose more than the amount invested. An investor could lose the full amount invested in ANDD if Anthropic's stock price rises by more than 50% in a single trading day.

ANUU, ANDD and ANSS are not yet trading. Their first trading date on Cboe is November 23, 2026.

Industry observers note that what Is the Difference Between ANDD and ANSS?ANDD seeks twice the inverse of Anthropic's daily move; ANSS seeks the inverse without additional leverage. If ANT falls 5% in a day, ANDD seeks a 10% gain and ANSS a 5% gain for that day, before fees and expenses. If ANT rises 5% in a day, ANDD seeks a 10% loss and ANSS a 5% loss.

Trump Justice Department Drops Attempt to Block Certification of Alaska Senate Election Result in "Voter Confusion" Case

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, I previously wrote about the Trump Justice Department's lawsuit seeking to block certification of the Alaska Senate election results, and the district court ruling rejecting it. The Trump DOJ argued that blocking the  results was necessary to prevent "voter confusion" caused by the presence of two candidates with very similar names on the ballot. As I  explained previously, Republican candidate Dan J. Sullivan can potentially be confused with Dan S. Sullivan (the incumbent GOP senator), and some voters might have accidentally chosen Dan J. even though they intended to back Dan S.

Industry observers note that today, as reported by prominent election law scholar Rick Hasen, DOJ filed a brief in the US Court of Appeals for the 9th Circuit seeking to dismiss the case. They also want the district court ruling vacated for procedural reasons (I will not opine on whether that's warranted). (Related: Industry News: Distributed Training: Train BART/T5 for ...)

It has been revealed that this brings to an end a lawsuit that was always a turkey of a case (though perhaps calling it that is an insult to turkeys!). As explained in my post on the district court ruling and in an excellent amicus brief filed by Rick Hasen, the case never had any merit, and a court decision ruling in its favor would have set a dangerous precedent.

Sources indicate that in addition, the election results show that Democratic candidate Mary Peltola got an absolute majority of the vote (about 50.1%), more than both Dan Sullivans and all other candidates combined. Thus, even if all of the 1.7% of voters who chose Dan J. actually intended to back Dan S., it made no difference. Alaska's system of ranked choice voting (under which, if no candidate gets a majority in the first round, votes get reallocated) might have further reduced any problem. But no reallocation took place here, because Peltola got an absolute majority to begin with. This is likely what led DOJ to finally thrown in the towel. (Related: Porting fairseq wmt19 translation system to transformer...)

Sources indicate that as noted in one of my earlier posts about the case, Democrats may well deserve blame for seeking to deceive voters in this way, even though it was never likely to change the result (evidence indicates Dan J. may have gotten on the ballot as part of a Democratic effort to siphon off voters from Dan S.). But for reasons outlined in the same post, this kind of thing is far from the worst example of exploitation of voter ignorance.

Sources indicate that peltola's win was mostly just part of the broader "Blue tsunami" which saw the Democratis gain 9 Senate seats, including in several traditional red states. But perhaps Peltola got over the top because of the immense popularity of her pet lizard! We could use more lizards on Capitol Hill.

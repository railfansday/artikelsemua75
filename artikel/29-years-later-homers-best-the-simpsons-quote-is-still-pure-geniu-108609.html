In a recent development, personally, my favorite-ever quote from Homer Simpson came during season 4 of The Simpsons, in an episode named “Whacking Day.” The episode is about the citizens of Springfield celebrating a local holiday named Whacking Day, when they lure all the snakes in Springfield to the center of town and then beat them to death with baseball bats. Everyone loves this holiday except Lisa Simpson, who finds it to be cruel.

Depressed at being the only one in Springfield who cares about the fate of the snakes, Lisa turns to her father.

Recent findings suggest that “Dad! Everyone likes Whacking Day, but I hate it. Is there something wrong with me?” asks Lisa.

“Yes, honey,” replies Homer in typical thoughtless Homer fashion.

“Then what should I do?” Lisa asks.

In reply, Homer gives her some advice that resonated with me more than it ever should have:.

“Just squeeze your rage into a bitter little ball and release it at an appropriate time. Like that day I hit the referee with a whiskey bottle. Remember that?”.

This is one of those lines from The Simpsons that didn’t just make me laugh, but kept running in my head over and over again for days afterward. Eventually, I realized it was because it pretty perfectly described my own rage issues, like when I would lash out at an especially slow driver in front of me, or some telemarketer who, at most, deserved only mild rage for trying to sell me a new warranty on my car. But just because it’s my personal favorite Homer quote, doesn’t mean it’s the best-ever quote from the Simpson family patriarch. (Related: Breaking: Japan debuts a ‘dumbphone’ that doesn’t conne...)

Recent findings suggest that with over 800 episodes of The Simpsons, there are literally hundreds of great Homer quotes The Simpsons writers and actor Dan Castellaneta have given us over the years — or, actually, decades. “D’oh!” is probably the single most famous Homer line, but it’s as much a sound as it is a quote, which probably doesn’t qualify as the best-ever line from the character. (It’s also a bit too easy a choice.).

Ideally, Homer’s best quote would do several things in one. It would tell you who Homer is, as well as speak to something much bigger than him — if it’s not completely universal, it should at least speak to some larger truth. It should have wisdom without being wise​​​​​​​, if that makes any sense.

It also has to be funny, which is a given.

Industry observers note that one of the most quoted Homer lines comes from the season 5 episode “Realty Bites,” where Marge becomes a real estate agent. When Marge first expresses interest in the profession, she says she’d like to “Give it a try.” In reply, Homer says, “I don't know, Marge,” before uttering a truism that cannot be argued with.

“Trying is the first step towards failure.”.

While this is certainly a good, quotable line that speaks to Homer’s laziness, there’s at least one even better, even more famous Homer quote that’s far more worthy of the mantle of his greatest line ever.

In the season 8 episode “Homer vs. the Eighteenth Amendment,” Homer battles a prohibition movement in Springfield. He eventually wins the battle and, at the end of the episode, he triumphantly shouts:.

According to recent reports, "To alcohol! The cause of, and solution to, all of life's problems.".

Sources indicate that that episode was written by John Swartzwelder, which means he probably wrote this line, though there's no guarantee, as Simpsons episodes have always been worked on in the Simpsons writers’ room, with every writer throwing in jokes. According to The Simpsons writer Josh Weinstein, who talked about the line in the old DVD commentary track for this episode, that line is, “One of the best, most truthful Simpsons statements ever.” It was deemed so good by the writers at the time that it was moved to the very end of the episode, just to give it a bit more of a triumphant moment.

In addition to that, in 2008, that line was included in Entertainment Weekly’s list of the "24 Endlessly Quotable TV Quips.”.

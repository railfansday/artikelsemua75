Most Americans picture sun-drenched mobile home parks across the South and Southeast when they think of manufactured housing, but more manufactured housing may soon start popping up across the Northeast and West.

A new economic research report from Realtor.com® senior economist Joel Berner takes an in-depth look at the manufactured housing guidelines in the 21st Century Road to Housing Act, which became law in June.

According to recent reports, berner found that cities across the Northeast—including Hartford, CT, Allentown, PA, and Albany, NY—have ideal conditions for a manufactured housing boom: “affordable land, unaffordable traditional newly introduced construction, and a low share of single-family starter home inventory.”If you’re not quite sure about what “manufactured housing" means, you’re not alone. For years, people seemingly used “mobile home” and “manufactured housing” interchangeably. But they’re not the same thing. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

For one, the “mobile home” classification was retired in 1980, though many still use it colloquially. That year, it was replaced by the term “manufactured home.” Broadly, manufactured homes are those built in a factory and then transported to a site.

For years, federal Housing and Urban Development code required that all manufactured homes have a permanent steel towing frame underneath. New provisions in the Road to Housing Act, meant to spur more affordable housing, eliminated that requirement.

The act further entitles these newly introduced chassis-free homes to the same financing, titling, insurance, taxes, transport, and installation as conventional homes.

The hope is that creating this latest category of manufactured home will help blunt one of the housing industry’s sharpest pain points: affordable starter homes. (Related: Industry News: Train a Sentence Embedding Model with 1B...)

Since the COVID-19 pandemic, the number of affordable starter homes for sale has markedly decreased.

It has been revealed that realtor.com research found roughly 300,000 fewer affordable “starter” listings on the market today than before the pandemic. The cost of those starter homes rose from $256,000 in 2019 to $344,000 in 2026. By comparison, the median price of a manufactured home in April 2026 was $134,800.

To understand what metros might see the biggest impact from this new type of manufactured home, Realtor.com scored the 100 largest metro areas on three metrics: the percentage of single-family starter homes (defined as listings priced at or below 80% of the local median) in the area; the relative price-per-square-foot premiums for traditional new construction; and the availability of affordable vacant land.

It has been revealed that six of the 10 opportunity metros were in the Northeast, including Hartford; Allentown; Albany; Rochester, NY; Syracuse, NY; and Worcester, MA. Other metros that made the list were Denver; Colorado Springs, CO; Albuquerque, NM; and Spokane, WA.

In Hartford, which ranked first, starter homes account for just 17.9% of single-family inventory, while site-built upcoming construction costs 29.1% more per square foot than existing homes.

Sources indicate that in third-ranked Allentown, single-family starter homes made up 22.9% of housing in the area, but newly introduced-construction costs were 33.6% more than existing homes. In sixth-ranked Albany, starter homes comprised 31.6% of the single-family inventory, but the price per square foot for new construction is 47% higher than for existing homes.

That’s nothing compared with seventh-ranked Rochester, where 33.7% of homes are starter but site-built new construction costs 97% more per square foot than existing homes.

Recent findings suggest that while the economics in these cities are compelling, another reason manufactured housing may become more popular in these metros is that frigid Northeastern winters limit conventional housing construction. Meanwhile, manufactured housing can be assembled year-round.

Before Hartford, Albany, or any other metro can begin taking advantage of the Road to Housing Act’s new manufactured housing rules, municipal zoning laws will need to change, Berner found.

According to Land Use Labs data cited in the report, only about 17% of zoned land nationwide explicitly permits manufactured housing communities, and many municipalities enforce minimum floor-size rules or design restrictions that block factory-built units.

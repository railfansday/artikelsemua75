Celtic extended their lead at the top of the Scottish Premiership table as they beat St Mirren at Parkhead on Saturday, but there were a couple of notable exclusions from the squad.

Martin O'Neill's men entered the international break full of confidence after successive wins against Besiktas and Kilmarnock at Celtic Park, and they maintained that form at home to St Mirren.

The experienced Northern Irish manager hasn't been afraid to omit people from the squad at times this season, as shown when summer signing Haissem Hassan was left out for the victory against Kilmarnock, having come off the bench against Besiktas days earlier. Rumours over the international break had suggested that the Egypt international was the most unpopular member of the dressing room, which O'Neill denied in his pre-match press conference ahead of St Mirren.

He said: "I did a thing with corporate fans the other night and we had a bit of fun with some questions. (Related: The one thing Putin, Trump and Xi agreed on — fewer lim...)

"Apparently something came out that Hassan was the most unpopular player in the dressing room, which is not true. I didn’t say this.

"I was joking about my own unpopularity — as a player because all I did was moan and then as a manager, which I don’t mind.".

Despite suggesting that a few players would be returning to the squad against St Mirren, Hassan was once again left out, with O'Neill suggesting it was down to rotation ahead of the Europa League trip to Torreense on Thursday.

The 74-year-old said: "He's not injured. We've got big games coming up. Some of the lads not involved today will be involved on Thursday. It's just a bit of rotation in the squad as much as anything else.".

While Hassan has shown his limitations defensively this season, he is one of their attacking players who offer the most threat in terms of taking defenders and beating them.

Alongside the 24-year-old, summer addition Oliver Sorensen was also left out of the 20-man squad on Saturday.

There was a lot of anticipation when Celtic signed Oliver Sorensen on loan from Parma at the end of the summer transfer window, with the Scottish giants also having an option to buy the Danish star.

The 24-year-old arrived with plenty of experience in Serie A and the Europa League, but he has failed to show up in a Celtic shirt so far.

Sorensen has made just one start in the Scottish Premiership this term and hasn't made an appearance since October 11, being left out of the squad entirely in the previous three league matches.

Having played just 236 minutes in total, it's clear that O'Neill doesn't fancy the midfielder, and it's hard to see him getting back into the XI at any point soon.

Speaking recently about Sorensen, Sky Sports journalist Anthony Joseph remarked:.

It has been revealed that sorensen was a bizarre signing, recommended by an agent to sort of fill in a gap in midfield because they knew that they weren't going to get Mahmic. Well, [they] weren't going to splash out on him and were in negotiations for Gineitis.

"Sorensen was meant to be a more attacking midfielder but every time I watch him I honestly don't know what he does!

It has been revealed that "I don't want to be disrespectful to him but I don't know what he brings to a football team. Every time he's on the pitch I can't see what type of player he is. I can't work it out and he's played plenty of games already.".

Parma paid around £7m upfront to sign Sorensen in 2025, so you'd struggle to think that their asking price for him would be much below that, if at all.

Given his limited minutes and the fact he isn't even making matchday squads, it's hard to see a way back for the 24-year-old under O'Neill from here.

Celtic, if they can, would be better off agreeing a termination of the loan deal in the January transfer window, to allow them to bring in another midfielder who can make a difference, like Gvidan Gineitis.

Okay, you'll have to stick with me for this one because it's going to take some explaining...

It has been revealed that so, all the way back in 2023, a supposed leak on the GTAForums (please beware of potential GTA 6 spoilers if you click this link) attracted relatively little attention. At the time, there was no way to prove that any of it was legit. (Related: Understanding BigBird's Block Sparse Attention...)

The "leak" in question revealed Rockstar's schedule, with the original poster claiming that a sequel to LA Noire would be the developer's next game, after GTA 6, and it was targeting a 2030 release.

Recent findings suggest that the reason it's being brought again almost three years later — chiefly via Reddit — is because as it turns out, the leaker did get a number of details about GTA 6 correct. Most notably, they managed to provide surprisingly solid info on the game's multiple endings — and they'd obviously be quite difficult to predict without some kind of insider knowledge.

Sources indicate that to be clear, not everything in the "leak" has ended up being correct. It's a little hit and miss, but then things can change in the space of two and a half years or so; it's possible that these inaccurate details were legit at or before the time of posting.

I think the deciding factor for the believers here is that Rockstar moving on to a newly introduced LA Noire would make some sense. (Related: How to train a newly introduced language model from scr...)

Assuming it's in line with the first game, you would think that it would be a much smaller production than something like Red Dead Redemption 3 — even with the tech and engine refinements the tech giant has made with GTA 6.

Plus, there is some LA Noire DNA in both Red Dead Redemption 2 and GTA 6; environmental and NPC interactions are key parts of Rockstar's most recent releases, and they arguably stem from LA Noire's investigative gameplay loop.

It's also worth remembering that after LA Noire's original developer Team Bondi was closed in 2011 (with Rockstar retaining the rights to LA Noire itself), its founder Brendan McNamara would go on to establish a newly introduced outfit called Video Games Deluxe.

Sources indicate that the Australian studio crafted LA Noire: The VR Case Files years later, and even took over post-launch support for GTA: The Trilogy following its incredibly shoddy launch. Video Games Deluxe was then acquired by Rockstar in 2025, and it was renamed Rockstar Australia. Hmmm.

Industry observers note that so some of this stuff seems to line up — but I don't think there's quite enough evidence here to nail the suspect. I mean, er, put your full trust in that original leak. Still, I wouldn't say no to another LA Noire.

Breaking: CrowdStrike to buy Zscaler in record-setting $43.9B cybersecurity deal

UPDATED 16:29 EST / NOVEMBER 23 2026.

CrowdStrike Holdings Inc. today declared plans to acquire Zscaler Inc., a fellow provider of cybersecurity software, for about $43.9 billion.

In a recent development, the all-stock deal values the latter company at $260.40 per share. That’s a 34% premium to Zscaler’s closing price on Friday, the day CrowdStrike’s takeover interest became public. The transaction would be the largest cybersecurity acquisition on record by a wide margin. (Related: Compound Interest Calculator: See Your Money Grow...)

It has been revealed that investors weren’t entirely thrilled. CrowdStrike’s shares fell 9% today, an indication that might think it’s an awfully large pill to swallow. Zscaler’s stock, however, rose almost 18%. (Related: Latest Update: Dividends Aren't What You Think The...)

The companies reported their quarterly results in conjunction with the acquisition announcement. Both topped expectations across the board. CrowdStrike posted adjusted net income of $354.9 million on $1.58 billion in revenue, while Zscaler reported a $208.4 million adjusted profit and sales of $949.3 million. The former company also boosted its full-year guidance.

According to recent reports, crowdStrike’s flagship Falcon platform uses lightweight programs called sensors to protect employee devices, cloud instances and other technology assets. Zscaler’s Zero Trust Exchange, in turn, protects the network connections that link together those assets. The platform is positioned as an alternative to virtual private network tools.

It has been revealed that the product portfolios of CrowdStrike’s and Zscaler overlap in some areas. Both companies offer tools that can protect artificial intelligence applications from threats such as prompt injections. They also compete in the secure browser category, although their respective offerings take different approaches to blocking malware.

Zscaler offers a tool that can automatically launch simulated cyberattacks against applications to find weak points. CrowdStrike joined the fray as well earlier this year with SafeMind, a set of custom AI models and related assets. It not only finds software vulnerabilities but also develops detections, automated workflows designed to spot hacking attempts.

It has been revealed that crowdStrike stated that it will use the network telemetry collected by Zscaler to enhance SafeMind’s training dataset. In 2024, the latter company disclosed that its platform blocked more than 9 billion threats per day. That activity, which has no doubt grown in volume since, should give CrowdStrike’s AI models a more complete understanding of hacker behavior.

Industry observers note that the organization’s intends for Zscaler also extend beyond AI training. CrowdStrike will incorporate the network protection provider’s software into its Flex offering, which enables customers to buy multiple cybersecurity tools via a single contract. That could help expand the adoption of Zscaler’s products among CrowdStrike customers.

Industry observers note that the companies have also identified other upselling opportunities. They disclosed today that about 8,000 Zscaler customers don’t yet use CrowdStrike products.

In a recent development, “With Zscaler, we’re building cybersecurity’s operating system: a single control plane to deploy AI, control AI, and enable any agentic application to run safely,” said CrowdStrike founder and Chief Executive Officer George Kurtz (pictured). “We see a path to become the largest AI safety and security organization the world has ever seen.”.

According to recent reports, crowdStrike expects to complete the acquisition in the second half of its 2028 fiscal year, which starts next February. The organization stated that the deal will be “significantly and immediately accretive” to free cash flow per share upon closing.

According to recent reports, crowdStrike expects Zscaler’s financial impact to grow over time. It estimates that the deal will unlock $500 million worth of annual cost synergies within 18 to 24 months, mainly in the form of data center, supplier and operational improvements. The company also sees Zscaler boosting its annual revenue run-rate by $1 billion within 30 months.

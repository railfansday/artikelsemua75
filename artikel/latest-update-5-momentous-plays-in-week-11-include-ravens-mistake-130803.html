Here are the five most momentous plays from Sunday's early and afternoon games.

Recent findings suggest that the Ravens coughed up Sunday's game in a colorful variety of ways. The first big shock was Justice Hill's brilliant run … which was capped by a gutting ending.

Baltimore led 12-7 and was deep in Carolina territory when Hill peeled off what appeared to be a 36-yard touchdown. But out of nowhere came Panthers cornerback Will Lee, who ripped the ball out at the final second. The fumble bounced out of the back of the end zone, giving the ball back to the Panthers in a shocking turn of events.

Replays demonstrated just how close Hill was to the end zone when Lee saved the day. Panthers head coach Dave Canales later called the forced fumble one of his favorite plays of his tenure. (Related: Animal control called to rescue alligator that turned o...)

The Ravens forced a punt on the next series, but — get this — they muffed the punt, setting the Panthers up in glorious field position. A few plays later, the Panthers scored a touchdown, giving them the lead at 14-12.

It was basically a 14-point swing — but the Panthers' lead was short-lived, as the Ravens ran back the ensuing kickoff for a touchdown, putting them back ahead.

Industry observers note that this easily could've been a Ravens blowout had a few plays gone differently early. And they weren't done yet!

A wild game that saw seven lead changes came down to the Ravens driving late and trailing 35-33 with just over a minute left. Even after all their mistakes, they had a chance to steal a road game and keep their dim playoff hopes alive.

Jacoby Brissett, who had a substantial game, was sacked with 1:30 remaining. About 25 seconds then ticked off the clock before he dumped a short pass to Hill, leading the Ravens to use their first timeout with 59 seconds remaining.

Brissett converted a fourth-and-1 sneak at his 31-yard line. The next snap came with 36 seconds left. The Ravens had three timeouts left at the start of the drive, and they had let almost a minute run off the clock to run four plays.

In a recent development, again, they were at least 30 yards from field-goal range. It was putrid clock management, and it seemed to make the Ravens tighter as they tried to win.

It has been revealed that brissett's next pass was tipped by the omnipresent Lee and intercepted by Tyrel Dodson to seal a Panthers win. The Ravens' sixth straight loss would seem to make a late-season Lamar Jackson return even less likely.

DK Metcalf's latest misstep cost the Steelers on Sunday.

Metcalf drew two personal foul calls in Sunday's 19-16 loss at the Eagles, getting disqualified with just under five minutes remaining in the third quarter. The second penalty came after a third-and-8 incompletion, and Metcalf wasn't directly involved in the play, with the ball going in the direction of Germie Bernard.

In a recent development, metcalf "just missed" poking the eye of the Eagles' Cooper DeJean, the Philadelphia defensive back told reporters postgame. He and Metcalf had been jawing all game to that point. The penalty pushed the Steelers back, though the immediate effect of his ejection wasn't felt, as Chris Boswell nailed the field goal to cut the Eagles' lead to 16-9.

It has been revealed that still, Metcalf left the game with one 2-yard reception and was gone for the remainder of a three-point Steelers loss. If there was a bright side, his absence gave an unexpected opportunity to Max Hurleman, who made multiple impressive catches, including a TD, in his NFL debut.

But with all due respect to Hurleman, for whom we're rooting, those catches should have been to the receiver the Steelers are paying $33 million per year.

The Buccaneers trailed 10-7 at halftime in Detroit. It appeared that Tampa Bay was about to take a second-half lead on its opening drive of the third quarter.

Irving ripped off an untouched 80-yard touchdown run, but the referees had other ideas. Luke Goedeke was flagged for holding, wiping out the play, and it appeared to be the right call, as Goedeke pulled the Lions defender to the ground.

Instead of a crowd-silencing TD, it was a first-and-long situation, backed up in Tampa's own end. Baker Mayfield's third-and-17 pass a few plays later came up a yard short, and the Bucs had to punt.

The Lions pushed the lead to 17-7 about three minutes later, with Jared Goff hitting Jameson Williams on a 57-yard bomb. The Bucs eventually cut the deficit to 17-14 before losing 31-21 in a game they never led.

In a recent development, J.J. McCarthy finished Sunday's win over the Jaguars with 173 yards passing, 33 more rushing, one touchdown pass and one critical tackle.

The Giants missed a field-goal try to open the second half, but their defense — which played great all game — forced the Jaguars to a three-and-out. Trying to pad a 20-6 lead, the Giants drove to the Jaguars' 32-yard line when disaster struck. Cam Skattebo coughed up a first-and-10 handoff, and Jaguars safety Antonio Johnson scooped it up.

Sources indicate that johnson had the look of a man trying to house it, continuing to look behind him for potential stalkers with a lot of green in front of him. But McCarthy raced over toward the sideline, and Johnson didn't appear to see him until it was too late. The QB took him down as the would-be free safety on the play.

The 42-yard return appeared to be the break the Jaguars needed, but it turned out that McCarthy's tackle saved seven points. The Jaguars turned the ball over on downs, and though the Giants couldn't immediately take advantage, saving the points was critical.

The Jaguars scored on three of their final four drives, and the Giants added only one more field goal, but McCarthy's tackle and Paulson Adebo's fourth-quarter interception of Trevor Lawrence were the massive plays the Giants needed to hang on.

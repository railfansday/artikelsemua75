Advanced Micro Devices (AMD -7.72%) briefly became a $1 trillion tech giant in September, but it lost that milestone after a prolonged artificial intelligence (AI) stock correction. However, the chipmaker is set to rejoin Nvidia (NVDA -3.30%) and Apple (AAPL -0.92%) as a trillion-dollar company. These are the catalysts that can drive the stock higher.

AMD has been viewed as the No. 2 graphics processing unit (GPU) chipmaker for several years, with Nvidia comfortably holding on to the top spot. However, Nvidia suddenly faces significant uncertainty with former CEO Jensen Huang out of the organization and angling for a comeback.

It has been revealed that nvidia at the moment doesn't seem receptive to a reunion, and that can cost the tech giant. Some investors argue that Huang's departure will make it difficult for Nvidia to continue innovating despite the competitive edge it has already obtained.

It has been revealed that the AI industry is moving rapidly, and leadership complexities can cost Nvidia valuable time. It can be the difference between missing out on the latest technology and watching hyperscalers turn to alternatives. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

This scenario positions AMD as the chipmaker with the most to gain from the confusion, and the manufacturer has been taking major steps to close the gap. For instance, AMD is making its chip fleet more attractive by making it much easier for developers to port existing Nvidia CUDA applications to its HIP platform.

It has been revealed that nvidia's CUDA was previously a major moat. Moving to another software essentially meant rewriting vast amounts of code, but that's no longer the case. If AMD can win over more businesses with an easier migration, it creates more headaches for a organization already full of them.

Industry observers note that although Nvidia has captured most of the headlines throughout the AI boom, AMD has been quietly accelerating its growth. Data center revenue surged by 97% year over year in the third quarter, while overall revenue reached $13.4 billion, up by 45% year over year. That also represents a 16% sequential boost. AMD CEO Lisa Su told investors that demand for its AI products is still accelerating. The chipmaker also remains confident that revenue growth will surge in the coming years, along with higher net profit margins.

AMD trades at a 30 forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio, which may be higher than what some investors are willing to handle. However, this same tech giant almost doubled its net income year over year. AMD wrapped up the quarter with an 18.2% net profit margin, and if it can get closer to Nvidia's 62% net profit margin, the stock should have an easy time reclaiming a $1 trillion market cap and marching toward new highs.

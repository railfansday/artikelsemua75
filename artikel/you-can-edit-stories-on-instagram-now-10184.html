Instagram has introduced a fresh feature that allows people to edit Stories after publishing them without losing existing views, likes, or replies.

Announced by Instagram head Adam Mosseri, the update gives users a 15-minute window to change text, music, stickers, and drawings after sharing a Story.

Industry observers note that previously, consumers had to delete and repost a Story to correct a typo, change its caption, or add music they had forgotten to include. The latest feature removes that requirement, allowing edits to be made while preserving the post’s existing engagement. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

The Verge notes that the ability to edit Stories was first spotted in development by reverse engineer Alessandro Paluzzi in April. It is now rolling out to customers. To make changes, users can open a published Story, select the three-dot menu in the bottom-right corner, and tap “Edit Story.” Edited posts will display an “edited” label when viewed by others. Industry observers note that the change also addresses some of the complications associated with deleting and reposting Stories, particularly when they form part of a sequence. Re-uploading content can disrupt the original order of posts, while creators and social media managers may also be concerned about the impact of reposting on engagement metrics.

“A lot of people have been asking for this, so I’m excited to get this one out there,” Mosseri indicates.

Alongside the editing feature, Instagram is introducing several changes to its Stories tools. These include new fonts, colors, and shapes for stickers, which will be organized into sticker sheets to make them easier to browse. The platform is also updating its multiple-photo selection process and adding new background options, including blur effects, colors and gradients.

Yesterday, PetaPixel reported on Instagram’s new tool that’s designed to help creators on the platform protect their work.

Called content protection, it allows eligible creators to monitor copies of their videos across Instagram and Facebook and decide how to respond when a match is detected.

EXCLUSIVE: As the controversial use of non-disclosure agreements (NDAs) rears its head once again in the UK entertainment industry, the head of Channel 4 News has told staff her division no longer engages in the practice.

Deadline understands Esme Wren sent an all-staffer on Saturday after the issue of Channel 4 News using NDAs was raised on BBC Radio 4’s Today programme. (Related: How to train a newly introduced language model from scr...)

Introducing the topic, Today host Nick Robinson stated: “Channel 4 News indicates they don’t use NDAs, well only for commercial matters, but others suggest they do and it’s difficult for people to get to the bottom of the truth in those cases where the nature of things makes it difficult to talk about.” Related Stories News Susan Sarandon Joins Natalie Dormer, Richard Armitage & Susan Wokoma In Signing Open Letter Calling For Entertainment Industry To "Acknowledge Role It Plays" In Enabling Bad Behavior News Russell Brand's Wife Promises Not To Pass On Further Prison Messages. Wren reacted to Robinson’s remarks by emailing staff and inviting questions during yesterday’s morning news meeting, we are told. Watch on Deadline.

Wren’s email, which Deadline has obtained passages from, said ITN “stopped using confidentiality clauses, which prevent people speaking freely about their time at ITN, over three years ago,” while “in a small number of cases where they were used in the past they have been lifted for both former and current employees.” She also stressed that ITN has “listened and apologised for things got wrong in the past.”.

ITN apologized unreservedly to those affected by NDA use after an independent review in 2024 found in a small number of historic cases, the use of confidentiality clauses in settlement agreements had been applied inconsistently or lacked clarity.

That review tasked ITN, which also makes news for ITV and Skydance UK network 5, with reviewing its use of NDAs and improving whistleblowing processes following allegations that it used NDAs to cover up gender pay discrimination, harassment and bullying. In a leaked recording obtained by Deadline from an ITN town hall a few weeks after the review came out, lead Channel 4 News anchor Krishnan Guru-Murthy criticized Channel 4 News’ management over the problem . This came after high-profile cases of ITN using NDAs were raised in parliament by British lawmakers.

An ITN spokeswoman mentioned: “ITN does not use NDAs to prevent people talking openly about their experiences with the manufacturer, including at Channel 4 News. We stopped using confidentiality clauses in settlement agreements in 2022, and also lifted them from the small number of people who had previously been subject to them. We have listened and learned, and apologised where we have got things wrong in the past.”.

Discussion about NDAs has returned to the fore in UK entertainment after Russell Brand was sentence to 10 years in prison for rape and sexual assault and allegations about David Walliams were published by The Guardian and ITN-operated ITV News, allegations that Walliams vehemently denies. Both celebrities were reported to have used NDAs, prompting British ministers to look into ways they can rapidly change the law to expand protections that stop NDAs being used to cover up harassment or discrimination. These protections are being implemented via the upcoming Employment Rights Act.

The UK’s influential Digital, Culture, Media & Sport Committee has annonced a high-profile probe into industry NDA use, with evidence sessions set to begin in two weeks. Witnesses and timings will be announced shortly and it feels highly likely that executives from ITN or Channel 4 will be invited to speak. Separately, Channel 4, where Brand used to host demonstrates, has been under pressure for historic NDA use, with CEO Priya Dogra telling the committee last week that the network uses confidentality clauses in a limited way with “pretty large caveats.” One lawmaker referenced Deadline’s exclusive report from last year that found Channel 4 had paid out nearly £5M ($6.5M) to ex-employees who signed settlement deals to prevent complaints escalating, but in doing so, stipulated that they do not discuss matters relating to the manufacturer.

Saudi Arabia and Bahrain reported attacks by Iran-linked forces over the weekend, as Iran's foreign minister said his government remains open to a deal to end the war with the U.S.

"Intercepted and destroyed a drone released by the Houthi terrorist militia toward the Riyadh area," the Saudi-led Coalition to Support Legitimacy in Yemen, which has been leading the fight against the Iran-backed group, stated Sunday in a post on X.

The coalition remarked late Saturday that its air defenses "intercepted and destroyed a ballistic missile" rolled out by the Yemen-based Houthis toward Saudi Arabia's western region, which includes some of the country's sensitive energy refining and export infrastructure. Neighboring Bahrain also mentioned it came under Iranian fire.

"As a result of the heinous Iranian aggression, a civilian industrial facility was targeted, causing a fire at the site," Bahrain's Ministry of Interior noted on X. The ministry later said two people, a Bahraini citizen and a Pakistani resident, were injured in the attack. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

Bahrain is home to the U.S. Navy's Fifth Fleet.

In a recent development, iranian state media reported late Saturday that the country's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps fired missiles and drones at U.S. military facilities in Bahrain.

According to recent reports, A separate Iranian media report claimed the IRGC's air defenses intercepted and destroyed a U.S. MQ-9 drone.

U.S. Central Command did not immediately respond to CNBC's request for comment on either Iranian report.

According to recent reports, meanwhile, Iranian Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi remarked his government remains open to a negotiated settlement to the war. An agreement aimed at developing a permanent peace deal signed in June between President Donald Trump and his Iranian counterpart Masoud Pezeshkian — the so-called Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding — later broke down.

"The Islamabad Memorandum of Understanding is no longer on the table and has no implementation relevance," Araghchi told the Iran newspaper.

"However, the commitments that the U.S. had accepted in that Memorandum of Understanding and the principle that the implementation of those commitments can pave the way for reaching a final agreement are still accepted by both sides," he said.

In a recent development, the war, which began with U.S. and Israeli airstrikes on Iranian targets on Feb. 28, has resulted in major disruptions to energy shipments through the Strait of Hormuz between Iran and Oman.

The United Kingdom Maritime Trade Operations reported that two tankers near Oman were struck by unidentified projectiles on Saturday.

Iran has imposed curbs on vessels moving through the strait, a major sticking point in negotiations with the U.S.

It has been revealed that oil prices have fallen for three straight weeks, however, as traders weighed strong Middle East flows and rising U.S. inventories.

According to recent reports, international benchmark Brent crude fell 3.4% for the week, while U.S. crude tumbled 5.2%.

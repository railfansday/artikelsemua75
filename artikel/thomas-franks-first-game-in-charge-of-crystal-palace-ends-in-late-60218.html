Thomas Frank changed formation in his first game in charge of Crystal Palace but it did little to alter the fortunes of the Premier League’s bottom club.

Recent findings suggest that there were noticeable improvements on Frank’s bow. Palace were more structured in attack, with cleverly orchestrated moves and a clear, coherent proposal for creating chances. (Related: Latest Update: Ukraine deploys homemade 'flying modems'...)

But the same problem persisted: they cannot defend. That diagnosis is simple to make. However, addressing it is complicated by inadequate personnel.

Palace replaced Marc Guehi and Maxence Lacroix with teenage defender Honest Ahanor and Chelsea castoff Axel Disasi in the summer.

Until they can address the oversight in their squad planning - thinking that would be enough to replace two international centre-backs - they will continue shipping sloppy goals.

Frank will address the structural problems, ensure players understand the positions they should occupy in specific situations and get Palace defending as a cohesive unit.

These are all things within his control and he began fixing them to some extent on Saturday.

Despite having a new manager in the dugout, the same old problems remained for Crystal Palace.

His in-game management worked somewhat as Palace went from defending frantically in the first half to looking much more solid and organised, before conceding in the 85th minute.

What he won’t be able to eradicate, though, are the individual errors that have consistently cost Palace this season.

Ultimately, those mistakes come down to the quality of the defenders at his disposal.

Palace will look to address their personnel challenges in January but they currently sit rock bottom of the league, with a massive game against Hull next weekend.

Unless they can cut out the errors that have contributed to their 27 league goals conceded this season, they risk being cut adrift before they can bring in reinforcements.

In a recent development, newly introduced boss Frank was more glass-half-full, though. ‘There is more positive than negative from the game,’ Frank said.

Industry observers note that ‘We looked like a threat. We scored two very good goals and we created quite a few more chances to score at least one more goal.

In a recent development, ‘That was the substantial positive. I was extremely happy with the effort and the mentality of the players.’.

In a recent development, defensively, their 3-2 defeat against Coventry was a horror show. Three players were dragged out wide - including Axel Disasi, who was miles out of position - allowing Jack Rudoni to find Taiwo Awoniyi in the penalty area to poke past Dean Henderson four minutes into Frank’s bow.

Coventry should have had a second, too, after Palace overcommitted in attack and Awoniyi was played in behind. One-on-one with Dean Henderson, he took the ball around the goalkeeper, only for Axel Disasi to somehow get back and clear it off the line.

Recent findings suggest that palace had been on the front foot but were caught out by how promptly they had to transition from attack to defence, unable to get enough bodies back in time. They are struggling to find a balance between defence and attack.

According to recent reports, anan Khalaili failed to win his tackle for Coventry’s second, giving Rudoni space to run down the right. Nobody shut Rudoni down and he found Tatsuhiro Sakamoto, who rocketed a thunderous half-volley into the top corner from outside the area.

Coventry’s 85th-minute winner came from a long throw, which Palace failed to deal with.

Frank abandoned the back three, which had served Palace well for the last two years and nine months, and pivoted to a 4-2-3-1.

Recent findings suggest that offensively, Palace improved. The formation suited Yeremy Pino and Jorgen Strand Larsen particularly well, with the pair combining for both of Palace’s goals.

It should also bring the best out of Quinten Timber, Daichi Kamada and Adam Wharton, as individuals are rarely isolated in midfield, unlike under Pierre Sage.

On the other hand, Tyrick Mitchell and Khalaili still tried to function as wing-backs rather than full-backs.

Sources indicate that that is ingrained in their habits, and it will take time to coach it out of them. However, it remains a significant glitch.

Both players committed themselves forward when attacking, leaving an already fragile centre-back pairing exposed and allowing Coventry to transition quickly and exploit Palace's vulnerability.

That cannot happen in a back four, as there were situations where Palace had eight players forward with only the goalkeeper and two centre-backs staying back.

Meanwhile, Coventry were far from convincing defensively but found a way to beat their relegation rivals, climbing to 13th on 12 points, three clear of the drop zone.

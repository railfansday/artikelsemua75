UPDATED 19:01 EST / NOVEMBER 20 2026.

Sources indicate that president Donald Trump today announced plans to impose a “tremendous tax” on U.S. companies that build data centers overseas. (Related: Hobbled at home, Trump turns abroad...)

“We’re not allowing data centers to go into other countries because China will take all of them, and we’re not allowing that to happen,” Trump told reporters during a White House briefing. (Related: Hakeem Jeffries and his team will seek to deliver a new...)

It has been revealed that that hints the blueprint may place particular emphasis on data centers optimized for artificial intelligence workloads. According to recent media reports, Chinese tech firms are using AI-optimized data centers in countries such as Malaysia to train their models. Such deals are designed to bypass U.S. restrictions on the export of advanced AI chips to China.

It has been revealed that if the tax initiative focuses specifically on AI data centers, it’s unclear what facilities would qualify. Cloud providers such as Amazon Web Services Inc. operate international data centers that include a significant number of graphics cards, but also run general-purpose workloads. Some of them were built before the advent of large language models.

In a recent development, last month, AWS revealed prepares to invest 18 billion euros in Germany next year. A part of the capital has been earmarked for data center capacity. A few weeks earlier, Google LLC committed 13 billion euros to building computing infrastructure and supporting assets in Finland. A significant latest tax on international data center projects may lead some industry players to revise their investment plans.

If Trump’s proposed tax is not focused on AI-optimized data centers alone, it could have even broader implications for cloud providers. He indicated that he did not discuss the plan with White House officials before announcing it. According to the Associated Press, there’s presently no information on how the tax would be implemented.

According to recent reports, “We want them to build it here. If they won’t go in Texas or they won’t go in California, they’re going to go in West Virginia,” Trump stated. “We have a lot of places that want them badly, and they’re great places.”.

It has been revealed that during this month’s midterm elections, voters in multiple states approved ballot measures designed to limit data center construction. On Thursday, a bipartisan group of lawmakers formed a congressional caucus dedicated to tackling AI risks.

Trump’s announcement of the data center tax plan comes a few days after the U.S. Commerce Department imposed newly introduced restrictions on overseas AI facilities. The rules are designed to curb Chinese companies’ access to cutting-edge graphics cards.

According to the Financial Times, at least two chipmakers have suspended some hardware shipments in response to the move. Nscale Global Holdings Ltd., a prominent neocloud, will reportedly have to secure a Commerce Department license to continue working with its largest customer.

When you're a fan of eggs, they can be pretty delicious however it is that they come off the stove or out of the oven. Even so, sometimes you're in the mood for something that doesn't require a lot of preparation yet is adaptable and tastes delicious. That's where scrambled eggs come in. They have a beautiful texture — whether you like them super soft and slightly gooey or a bit on the crispy side. There are a lot of different techniques, and some ways to cook them are better than others – like cooking them in water is probably a bad idea. Following some expert tips for scrambled eggs can get you to full-on restaurant quality; for example, fresh eggs at room temperature will give you great-tasting results that cook evenly. But when you want to keep it super simple at home, you can improve your everyday scrambled eggs by adding in a little bouillon. (Related: How to generate text: using different decoding methods ...)

In a recent development, either beef or chicken bouillon will work. Beef bouillon will give your eggs a deep, unctuous taste that can be delicious, but may be a bit heavy if you're not looking for something super bold. Using a little chicken bouillon, on the other hand, will add some bright, salty notes that still have plenty of extra umami. Chicken bouillon will bring out the natural savory, even slightly sweet flavor of the eggs without overpowering them — which makes sense because they're both, well, chicken. Whichever you choose to use, bouillon makes a super easy addition to your scrambled eggs that really only takes a few extra seconds of work. (Related: Latest Update: How to train a newly introduced language...)

As far as the bouillon goes, really anything will work — although it is often easier to get exact smaller measurements with powdered jars or pastes, like Better Than Bouillon Premium Roasted Chicken Base. Most standard bouillon cubes are equal to 1 teaspoon of bouillon powder or paste, but it can differ between brands, so take a look at the label. You'll want about a quarter teaspoon per three eggs. You can simply whisk the bouillon into the raw eggs with whatever seasonings and dairy you'd normally use (although you may want to lay off extra sodium because it's already quite salty), then scramble them to your preference. You can even wait to add the bouillon until the eggs have begun to set, then remove them from the heat and fold in the bouillon, so it just sort of melts in.

In a recent development, if you want to go beyond milk or cream, you can try it with a little crème fraîche, sour cream, or cream cheese, or even a Gruyère or sharp cheddar. Pepper is a must to bring out your egg's full potential, and you might want to even try a pinch of sugar for a hit of sweetness that blends in nicely — especially with chicken bouillon. As for the veggies and herbs, this trick will go great with pretty much everything you usually like to see in your eggs; just consider the flavor profile if you're using beef versus chicken. You can even use canned veggies in your scramble; for example, canned tomatoes are subtly sweet and tangy, pairing excellently with chicken bouillon while canned mushrooms add umami and even more depth to beef.

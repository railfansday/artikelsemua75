In a recent development, youTube has reached an agreement in principle to pay around $3B to thousands of US school districts that accuse it of harming students’ mental health, Bloomberg reported. The deal includes latest safeguards for teens and needs most districts to sign on.

Industry observers note that youTube is finalising a settlement of around $3B with thousands of US school districts, Bloomberg reported on Friday, citing people familiar with the matter. The districts accuse it of building an addictive platform that harmed students’ mental health. (Related: Industry News: Judge blocks construction of Trump’s arc...)

In a recent development, it would be the largest corporate settlement ever reached with US school districts. The agreement in principle also includes latest safeguards for teens. Google declined to comment, as did the lawyers leading the districts’ cases. (Related: Leading House Democrat targets Trump’s inner circle for...)

The deal needs a majority of the districts to sign on and will likely take a few months to finalise. The districts want money back for the costs of social media addiction, such as extra counselling for students. Schools are a key market for Google, which also faces pushback from its own staff over AI in student Search.

According to recent reports, more than 10,000 lawsuits have been filed against YouTube, Meta, Snap and TikTok over the past five years. They claim features such as endless scrolling and autoplay have caused addiction, anxiety and depression in young people. Earlier this year, a Los Angeles jury found Meta and YouTube liable in the first bellwether trial.

Meta agreed in August to settle with nearly every US state for about $18B, by its own count, and to cap teens’ scrolling at two hours a day. In May, YouTube paid just over $2M to a Kentucky school district that also sued Meta, Snap and TikTok. In June, it settled with a teenager to avoid a second California trial.

The next school case, brought by a district in Tucson, Arizona, is due to go to trial in February. Meta, TikTok and Snap remain defendants.

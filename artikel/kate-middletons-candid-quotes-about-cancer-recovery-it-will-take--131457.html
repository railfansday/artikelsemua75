In an Instagram post shared January 14, 2025, Kate confirmed she was officially in remission.

“It is a relief to now be in remission,” she wrote at the time. “And I remain focussed on recovery. As anyone who has experienced a cancer diagnosis will know, it takes time to adjust to a fresh normal.”.

Visiting The Royal Marsden that same month, Kate told staff, “You think treatment’s finished, crack on with that … daily tasks, but that’s still like a real challenge.”.

It has been revealed that she also acknowledged the ripple effect on loved ones, saying, “I feel like it’s sometimes for the loved ones around us. They need support just as much as I did as the patient.”.

During a visit to Colchester Hospital in Essex in July 2025, Kate offered one of her most honest assessments of post-treatment life.

“You put on a sort of brave face, stoicism through treatment. Treatment’s done, then it’s like, ‘I can crack on, get back to normal,’ but actually, the phase afterwards is really, really difficult,” she stated, while speaking with local patients. “You’re not necessarily under the clinical team any longer, but you’re not able to function normally at home as you perhaps once used to. Someone to help talk you through that, show you and guide you through that sort of phase that comes after treatment, I think is really valuable.”. It has been revealed that she continued, “It’s a roller-coaster, it’s not smooth, like you expect it to be. But the reality is you go through hard times.”.

After completing the National Three Peaks Challenge in June 2026, Kate explained via Instagram why cancer care has to go beyond medicine.

“Cancer doesn’t just affect the body. It changes how you think and feel and profoundly affects every aspect of life. I know this personally, and that the journey through and beyond treatment requires more than medicine alone,” Kate wrote via Instagram. “Healing, whether personal or collective, is not just about fixing what is wrong. It is about finding balance in how we live. Between effort and acceptance, between control and trust, between thinking and simply being. Because in the end, bravery isn’t just about pushing forward. It is about knowing how to stay grounded, connected and present, no matter the terrain or landscape you are walking.”.

Speaking to Country Life for a profile published in November 2026, Kate revealed what she was told in the hospital about the road ahead.

Industry observers note that “I was told in hospital that my recovery would not be linear, there will be good days and bad days, that it will take time,” she told the outlet. “This is the same in nature. It indicates us that periods of rest, reflection and renewal are equally as important as moments of growth.”.

She also credited the outdoors with helping her heal on a deeper level.

Recent findings suggest that “Yes, serious illness creates a lot of physical challenges, but there is also a deeper part to all of us. Nature has allowed me to find a deeper connection to my mind, body and spirit,” Kate explained. “I think that’s because nature offers something unique. It doesn’t ask anything of us.

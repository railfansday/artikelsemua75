Recent findings suggest that bloom Energy (BE -13.97%), a developer of solid oxide fuel cells (SOFCs), was once considered a niche green energy stock. But over the past two years, its stock has rallied more than 530% as the data center market's rapid expansion generated strong tailwinds for its business. Bloom was also added to the S&P 500 (^GSPC -2.43%) this September. (Related: How to Start a Side Business While Working Full-Time...)

Bloom's SOFCs can convert natural gas, biogas, propane, and hydrogen into electricity without any combustion. Its systems can often be deployed in less than two months and bypass traditional utilities that often require years to set up new connections. Its new Power Connect System could further reduce its on-site installation time by over 40%. (Related: How to Budget for a Wedding Without Going Broke...)

Sources indicate that A growing number of data center operators -- including Oracle (NASDAQ:ORCL), CoreWeave (CRWV -11.27%), Nebius (NBIS -13.77%), and Equinix (EQIX -3.25%) -- are using Bloom's SOFC systems to expand their AI infrastructure. That's why its backlog hit $20 billion at the end of 2025, and why it expects its revenue to more than double from $2.0 billion in 2025 to at least $4.4 billion in 2026. But will its stock head even higher over the next five years?

From 2020 to 2025, Bloom's revenue and adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) increased at CAGRs of 23% and 82%, respectively.

NYSE · As of 2:15 PM ET 11/20/26.

According to recent reports, but from 2025 to 2028, analysts expect Bloom's revenue and adjusted EBITDA to grow at CAGRs of 73% and 126%, respectively. With an enterprise value of $50 billion, it still looks like a bargain at 25 times next year's adjusted EBITDA.

Recent findings suggest that however, that's mainly because Bloom's stock has pulled back more than 50% from its all-time high of $345.85 on June 22, 2026. That pullback can be attributed to the Fed's rate hike in September, which made it more expensive to fund newly introduced data center projects; a major delay at Oracle's Project Jupiter AI data center campus; and a short-seller's allegations that Bloom's supply chain was dependent on China. The Democratic sweep of the midterm elections could also throttle the construction of new data centers with tighter regulations.

According to recent reports, those challenges compressed Bloom's valuations, but its longer-term catalysts remain intact. The data center market is still growing, and tighter environmental restrictions could actually boost its sales of SOFC systems. Oracle should get Project Jupiter back on track, Bloom has directly refuted the short-seller's claims, and it's gradually expanding into more non-AI markets. Bloom's free cash flow -- which turned negative in the third quarter of 2026 after four consecutive quarters in positive territory -- should improve as those tailwinds kick in.

If Bloom matches analysts' estimates through 2028, grows its adjusted EBITDA at a 25% CAGR through 2031, and trades at a more generous 20 times its current-year adjusted EBITDA by the final year, its stock price could rise more than 145% over the next five years.

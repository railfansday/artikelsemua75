Sources indicate that 2026 is the year of the horror genre! Early in the year, 28 Years Later: The Bone Temple set the tone for genre fans with its sincere twist on the infected’s origins; then Sam Raimi’s return to genre with Send Help gave fans a dark comedic twist, and with franchise sequels like Scream 7, Scary Movie, and Insidious: Out of the Further, fans returned to familiar territory.

This year, numerous small-budget features also left a lasting impression. Kane Parsons’ Backrooms adapted the viral liminal-space horror to fans’ satisfaction and pulled in over $400 million worldwide on a meager $10 million budget. Then there’s YouTuber-turned-director Curry Baker’s Obsession. It is hands down the breakout supernatural psychological horror of the year, rising to become a massive cultural phenomenon and worldwide sensation, let alone becoming one of the most financially successful movies ever made.

Recent findings suggest that we follow socially awkward Bear Bailey (Michael Johnston), consumed by infatuation with his longtime crush, Nikki (Inde Navarrette). Unable to express his feelings, Bear breaks open a novelty trinket called the "One Wish Willow" and wishes for Nikki to love him more than anything else in the world. It works instantly, transforming Nikki into an intensely devoted partner. Things take a turn when her affection quickly escalates into erratic, violent, and even lethal behavior. As bodies pile up, Bear realizes Nikki’s true consciousness is trapped inside her own body, forcing him to confront the horrifying cost of reversing the wish. The film took off with younger audiences, who related to the themes of abusive relationship dynamics and coercive control. The film explores the toxic “nice guy” entitlement, exposing how seemingly harmless infatuation can curdle into selfishness and emotional coercion. The loss of autonomy and consent is reflected in Nikki’s real self being trapped inside her own body. And the narrative mostly warns the audience to be careful about what you wish for.

In a recent development, with its thematic relevance and a gripping narrative, Obsession became a breakout hit, earning $286 million worldwide on a $750,000 budget. Further, it received wide praise for Navarrette’s brilliant performance, where she balances manic devotion with flashes of trapped terror, while Johnston was praised for capturing Bear's shift from pathetic to monstrous. After enchanting audiences in theatres, Obsession is now casting a spell on streaming services. The feature has spent over 185 days on Apple TV Store’s global top 10 charts. It has also spent over 140 days on Prime Video.

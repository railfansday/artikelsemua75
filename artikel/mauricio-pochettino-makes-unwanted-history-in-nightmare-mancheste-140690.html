Mauricio Pochettino made unwanted history on his debut as Manchester United manager on Sunday.

The Argentine watched on in anguish as his new side were thumped 4-0 by Liverpool at Anfield, thanks to goals from Dominik Szoboszlai, Florian Wirtz, Rio Ngumoha and Victor Munoz.

It started horrendously for the 54-year-old as Leny Yoro was sent off just eight minutes in for denying Alexander Isak a clear goal-scoring opportunity, and that was verified by the Premier League Match Centre on social media.

Pochettino, the former Tottenham and Chelsea head coach, has become the first manager in Premier League history whose team has conceded twice and had a man sent off in the first half of their first match in charge.

There was more positive history for the victors, with Ngumoha breaking the fixture’s record for youngest goalscorer as he slid in the third from Bradley Barcola’s breath-taking break, as the Frenchman squared for his opposite winger to slot home beyond Senne Lammens.

He overtook Michael Owen, who was spotted pitchside on TV punditry duty, at just 18 years and 85 days.

The young England star also became the youngest Liverpool player to score and assist in a Premier League game since Owen on Boxing Day back in 1997, when the striker did the same against Leeds United.

The result leaves Liverpool fourth in the Premier League table, seven points off league leaders Arsenal, while Pochettino’s United are mired in the relegation zone, having won just once in 12 matches in all competitions.

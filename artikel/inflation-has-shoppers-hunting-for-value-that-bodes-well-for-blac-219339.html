Inflation has shoppers hunting for value. That bodes well for Black Friday

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Economic headwinds have made consumers more cautious — but latest data and reports from retailers are painting a picture of robust holiday spending through Black Friday and beyond.

The macroeconomic environment has squeezed wallets. High inflation, fluctuating gas prices and labor uncertainty have dampened consumer sentiment and contributed to a pullback in spending. October's consumer price index rose a seasonally adjusted 0.4%, coming in higher than expected, while the personal consumption expenditures price index accelerated to a seasonally adjusted 3.8% in October compared with last year. Meanwhile, October retail sales grew at their slowest pace in nearly a year, according to the CNBC/NRF Retail Monitor.

The Conference Board's Consumer Confidence Index slid to its lowest since 2014 in September, with respondents citing concerns about inflation and the job market. It's translated into caution from some retailers like Walmart and Lowe's, which have reported slower sales, smaller baskets and drops in major-ticket and discretionary purchases. Despite that pullback, recent earnings reports and end-of-year forecasts suggest consumers are still willing to shell out for the holidays.

Planned spending on gifts and nongifts is estimated to be $1,049 on average this year, up 5.9% from $990 last year, according to The Conference Board. Adjusting for inflation, that figure is still up 3.2% year over year.

It has been revealed that holiday sales are expected to top $1 trillion this year, but that growth is boosted by inflation. Though consumers have said they're willing to spend, experts have noted that shoppers are still emphasizing value and looking for deals where they can. Adobe data found that in the first 22 days of November — what it considers the beginning of the holiday season — consumers spent $82.5 billion online, marking a 9.1% year-over-year increase. That's translating to the Black Friday and Cyber Monday week, too, as consumers take advantage of deals that started days before the traditional Friday blitz. Salesforce is predicting a record-breaking holiday week between Nov. 24 and Nov. 30, as it expects online sales to reach $357 billion globally and $84 billion in the U.S., both marking 6% year-over-year increases.

According to recent reports, the report also found that retailers are offering bigger markdowns than last year, with discount rates up 8% in the U.S. in November.

And according to Deloitte's Black Friday survey, participation in the shopping holiday this year is expected to be the highest seen since 2016, with 87% of shoppers reporting they blueprint to buy during Black Friday and Cyber Monday sales.

That number is up from 82% in 2025. Consumers are expecting to spend an average of $624 this holiday, similar to last year's numbers, and Generation Z is going all in on those deals, according to Deloitte, which surveyed 1,200 consumers in mid-October. That echoes similar behavior from the cohort last year.

"It's sort of the mix of feeling the squeeze on prices, people thinking about their economic condition and their financial position, but still wanting to protect the holidays," Brian McCarthy, retail strategy lead at Deloitte, told CNBC in an interview.

It has been revealed that he added that even as shoppers pulled back on spending over the past few months and delayed major purchases, that spend is now being more heavily concentrated into promotional windows like the holiday season. They're searching for more deals, becoming more savvy and deferring some spending.

The Deloitte data also found that 40% of Gen Z shoppers proposal to use buy now, pay later for their shopping this holiday season.

"People do care about the holidays and want to make sure they can buy those gifts to share with their friends and family, but it's another way of deferring when they're going to have to pay for that item," McCarthy noted.

On the other side of the cash register, retailers are expecting similar holiday shopping behavior and reported optimism about the consumer in earnings reports over the past two weeks.

Recent findings suggest that walmart CFO John David Rainey told CNBC last week that the company is continuing to see a resilient consumer. The retailer is leaning into value with rollbacks and deals, and Rainey said the holiday quarter was "off to a pretty good start" as macroeconomic trends start to show signs of potential improvement.

"The fact remains that payrolls are healthy, the equity markets are holding up in the face of geopolitical uncertainty, and even though consumer sentiment is somewhat lower, it hasn't translated into reduced economic output," Rainey mentioned.

Target executives also said last week that the retailer is optimistic about holiday spending this season. The company is pricing thousands of products at $5 and under and investing in elevated products while prioritizing low prices, they said.

Industry observers note that "We're encouraged by the start of the quarter that we've seen so far," CEO Michael Fiddelke told reporters following the tech giant's third-quarter earnings report. "We're really excited for what guests are going to find at Target this holiday season.".

Best Buy CEO Jason Bonfig told CNBC on Tuesday that while the tech giant is seeing consumers place higher emphasis on value, those shoppers are also looking to upgrade products, sometimes into "more premium price points.".

Lowe's is also expecting a solid holiday season, with CEO Marvin Ellison telling CNBC last week that the company's November sales to date are in line with its expectations. The home improvement company is also emphasizing deals for customers who may be more price-conscious, but Ellison said he's still "optimistic.".

Executives at TJX Companies, a retailer known for its value proposition, said on an earnings call with analysts last week that the organization is expecting sales momentum to accelerate throughout the holiday season.

"In the current environment, we believe value will be top of mind for many consumers this holiday season," CEO Ernie Herrman stated on the call.

Still, TJX said warmer weather in the beginning of the fourth quarter led to a sales slowdown. The tech giant also reported weak fourth-quarter guidance, coming in below Wall Street expectations for earnings and same-store sales. Competitor Burlington Stores also posted weak sales and provided a lower-than-expected outlook.

Gap Inc. saw a similar trend. CEO Richard Dickson told CNBC that the company's sales lagged at the beginning of the quarter due to warmer weather. The retailer posted flat comparable sales for its fiscal third quarter last week and reported sinking sales at its Athleta and Banana Republic banners. It also lowered its guidance.

But Dickson said the retailer is optimistic about colder weather beginning to drive spending again.

"We're entering the peak holiday season and selling weeks with pent-up demand and hopefully maintained good weather for shopping," Dickson mentioned. "This could be, and would be, a source of upside.".

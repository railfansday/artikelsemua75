According to recent reports, tesla plans to wind down Cybertruck production next year as Elon Musk shifts attention to its Cybercab. Here's what the change means.

It has been revealed that tesla Cybertrucks have dominated headlines even before they hit the road. But now it appears their time in the automotive world spotlight might be coming to an end. The company’s CEO Elon Musk says Tesla will wind down Cybertruck production at its Texas factory to make room for the Cybercab, the company’s self-driving taxi. Learn why the company is changing direction and what the news means for the Cybertruck.

Austin American-Statesman/Hearst Newspapers/Getty Images.

In a Nov. 6 post on X, Musk wrote, “Tesla will be winding down production of the Cybertruck at Gigafactory Texas around the middle of next year to make room for increased Cybercab production.”.

Tesla began delivering Cybertrucks in late 2023, but sales haven’t lived up to expectations. According to Kelley Blue Book estimates published by Cox Automotive, U.S. sales fell by nearly half in 2025. The truck has also faced criticism for its price, design, and quality issues, as well as several safety recalls.

Current owners can continue driving their Cybertrucks, and existing warranty terms still apply. Cybertruck lead engineer Wes Morrill also addressed concerns about future repairs. “Tesla will maintain spare parts and service will continue support,” he wrote on X on November 6. He didn’t specify how long that support would last. (Related: Build A Racetrack codes (November 2026)...)

For anyone still waiting on a Cybertruck, the news is less encouraging. In a Nov. 9 post on X, Musk wrote, “We will try to make as many as possible before shutting down the line in about 8 months, but will have to return deposits for a lot of people.”.

Tesla hasn’t said which customers will receive their trucks or how it plans to handle the remaining orders.

Sources indicate that the Cybercab is Tesla’s two-seat electric car designed to drive itself. It doesn’t have a steering wheel or pedals because it won’t need a human driver. Tesla has already started offering Cybercab rides in parts of Austin. The manufacturer aims to expand the service, although the vehicles aren’t widely available yet.

Industry observers note that musk denies that Tesla is ending production because the Cybertruck isn’t profitable. He states the manufacturer can put its Texas factory to better use by making Cybercabs.

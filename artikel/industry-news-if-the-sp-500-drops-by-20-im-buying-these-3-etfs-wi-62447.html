In a recent development, declines of 20% or more in your portfolio are unquestionably painful. But they can also be opportunities. By buying the dip and taking advantage of discounted share prices, you can actually improve your portfolio's long-term returns.

Here are three exchange-traded funds (ETFs) that I'd target if the buying opportunity presents itself.

It has been revealed that the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF (VOO -3.42%) isn't going to be the most exciting choice in the world, but it's perhaps the most logical. Sometimes it's best not to try to find the next substantial winner. By buying this fund, you'd simply be participating in the market's broad recovery. It's the act of buying on sale that matters most.

NYSEMKT · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

What I find interesting about the Vanguard S&P 500 ETF is that the combination of strong earnings growth with only modest returns this year has pushed the forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of the index all the way down to 19, the lowest it's been since 2023.

The ratio bottomed around 15 during the worst of the 2022 bear market, a level that could be reached again with another 20% drop (assuming that earnings expectations hold). That could bring buyers back into the market.

Sources indicate that the Invesco QQQ ETF (QQQ -5.67%) would be a more aggressive play on an eventual stock market recovery. Given its heavy tech and growth tilts, it's reasonable to think that this fund might decline more than the S&P 500 in a bear market. That's exactly what happened in 2022. The counter-argument is that it could also rise faster on the way back up.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

It has been revealed that investors maintain a fascination with tech and artificial intelligence (AI) stocks. Whenever sentiment has turned higher over the past few years, tech usually found itself leading. Even during 2022 when tech was one of the worst-performing sectors, it was the one that investors returned to fastest.

Recent findings suggest that the Invesco QQQ ETF isn't a pure tech ETF, of course, but a lot of people use it as a proxy. Assuming the AI revolution narrative has years to go, which seems pretty likely, the opportunity to get back into the trade at much lower prices should prove enticing.

Sources indicate that the Avantis U.S. Small Cap Value ETF (AVUV -1.82%) might be a bit of a surprise pick, but there comes a point when the value is too good to ignore. It's the value factor that can help cushion against losses in a down market. But it's also the fact that small-caps tend to outperform in recoveries that makes this option particularly compelling.

NYSEMKT · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

This portfolio currently trades at a forward P/E of around 11. With another massive pullback in stocks, that ratio could actually slide into the single digits. But the value story isn't enough. The small-cap space (and small-cap value especially) is filled with a lot of unprofitable companies that very well might not survive another bear market.

The Avantis U.S. Small Cap Value ETF, however, targets more profitable companies with strong fundamentals and cash flows. The quality factor is a key selling point, not to mention that value and small-caps both tend to do well in recovery scenarios.

According to recent reports, each of these ETFs has a compelling case as a buying opportunity during a bear market. They may all react differently to conditions, but it's the opportunity to buy at deeply discounted prices that's the most attractive feature. In that case, what you buy isn't as important as the idea that you're just buying, period.

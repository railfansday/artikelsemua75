Grant McCann’s return to Doncaster Rovers has been an undoubted success, but the cracks are beginning to show.

Rovers have slumped into the relegation zone of League One, been embarrassed by non-league opposition in the FA Cup, and lacked the signs of a team who could improve.

McCann’s men are flailing. They have won just one of their most recent five league fixtures. That was an impressive victory against a sub-par Sheffield Wednesday side, who sacked Henrik Pedersen in the aftermath.

According to recent reports, they have shipped six goals away to Milton Keynes Dons, three at Luton Town, and a further three at Kidderminster Harriers. The latter saw them exit from the FA Cup at the first time of asking, further worsening their away form.

In the league, Rovers have won just once away from home this season, losing five and drawing the other of their seven trips. Their xG does place them 16th in the table, but their xG conceded puts them in the relegation zone with poor defensive statistics in particular, according to FotMob. No one has scored more than three goals for them in league action so far this term, with a form in parallel to the run that was play-off worthy form after the closure of the January transfer window last term.

That is the key for McCann. The form of his Doncaster team in the second half of campaigns.

Industry observers note that rovers collected 30 points in the last 18 games of the 2025/26 season, with only five teams managing more. That included both automatically promoted sides, and two of the teams who finished inside the play-offs alongside one who landed just outside. It was the same the prior season, with their form after the closure of the January transfer window better than everyone else in League Two on route to their title win.

That theme can be stretched even further back, with the 2024/25 season seeing Doncaster manage the best record of any team in the fourth tier between the beginning of February and end of the campaign. That included a perfect home record of eight wins from eight, and only a single loss away from the then Eco-Power Stadium.

That season may have ended in bitter disappointment, with a penalty shootout defeat to Crawley Town in the semi-finals of the play-offs, but it was the first of three consecutive campaigns where post-January results had fixed a problem caused by early-season woes.

This time around it feels different. Chunks of the Doncaster fanbase have turned on McCann, not for the first time, with pointed chants directed at the Rovers manager following their chastening loss at Kidderminister. The dressing room and then the boardroom could follow suit if results, and performances, do not turn in the right direction.

Sources indicate that the fixture list has not been kind either, with high-flying trio Bradford City, Reading, and league-leaders Huddersfield Town the next three opponents for the struggling Doncaster. McCann needs to get his team creating more chances, finishing them at a more consistent rate, and defending in a more cohesive and structured manner at the opposite end of the pitch.

To expect a trend to continue for a fourth season in a row might be asking for one too many miracles. If underwhelming performances continue to stack up and points continue to be lost, then Rovers could end up in a hole that even the fortunes of McCann’s recent second-half of the season displays can not dig them out of.

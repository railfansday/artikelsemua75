Industry observers note that even for a large and successful firm, it's never easy to maintain a dividend, let alone increase it on a regular basis. Happily for income investors -- or really, anyone who's looking for a steady source of yield -- there's a long list of stocks that pump out a constantly rising flow of dividends. (Related: Industry News: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

According to recent reports, three prominent examples of this are beverage titan Coca-Cola (KO -1.14%), big pharmaceutical organization AbbVie (ABBV +0.79%), and cleaning and household products mainstay Clorox (CLX -0.59%). This trio has kept their dividend raise trains running for decades; read on for more about these ultra-steady lifters and whether their steadily increasing payouts support the buy cases for these stocks.

Of our trio, Coca-Cola takes the crown for longest current dividend raise streak, at a tough-to-beat 64 years. Speaking of crowns, this makes it a Dividend King, i.e., one of the select few publicly traded companies that have upped their payout at least once annually for a minimum of 50 consecutive years.

Like clockwork, this past February Coca-Cola declared its latest dividend raise, cranking it almost 4% higher to a new quarterly amount of $0.53 per share.

In a recent development, thanks to its asset-light business model -- the manufacturer mainly sells the syrups for its beverages, generally leaving capital-intensive processes like bottling to others -- it's highly profitable. It also generates very substantial free cash flow (FCF). (Related: How to train a newly introduced language model from scr...)

Coca-Cola also manages to grow its fundamentals in most quarters, an accomplishment given its already considerable presence and the current unpopularity of unhealthy food and drink. In its third quarter, the organization's net revenue rose by 7% to $13.4 billion, while non-GAAP (adjusted) net income rose 11% to $3.9 billion. FCF was over $4.5 billion, far more than enough to finance the under $2.3 billion in dividend payments that quarter.

Unless the bottom falls out of demand for the world's favorite soft drink and the many other tipples in the company's portfolio -- a highly unlikely possibility -- Coca-Cola should continue to grow, and easily be able to keep raising its dividend.

It has been revealed that the company's next distribution will occur on Dec. 15, for investors of record as of Dec. 1. It yields 2.2% at the most recent closing stock price.

Another Dividend King is AbbVie, although I have to note that this is due in part to the fact that the tech giant was spun off from fellow pharmaceutical -- and longtime dividend raiser -- Abbott Laboratories (ABT +0.31%).

Industry observers note that regardless, AbbVie is all too happy to inherit its throne and stay there. At the end of October, it confirmed a 5% dividend raise to a quarterly $1.82 per share.

AbbVie has generally been a popular stock this year, not least because it has a strong commercial portfolio anchored by the twin blockbuster drugs Skyrizi and Rinvoq. And one fading blockbuster also in the company's immunology lineup, Humira, still generated sales of $683 million in its third quarter.

Industry observers note that in its commercial portfolio and across numerous development programs, AbbVie is admirably diversified in important therapeutic areas (although immunology still accounts for over half of total sales). It currently boasts an enviable collection of current and potential revenue streams.

The only catch with AbbVie these days is that its popularity has made it expensive and dampened the dividend yield (2.4% these days). For those who don't mind paying a premium for a top firm in the sector, though, the rewards could be considerable.

In a recent development, the first payout of AbbVie's freshly raised dividend will occur on Feb. 16, 2027, for stockholders of record as of the preceding Jan. 15.

Recent findings suggest that clorox is on the cusp of Dividend King status, as its raise streak reached 49 years at the end of July. It most recently increased its quarterly payout by $0.01 per share to $1.25.

It has been revealed that clorox might be trying to save a little coin because of the expense of its latest acquisition -- Gojo, the firm best known for the Purell line of sanitizers. The deal, which was announced in January and closed in April, had a net price tag of more than $1.9 billion after expected tax benefits.

That's a significant swallow for Clorox, as its latest annual revenue figure was $6.7 billion, and in the quarter before the announcement it had only $227 million in cash.

Recent findings suggest that the absorption of Gojo was a major factor in Clorox's 33% year-over-year rise in net sales in the first quarter of fiscal 2027, and the 82% pop in adjusted earnings per share. A low basis for comparison with the weak year-ago quarter also helped.

According to recent reports, the Gojo deal, although it's sensible and complementary to Clorox's business, feels like a grow-by-acquisition move for a company focused on relatively mature product categories. So far this year, it's struggled with higher transportation costs and commodity prices, and those factors remain in effect. The Gojo addition notwithstanding, I don't foresee many potentially high-growth catalysts for this stock.

That said, Clorox's beaten-down price could be attractive to bargain-hunting income investors, as its current dividend yield is a lofty 4.8%.

Recent findings suggest that the tech giant's next dividend distribution will take place on Feb. 12, 2027, to investors of record as of Jan. 27, 2027.

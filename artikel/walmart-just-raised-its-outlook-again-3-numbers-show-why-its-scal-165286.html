Industry observers note that walmart's fiscal 2027 third-quarter (ended Oct. 31) financial results were very encouraging. It beat analyst estimates on the top and bottom lines. And following last quarter's renewed outlook, management once again raised sales and profit guidance. This is a positive trend as the critical holiday shopping period is upon us. The retail stock's scale is the highlight. Here are three numbers that show why this key competitive advantage continues to grow.

Recent findings suggest that the first number is 23%. That's how fast Walmart's U.S. e-commerce sales grew in the third quarter. By comparison, Walmart's U.S. comparable sales, which measure growth at stores and digital channels open at least a year, rose 2.9% (excluding fuel).

This might come as a surprise. After all, Amazon (AMZN +3.47%) wrote the online retail playbook, and it dominates online shopping.

But Walmart has something the e-commerce giant can't easily copy. Its 5,200 domestic stores sit within 10 miles of 90% of the U.S. population. Many of those sites were secured decades ago. Today, they double as delivery hubs.

Sources indicate that that creates density for the logistics network. More orders flowing through the same buildings and trucks means a lower cost per package. And Walmart can spread its heavy investments in automation over hundreds of billions of dollars in annual sales. That's hard to compete with.

Industry observers note that walmart U.S. advertising revenue jumped 37% in the third quarter. This is where scale starts paying a second dividend. And it reveals the business is leaning into the advantage that its data offers.

Recent findings suggest that walmart Connect, the organization's ads platform, sells brand placements across its website, app, and stores. The pitch is simple. Walmart knows what 150 million weekly U.S. shoppers actually buy, not just what they click on. Brands want to reach those intentional shoppers at the moment of purchase.

Sources indicate that there's a positive feedback loop here. Walmart's massive revenue base gives it negotiating leverage with suppliers. That leverage helps keep prices low, which drives customer traffic. And traffic is exactly what advertisers pay for.

Industry observers note that the economics are exceptional. The audience and infrastructure already exist, so each additional ad dollar costs little to bring in, leading to "over 70% margins," according to Walmart Connect senior vice president Ryan Mayward. Groceries, by contrast, are a low-margin business. Ad revenue has a much bigger positive impact on the bottom line.

Recent findings suggest that global membership fee revenue grew 17%. This includes Sam's Club membership fees and Walmart+, the company's subscription service that goes head-to-head with Amazon Prime and offers free delivery and other benefits.

Membership fees are recurring and high-margin. Customers pay up front, and Walmart's job is to make that fee feel like a bargain. Its lean supply chain helps to make that possible. Low operating costs allow the organization to bundle perks such as free delivery, streaming, and discounts without giving up its price advantage.

In a recent development, that changes shopping behavior. People will favor spending with a retailer they've already paid, and members spend four times more than non-members. More spending makes Walmart larger. A larger Walmart negotiates better prices. And these better prices attract more members.

This is every retailer's dream. A paid membership program can only work if the value proposition is strong enough. Walmart's everyday low prices lay the foundation.

Put these three numbers together, and the quarter's most important figure comes into focus. Reported operating income rose 5.5% (on a constant-currency basis), faster than revenue. Over the past three years, adjusted operating income climbed 24%, while sales were up 17%.

According to recent reports, walmart's success in e-commerce, advertising, and memberships is a tailwind. Shareholders should watch whether profits keep outgrowing revenue. This would indicate that the scale-based flywheel is still working.

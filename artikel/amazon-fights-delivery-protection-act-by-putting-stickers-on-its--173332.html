📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Amazon is placing a sticker on some of its delivery boxes to urge customers to oppose a New York City bill.

In a recent development, this weekend, some Amazon shoppers reported getting a delivery from Amazon with a sticker on it that reads: “NYC Council could add up to $5 per order. Act now to protect low prices.” The sticker reportedly includes a QR code that leads to a link for contacting NYC Councilmembers. The sticker encourages the package’s recipient to “Scan to tell your council member: no increases for Latest Yorkers.”.

Industry observers note that amazon is looking to stoke opposition to Introduction 0518-2026 (518), also known as the Delivery Protection Act. The proposed law would require Amazon and other companies with last-mile delivery facilities in the city, such as FedEx, to directly employ third-party contractors Amazon uses for making deliveries. Amazon previously sent mailers to NYC residents regarding a new headquarters (which was subsequently scrapped), but political messaging on packages is unprecedented for Amazon, which hasn’t used its delivery boxes to lobby for political causes before. Last week, Amazon made another uncharacteristic move by showing NYC shoppers a homepage banner ad—which I see when logging in to Amazon as of this writing—and an in-app pop-up that also urges opposition to the bill. The banner and pop-up include a link to a blog where Amazon indicates the law would force it to consider moving operations outside of NYC and lead to “slower and more expensive delivery for millions of customers in Newly introduced York City, while also threatening small businesses and their thousands of employees.” Mayor Zohran Mamdani and the Amazon division of the International Brotherhood of Teamsters, however, support the bill.

According to recent reports, by using its packages, website, and app to advocate against the bill, Amazon hopes to leverage its large customer base to push a political ideal. This raises concerns about whether it’s presenting the debate fairly, the appropriateness of using an e-commerce platform for political purposes, and whether we’ll see more political messaging while shopping with Amazon or other companies in the future.

As it stands, some shoppers are already put off by Amazon’s lobbying.

“Something about this just feels off to me,” a Reddit user who apparently received a package with the sticker wrote.

In a statement to CNBC last week, Amazon spokesperson Kelly Nantel said the organization is using the homepage and in-app messages to be “transparent with our customers” about what the proposed law “would cause them if it passes as written.”.

As of Friday, Amazon shoppers had sent over 60,000 emails to NYC Councilmembers opposing the bill, NBC New York reported.

The City Council’s Committee on Consumer and Worker Protection will decide whether to advance the bill to the entire council. The date for that vote hasn’t been set yet.

Similar to a recent iPhone feature, Google appears to be working on “Fast Switch” as a way to share one SIM and phone number on two Android phones at once, and it sounds pretty cool.

Through the SIM Manager app on Android, the folks over at Android Authority noticed strings that tell a pretty clear story.

A upcoming feature called “Android Fast Switch” would let you “use your phone number on up to two phones,” with the SIM automatically switching to “whichever phone you unlock.”.

“Android Fast Switch” sounds nearly identical to Handoff on iPhone, a feature that lets iPhone owners share one SIM between two iPhones and quickly switch devices on the fly.

Sources indicate that other strings explicitly say that you can “Use a SIM on two phones” and specify that both devices would need to be logged into the same Google account. One phone would be the “Primary” with the other as a “companion.”. (Related: My Journey to a serverless transformers pipeline on Goo...)

There are still plenty of unknown details here, including what data might sync between devices as well as how carriers will respond. On iPhone, carriers either charge extra for this feature ($5 at T-Mobile, $10 at Verizon), or don’t support it at all (AT&T). We also don’t know what devices may be supported – this could easily end up as a Pixel-only affair. (Related: Deploy Hugging Face models easily with Amazon SageMaker...)

This would make my decision to buy device like Clicks Communicator much easier.

As per usual with these early teardowns, though, the actual launch of a feature like this could be weeks, months, or even years away. Still, the idea itself is rather exciting.

Sources indicate that iPhone’s Handoff feature always sounded useful, but I personally always questioned why it would be useful on iPhone. With so little variety in hardware, the utility of switching between two iPhones seems somewhat dubious, outside of perhaps using a Mini or Air as a situational device alongside a Pro device.

On Android, the combinations are endless. It all just depends on what devices Google supports. What devices would you switch between?

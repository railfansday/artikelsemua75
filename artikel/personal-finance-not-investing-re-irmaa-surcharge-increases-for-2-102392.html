Has anyone here been able to do a calculation that produces the reported $141,000 MAGI threshold?It should be the $107,000 threshold for the tier in 2019 adjusted by the change in the average of the CPI-U values for the 12 month ending August 2026 from the 12 months ending August 2018, with the result of that adjustment rounded to the nearest multiple of $1,000.Those are the results using the non seasonally adjusted CPI-U. Other parts of the law about inflation adjustments using CPI-U explicitly state that the non seasonally adjusted values are used. The law about the adjustments to IRMAA describes the adjustment states: "the percentage (if any) by which the average of the Consumer Price Index for all urban consumers (United States city average) for the 12-month period ending with August of the preceding calendar year exceeds such average for the 12-month period ending with August 2006 (or, in the case of a calendar year beginning with 2020, August 2018)."Is there a document that explicitly states the seasonally adjusted values are used?I have been trying to do the same thing and getting the same result. What it tells me is that CMS didn't just use the rounded 11 values it had. They also didn't use the value that the Treasury dept used for its TIPS calculations which was 325.604 I believe.To get the IRMAA thresholds they published, they would have needed to use an Oct value of 328.635 to 329.648. Anything in that range gives the published IRMAA thresholds. However, I can't come up with any plausible reason for them using an Oct value that high. CMS have been very opaque about how they are treating that missing October value. Next year, hopefully we will have a real 12 month average to work with.Here is an idea, but it doesn't make any sense. The average of the 11 month values that I got was 329.6563 rounded to 4 decimals. That gives an unrounded value of the threshold of $141,500.42 That would round to the $142,000. If the CPI-U was rounded to 3 decimals it would be $141,500.29 which would again round up to $142,000. Even if they rounded to two decimal places it ends even higher. However, if you truncate the value to two decimal places, the calculated value becomes $141,497.71 which rounds down to $141,000 while still keeping the other tiers $112,000, $177,000 and $212,000. Could some fumble fingered CMS employee accidentally typed 329.65 instead of 329.656? Even if they typed "329.655" instead of "329.656" that would be enough to change that one tier and not touch the others. Just think, a 1/1000 data entry error could have swung the MFJ IRMAA MAGI threshold by $2,000. Anyway, we may be the only two folks around here that care about these more arcane issues.

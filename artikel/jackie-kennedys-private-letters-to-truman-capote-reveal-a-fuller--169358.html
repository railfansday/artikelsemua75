It has been revealed that first Lady Jacqueline Kennedy’s sister, Lee Radziwill, was one of celebrated author Truman Capote’s “Swans,” developing a close, if complicated, friendship with the novelist. But Jackie had her own relationship with Capote, one that is less widely known. The pair corresponded with each other in the 1960s and Kennedy may have even gotten an early look at Capote’s 1966 novel, In Cold Blood.

Industry observers note that “I have your precious book and am so overjoyed,” she wrote to Capote in an undated card, presumed to be from June 1963. “The minute Jack takes off for Europe I will retreat to the country + indulge myself by reading every page—not just the latest ones.”. That letter, along with five others between Kennedy and Capote, will soon hit the auction block, ELLE Decor can reveal. The notes, three of which are written on White House letterhead, will be sold together in a single lot through Cornell Auctions, estimated to fetch between $40,000 and $80,000. Together, they reveal fresh details about the friendship between the former first lady and one of America’s great authors. (Related: Breaking: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Faster ...)

It has been revealed that available for the first time ever, these private letters are part of a larger auction of Capote-linked items accumulated over decades by his biographer, Gerald Clarke, author of 1988’s Capote. The sale, “The Magpie and the Swans: The Possessions of Truman Capote,” will take place on November 21.

Kennedy’s notes are all believed to have been written in 1963 and 1964, with four bearing postmark dates. During that period, Kennedy suffered personal tragedies, losing her infant son Patrick days after he was born as well as her husband, President John F. Kennedy, who was assassinated in November 1963.

Recent findings suggest that the letters indicate a warm relationship between Kennedy and Capote. In several of the messages, the first lady makes reference to various presents and mementos she received from the writer.

Industry observers note that one particularly meaningful gift came in the wake of President John F. Kennedy’s death, after Jackie and Capote saw each other at a gathering hosted by decorator Billy Baldwin. “The most beautiful porcelain rose that I ever saw or dreamed of—came to me the other day from you,” the former first lady wrote in a three-page December 1964 missive, handwritten on mourning stationery bearing a crest.

In another letter, Kennedy makes reference to a gift she sent to Capote, inspired by a visit to the novelist’s home. “In your Japanese-Victorian room I spotted two large apothecary jars of the same kind—I found this one in 1955 in Paris—so it has been with me for many years in many places,” she wrote in an undated letter, believed to be from December 1964.

Handwritten on black-bordered stationery, that note was addressed to Capote’s house at 70 Willow Street in Brooklyn Heights, where he wrote Breakfast at Tiffany’s and In Cold Blood.

Beyond the Kennedy notes, also up for auction is Capote’s four-piece formal dinner invitation from President Lyndon B. Johnson in 1966, which has an estimate of $600 to $2,000. Other highlights include a German pencil sharpener in a leather pouch; a chalkware bulldog; a monogrammed duffel bag; and jewel-like paperweights made by the author himself.

Taken together, these pieces paint a fuller picture of Capote’s life and work as a writer, as well as Kennedy’s enthusiastic support of his talents. “I keep thinking what power a great writer has. All the things you write move people,” she wrote. “I am glad you became a writer.”.

“The Magpie and the Swans: The Possessions of Truman Capote” will take place November 21 starting at 11 a.m.; bidding can be done online via LiveAuctioneers or in person at Cornell Auctions in Bellport, New York.

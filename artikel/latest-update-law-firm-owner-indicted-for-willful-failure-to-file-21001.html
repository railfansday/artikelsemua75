An indictment was unsealed today in the District of Colorado charging a Colorado Springs, Colorado, man with willfully failing to file individual and corporate tax returns for the years 2020 to 2025.

It has been revealed that the following is according to the indictment: Terry Fjeld was the owner and 100% shareholder of Terry Fjeld PC, a Colorado law firm organized as an S corporation that primarily litigated Social Security Administration benefits claims for clients. Since at least 2015, Fjeld and Terry Fjeld PC allegedly received IRS forms that reported fees the law firm received for legal services. (Related: Is Social Security Taxable? How to Calculate What You O...)

The law firm also allegedly issued annual tax forms to Fjeld reporting the wages he earned from the law firm. According to the indictment, from approximately 2018 through 2025, the IRS issued more than 20 separate notices to Fjeld informing him of his obligation to file individual tax returns, and multiple tax preparers also informed Fjeld that he had a legal obligation to file federal income tax returns and pay taxes to the United States. One tax preparer allegedly even prepared draft returns for certain years and provided those returns to Fjeld. But according to the indictment, Fjeld has nevertheless willfully failed to file individual or corporate tax returns since approximately 2010. Fjeld faces a maximum penalty of one year in prison for each count of willful failure to file a tax return.

Recent findings suggest that assistant Attorney General Colin M. McDonald of the Justice Department’s National Fraud Enforcement Division and U.S. Attorney Peter McNeilly for the District of Colorado made the announcement.

IRS Criminal Investigation is investigating the case.

Industry observers note that assistant Deputy Chief Eric B. Powers and Trial Attorney David Scollan of the Fraud Division’s Tax Section are prosecuting the case.

On April 7, the Department of Justice revealed the creation of the Fraud Division. The Fraud Division is laser-focused on investigating and prosecuting those who commit fraud against the American people. The Department’s work to combat fraud supports President Trump’s Task Force to Eliminate Fraud, a whole-of-government effort chaired by Vice President J.D. Vance to eliminate fraud, waste, and abuse within Federal benefit programs.

Sources indicate that an indictment is merely an allegation. All defendants are presumed innocent until proven guilty beyond a reasonable doubt in a court of law.

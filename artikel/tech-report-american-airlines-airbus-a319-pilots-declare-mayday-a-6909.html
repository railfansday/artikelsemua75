Industry observers note that an American Airlines flight, an Airbus A319 departing from Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport (PHX), declared Mayday on Thursday shortly after takeoff. It happened after the crew on the trailing flight, operated by SkyWest Airlines, warned air traffic control (ATC) when they reportedly saw flames from one of the aircraft's engines. AA3230 was a scheduled flight departing from PHX to Portland International Airport (PDX). The flight promptly returned to Phoenix as the incident was unfolding.

The pilots onboard the American flight bound for PDX had initially confirmed to ATC that the aircraft experienced hydraulic problems shortly after departure, and as a result, the flight would need to return to Phoenix.

Moments later, according to Flightdrama, the crew came across the radio declaring 'Mayday, Mayday, Mayday' and requesting vectors to return to Phoenix (a specific compass heading which is assigned by local ATC that assists in guiding a pilot's flight path using surveillance data like radar). American confirmed the incident in a statement to Simple Flying late Thursday.

Recent findings suggest that "American Airlines flight 3230 returned to Phoenix (PHX) due to a mechanical glitch. The aircraft landed safely, taxiied to the gate under its own power and was taken out of service to be inspected by our maintenance team. Customers deplaned normally and will re-depart this evening on a new aircraft. Safety is our top priority and we thank our team members for their professionalism and apologize to our customers for the disruption in their travel plans.".

In a recent development, as the crew declared Mayday, ATC asked a series of questions, including whether the plane needed to burn off excess fuel. The pilots confirmed that 120 people were onboard. According to flight data from Simple Flying's Flight Tracker, AA3230 was expected to depart at 3:58 PM. The flight was briefly delayed and eventually pushed back from the gate in Phoenix to 4:14 PM, 16 minutes behind schedule. Taking off from the Arizona airport heading west, the jet spent a total of 20 minutes in the air before touching down safely in Phoenix at 4:34 PM.

Upon arrival in Phoenix, the plane landed safely and taxied to the gate without assistance. The plane has since been removed from service, and passengers deplaned normally and were rebooked onto a replacement aircraft later that evening, which departed at 5:45 PM and arrived in Portland at 7:54 PM local time.

In a recent development, the jet at the center of the incident was N703UW. According to ch-aviation, the 28-year-old A319-112 carries serial number 904 and was initially delivered to US Airways in 1998, then transferred to the American fleet following the airline's 2013 merger. The aircraft is configured with up to 132 seats in a two-class layout (including Domestic First and Main Cabin).

According to recent reports, following the recent incident on the flight departing Phoenix, the aircraft remains grounded while further inspections are undertaken by the carrier's maintenance teams. The quick actions by the crew onboard, and the quick spotting by the following SkyWest service, helped relay vital information to air traffic control about the unfolding situation in Phoenix.

The crew onboard AA3230 took rapid action, with clear coordination, to establish critical emergency details early, allowing ground and air teams to coordinate without delay and ensure the aircraft's prompt return to the ground and the safety of all occupants onboard.

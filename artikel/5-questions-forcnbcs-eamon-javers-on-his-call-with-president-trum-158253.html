Eamon Javers, CNBC’s senior Washington correspondent, was the one who initially talked to the president, telling TVNewser that Trump called him as he was headed to CNBC’s DC bureau.

In a follow-up to our recent 5 Questions For… series, which covered Huang’s unexpected departure, Javers details how he learned of the reinstatement, his conversation with the president, and the coordination involved with the control room.

How did you hear about the news of Jensen Huang’s return?

I was walking my daughter to her bus stop this morning, and my phone blew up with texts from our bureau. It’s maybe the most important business story of the year, and I was a little frustrated that I was out of position when it broke. So I began texting sources for comment.

TVNewser: How did the call with President Trump occur?

Recent findings suggest that javers: He called me as I was in my car on the way to our CNBC bureau. I wasn’t expecting it at all. But we have talked on the phone from time to time, and I had tried to reach him earlier in the morning for his comment. So, I pulled over on the side of the road, called my desk, and was able to patch into the control room for a live interview. The whole process took about 4 minutes, and then we went live for nearly an hour. Our producers, control room team, and anchors did an incredible job of rolling with it and reacting very, very rapidly so we could make it happen.

TVN: What was it like to talk to the president in real time during such a unique moment?

It has been revealed that javers: It’s always an honor to talk to the president of the United States, and today it was a unique opportunity because there was so much that was unknown about the role he played behind the scenes. I was focused on all of the aspects that were still unknown, and trying to get the president to explain the who, what, when, and why of how this happened.

TVN: How were you coordinating the conversation with the newsroom and control room?

Javers: We didn’t coordinate it or prep in advance at all. It happened too swiftly. And your audience will understand the technical aspect of this, which is that on a phoner, you can’t communicate with the control room during the interview. On TV, they can speak to you in your earpiece to communicate how long you have left, if the anchors want to get in a question, and all of the production stuff you need in real time. Here it was just — do it live.

TV: What are the business ramifications of such a re-appointment?

Javers: We’ve really never seen anything this extraordinary in corporate America — at least in my lifetime. I think it’s too early to say how this will play out, and certainly too early to say whether it will be successful or not. One thing is for sure: This story isn’t over yet.

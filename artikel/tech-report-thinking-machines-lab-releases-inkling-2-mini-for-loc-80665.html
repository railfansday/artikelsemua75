According to recent reports, thinking Machines Lab released Inkling 2 Mini on November 20, 2026, an open-weights model built to run on consumer laptops. It has 26.4 billion total parameters and a memory footprint of about 20GB, using repeated passes through its middle layers to improve reasoning.

The full weights of Inkling 2 Mini and a companion research model, TML-d24-varloop-research, are available on Hugging Face. Tinker offers inference and fine-tuning, with chat in Tinker Playground, while local options include Ollama on Mac systems with at least 32GB of RAM and GGUF versions through Unsloth and llama.cpp. (Related: Tech Report: How to train a fresh language model from s...)

A company demonstration demonstrates Inkling 2 Mini running offline on a MacBook Pro in airplane mode during a flight, where it built a playable flight simulator in a few minutes. Another demonstration uses one offline MacBook to power all 20 residents of Thinkyville, a simulated pixel town, illustrating how multiple persistent agents can run locally. Inkling 2 Mini is a Mixture-of-Experts transformer, meaning only a subset of its specialized components is used for each token. Its 24 layers are divided into four blocks of six. The first and last blocks run once, while the two middle blocks share weights and run three times, producing 48 layer applications per token from 24 stored layers. The model has 3.4 billion active parameters and 5.4 billion weight activations per token once looping is included.

In a recent development, attention residuals let each layer learn its own mixture of earlier block outputs instead of adding every output into a single running sum. The second and third passes also receive a gated message from the preceding pass. That gate initially blocks the message and learns to admit more of it during training, allowing looping to be introduced without destabilizing the model.

In the company’s training comparison, an unpruned non-looping model cost 7.5% less compute to train but required 140% more inference compute to match the looped version’s loss on 512,000-token agentic traces.

In a recent development, training began with 384 experts and eight selected per token. Two-thirds were pruned during midtraining, leaving the 128 most-used experts in each layer, after which the model trained on 1 trillion tokens distilled from a larger internal Inkling model to recover its earlier performance. Twenty layers were trained and are deployed in 4-bit NVFP4, with the final four in 8-bit MXFP8; the firm indicates matching training and deployment precision avoids conversion loss from post-training quantization.

It has been revealed that thinking Machines Lab reports scores of 48.2% on DeepSWE v1.1 and 77.9% on the 51-task SWE-bench Pro v2 Hard evaluation, compared with 42.2% and 73.8% for Qwen3.8-27B, respectively, alongside 46.5% on Vals Index v2.2. Its comparison notes that evaluation setups vary by model.

The company says looping improves reasoning without enabling the weights to store more facts; knowledge is mostly a function of total parameter count. Inkling 2 Mini’s AA-Omniscience result is in line with other models of its size, while its leads among similar-sized open-weights models on BrowseComp, DeepSearchQA and MCP Atlas come from efficient use of search and tools.

Sources indicate that the tech giant also indicates Inkling 2 Mini is not the strongest small model for multi-turn chat or instructions with many constraints, reporting 50.0% on MultiChallenge and 33.4% on ComplexConstraints v1.1. It trails larger models on reasoning-intensive benchmarks including CritPt v2 and HLE-Diamond.

In a recent development, looping complicates chain-of-thought monitoring, the practice of reading a model’s written reasoning to oversee its behavior. A conventional transformer must externalize reasoning that needs additional sequential steps; a looped model can perform more of those steps internally before producing text. Reasoning that never enters the text cannot be inspected by that kind of monitor.

Because Inkling 2 Mini supports only a fixed number of passes, the lab trained TML-d24-varloop-research with one to five passes, mostly three, and conducted reinforcement learning at three. On a Think Fast subset, the organization reports that its no-chain-of-thought time horizon, defined as the human completion time of tasks it can finish at 50% reliability without written reasoning, rose from 16 seconds at one pass to 37 seconds at three before roughly flattening. N-Hop Lookup accuracy continued improving through ten loops even though training had not exceeded five passes.

It has been revealed that in the firm’s initial analysis of GPQA Diamond questions answered correctly at every tested depth, average reasoning traces fell from 3,703 tokens at one pass to 1,661 at five. Mean sentence length changed only from 8.9 to 8.7 words, and the share of words found in an English dictionary moved from 94.6% to 94.4%; the shorter traces were more direct and involved less backtracking.

Recent findings suggest that the agentic tests used a helpful-only fine-tune of Inkling 2 Mini and the variable-loop model in LinuxArena. With Qwen3.6-35B-A3B as a weaker monitor, the organization found no clear trend in stealth rate as loop count increased; restricting the monitor to tool calls and outputs left a significant number of attacks undetected.

These monitorability results are preliminary. Reinforcement learning only at three passes means the experiments do not establish what a model trained at higher pass counts would learn, and the company cautions that such a model might compress its reasoning more aggressively.

Recent findings suggest that for oversight researchers, the open question is whether the legibility observed at this scale survives larger models and training regimes that optimize deeper loops. The fixed and variable open-weights releases provide a direct way to investigate that question across looping depths.

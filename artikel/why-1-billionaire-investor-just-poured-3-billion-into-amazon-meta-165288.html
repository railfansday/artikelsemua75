Recent findings suggest that billionaire Stephen Mandel's Lone Pine Capital fund was busy loading up on shares of Amazon (AMZN +3.47%), Meta Platforms (META +6.55%), and Alphabet (GOOGL +6.34%) (GOOG +5.88%) in the third quarter, investing an estimated nearly $3 billion in the trio of hyperscalers. All three are now top-10 positions. (Related: Breaking: Distributed Training: Train BART/T5 for Summa...)

Let's look at what may have attracted Lone Pine to these AI stocks.

Amazon was Lone Pine's biggest purchase in Q3, with the fund pouring an estimated $1.4 billion into the e-commerce and cloud computing leader to establish a new position and make it its largest holding at over 9% of its portfolio. It's easy to see what Lone Pine may like about Amazon, with both its core businesses firing on all cylinders.

Amazon's cloud computing unit, AWS (Amazon Web Services), is its main driver, with revenue accelerating as the company aggressively builds out its AI infrastructure. The company is getting strong returns on its investments, locking in five-year or longer contracts and recouping its investment within two to three years. As it grows its AI accelerator business, I think its payback period could shrink, and demand for its chips is so high that it intends to begin selling them externally late next year. Overall, Amazon sees the potential for AWS to grow into a $1 trillion revenue business.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/23/26.

Recent findings suggest that meanwhile, often overlooked is how strongly Amazon's e-commerce business has been performing. The company is the world’s leading manufacturer and operator of mobile robotics. Combined with its investments in AI, this is driving incredible operating efficiency and surging profitability in this business.

Lone Pine was also aggressively buying Meta shares in the quarter, with the newly introduced position becoming the fund's third-largest holding.

Industry observers note that meta has demonstrated that it has one of the best AI flywheel businesses, with investments in algorithm improvements that lead to users being fed more relevant content and ads. This, in turn, has been boosting ad loads and increasing conversions and ad prices, driving strong revenue growth.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/23/26.

Recent findings suggest that meanwhile, Meta is not sitting still. The introduction of its AI Muse agents puts it in a strong position to take a leadership role in the emerging consumer AI agent market. While Meta's AI Muse agents can perform many tasks for consumers, Meta benefits the most when they facilitate transactions on users' behalf, as it will receive either affiliate or transaction fees. This will give Meta a whole new non-advertising revenue stream and position the company as a major player in e-commerce. Just the ability to monetize its Facebook Marketplace with these agents should be a nice growth driver.

According to recent reports, lone Pine significantly increased its Alphabet position in Q3, making Alphabet its seventh-largest holding. As the firm with the most complete AI stack, Alphabet is a popular holding among many large hedge funds.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/23/26.

It has been revealed that alphabet's biggest edge is the ecosystem it builds around its Tensor Processing Units (TPUs), making its computing platform a strong alternative to Nvidia (NVDA +31.48%). This has given it a large cost advantage in developing and running inference on its own AI models, as well as in cloud computing, where it can get a payback within a year when using its own chips. Semianalysis recently reported that TPUs have shown a price-to-performance edge over Nvidia's GPUs. This comes at a great time as Alphabet also begins selling these chips externally to customers.

In a recent development, alphabet's core search business, meanwhile, continues to see growth driven by increased usage of its AI-powered tools, including AI Overviews and AI Mode. While search is still in a bit of a transition with AI, it should benefit as Alphabet creates latest ad formats. Meanwhile, its ownership of Chrome and Android, and a revenue-sharing deal with Apple (AAPL +1.90%) , still give it a huge distribution advantage.

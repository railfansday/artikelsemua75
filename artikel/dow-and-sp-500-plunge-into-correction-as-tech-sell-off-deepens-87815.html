The major stock indexes extended their losses on Friday, as the Dow Jones Industrial Average and S&P 500 briefly plunged into correction territory, defined as a 10% decline from their last peak.

By the closing bell, the S&P 500 had fallen 265 points, or 3.70%, while the Dow Jones dropped 949 points, or 1.93%. The tech-heavy Nasdaq composite tumbled 5.28%, wiping out its gains for the year. (Related: “Determined” woman completes 7 marathons in a year whil...)

The sell-off, driven by a wipeout in tech and AI stocks, comes a day after Anthropic raised a staggering $81.34 billion during its IPO on Thursday, lifting the firm's valuation to $1.414 trillion.

Anthropic shares were down 12% on Friday, giving back most of their gains after jumping 17% in their debut session. (Related: Chipotle is giving customers free burritos with Thanksg...)

Sevens Report Research's Tom Essaye said Thursday's sell-off primarily hit the tech sector, but added that "weakness is extending this morning and, like yesterday, pulling the broad market lower (although there was no latest negative news overnight in the tech sector).".

Recent findings suggest that concerns over inflation, a potential U.S. government shutdown in December, and continued conflict in the Middle East have kept some investors on edge. That, coupled with a recent spate of tech outages—including the FAA outage last Friday and the Microsoft outage last Thursday—have created a sense of unease among tech investors.

AI investors were already feeling wary—as the burgeoning AI industry matures, it has yet to reach profitability. Anthropic reported $33.24 billion in revenue in the first three quarters of this year, but a $104 billion net loss for the same period.

One sector that is seemingly bucking the downward trend is real estate, which is performing better than the overall S&P 500. The S&P 500 Real Estate Sector is up 6.95% since the beginning of the year, while the overall S&P is up 3.15%.

Housing stocks—especially those of major homebuilders—have shown relative strength this month due to continued institutional investment from firms such as Berkshire Hathaway.

Despite headwinds for homebuilders, there are reasons for optimism following the midterm elections and the passage of the 21st Century Road to Housing Act, which passed with bipartisan support in July.

Nobody had to find a clever bug here. On 17 November 2026, someone logged in to Rackspace Spot with credentials that had leaked, and for most of that day they could do what real customers do. That is the part that bothers me. According to Rackspace's notice to customers about the Rackspace Spot breach, as reported by BleepingComputer, the access ran from about 00:20 to 22:00 UTC. Call it nearly 22 hours. (Related: Nebraska voters appear to reject online sports betting ...)

If your website, store or app runs on someone else's cloud, this is worth ten minutes of your week.

In a recent development, rackspace Spot is a managed Kubernetes cloud. You rent servers through an auction, and Rackspace runs the clusters for you. Your apps run there, and so do the passwords and keys those apps need.

Rackspace notes it revoked the affected tokens and sessions, removed the unauthorized pods, rotated keys and tightened access controls and monitoring.

Recent findings suggest that then the outage. The Spot console was down for days during the cleanup. On Rackspace's own GitHub discussion board for Spot, Sirish Raghuram, co-founder of Platform9, which built Spot with Rackspace, wrote on 20 November that "an observability system was breached using a compromised credential." He called it a critical error. A Spot team post on the r/rxt_spot subreddit noted service was fully restored on the morning of 21 November, Pacific time. Spot's status page now notes new sign-ups are paused until 25 November.

According to recent reports, two things are still unknown. How many customers were affected, and whether data was actually copied out. BleepingComputer asked Rackspace both questions and got no answer.

The notice covers Rackspace Spot customers whose organizations were hit. It is not a notice about every Rackspace service, and I have not seen anything saying other Rackspace products were touched.

Your site has secrets too: the database password, the payment gateway key, the email service key, the Git token your developer deploys with. They sit somewhere at your host. If someone with a stolen login can read that place, they hold your keys too.

Sources indicate that I wrote about how fast this goes in the Docker Hub leaked keys case, where a key was abused seconds after it went public. Same lesson here, slower clock.

If you are a Spot customer, Rackspace's list is clear. Reissue your Spot API tokens. Replace refresh tokens used in automation. Download a fresh kubeconfig. Rotate every credential stored in your cluster Secrets. Then go through the logs of your databases, container registries, Git repos, cloud storage and third-party services back to 17 November.

Rackspace also shared signs to look for: the IP address 23.224.79.106, the range 23.224.0.0/15, and pods or objects whose names start with i1, glm- or probe. A pod called probe can be harmless, so treat a match as a reason to look, not as proof.

Recent findings suggest that one detail people get wrong: changing the value inside the cluster is not enough. A database password or API key that was read stays valid until you revoke it at the service that issued it. Make the new key first, kill the old one, then update the Secret and restart what uses it.

Mauricio Pochettino, Andoni Iraola and a sliding-doors moment that changed everything

As Andoni Iraola’s Liverpool face Mauricio Pochettino’s Manchester United at Anfield, it wouldn’t have taken too much for a few roles to be reversed.

Sources indicate that according to numerous sources, Iraola was sounded out by United as recently as March. The club had not yet made any decision on Michael Carrick’s future, and were surveying options.

Sources indicate that iraola was – and remains – one of the most admired coaches in the game. In fact, he might even have been described as a young Pochettino.

Sources indicate that iraola’s intense pressing football is clearly ideologically aligned with the latest United manager’s, and initially made waves at an under-resourced but over-performing team in LaLiga. For Pochettino’s Espanyol, read Iraola’s Rayo Vallecano.

It has been revealed that while United eventually persisted with Carrick, it is felt Iraola had his own considerations about moving to Old Trafford and wanting to keep options open. Chelsea were interested, and other figures insist that a deal with Liverpool was in progress by April. The club denied at the time, but things moved markedly promptly once they dispensed with Arne Slot. (Related: Simple considerations for simple people building fancy ...)

If Liverpool’s form since has been understandably erratic, both the hierarchy and football leadership are very happy with how Iraola is settling in. They can see progress and sense a real “hunger”, which they find especially encouraging.

That may seem simplistic, or even somewhat nebulous, but there is something more substantial to it. Pochettino uses the word a lot, as can be seen with a basic search of his book ‘Brave Newly introduced World’. (Related: Hyperparameter Search with Transformers and Ray Tune...)

The substantial question as he starts the United job is about whether he still has that same hunger.

Some who know Pochettino believe he is still recovering from what ended up a sour experience at Paris Saint-Germain.

Sources indicate that he had been a genuine idealist at Tottenham Hotspur, especially as regards the football he felt was non-negotiable. Pochettino then arrived at PSG and had to compromise everything, since that was the only way to accommodate the three stars of Neymar, Kylian Mbappe and Lionel Messi. As one source notes, “he ended up hating picking the team every week”. Everything had to be fitted around three admittedly sensational talents who just didn’t share Pochettino’s vision.

Sources indicate that the role, consequently, just became a job to him… in a way he would never have liked. Pochettino generally wants his clubs to feel like callings.

Through that, it could be said he has almost mirrored United in making the wrong choices at crucial moments. If the Old Trafford hierarchy should actually have pushed for Pochettino in 2016 or 2018, he almost certainly should have left Spurs in 2018.

By the same token, Pochettino probably waited too long after leaving Spurs to pick PSG, and it was similar between the latter role and Chelsea.

Although his time at Stamford Bridge had totally different dynamics to PSG, it felt another case of just taking a job for the sake of a job.

And that’s why United feels so different.

Recent findings suggest that this isn’t just another pay cheque to stay in the game. It’s the one he has long dreamed of.

Does that actually mean everything now might have aligned so a manager and club who have consistently made questionable decisions now have both made a good one?

As one figure who has experience of revivals at clubs like Barcelona and Arsenal claims, it’s where the profile - and the “hunger” - is so important.

United, after all, are no longer just a case of a good manager dropping in to apply their approach, as might have been the case in 2018 or 2021.

It’s a full-on revival job, where a coach really has to get their hands underneath it.

A manager has to feel it. They have to have that… hunger.

This is precisely why a lot of data now reveals that coaches reach a peak by their mid-40s, with their best spells generally lasting around a decade. Their ideas tend to be fresh and they have that all-consuming obsession to really impose them.

Sources indicate that sir Alex Ferguson, appropriately, put it best back in 1988. “This isn’t just a job to me. It’s a mission. I am deadly serious about it. Some people would reckon too serious. We will get there, believe me. And when it happens, life will change for Liverpool and everyone else - dramatically.”.

According to recent reports, iraola, as mild-mannered as he seems, has some of that mentality now. He believes he can eventually instil his ideas into any group of players he’s given.

United would of course contend that they have tried the approach of up-and-coming coaches. Ruben Amorim and Erik ten Hag – even though the latter was 52 at the time – fit that profile, but that ensured they didn’t yet have the experience to carry the weight of what United are. The PSG experience does serve Pochettino well in that sense.

Just because a specific coach doesn’t work out, though, it doesn’t mean the idea is wrong.

The dilemma nevertheless displays how precise United need to be to get it right, given their long-term dysfunction.

You need hunger but you also need experience, and that is quite a small circle in a Venn diagram.

According to recent reports, is Pochettino now in it? The feeling is that United have sparked his idealism. This is more than a job to him, as Ferguson put it.

Even if he had to arrive late this week, for example, work was already being done.

Sources indicate that the manager’s son, Seb Pochettino, and long-time assistant Jesus Perez have been at the club since Monday, having pretty much started work the second it was looking likely. Perez has already been trying to shape things, taking training, with the videos initially sent back to the States.

The players were meanwhile impressed by the initial meetings with the manager, which isn't always a given. It of course helps that Pochettino is probably the coach with the best reputation on joining since Jose Mourinho.

Industry observers note that some longer-serving figures at United have rolled their eyes at the experience of “more change”, but realise it’s necessary. The talk has been of “musical chairs”.

According to recent reports, had the music stopped at a different time, Iraola might have been there, or the club might even have been revitalised through Pochettino already. Pochettino might have already joined United at many points in the past.

It has been revealed that but Pochettino knows there’s no point dwelling on any of that. He needs to seize the moment.

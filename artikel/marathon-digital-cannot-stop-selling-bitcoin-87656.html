Bitcoin mining giant MARA Holdings, formerly known as Marathon Digital, continues to offload its Bitcoin holdings, with another multimillion-dollar transfer spotted as the manufacturer's selling spree shows no signs of slowing down.

Recent findings suggest that according to blockchain analytics platform Lookonchain, MARA transferred another 366 BTC, worth approximately $23.33 million, from its wallets on Nov. 18.

The latest transaction follows a series of substantial Bitcoin sales by the mining company throughout 2026. (Related: Industry News: Supermicro sued by shareholders over cla...)

Lookonchain estimates that MARA has already sold a staggering 40,581 BTC this year, generating approximately $3.03 billion at an average selling price of $74,446 per coin. (Related: Score TP-Link Archer with full Wi-Fi 7 support and 10 G...)

However, it remains unclear whether the latest 366 BTC transfer represents another completed sale or an internal movement of funds.

MARA's aggressive selling strategy is confirmed by its latest quarterly financial disclosures.

According to its Nov. 9 SEC filing, the organization sold approximately 26,984 BTC for $1.9 billion during the first nine months of 2026.

Following the end of September, MARA liquidated another 13,597 BTC at an average price of approximately $81,039, raising an additional $1.1 billion.

These transactions bring its total disclosed Bitcoin sales to 40,581 BTC.

Notably, the tech giant used $750 million from its latest sales to fully repay Bitcoin-backed loans, releasing 17,477 BTC previously pledged as collateral.

Interestingly, MARA has explicitly signaled that it intends to continue selling Bitcoin as part of a broader shift in its corporate strategy.

However, it began allowing sales of newly mined Bitcoin in 2025 before expanding the policy in 2026 to include coins already held on its balance sheet.

In its latest shareholder letter, MARA noted it looks to continue monetizing Bitcoin to strengthen its financial flexibility.

The manufacturer specifically identified artificial intelligence infrastructure, upcoming convertible debt obligations and potential share repurchases as reasons for building up cash reserves.

The selling comes as MARA faces considerable pressure on its core mining business.

Third-quarter revenue declined 35% year over year to $164.2 million, compared to $252.4 million in the corresponding period of 2025.

Latest Update: SK Hynix will become Kioxia’s largest shareholder through a bond swap

SK hynix’s exhibition booth at the Supercomputing 2026 (SC26) conference.

Recent findings suggest that SK Hynix will become the largest shareholder in Kioxia, its Japanese rival in flash memory, by converting bonds it has held since 2018 into shares. It will hold an indirect stake of about 14.1%, it remarked in a filing on Friday.

Bloomberg’s Yoolim Lee first reported the filing. The move puts SK Hynix ahead of Toshiba, Kioxia’s former parent, which held 10.46% in early November, according to Korea JoongAng Daily. (Related: Breaking: Build A Racetrack codes (November 2026)...)

It has been revealed that SPC2 is a special-purpose company set up for the 2018 deal. SK Hynix will convert 129bn yen ($810m) of its bonds into 129,000 shares. It will also buy Bain Capital’s remaining 1,001 SPC2 shares for 100m yen. The steps close in December, leaving SK Hynix as SPC2’s sole owner.

Sources indicate that apart from the payment to Bain, the deal adds no new money, MoneyToday reported, citing the firm. SK Hynix called it a follow-up step after it sold out of a separate Kioxia fund in June. Gains from that sale funded about 7tn won ($5.2bn) of special dividends in the third quarter. Industry observers note that SK Hynix joined the Bain-led group that bought Kioxia, then Toshiba Memory, in 2018. It put in 395bn yen at the time.

According to recent reports, both companies make NAND, the chips used for storage in phones and AI servers. SK Hynix held 19% of the global NAND market in the second quarter and Kioxia 14%, according to Counterpoint figures cited by Bloomberg. Together that tops market leader Samsung’s 28%.

Sources indicate that the 2018 deal caps SK Hynix at 15% of Kioxia’s voting shares until 2028, unless Kioxia agrees otherwise. Kioxia lists the stake as a risk factor in its annual report, citing a possible conflict of interest, Bloomberg reported.

Industry observers note that both sides say nothing changes in how Kioxia is run. A Kioxia spokesperson noted its management will stay independent.

Industry observers note that “We at the moment have no prepares to further buy or sell shares in Kioxia. We will continue to respect Kioxia’s independent management going forward,” an SK Hynix official said.

Masahiro Wakasugi of Bloomberg Intelligence sees little change in the short run.

The move is “unlikely to materially change near-term NAND competition,” Wakasugi wrote.

In a recent development, the stake still gives SK Hynix some say over massive decisions, he added. That includes the power to oppose a possible deal between Kioxia and Sandisk.

Recent findings suggest that separately, SoftBank’s Masayoshi Son reportedly explored a buyout of SK Hynix itself. The Korean tech giant reported a 632% profit jump in October, even as memory stocks slid.

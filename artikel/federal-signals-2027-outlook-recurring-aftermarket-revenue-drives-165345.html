A municipality needs to clean its sewer lines, and a massive, specialized vacuum truck pulls up to do the job. That vehicle, likely branded as a Vactor or a Truvac, is just one small piece of the heavy-duty machinery keeping critical infrastructure functioning across North America. Federal Signal (FSS +5.81%) builds these essential machines, along with the sirens and lightbars that clear the way for emergency responders. The stock, which trades at $115.03 as of Nov. 23, 2026, has delivered a 6% return to shareholders over the past year.

In a recent development, federal Signal is the exact type of uber-niche industrial stock that I can't help but love. There isn't anything overly glamorous about the business (unless you're a big fan of street sweepers or vacuum trucks, at least); the stock simply outperforms over time. Over the last decade, FSS stock has generated annualized total returns of 22.3%, compared with the S&P 500's 13.9%.

In a recent development, leading the charge for Federal Signal's history of outperformance is its No. 1 market share across many niche vehicle platforms:.

It has been revealed that the manufacturer also believes it is the No. 3 or 4 player in refuse collection vehicles, the top dog in warning signals and industrial signaling equipment, and the No. 2 seller of public safety equipment (police car lights, for example).

Recent findings suggest that most importantly, it sells aftermarket parts for all of the vehicles and equipment mentioned. These aftermarket sales are a core part of Federal Signal's vision for the future and already account for 21% of total sales. They provide recurring, high-margin revenue that has helped the organization's net income margin double from 5.7% in 2016 to 11.7% today.

Best of all for investors, Federal Signal isn't an old-school, low-growth industrial firm. Successfully acquiring and integrating 18 businesses since 2016, FSS has grown sales at an 11.6% annualized clip over the last decade. With an impressive 15.5% return on invested capital, management seems to do a great job of targeting niche acquisitions that generate outsize returns over the long haul.

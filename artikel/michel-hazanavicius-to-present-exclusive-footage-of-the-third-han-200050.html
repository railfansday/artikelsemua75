Michel Hazanavicius will unveil exclusive footage of his upcoming film The Third Hand as part of an AI case study at the Les Arcs Film Festival in December.

The Oscar-winning director and post-production firm Digital District will present the footage as they explain their method and tools for using AI on the film. (Related: Director of Machine Learning Insights...)

The Third Hand stars François Civil as a French soldier who is wounded in battle in World War One, then wakes up in the laboratory of a mad scientist, who has grafted a German hand onto his abdomen. Civil is joined in cast by Bérénice Bejo (The Past), Rebecca Marder (The Stranger) and veteran actor Michel Fau (The Great Arch). Watch on Deadline.

Billed as a “fantastique”, silent film in color, the movie sees Hazanavicius return to the silent genre for a second time after his Oscar-winning black and white film The Artist.

The case study will be followed by a discussion with David Cormand, Member of the European Parliament, on how Europe can regulate AI while protecting creators’ rights and cultural sovereignty.

Hazanavicius is attending France’s Alpine Les Arcs Film Festival this year as the Ambassador of its Talent Village which will see him share lessons from his career with the eight emerging directors.

Recent findings suggest that he follows in the wake of ldikó Enyedi, Ruben Östlund, Juho Kuosmanen, Nadav Lapid. Isabelle Huppert and Thomas Vinterberg.

This year’s participants include Greta Díaz Moreau, (Spain), Roxanne Krimizi and Michalis Kimonas (Greece), Helmi Donner (Finland), Juri Krutii, (Estonia), Jakub Cyryl Grabarczyk (Poland), Hae-Sup Sin, (Switzerland, Korea), Nadja Josephson (Sweden) and Vida Skerk (UK, Croatia).

According to recent reports, hazanavicius won international acclaim with The Artist which won five Academy Awards including Best Picture in 2012, and remains to this day the only French production to have received that honour. His last feature The Most Precious of Cargoes saw him direct animation for the first time.

Recent findings suggest that the AI case study is part of the conference program of the Les Arcs’ Industry Village. Other panels will include “Beyond Counting: Ten Years of Gender Equality Policies in European Cinema” and a European Producers Club discussion on the upcoming revision of the EU framework for audiovisual media services regulation and implications for the audiovisual sector.

The Les Arcs Film Festival runs from December 12 to 19.

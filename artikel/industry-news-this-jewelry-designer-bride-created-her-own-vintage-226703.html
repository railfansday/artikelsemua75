Industry News: This Jewelry Designer Bride Created Her Own Vintage-Inspired Dress for Her Polish Countryside Wedding

When the Polish jewelry designer Natalia Kopiszka was planning her August 2026 wedding to actor Aleksander “Olek” Kaleta, she found herself at a bridal fashion crossroads, stuck between two stylistic pillars of her own brand: vintage and modern.

Industry observers note that first, the Warsaw-based designer of Kopi traveled to Paris to shop for a vintage wedding dress, but returned home empty-handed. “I didn’t find anything that I liked, so I thought: I will design my own wedding dress,” Natalia claims. (Related: Show, Don't Certify: How to Prepare for Your First Data...)

According to recent reports, for the body of her ceremony dress, she ordered fabric—silk taffeta—from Etsy. “I was on a mission to find the absolute dream silk fabric,” she indicates. “I started by checking out local fabric shops and silk wholesalers, but I just couldn't find the right silk taffeta with that delicate, subtle sheen anywhere.” She searched online and stumbled upon a listing from a Newly introduced York-based seller, which she ordered on the spot. Luckily, “it turned out to be even more breathtaking in person than in the photos,” she says. (Related: Industry News: Running an AI Agent on a Local LLM: What...)

According to recent reports, then, on a weekend trip to Amsterdam with a friend, she wandered into a vintage store where she found antique lace, which she used for the bodice detailing. “The lace is truly unique because it dates back to the early 20th century, and I only had a tiny piece of it—which happened to be just enough to trim the bodice,” Natalia says. “It was such a special way to check off the ‘something old’ tradition.” She sketched the dress, then shared her designs with a dressmaker who sewed it, making her vintage-modern dream a reality.

Natalia and Olek first met in February 2021 at a house party in Warsaw. “For me, it was love at first sight,” Natalia recalls. “A friend of mine who lived in the apartment below me threw a get-together, and her roommate happened to be friends with Olek. As soon as he walked in, we instantly locked eyes. I’m pretty sure he was the one who came over and started the conversation first,” she adds. “It also turned out that Olek lived directly across the street from my apartment, so we used to wave at each other from our windows. Ever since that night, we started seeing each other almost every single day.”.

After a year, they moved in together in Warsaw. In 2024, Olek proposed to Natalia during a trip to Italy, in the coastal town of Polignano a Mare. “It was a warm, romantic evening with a huge moon, and surprisingly, I didn't see it coming at all,” Natalia states. “It turned out to be such a sweet, funny moment because neither of us could stop laughing.”.

Recent findings suggest that on August 8, 2026, the couple held their wedding at Pałac Pstrokonie, a historic 19th-century palace in a small village outside of Warsaw. They worked with the wedding planner Kasia Fastyn, who brought Natalia’s vision—a romantic, whimsical wonderland of sorts—to life, pairing them with the event studio MyBigDay, who designed the florals and draped their dancing tent in pink fabric.

As a jewelry and accessories designer, Natalia zeroed in on all the details of her wedding looks from the beginning. She knew she didn’t want a traditional floral bouquet, for example, so she opted for a small silk bag in the shape of a bouquet from the Upcoming York-based designer Camille Albertine, after finding her work on Instagram. Albertine custom-made Natalia’s bouquet purse in the same fabric as her dress. “Everyone was so surprised that I didn’t have a typical bouquet,” Natalia notes. “It’s versatile enough that I can wear it with everyday outfits. It’s going to stay with me for a long time, and I’ll even be able to pass it down through generations.”.

It has been revealed that for her reception look, the bride changed into a dress by the Polish designer Magda Butrym, pairing it with an unexpected accessory: a crochet veil cap that her sister made. “It was a really simple dress, also silk, but I put something really crazy on my head,” Natalia says.

In a recent development, natalia incorporated her own jewelry into the crochet cap-like veil, which was bedazzled with charms and pendants. “They are specially made charms with little lobster clasps so you can clip them onto the crochet work however you like. They’re gold-plated and feature Swarovski crystals,” Natalia states. (Charms are a signature of Natalia’s brand, she explains, so it was only fitting to work them into her bridal looks.) Her love of historical pieces, particularly from the Victorian era, inspired the final design, which included blue stones for her “something blue.” A look inspired by it is now for sale as part of Kopi’s “I Do” bridal line.

The bride also made Olek a pocket square with the same vintage lace as her dress, so they were matching (and so he had “something old” as well). They completed their looks with wedding bands that Natalia designed. “Mine has a retro feel, crafted in gold and set with 12 small diamonds,” she notes of the rings. “Olek went for a classic look—a simple, wide gold band with my nickname engraved on the inside.” Reflecting on the wedding months later, Natalia says: “Everything was perfect.”.

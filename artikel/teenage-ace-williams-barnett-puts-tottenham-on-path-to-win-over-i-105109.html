Not since the emergence of a certain Harry Kane has there been this much excitement about a player from Tottenham’s academy. On his first senior start, Luca Williams-Barnett showed just why as the 18-year-old became their youngest Premier League goalscorer since Gareth Bale in 2007 before teeing up Dominic Solanke with a mesmerising and match-winning assist against Ipswich.

It has been revealed that that made it four league victories from their last five matches for Roberto De Zerbi’s side and the Italian could only have enjoyed the view from the stands as he served his one-match touchline ban. Tottenham have struggled to match their London rivals Arsenal and Chelsea’s record of producing homegrown players in recent years but they appear to have struck gold with Williams-Barnett. Harry Winks was the last academy graduate to score on his debut more than a decade ago – a feat that Kane also achieved two years earlier against Sunderland. Yet after Spurs decided to allow the England Under-21s striker Will Lankshear to join Middlesbrough in the summer, there was no chance that Williams-Barnett would be allowed to follow him out of the door. He became the 900th player ever to represent Spurs when he made his debut last year and the teenager left the field here to a standing ovation that he will never forget. “I told him to play like he plays in the park with his friends, with the same freedom,” noted De Zerbi. “And to play and to work hard without the ball, like [Ousmane] Dembélé, like Raphinha, like [Lamine] Yamal. Luca is a special player. I’m not surprised because his talent is bigger than you think. He’s very young but at same time he’s very mature. He is older than his age.”.

Asked whether his protege has the potential to be compared to the Barcelona sensation Lamine Yamal, De Zerbi added: “Yes, I think so. One of the best things in my career I’ve seen today on the bus to the stadium. He was watching [clips] on YouTube of Ronaldinho, Hazard. I think he’s a special guy, because if one hour before you are debuting in the Premier League, you are watching Ronaldinho, Hazard, Neymar, all these types of talent. Having the qualities is one thing but he has the passion for football, too.”.

It has been revealed that after a promising start to the campaign, Ipswich’s momentum has stalled – they have now gone five games without a victory. But Gary O’Neil, who has already seen 16-year-old Max Dowman score twice for Arsenal against his side in the Carabao Cup, acknowledged that they had been second best throughout. “You need things to be perfect when we come to a place like this and we didn’t make things easy for ourselves,” he said.

Sources indicate that de Zerbi dropped a huge hint that Williams-Barnett could be handed his opportunity to start after two impressive cameos against Crystal Palace and Leeds. “Maybe, maybe,” he stated on Friday. It turned out that the decision was actually taken last week, although the absence of Sandro Tonali due to illness forced Spurs into a reshuffle in midfield as Mateus Fernandes was handed the holding role.

It has been revealed that solanke has struggled to fill the void left by Kane since joining from Bournemouth in 2024 but looked in determined mood and he was inches wide with a swivel and shot from inside the area. A driving run from Williams-Barnett had the home supporters off their feet, with Solanke again going close from the resulting corner. It then needed a timely block from the teenager to stop Ipswich breaking downfield.

Recent findings suggest that his massive moment arrived less than 60 seconds later. Porro’s ball to the back post should have been dealt with by Abdoul Ouattara, only for a moment’s hesitation to allow Williams-Barnett to muscle in front of him and apply the finish with his knee. “He’s one of our own,” sang the Tottenham fans in delight.

Mohammed Kudus was unfortunate to see his superb effort from a free-kick cannon off the inside of the post and away to safety. Williams-Barnett will be disappointed not to have scored his second when he miskicked inside the six-yard box with the goal gaping. James Maddison was then denied by a brilliant save from Christian Walton and Williams-Barnett left the field at half-time looking very pleased with himself as he waved to his family in the crowd.

His smile grew even broader after Darnell Furlong was left bamboozled by a direct run down the left flank before the youngster delivered the ball on a plate for Solanke to score his first goal here since March. The defeat could have been worse for Ipswich had it not been for Walton, and O’Neil will know they are in danger of being sucked into trouble if this winless run continues. There are no such concerns for Tottenham these days. Thanks to Williams-Barnett, their future looks much brighter than a few weeks ago.

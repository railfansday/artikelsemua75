Nvidia stock staged its biggest single-day rally since 2003 on Monday, November 23, after the company’s board reinstated co-founder Jensen Huang as president and CEO. Shares surged 31.48% to close at $165.05, adding approximately $952 billion to Nvidia’s market capitalization. The announcement also triggered a broader recovery across technology stocks, lifting the Nasdaq Composite by 6.51%.

In a recent development, president Donald Trump’s intervention added another dimension to the development, turning Huang’s return into a major corporate and political story. Investors now face a different question: whether the leadership reversal can sustain Nvidia’s recovery after its recent market turmoil. Sources indicate that trump publicly welcomed Nvidia’s decision and claimed he had played a significant role in convincing the board to reinstate Huang. According to reports, he asked Vice President JD Vance to engage with the company over the weekend, while senior administration officials provided input to Nvidia’s directors.

It has been revealed that nvidia confirmed that its board voted unanimously to restore Huang after discussions and meetings with him. The tech giant noted it had received input from senior U.S. government officials and was confident that his reinstatement posed no national security risk.

In a recent development, trump described the board’s decision as the correct move, while taking public credit for the reversal. The intervention highlights how closely Nvidia’s leadership has become connected to Washington’s broader technology and national security priorities.

Huang’s departure on November 12 followed reports about his personal relationships, including an alleged relationship with a Chinese businesswoman suspected by U.S. officials of having intelligence links. The allegations triggered national security questions and intensified scrutiny around Nvidia’s leadership.

The board initially resisted bringing Huang back, leaving CFO Colette Kress to serve as interim CEO. However, after further discussions and government input, directors reversed their position.

It has been revealed that the controversy is not entirely resolved. Congressional investigations and questions surrounding potential violations of U.S. chip export restrictions remain ongoing. Huang is also expected to face congressional questioning in December.

Recent findings suggest that nvidia’s 31.48% rally helped drive a broad technology recovery. The Nasdaq Composite climbed 6.51%, while the S&P 500 gained 4.73% to close at 7,237.17. The Dow Jones Industrial Average advanced 3.01%.

Industry observers note that AI-linked companies and data-center stocks also recovered as investors returned to positions that had suffered during the previous week’s sell-off. In a recent development, however, the recovery also reflects how heavily the broader AI trade had been affected by uncertainty surrounding Nvidia. The company remains a central holding and supplier across the artificial intelligence ecosystem.

Nvidia stock closed Monday at $165.05, placing the $165 region at the center of its near-term price action. Following the sharp rally, investors will watch whether the stock can maintain its upward momentum rather than surrender its gains.

According to recent reports, A sustained move above $172 could strengthen the recovery and bring $179 into focus, followed by $190. On the downside, a break below $157–$159 could weaken the rebound and expose the $149–$152 region.

According to recent reports, jensen Huang’s return has removed a major leadership overhang, but Nvidia’s recovery now faces a test beyond Washington’s support. Investors will look for stability above $165 and a sustained break above $172 to confirm that buying momentum is holding.

Meanwhile, congressional scrutiny, export restrictions and questions over AI spending remain crucial risks. Nvidia has regained Wall Street’s attention with a historic rebound, but its ability to sustain the rally will ultimately depend on business performance, investor confidence and regulatory clarity.

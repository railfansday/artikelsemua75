Industry observers note that hershey operates as a dominant force among consumer discretionary stocks through its massive portfolio of chocolate, sweets, and mints. The manufacturer sells its products through wholesale distributors and mass merchandisers, with McLane Company accounting for approximately 27% of consolidated net sales in 2025. Customer concentration like this adds a layer of risk to the business since the loss of such a large partner could significantly impact overall sales.

According to recent reports, in the 12 months ended Sept. 27, 2026, revenue reached close to $12.3 billion, an increase of roughly 6.7% over the previous 12 months. Recent findings suggest that chipotle Mexican Grill faces significant risks related to food safety and potential food-borne illness incidents, which can severely damage its brand reputation and increase operating costs. The tech giant also deals with intense competition from traditional restaurants and newer entities like ghost kitchens or delivery services such as Starbucks (SBUX -1.28%). Furthermore, the business must navigate legal and regulatory challenges regarding labor practices.

Hershey is sensitive to the availability and pricing volatility of key raw materials such as cocoa, sugar, and dairy. Supply chain disruptions caused by climate change or natural disasters could negatively impact its manufacturing operations across its global facilities. The organization also faces pressure to successfully implement digital transformation and strategic business realignment programs to stay competitive against other snack manufacturers in a rapidly evolving retail environment.

Hershey appears cheaper than Chipotle Mexican Grill based on traditional metrics.

Recent findings suggest that chipotle is a story about growth and Hershey is about rebounding profitability, and knowing that can help find what the best fit for a portfolio is. For Chipotle, revenue increased 9.6% in the third quarter of 2026 to $3.3 billion. Transactions increased by 0.8%, comparable sales rose 2.1%, and digital sales jumped 38.8%. On top of that, Chipotle opened 84 fresh locations and expects to open up to 370 new restaurants by the end of 2026. That said, its operating margin dropped from 15.9% to 13.7%, and earnings per share dipped 6.9% despite the noticeable increase in revenue growth. Overall, while revenue growth and location expansion look impressive, shareholders will want to make sure higher costs aren't cutting too deeply into profitability.

For Hershey in its third quarter, while revenue increased only 3%, adjusted earnings per share (EPS) rose 103% and net income climbed 112%. Gross margin also increased from 32.6% to 46.1%. That remarked, organic sales increased only 0.8%, while international sales fell 7.5%. What Hershey is seeing appears to be improved results driven by better margins and lower costs, rather than pure consumer demand.

Recent findings suggest that between the two, if you prioritize more long-term sales growth potential, Chipotle may be the better fit for a portfolio. For anyone looking for a more mature business model with improving profitability, Hershey may be the better option.

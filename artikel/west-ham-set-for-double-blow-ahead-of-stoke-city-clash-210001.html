In a recent development, the London Stadium is a tough venue to fill for West Ham given its huge capacity, even when everything is going well. Therefore, there was always likely to be a reduced attendance against Stoke City on Saturday anyway, given the Hammers’ recent performances and results.

But it looks set to get considerably worse, with many of those using public transport to get to the stadium facing significant disruption. (Related: How to train a latest language model from scratch using...)

Sources indicate that A number of railway engineering works are scheduled for the weekend of 28-29 November, coinciding with West Ham’s 3pm Championship kick-off against the in-form Potters. Supporters travelling from Essex in particular will need to allow additional time for their journeys.

Essex and Shenfield: National Rail has confirmed disruption to Greater Anglia services, with replacement buses connecting passengers to the Central line at Newbury Park.

Lea Bridge: Engineering works mean Hertford East services normally terminating at Stratford will be diverted to Liverpool Street. There will also be no trains between Meridian Water and Stratford.

There will be no Elizabeth line services through central London due to additional engineering works, although Stratford station itself remains open.

Supporters can also consult West Ham’s official stadium travel guide, which includes information on accessible shuttle buses.

Industry observers note that given the growing frustration amongst West Ham supporters, I wouldn’t be at all surprised if the travel hassle caused by the trains is enough to dissuade the 50/50s from attending the London Stadium. As a result, I’d expect a lot of empty seats on Saturday. (Related: Industry News: Train a Sentence Embedding Model with 1B...)

For all those attending, please ensure you check travel before leaving.

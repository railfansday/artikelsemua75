By David Kretzmann – Updated Nov 23, 2026 at 9:59AM EST.

Industry observers note that nvidia (NVDA +31.21%) has reinstated Jensen Huang as president, CEO, and board member, effective immediately. Shares were up about 27% in early trading Monday. The board has settled who leads the manufacturer. It has not finished its probe into reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort, and it is adding national security checks in the meantime. Recent findings suggest that the board based its decision on its talks with Huang and on input from senior U.S. government officials. That input is why it says his return poses no national security risk. For a chipmaker whose top products fall under U.S. export rules, Washington's view carries more weight than any internal review could. (Related: Latest Update: Transformer-based Encoder-Decoder Models...)

The weak spot is timing. Huang is back before the independent investigation ends, so the board is betting its findings won't change the picture. The release also notes Nvidia is still cooperating with government investigations and inquiries, but it doesn't say what they cover.

The lasting change is oversight. A new committee of independent directors will watch trade compliance and national security risk. Nvidia is also searching for a chief compliance officer, a new role that will report to a board committee. That gives directors a direct line into compliance.

Sources indicate that watch whether Nvidia shares the investigation's findings when it ends. Then watch who fills the compliance job and what national security experience they bring.

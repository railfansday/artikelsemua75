The YieldMax GOOGL Option Income Strategy ETF (GOOY) is an actively managed exchange-traded fund designed to provide indirect exposure to Alphabet (GOOGL) through a synthetic covered call strategy or a synthetic covered call spread. Through the combination of simultaneously holding long and short exposure to Alphabet Inc. (GOOGL) shares, GOOY will gain long exposure to GOOGL while earning options premium on the short call options to earn investors weekly income. While the strategy can work exceptionally well for income investors during normal market operations, volatility risk can create some challenges in the strategy that must be considered before making a final investment decision.

GOOY was rolled out by YieldMax on July 27, 2023 on the NYSE Arca Exchange. The ETF has a gross expense ratio of 114 bps, a relative premium when compared to peer strategies. This is likely the result of YieldMax being the first mover in releasing this type of single-stock fund with competition offering similar ETFs at more competitive prices.

Despite the higher expense ratio, GOOY right now exhibits a higher asset base and relative liquidity with $172 million in net assets with an average of $4 million in share value changing hands on a daily basis. According to recent reports, the ETF was designed to provide investors with weekly income derived from options premium earned from the covered call strategy. GOOY paid out $5.01/share over the last twelve months for a yield of 56%. While the fund exhibits a high yield, investors must consider that the majority of the distribution is derived from return of capital [ROC]. Return of Capital is essentially a return of an investorâs initial investment in the form of a dividend. ROC will reduce the investorâs cost basis over time as a tax-deferred benefit. Once the cost basis reaches $0, additional ROC will be taxed as short-term capital gains, or ordinary income.

According to recent reports, these types of funds can be used by a variety of investors seeking equity-like exposure while realizing returns in the form of ROC. These funds can also be used for long-term estate planning resulting from the step-up in cost basis upon transfer of assets when held in a taxable account. This means that the heirs can sell out of the position at a cost basis that matches the market price of the ETF, significantly reducing their tax exposure.

According to recent reports, as a result of the high ROC, GOOY will exhibit significant NAV erosion over time. This can add some intricacies when comparing the price performance of GOOY to GOOGL shares. For a more comparable chart, investors should view performance on a total return basis.

GOOY was designed to provide indirect exposure to GOOGL shares through the use of synthetic covered call and a synthetic covered call spread strategies. These strategies are similar in nature with one core difference.

A synthetic covered call strategy functions similarly to a covered call strategy in which it will gain both long and short exposure to the underlying asset. The only difference is that the long exposure will be through the use of holding call options rather than the underlying asset. The short position will be the same in which the ETF will sell covered call options with a higher strike price relative to the long call options. This effectively creates both long and short exposure to the underlying asset.

A synthetic call spread takes the synthetic covered call strategy and adds a layer of protection in order to reduce the risk of loss on the strategy during a sharp upswing in the underlying shares. In this strategy, GOOY would purchase call options with a strike price above the covered calls, allowing the fund to purchase shares at the higher strike price to cover the delivery of shares for the covered calls.

Industry observers note that while this strategy will work during normal market operations, volatility may add significant risk to the performance of the options strategy. For example, if GOOGL shares were to undergo a sharp decline below the strike price of the long call options, the long call options will expire worthless, resulting in a loss to the ETF. The continuation of a bear market can result in the deterioration of NAV, significantly impacting the value of each share for investors in the fund.

It has been revealed that GOOY can be best utilized by income-oriented investors seeking exposure to GOOGL. Rather than building an equity portfolio of individual stocks, investors may consider GOOY and other options income strategies to earn returns in the form of distributions rather than share price appreciation. While the strategy isnât perfect as investors will pay fees for holding the ETFs, the trade-off is the simplification of portfolio management in which the investor can raise cash through distributions rather than regular selling and rebalancing.

Given that GOOY can limit the full upside potential with respect to GOOGL shares during periods of strong share price performance, the ETF may not be appropriate for long-term investors seeking capital growth.

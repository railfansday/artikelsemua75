Critical Role is by far the most popular Dungeons & Dragons actual-play show, with more than a decade of role-playing antics including one-shots, live indicates, and hundreds of hours of tabletop campaigns. Its adventures are so beloved that the manufacturer has even adapted them into the Prime video animated series The Legend of Vox Machina and The Mighty Nein.

Industry observers note that despite that popularity, one thing still makes it hard to get into the show: the length of its episodes. Critical Role episodes typically run three to four hours, but during the Convergence arc of its latest campaign, episodes stretched to around five hours. Episode 31, "Trick of the Light," ran for a staggering 6 hours and 18 minutes. (Related: Scientists find “field” of washing machines in ocean du...)

After that peak, Critical Role episodes settled back into a range of 3 to 4 hours. In the latest episode of the Fireside Chat ask-me-anything series, Critical Role co-founder and creative director Marisha Ray explained that shorter episodes are here to stay. (Related: Latest Update: F1 star Max Verstappen played 40 hours o...)

Asked via Critical Role’s Discord server about why episodes have recently gotten shorter, Ray responded,.

According to recent reports, episodes of Critical Role’s Campaign 4 typically air on Thursdays at 7 p.m. Pacific Time, which is 10 p.m. Eastern Time and 3 a.m. for folks like me in the U.K. Staying up for three or four hours is a huge commitment, and even devoted Critters might need to go to bed before finishing.

Ray explained that Critical Role’s cast and crew also wanted shorter episodes.

Industry observers note that “There are several reasons: there are laws, our crew wants to go home, we want to make sure that they’re not starving by the end of the night, they want to go home and see their children,” Ray remarked. “With production, if you go past certain times and you go into what are called meal penalties, or you start going into overtime, and don’t get me wrong, we're super happy to pay it all, and pay it all the time here in Critical Role. But also, we don’t want to be toxic about that. We want to be mindful of that.”.

It has been revealed that ray said the cast and crew typically shoot Critical Role episodes at night, often after a long day of voice acting or administrative work. The cast also stays behind for 10- to 20-minute Cooldown episodes. That's a lot of work.

We can all relate to the cast’s despair when an episode ends, and we’re left wanting more. But what matters more is giving the cast and crew of Critical Role the time and space they need to rest instead of pushing themselves to exhaustion.

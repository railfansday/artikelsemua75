Recent findings suggest that space may be infinite, but truly incredible space operas are anything but. For there are thousands of galaxies and parallel worlds in the sci-fi genre, but only a few are worth getting lost in. There lies the magic of one of Apple TV's greatest sci-fi shows. It immerses viewers in a space opera so grand and scintillating that it is almost impossible not to be in complete awe.

Recent findings suggest that loosely based on Isaac Asimov's book series of the same name, Apple TV's Foundation is not another run-of-the-mill, bottom-tier sci-fi show. Its sheer brilliance lies in its ability to significantly drift away from its source material. And, yet, somehow retain the essence of Asimov's scale and themes. Unlike most epic sci-fi demonstrates, Foundation initially stumbled and struggled to find its feet. (Related: LA Noire 2 leak resurfaces after source gets GTA 6 endi...)

Foundation season 1 earned a mixed reception from viewers and critics, with many calling out its extreme creative liberties. However, just when it looked like the Apple TV series would turn into another forgotten sci-fi adaptation, it leveled up like a caterpillar discovering its wings. Although still a little divisive among book readers, Foundation is undoubtedly an S-tier sci-fi series. (Related: Redditor forced to bring desktop PC in checked luggage ...)

Instead of delivering a direct page-to-page translation of Asimov's books, Apple TV's Foundation uses the original sprawling universe as, quite literally, its "foundation." Since "the beginning of wisdom is the definition of terms," Foundation arms itself with the Socratic method and borrows some of the core concepts from the source material, including psychohistory.

In a recent development, then, it ingeniously takes a detour and adds a lot of original lore to the mix. One of the biggest challenges with adapting Isaac Asimov's stories was that they span across centuries. The Apple TV series does not completely abandon the original long-term storytelling. However, it also refuses to change its main characters every few chapters like the books.

Industry observers note that the show seems to understand that, in audiovisual storytelling, viewers need long-term rootable characters. A narrative cannot merely survive on abstract ideas, mathematical predictions, history, and constantly shifting perspectives. Owing to this, Apple TV's Foundation introduced everything from cloning to digital consciousness to be able to have more long-running character arcs.

It must be noted, though, that the Apple TV sci-fi show is not the best adaptation. In 3 seasons, so far, it has drifted far enough from the books to be seen as its own thing. While there are still some overlaps between Asimov's novels and the TV series, the show is better off being treated as an original and S-tier addition to sci-fi in its own right.

Sources indicate that one of Foundation's biggest strengths is its epic visual flair. Like most sci-fi shows on Apple TV, Foundation does not look like it is bogged down by limited small-screen budgets. Even some of the most abstract ideas from the original books are captured with such brilliant tangibility in the series that it is hard not to forgive it for its creative liberties.

From the colossal Star Bridge collapsing onto Trantor to the brutalist geometry of "The Vault" on Terminus, every frame is stunning with immense attention to detail and realism.

When these visuals are paired with Bear McCreary’s compositions, Apple TV's Foundation almost feels as grand and mythic as movies like Dune and Interstellar. It can still be debated whether the sci-fi space opera should have taken such a significant leap with its creative liberties. After all, an "adaptation" is only as good as its ability to "adapt.".

But perhaps that is exactly where the real genius of the Apple TV sci-fi series lies. It does not merely change its source material to dumb down its story for the screen. Despite the changes, it is still an intelligent space opera that understands that fresh ideas and worlds are waiting to be born out of Isaac Asimov's brilliant literary works.

Isaac Asimov's novel series is brought to life in Foundation, a television adaptation of the epic sci-fi saga created for Apple TV+. The show follows a group of exiled humans that find themselves as the last chance to save the Galactic Empire, primarily from itself.

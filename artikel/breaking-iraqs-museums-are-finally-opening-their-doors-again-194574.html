📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

In a recent development, in 2022, Iraq’s National Museum in Baghdad reopened after being closed for three years following political unrest. Then in 2024, the museum closed again, this time for renovations. Now, it’s scheduled to open on December 1.

The Art Newspaper reported that the museum will open with several upcoming galleries, such as the Abbasid and Islamic galleries. There will also be specially curated wings.

The museum will display artifacts including winged bulls (lamassu) and stone carvings depicting legendary Assyrian monarchs, ancient clay tablets and historical prisms recording legal codes, calligraphic friezes, stone carvings, glassworks, and mosaics that have been recovered and restored. According to the newspaper Iraqi News, the museum’s doors are opening to domestic visitors, international tourists, and researchers from around the world. In a recent development, the reopening of the museum has been a long time coming. In 2003, the National Museum was the subject of looting during the US-led invasion. Ali Al-Dakhili, Iraq’s Minister of Tourism and Antiquities, has said that reopening the museum in December, rather than January 2027, is a priority, and that he also has looks for “a newly introduced building that befits the historical and civilizational value of Iraq.”. Paul Collins, the keeper of the Middle East Department at the British Museum, told the Art Newspaper that the reopening of the museum “is an vital moment, reflecting Iraq’s strong commitment to preserving its world-renowned cultural heritage and promoting heritage tourism.”.

As Iraq rebuilds itself after years of political unrest, the museums’ opening is a big step for the country’s cultural sector.

The Abbasid Palace in Baghdad opened its doors on November 15, and Iraq’s first Jewish museum, the Shamash Museum, opened its doors on October 22. Many of Iraq’s museums are also close to its large-scale monuments that are part of the city’s charm, such as the Al-Shaheed Monument designed by Iraqi sculptor Ismail Fatah al-Turk, the Monument to the Unknown Soldier designed by Khaled al-Rahal and Marcello D’Olivo, and the Saving Iraqi Culture Monument designed by Mohammed Ghani Hikmat.

According to recent reports, tottenham transfer news: Tottenham Hotspur are one of four clubs tracking Hakan Calhanoglu, according to Tuttomercatoweb. The Italian outlet lists Galatasaray, Juventus and Como alongside Spurs, although it gives no detail on how far Tottenham’s interest has progressed.

The timing matters because the Inter midfielder’s contract expires on 30 June 2027, which lets him agree a pre-contract with a foreign club from January. Inter want to extend but are trimming wages for their over-30s. Calhanoglu earns roughly £5.7m a year, and Inter are discussing £4.4m plus bonuses. He prefers to stay but hopes to avoid a heavy cut, and no formal offer exists yet.

Recent findings suggest that calhanoglu turns 33 in February. FotMob indicates three goals in 485 minutes from nine Serie A appearances, 91.4% pass accuracy, 20 chances created and a 98th-percentile ranking for shot attempts among Serie A central midfielders.

Passing range, long-distance shooting and dead-ball quality remain his strengths. He wins only 44.4% of his duels, though, completes a quarter of his dribbles, and his recovery speed will keep dropping with age. FotMob values him at about £6.4m, down from a £39m peak in 2024. Roberto De Zerbi wants his midfield to dictate play from deep, which suits Calhanoglu’s skills. Spurs have 15 points from 11 league games after beating Ipswich 2-0 on Saturday, their fourth win in five. Yet Sandro Tonali (£100m) and Mateus Fernandes (£85m) joined in July, and Rodrigo Bentancur, Conor Gallagher and Archie Gray compete for the same places.

Not as a priority. Money isn’t the obstacle. Tonali reportedly earns more than £275,000 a week, so Calhanoglu’s wages would barely register, and a free transfer costs no fee. Squad space is the problem. Spurs bought Tonali and Fernandes to run De Zerbi’s base for years, and a 33-year-old in the same role would either sit on the bench or take minutes from Gray and Lucas Bergvall, whom the club needs to develop.

According to recent reports, spurs would also struggle to sell the project without European football, while Galatasaray can reportedly offer higher wages. The case only strengthens if Tonali or Bentancur leaves, or if De Zerbi wants a penalty and set-piece specialist. Neither applies now, and nothing forces Spurs to act in January.

Industry observers note that calhanoglu’s output is real, but it comes with a decline in athleticism that a 33-year-old can’t reverse. Spurs would be paying for a short-term fix in a position where they have already invested £185m in the long-term answer. (Related: Industry News: Simple considerations for simple people ...)

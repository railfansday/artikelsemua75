It has been revealed that since its inception in 1990, the Women's Ice Hockey World Championship has almost always been held in the spring — apart from 2021 and 2022, when the COVID-19 pandemic saw it moved to the summer. This year, though, the tournament, being hosted by Denmark from November 6 to November 16, is being held in November for the first time.

According to recent reports, the idea is make it easier for all of the world's best players to participate. Since its inception in 2023, the North America-based Professional Women's Hockey League (PWHL), widely considered the world's best, has had to pause its season to allow players to take part in the Worlds. This is no longer necessary, as the PWHL's regular season doesn't open until December. (Related: Transformer-based Encoder-Decoder Models...)

"This gives the women's game a unique selling point," International Ice Hockey Federation (IIHF) President Franz Reindl recently told the SID news agency. "Because the PWHL season begins after this, we can be certain that the very best players are participating.".

Sources indicate that previously, the teams were divided into a stronger group and a weaker group, with the idea being to avoid, as much as possible, lopsided scores. This is because women's hockey is not as balanced as the men's game, with Canada or the United States having won every tournament since its inception.

Sources indicate that under the old system, all five teams in the stronger Group A advanced to the quarterfinals. Under the latest format, four of the five teams advance from both groups. This also sees powerhouses the US and Canada in separate groups, meaning the marquee matchup can't occur early in the tournament. Recent findings suggest that as noted above, either Canada or the United States has won every edition of this tournament — and indeed the two countries have split Olympic gold medals between them (Canada with five, USA with three) since the sport was introduced to the Winter Games in 1998. There is nothing to suggest that this is about to change in Herning and Esbjerg, although Canada enters the tournament with a vastly different roster from the one that won Olympic silver in Milano in February.

Among those looking to break this North American domination are Finland, the only other team ever to have made it to a world championship final and Switzerland, who took bronze in Milan.

The United States and Canada will play in generic kits provided by the International Ice Hockey Federation's official supplier due to an ongoing commercial rights dispute.

The Canadian federation, Hockey Canada, mentioned in a statement on Wednesday that its players would be wearing jerseys designed by True Hockey instead of their Bauer jerseys featuring the national team logo. USA Hockey has confirmed that its players too will be wearing jerseys without its national emblem.

"While we have yet to see the jersey Canada will be playing in on Friday, our team remains focused on the tournament and representing our country proudly on and off the ice," Hockey Canada noted.

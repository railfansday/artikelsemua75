Sources indicate that A jury has found Huawei guilty of stealing US trade secrets, as reported earlier by NBC News and Bloomberg. The verdict, handed down in a federal court in Newly introduced York, concludes a years-long legal battle that accused the Chinese telecom giant of stealing source code and proprietary technology from several companies, including T-Mobile, Cisco, and Motorola.

The Department of Justice’s 2018 criminal indictment against Huawei came to light following the arrest of the manufacturer’s CFO, Wanzhou Meng, whom the agency accused of fraud. Though the indictment against Meng was eventually dismissed, she admitted to misleading banks over Huawei’s operations in Iran as part of a deferred prosecution agreement. The US government blacklisted Huawei in 2019 over national security concerns.

In total, the jury found Huawei guilty of 10 of 12 criminal counts, including money laundering, violating US sanctions, bank fraud, wire fraud, and conspiracy to defraud the US. The company was acquitted of engaging in a criminal racketeering conspiracy and obstruction of justice. Huawei’s sentencing is set for September 14th, 2027, where it may face “financial penalties including criminal fines, forfeiture, and restitution,” according to the DOJ.

“For over two decades, Huawei built its global empire on a foundation of fraud, theft, and deception,” Tysen A. Duva, the assistant attorney general of the DOJ’s criminal division, claims in a statement. “Huawei’s crimes threatened the U.S. financial system, took advantage of American companies that compete honestly in the global telecommunications market, and undermined our national security.”.

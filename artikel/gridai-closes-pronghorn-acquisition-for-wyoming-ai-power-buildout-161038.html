Recent findings suggest that gridAI Technologies confirmed on November 23, 2026 that it had completed its all-stock acquisition of Pronghorn Resources, gaining a producing natural-gas business in Wyoming’s Powder River Basin for its planned AI data-center power platform. The company is targeting an initial 300 MW of dedicated power delivery in 2027.

The closing also assigned GridAI exclusive Wyoming territorial rights under a joint venture with 1st American Nuclear Co., or FANCO. Its development plan combines the acquired gas production, land, water and pipeline infrastructure with third-party fuel procurement, existing substations and dedicated behind-the-meter generation for large AI computing sites. Pronghorn’s operating assets include approximately 100 million cubic feet per day of gross gas production and about 3,200 producing wells. GridAI estimates that output could fuel up to approximately 600 MW of gas-fired generation, depending on plant efficiency and deliverable volumes. That is a fuel-supply equivalency, not generation capacity already installed. (Related: 3D Asset Generation: AI for Game Development #3...)

Sources indicate that the asset base also includes roughly 1 million acres of mineral and leasehold interests and more than 450 miles of owned pipeline and gathering infrastructure. Prior owners invested approximately $1.7 billion in the infrastructure, excluding well costs. Pronghorn also produces approximately 14 million gallons of water daily, with potential applications in industrial power operations and data-center cooling.

Estimated reserves total about 2 trillion cubic feet on a 3P basis, meaning proved, probable and possible reserves. GridAI cautions that probable and possible reserves are less certain to be recovered than proved reserves, and that the figure is not an estimate of proved reserves prepared under SEC definitions.

Recent findings suggest that connections to regional systems including Kinder Morgan and Western Midstream provide access to gas beyond Pronghorn’s own production. GridAI expects to purchase third-party gas and transport it to planned power sites, subject to transportation capacity and commercial arrangements. Chief Executive Officer Jason Sawyer said the connections provide “the flexibility to supplement our own production with third-party gas supply as customer power demand grows.” GridAI is also pursuing well rehabilitation and new drilling while preserving opportunities for standalone gas sales.

The closing follows definitive agreements signed on November 12, 2026 and detailed in a Form 8-K filed on November 17, 2026. The agreements provided Pronghorn’s sellers with consideration representing 66 million common-equivalent shares, composed of common stock and Series I Convertible Preferred Stock. SIGMA9 Capital’s separate consideration for assigning the FANCO rights also consisted of common and preferred stock.

According to recent reports, preferred-share conversion requires stockholder approval and Nasdaq approval of the underlying common shares, and remains subject to beneficial-ownership limits. The sellers’ transaction shares carry an initial 30-month lock-up. No debt financed the acquisition consideration, although Pronghorn’s gas-prepayment, plugging-and-abandonment and other contractual obligations remain with Pronghorn.

Discussions are underway with hyperscale data-center developers, high-performance-compute operators and prospective power buyers about long-term power supply and infrastructure partnerships. GridAI cautions that these discussions may not produce definitive commercial agreements. Its identified sources of potential economic value include gas sales, power-supply agreements, water supply and ownership interests in the data-center campuses it helps develop.

Industry observers note that the FANCO rights provide a framework for the Bridge Power strategy, which combines commercially available generation technologies with possible later integration of advanced nuclear power. FANCO’s EAGL-1 small modular reactor platform contemplates 240 MWe units, with six-unit clusters designed for approximately 1.44 GWe of baseload generation. Deployment remains subject to technology development, commercial availability, licensing, financing and regulatory approvals.

Natural gas is GridAI’s immediate priority, while financing, pending permit and operatorship transfers, equipment and contractor availability, and customer agreements remain execution risks. The closing adds fuel production and delivery infrastructure. Meeting the 2027 target now depends on financing and building generation facilities, completing those transfers and converting commercial discussions into definitive power agreements.

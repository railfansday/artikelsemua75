Ripple had a substantial week in late October, striking deals with Robinhood and Coinbase to support its stablecoin, Ripple USD (RLUSD +0.00%). The blockchain organization is also the largest holder of XRP (XRP -7.44%), and CEO Brad Garlinghouse has said that XRP and stablecoins like Ripple USD are complementary rather than competitive.

Sources indicate that with that in mind, Ripple USD's success may seem like good news for XRP. I'm not convinced; these deals look more like XRP getting left behind.

On Oct. 27, Robinhood announced Ripple USD was live on Robinhood Chain and Bitstamp, the cryptocurrency exchange it acquired last year. It will be available in the Robinhood app later this year. Robinhood will use Ripple USD on the XRP Ledger (XRPL) to settle institutional trades on Bitstamp, and it will integrate Ripple Treasury into its global treasury operations. Industry observers note that A day later, Ripple and Coinbase announced a partnership, with the crypto exchange making Ripple USD available on Coinbase Prime, Coinbase Exchange, and the Coinbase Retail app. Coinbase Global Exchange customers will eventually be able to use the stablecoin as collateral. Industry observers note that the problem for XRP holders is that Ripple USD isn't reliant on the cryptocurrency or its blockchain. About 74% of the Ripple USD supply is on Ethereum, according to DeFiLlama data from Nov. 19, compared to 25% on the XRPL and the rest on other blockchains. Coinbase's stablecoin integration supports Ethereum, Base, and XRPL.

In a recent development, in fairness, there's still a sizable amount of Ripple USD on the XRPL, and it's nearly 90% of the blockchain's total stablecoin supply. XRP is the native cryptocurrency on the XRPL and is used to pay transaction fees. However, this is where the network's low fees can be a double-edged sword. Transaction fees start at 0.00001 XRP, meaning even Ripple USD activity doesn't generate much demand for XRP.

CRYPTO · As of 2:20 PM ET 11/20/26.

An even deeper issue is that Ripple USD cannibalizes XRP's primary use case. The pitch for XRP is that it can serve as a bridge currency for financial institutions to send and receive cross-border payments. But a stablecoin pegged at $1 works much better as a bridge currency than a volatile cryptocurrency like XRP. The more Ripple USD grows, the less XRP is needed.

Ripple USD is having a successful 2026, going from a market cap of $1.3 billion at the start of the year to $2.8 billion as of Nov. 18, becoming one of the 10 largest stablecoins in the process. XRP, on the other hand, has plummeted from a market cap of $122 billion in January to $63 billion. Institutional demand has fallen, with spot XRP ETFs losing $19.1 million in the week ending Nov. 13, the largest weekly outflow in eight months.

It has been revealed that the partnerships with Robinhood and Coinbase show that Ripple is building a legitimate stablecoin business. That's good for Ripple and Ripple USD, but the success doesn't flow back to XRP.

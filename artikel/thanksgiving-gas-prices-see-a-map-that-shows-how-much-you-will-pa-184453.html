That’s where some good news and bad news come in. The good news is that gas prices have been falling recently. The bad news is that this Thanksgiving period may be the most expensive for gas prices on record. Here’s what you need to know. The most expensive Thanksgiving on record? Anyone with a car knows gas prices have been one of the biggest economic burdens of 2026. Thanks mostly to the continuing war in Iran, oil prices have surged, which has directly correlated with skyrocketing prices at the pump. In May, the national average topped $4.50 per gallon of regular unleaded. Thankfully, prices have fallen since then, and today’s national average price for a gallon of regular unleaded is down to $3.61. That’s 17 cents lower than the average of $3.78 a week ago, and 69 cents lower than the national average of $4.30 a month ago. That’s all good news. But unfortunately, there’s some bad news too. People drive a lot during Thanksgiving week as they go to and from their festive destinations. That means they fill up at the pump a lot. And this Thanksgiving week, gas prices are expected to exceed or be near Thanksgiving records. In a November 19 post, the American Automobile Association (AAA) noted that national average gas prices have been dropping as Thanksgiving approaches. On November 19, the national average was $3.72—eleven cents higher than today’s average. However, the organization also noted that unless gas prices dropped another 15 cents from that average, this Thanksgiving Day would see the highest national average gas prices on record. That milestone is currently held by Thanksgiving Day 2022 (November 24). On that day, the national average was $3.58 per gallon. If today’s national average of $3.61 does not drop another 3 cents before Thursday, this Thanksgiving will have the highest gas prices ever. And even if it drops below the 2022 record, it will still be significantly higher than Thanksgiving Day gas prices last year, when the national average hovered around $3.10 per gallon for regular unleaded.

Roy Keane accused visiting Manchester United supporters at Anfield of “applauding rubbish” after Liverpool romped to a 4-0 win.

Mauricio Pochettino’s first game in charge got off to the worst possible start when Leny Yoro was red-carded after eight minutes.

Dominik Szoboszlai and Florian Wirtz made it 2-0 to Liverpool inside 13 minutes, after which United’s 10 men never looked like responding.

Two goals in the second half from Rio Ngumoha and Victor Munoz further laid bare the size of the task facing Pochettino as United were left second-bottom of the Premier League.

Some travelling fans offered encouraging applause to the players at the end, a reflection of perhaps of the losing cause they had fought after losing Yoro so early but, speaking on Sky Sports, Keane suggested it was a mark of how low standards have fallen.

“Has a team ever got a result before with 10 men? It can be done,” he noted, taking concern with a comment made by Gary Neville highlighting the importance of the red.

“Are you telling me Man Utd couldn’t have done better and everyone is saying it’s not bad. At least show a bit more fight, a bit more quality, I can’t believe what I’m hearing.

Sources indicate that “Fans are applauding the players, applauding what? Applauding rubbish. When do the players take responsibility for some of the decision-making? Liverpool should have scored eight or nine.

“I feel like swearing. How can anyone say 4-0 is not too bad?”.

Keane fumed over United’s failure to get the basics right on a dire afternoon, with Matthijs de Ligt in particular struggling in his first league start in over a year.

"If you do the basics right, you give yourself a bit of a chance so no matter how good you think you might be or a massive club you are, if you're not doing the basics right, you're going to be constantly under pressure and constantly getting criticised,” he added.

"If that happened against a pub team, they'd be criticising you and this is a substantial game.

"You're talking about starting well, being solid, but again, we go back to the defensive shape and one stray ball over the top puts somebody in on goal.

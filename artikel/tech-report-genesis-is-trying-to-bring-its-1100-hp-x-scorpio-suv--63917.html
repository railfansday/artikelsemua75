📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that genesis might be a carmaker that has so far largely stuck to meat-and-potatoes models like sedans and SUVs, but the Korean luxury brand has also pumped out quite a few tantalizing concept cars in recent years. Some of them, like the G90 Wingback and Magma GT supercar, have seemed almost production-ready; others, like the X Gran Equator and X-Scorpio off-roaders, less so. But according to Automotive News, the wild X-Scorpio concept could in fact wind up in showrooms—and none other than Hyundai CEO Jose Muñoz is the one spreading the news.

According to recent reports, “We are working with designers to try to put [the X-Scorpio] into the market,” Muñoz told AN. The feedback that Genesis received from dealers and potential customers was so positive, he mentioned, that “we are going to give it a try.”. Recent findings suggest that if the X-Scorpio does reach showrooms, it would mark yet another flank in the Hyundai Motor Group’s fast-moving and wide-ranging onslaught of off-road trucks and SUVs. Hyundai and Kia have both made clear plans for multiple body-on-frame vehicles, including ones inspired by the Hyundai Boulder concept revealed at this year’s Newly introduced York Auto Show; Genesis itself, meanwhile, has confirmed the luxurious X Gran Equator will enter production, potentially rivaling everything from the Lexus LX to the Mercedes-Benz G-wagen. (Related: AAWireless releases newly introduced, smaller Nexus and...)

According to recent reports, still, none of those are anything like the X-Scorpio. In fact, there’s nothing quite like it on sale today, period. The concept, at least, is something of a cross between a Local Motors Rally Fighter and a Lamborghini Huracan Sterrato, with a twin-turbo V-8 putting out 1100 horsepower and 850 pound-feet of torque that gets sent to 40-inch off-road tires on 18-inch beadlock wheels. It was designed, the tech giant says, for the Middle East, where screaming across open terrain and sending it off sand dunes is, as the press release politely puts it, “a favorite pastime”—but considering how those are also quite popular here in the United States (see: TRX, Ram; Raptor, Ford), it seems likely a production X-Scorpio would also be a hit here.

Of course, talk is cheap, even if it’s an automotive CEO saying it. Genesis does indeed have big intends for a bunch of newly introduced cars over the next few years, with 22 all-new or revised models coming by 2030. But whether there’s room on the balance sheet to develop for a bespoke off-road sports car with no direct competitors seems like a fairly large question mark, so let’s just keep our fingers crossed for now.

Science Corporation, the medical technology startup founded by former Neuralink president Max Hodak in 2021, raised a $400 million Series D, the tech giant noted in a blog post.

Investors in the new round valued the organization at $3.5 billion, a person familiar with the deal told TechCrunch. Those backers included Khosla Ventures, the Ackman Oxman Institute, Tenet Healthcare, 885 Capital, Y Combinator, and PLC Neurotech. Hodak, who talked his way into a graduate neuroscience lab as a college student and has pursued biotechnology ever since, co-founded Science to leverage newly introduced technologies to understand and interact with the brain.

Sources indicate that science’s first product is PRIMA, a wearable and implantable chip that helps people with age-related macular degeneration recover some vision. The device is approved in Europe and available in Germany, as well as being trialed for latest conditions.

Hodak unveiled that the device is now being trialed for patients with another blindness-causing condition, Stargardt disease, while onstage at TechCrunch Disrupt. (Related: Tech Report: “Determined” woman completes 7 marathons i...)

Industry observers note that with the funding, the manufacturer aims to bring the device to the United States through the FDA’s Humanitarian Device Exemption, which lets experimental medical technology be used to treat rare conditions. Science has convinced the FDA that advanced macular degeneration in both eyes is uncommon enough to let them seek approval to use the device on people in the U.S.

Industry observers note that still, the startup’s major ambition and founding purpose is connecting directly to the human brain through a bio-hybrid sensor built from a combination of cultured neurons and silicon. The company is right now designing initial human trials of the device with Yale neurosurgery professor Murat Günel, who told TechCrunch it could offer potential treatments for Parkinson’s disease, among other brain injuries and illnesses.

Let’s face it, Neil McCann was never the most popular of appointments with Kilmarnock fans, but not even his harshest critics could deny that he did a fine job in keeping the club in Scotland’s top flight last season.

Unfortunately for him, it looks increasingly likely that he isn’t going to get the chance to repeat the trick this time around.

The writing already appears to be on the wall for the 52-year-old. A question of when, rather than if, he is given his jotters.

According to recent reports, by all accounts, he hasn’t lost the dressing room. Which is just as well, because he has well and truly lost the punters, whose collective mood shifted from angry to apathetic over the course of 90-odd thoroughly entertaining minutes at Rugby Park on Saturday. (Related: Course Launch Community Event...)

When that happens, it’s a long way back.

According to recent reports, mcCann knows this himself. It is not as if he is operating with the blinkers on.

It's a tough watch for Neil McCann as his Kilmarnock team go down 4-2 to Aberdeen.

His assessment of his team’s performances week-to-week tends to be pretty honest, as it was again this weekend.

The problem is, as he put it, that he is coming across ‘like a broken record’.

His team are hopeless at the back and toothless up top, with some encouraging stuff in between. If the supporters are sick of hearing it, imagine how scunnered McCann must feel saying it. He certainly looks it.

If they continue to defend like this, relegation is a nailed-on certainty.

They shipped four here — and it could have very easily been a few more — to take their tally to 33 conceded for the season in just 14 games. It’s laughable, there’s no other way to dress it up. (Related: Deploy Hugging Face models easily with Amazon SageMaker...)

Aberdeen’s Kevin Nisbet scored twice in four first-half minutes, capitalising on a woeful clearance from keeper Nico Mantl to put the Dons ahead, before curling a free-kick straight through the weakest wall you’ve ever seen in your life to double the visitors’ advantage.

Fresh Dons boss Mario Frick can't hide his delight after victory in first game in charge.

According to recent reports, the third goal — shortly after Greg Kiltie had given McCann’s men a lifeline — was the worst of the lot. Former Rugby Park skipper Brad Lyons ghosted in behind the completely unaware Michael Schjonning-Larsen to head the ball home after Cathal McCarthy failed to stop Nesta Guinness-Walker’s cross from the left touchline.

Shocking stuff all round, and exactly the type of goal which has killed Killie all season — and will continue to do so until something drastic changes.

Let’s not forget, the recent international break afforded McCann and his staff a fortnight to work on things on the training pitch ahead of what is going to be a gruelling winter schedule.

Sources indicate that while the Killie manager will ultimately be the one to pay the price, his players need to have a right good look at themselves too. There was simply no sign of anyone in that backline taking any sort of responsibility.

Kilmarnock’s next three games are against St Mirren, Hibernian and Motherwell — the three sides at the moment directly above them in the table.

Sources indicate that an absolute minimum of four points from that run is required, otherwise this is going to get rather ugly. In truth, defeat in Paisley this weekend may well be the final nail in McCann’s coffin.

On the other hand, things are finally starting to look up for the Dons.

New manager Mario Frick has promised to bring heavy metal football to these shores, and if Saturday is anything to go by, the ex-Liechtenstein international is a man of his word.

McCann's job looks to be on the line after bottom club Killie succumbed to another defeat.

His team were a joy to watch going forward here, particularly in the first half.

It was a million miles away from the kind of turgid football offered up under his predecessor as Dons manager, Stephen Robinson.

Speed, intensity, aggression, quality. The performance was full of it.

You could count the number of times the Dons faffed about with the ball on one hand. Every time there was an opportunity to get forward, they took it.

Ante Palaversa, making his first appearance of the campaign, was at the heart of it.

For whatever reason, Robinson never fancied the Croatian, a stance which perplexed the Pittodrie faithful. Indeed, the 26-year-old — once on the books of Manchester City — was deemed surplus to requirements over the summer.

The Dons will be delighted there were no takers.

Sources indicate that he was outstanding here as he commanded that space between midfield and defence without breaking sweat. Nobody in Killie colours could get near him.

Both Nicky Devlin and Guinness-Walker bombed up their respective wings all day, while some of the link-up play between Nisbet — who should have had a hat-trick before Findlay Marshall wrapped things up late on — and Hicham Mahou was delightful.

Defensively, they were all over the shop at times, and no doubt they will be punished heavily at Ibrox on Tuesday night if they are as open as this.

In a recent development, but this was a display to simply be enjoyed rather than picked apart. It makes a change.

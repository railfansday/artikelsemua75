This year, for my Thanksgiving story, I’m writing something experimental. I’ll treat the art of our country in 1863. That’s the year President Lincoln issued his proclamation designating the last Thursday in November as a national day of “Thanksgiving and Praise to our beneficent Father, who dwelleth in the Heavens.”.

I’m an art historian and don’t believe that artists quaff a surfeit of blood-orange champagne mules, sing “Auld Lang Syne,” do a countdown if they’re sober enough to count backward, and decide — snap — to change styles or themes. That year — 1863 — was different, though, and that Thanksgiving was different. Eastman Johnson (1824–1906) was our savviest painter, tuned to the moment and its nuances.

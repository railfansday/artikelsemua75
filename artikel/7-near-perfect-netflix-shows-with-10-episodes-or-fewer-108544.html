Created by Pendleton Ward and Duncan Trussell, The Midnight Gospel is an adult animated TV series that takes the audience to a dimension known as the Chromatic Ribbon to meet spacecaster Clancy Gilroy. Through an unlicensed multiverse simulator, Clancy travels to strange worlds on the brink of disaster to interview some of their residents for his spacecast. In these conversations, they cover themes like death, grief, love, and magic, but also go on more philosophical routes, with themes like transcendence and existentialism.

In a recent development, the Midnight Gospel is one of the best but most underrated animated reveals on Netflix, with wonderful (and trippy) animation and depth like no other show, thanks to the themes covered in every episode. In just eight episodes, The Midnight Gospel takes viewers into a surprisingly deep and emotional journey through bizarre worlds that will leave them thinking about some topics for a long time.

According to recent reports, based on Walter Tevis’ 1983 novel of the same name, The Queen’s Gambit takes viewers back to the 1950s and 1960s to meet Beth Harmon (Anya Taylor-Joy). Beth is an orphan who lost her mother when she was nine, so she grew up in an orphanage. There she also learned how to play chess, and years later, she’s determined to become an elite chess player. However, her upbringing at the orphanage also led her to develop a substance-use disorder, which can mess with her goals as a chess player.

According to recent reports, the Queen’s Gambit is one of Netflix’s best TV shows of all time, being a critical success and praised for its cinematography, performances, and themes, as well as for its accurate depictions of high-level chess. The Queen’s Gambit is only seven episodes long, but it’s so well-made that it tells Beth’s entire story in a limited number of episodes.

Unbelievable is a true crime drama miniseries based on the 2015 news article “An Unbelievable Story of Rape”, about the Washington and Colorado serial rape cases between 2008 and 2011. Unbelievable follows Marie (Kaitlyn Dever), who is charged with a crime for reporting that she was sexually assaulted. Detectives Grave Rasmussen (Toni Collette) and Karen Duvall (Merritt Wever) take her case, and they work hard to identify Marie’s abuser.

According to recent reports, unbelievable was a critical success, presently holding a near-perfect Rotten Tomatoes critics score of 98%. Unlike most true crime dramas, which mostly focus on the crimes, Unbelievable is more focused on the survivors, their stories, and the impact of abuse. Surely, Unbelievable isn’t an easy watch as it addresses heavy and real problems, but it’s one of the best Netflix miniseries to date.

Unorthodox is a German drama miniseries inspired by Deborah Feldman’s 2012 autobiography of the same name. Unorthodox tells the story of Esty Shapiro (Shira Haas), a 19-year-old Jewish woman in an arranged marriage as she’s part of the Satmar sect of the ultra-Orthodox community in Fresh York City. Unhappy with her life, Esty runs away to Berlin to live with her estranged mother, and does her best to adapt to a secular life, reject the beliefs she grew up with, and truly explore the world.

Sources indicate that however, Esty’s life takes a turn when her husband learns of her pregnancy, and so their rabbi sends him to Berlin to find her. Unorthodox has been praised for its adaptation of a real-life story that addresses serious yet delicate topics, and Haas’ performance. Unorthodox is one of Netflix’s best miniseries to date, and a perfect binge-watch with just four episodes.

It has been revealed that based on Stephanie Land’s memoir of the same name, Maid tells the story of Alex Russell (Margaret Qualley), a young mother who leaves her abusive boyfriend, Sean (Nick Robinson), and moves into a shelter with her two-year-old daughter, Maddy. Alex gets a job at Value Maids cleaning houses, but what Alex really wants is to be a writer. What follows are Alex’s struggles to not only raise Maddy and give her a home, but also to deal with Sean and her own dysfunctional family.

Maid was one of Netflix’s biggest hits of 2021, praised for its performances, themes, and how it handled Alex’s story. Maid isn’t an easy watch all the time, as it addresses themes like domestic violence, but its overall quality, along with being just 10 episodes long, makes it worth the time.

According to recent reports, one of Netflix’s most recent and biggest hits is Adolescence, a psychological crime drama miniseries created by Jack Thorne and Stephen Graham. Adolescence introduces viewers to the Miller family: father Eddie (Stephen Graham), mother Manda (Christine Tremarco), daughter Lisa (Amélie Pease), and 13-year-old son Jamie (Owen Cooper). One day, Jamie is arrested on suspicion of the murder of his classmate, Katie Leonard, and so the show covers the interrogation, investigation, and how all of this impacts the Millers and their community.

Industry observers note that adolescence has been praised for its cinematography, writing, pace, direction, and performances, and in just four episodes, it keeps the audience engaged from beginning to end, with increasing suspense, lots of drama, and addressing serious and important themes that will leave the audience with a lot to think about. Like other entries on this list, Adolescence might not be an easy watch at times, but it’s a must-watch.

Netflix has a variety of horror TV demonstrates, but the one widely regarded as its best is Mike Flanagan’s The Haunting of Hill House. Loosely based on Shirley Jackson’s 1959 novel of the same name, The Haunting of Hill House follows the Crain family – parents Hugh (Henry Thomas) and Olivia (Carla Gugino), and children Steven, Shirley, Theo, Luke, and Nell – as they move into the title mansion to renovate it and later sell it. When unexpected repairs keep them there longer than planned, they all begin to experience increasing paranormal phenomena that lead to a tragedy.

Years later, when another tragedy hits the family, the Crains reunite and are forced to confront their past at Hill House, both individually and as a family. The Haunting of Hill House is a horror masterpiece, with strong acting, complex characters, emotional depth, and genuine horror that doesn’t rely on jumpscares, instead coming from the trauma and struggles of the Crain family.

Industry observers note that one of the hottest exchange-traded funds (ETFs) this year has been the iShares Semiconductor ETF (SOXX -2.75%). This ETF, which tracks an index that features the 30 largest U.S. semiconductor stocks, has returned an impressive 37% this year and 49% over the past 12 months. According to recent reports, but since the recent peak of $196 per share on Oct. 6, the day the Nasdaq Composite (^IXIC -2.25%) reached an all-time high, the ETF has been in a downward spiral, with the price dropping about 30% to $138 per share at this writing.

A combination of factors caused the meltdown, including overvalued large caps, concerns about overspending on artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure, and negative sentiment toward building more data centers — particularly as Democrats swept into Congress. Also, the Nvidia (NVDA -3.30%) scandal and the resignation of its longtime CEO Jensen Huang pulled down semiconductor and technology stocks pretty much across the board.

So, it has been a rough couple of months for semiconductor stocks, but here's why I think the iShares Semiconductor ETF will have another strong year in 2027.

Industry observers note that the sharp sell-off of semiconductor stocks should serve as a reset of sorts. The correction was overdue because many top chip stocks had become overvalued amid rapid growth over the past three to four years.

This reset should bring their valuations down to more attractive, sustainable levels, potentially sparking a bounce-back rally in 2027.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/19/26.

A slowdown in data center development is certainly on the table, if not likely, given that it was a major glitch in the recent election campaigns. That may have a longer-term impact, and it may not -- only time will tell. There just may be more restrictions and parameters on where data centers are located and how they operate.

But it shouldn't have a major effect on chip stocks in 2027. Hyperscalers have a huge amount of backlog of contracts on the books waiting to be executed, and many chipmakers are already sold out of AI chips for 2027 and into 2028.

According to recent reports, also, the supercycle created by the supply-demand imbalance among memory and storage stocks remains. Supply is not expected to catch up to demand for at least a few years, and that gives chipmakers huge pricing power. A potential slowdown in building AI data centers could help ease the supply-demand imbalance somewhat, but likely down the road beyond 2027.

So, the correction in chip stocks might be painful right now, but I think it makes for a more attractive buying opportunity for the iShares Semiconductor ETF heading into 2027.

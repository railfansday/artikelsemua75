Industry News: Lions score touchdown to end first half after Dan Campbell opts for safety on Bears' illegal batting penalty

According to recent reports, the high-powered Thanksgiving matchup between the Detroit Lions and the Chicago Bears delivered early, with a wild sequence just before halftime. After accepting a potentially risky safety on an unusual Bears penalty, the Lions converted a touchdown to close out the first half with a 19-10 lead.

Though potentially a game-changing opportunity, the sequence wasn't enough to get the win: After a high-scoring second half, Chicago eventually pulled off a stunning 39-35 win off a last-second touchdown.

The Lions' late first-half sequence started after the two-minute warning, with quarterback Caleb Williams getting sacked and fumbling the ball at the Bears' 2-yard line.

Once out of Williams' hands, the ball bounced past multiple players into the Chicago end zone. Bears offensive lineman Darnell Wright, in an effort to stave off a safety, used a close fist to bat the ball out of the end zone, where Lions defensive back Brian Branch was able to get his hands on it.

But Wright's efforts to get the ball out of the end zone were not allowed: The lineman was flagged for "illegal batting," which results in a safety if the foul occurs in the end zone.

The situation presented Lions head coach Dan Campbell with a decision: Take the safety and try to put together a touchdown drive before halftime, or decline the penalty to get the ball and set up a red-zone drive.

Campbell decided to go for the safety, giving him and the Lions a minute and change to grab a touchdown and justify the choice.

Initially, the drive got off to a concerning start: The Lions, who had already been called for two false starts earlier in the quarter, picked up two more false start penalties in a row to set up third-and-12. Jared Goff then erased some of the distance by finding Jahmyr Gibbs for an 11-yard reception, but the catch fell just short of first down.

On fourth-and-1, though, Detroit pulled off a gutsy and successful quarterback sneak, giving Goff the 2-yard rush to keep the drive going. That set up a massive 39-yard throw from Goff to Jameson Williams, before another first down to Gibbs, and a successful touchdown throw to Sam LaPorta.

Sources indicate that in an interview on the broadcast after halftime, Campbell told CBS that the number of timeouts left — the Bears had two at the time, while the Lions had all three — and his faith in his players led him to go for the full nine-point play. He praised Williams and Goff for their plays, and mentioned that he "just felt good" about the offense's chances to find the end zone.

The touchdown ended up being a crucial play, and Detroit and Chicago spent much of the second half trading scores. But it wasn't enough to stave off a Bears win.

According to recent reports, illegal batting, the penalty that sparked the whole sequence, is a relative rarity in the league. One of the only recent examples came against the Lions, with Seattle Seahawks linebacker K.J. Wright batting a fumble out of the end zone in 2015, though the foul was not flagged. The NFL later admitted that the penalty should have been called.

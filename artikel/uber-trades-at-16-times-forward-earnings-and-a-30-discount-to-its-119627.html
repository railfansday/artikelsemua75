When looking at a list of the most innovative and disruptive businesses this century, the investment community will agree that Uber Technologies (UBER -3.25%) is impossible to ignore. It completely upended the taxi industry, leveraging the prevalence of smartphones, the app economy, and faster connectivity to become a global technology giant. The impressive position Uber has built, however, hasn't prevented the growth stock from lagging the broader market in the past five years. And it now trades at what appears to be a very compelling forward price-to-earnings ratio of 16.4, even though its financial performance is strong.

Management might be feeling the weight of the market's worries about the potential threat that autonomous vehicles (AV) will pose for the firm's operations. There's a valid argument to be made that this exciting latest technology could end up being a major opportunity for Uber, though. And that alters the investment thesis, signalling that shares are attractively priced right now.

When investors hear about developments within the AV industry, two companies come to mind. The market leader is Alphabet's (GOOGL -2.70%) (GOOG -2.59%) Waymo. It's backed by one of the most dominant technology companies in the world, which means it has top-tier talent and software/hardware capabilities that are hard to beat.

Recent findings suggest that waymo is winning the race. The platform currently operates in 15 U.S. cities. There are intends to expand domestically, as well as in London and Tokyo. It completed 500,000 fully autonomous trips per week in March.

It has been revealed that there's also Tesla (TSLA -7.64%), which is winding down the Cybertruck to shift factory production to the two-seater Cybercab. Its robotaxi service offers full self-driving rides in eight U.S. cities. Despite a slow rollout, Elon Musk has high hopes.

Assuming that both Waymo and Tesla make rapid advancements in AV tech, expand to new markets, grow their user bases, increase miles driven, lower costs, successfully navigate a patchy regulatory environment, and maintain strict safety standards, then it's possible their ride-hailing apps more effectively compete with Uber's leadership position in mobility.

And that's the threat that's harming sentiment toward Uber stock. There is terminal risk from AVs.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

Sources indicate that uber is still operating at full strength. It ended the third quarter with 215 million monthly active owners. Mobility gross bookings jumped 18% year over year to $29.5 billion. And operating income was well over $1.4 billion, up 30%.

Sources indicate that the business has built up a powerful network effect, matching riders with drivers in cities around the globe. The key is that it directly controls demand and manages the customer relationship. That puts it in an advantageous position to essentially become the AV commercialization layer.

Sources indicate that if you're an AV software developer, auto manufacturer, or any other related technical provider, you've likely incurred sizable capital investments. And it's preferred that monetization happens sooner rather than later. Plugging into Uber's platform seems like the best solution to maximize vehicle utilization. it completed 4.1 billion trips just in the last three months.

Recent findings suggest that management isn't waiting around. It's actively partnering with AV companies, most notably with a total planned $10 billion capital commitment, to drive supply onto its network. It already invested in Rivian Automotive, Lucid Group, and Nuro, for instance. And it's entered into car purchasing agreements.

In a recent development, the company is targeting about 700 fully driverless vehicles on its platform by year-end. By the end of 2027, it's aiming for more than 2,000. These are tiny figures in the grand scheme of things.

Uber believes that human drivers will also play a crucial role for a long time. This assumption is supported by the simple fact that rider demand fluctuates wildly depending on the time of day. Human drivers can handle demand spikes. If all rides were from AVs, on the other hand, many of these cars would be idle most of the time, which would be financially wasteful.

Recent findings suggest that still, it would be naive to think that Uber is in the clear. AVs pose a real long-term threat. It's something investors should monitor closely.

It has been revealed that however, the business is leaning on its favorable hand by setting itself up to remain a leader in mobility. The current valuation more than reflects the market's negative view.

Getting Out of a Car Loan – Sell or Refinance Before You Fall Behind

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that giving back a financed car leaves you owing whatever its sale doesn’t cover. To get out of a car loan, have a buyer, a new lender, or a dealer pay it off. Ask your lender for the payoff amount, compare it with what the car would sell for, and choose your exit before you miss a payment.

Those two numbers tell you whether a sale would pay off the whole loan. Your payoff amount can differ from the balance on your statement, since it includes interest up to the day you pay it off and any unpaid fees.

Look up the car’s private-sale and trade-in values on Kelley Blue Book or Edmunds. Say your payoff is $18,000 and the car sells privately for $19,500. The sale pays off the loan and leaves you $1,500. Add $7,500 of savings, and you can buy a $9,000 used car outright. (Related: Get an RTX 5070 for just $839.99, a rare savings in the...)

Stopping payments costs the most. In many states, a lender can take the car without notice after a missed payment, sell it, and bill you for whatever the sale doesn’t cover plus its repossession costs. Giving the car back yourself, called a voluntary repossession, may cut those costs, but the Federal Trade Commission says it can still go on your credit report, where most negative information can stay for seven years.

Call your lender for the payoff amount before your next due date, and ask on the same call whether it allows a loan transfer or a hardship deferral.

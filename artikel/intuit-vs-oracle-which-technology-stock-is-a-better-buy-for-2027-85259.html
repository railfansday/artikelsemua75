According to recent reports, intuit serves as the backbone for personal and small business finances, while Oracle provides the essential databases and cloud infrastructure that large organizations require. Both companies are presently integrating artificial intelligence to enhance their platforms. Sources indicate that intuit provides essential financial technology solutions for millions of global consumers and small businesses through its core brands like TurboTax, QuickBooks, and Credit Karma. The firm currently serves approximately 93 million users and relies on major distribution platforms like the Apple (AAPL -1.90%) App Store and Google Play Store. To support its vast software ecosystem, it maintains strategic partnerships with major cloud infrastructure providers including Amazon (AMZN -4.10%) and Alphabet (GOOGL -2.70%) (NASDAQ:GOOG).

According to recent reports, in the 12 months ended Oct. 31, 2026, revenue reached nearly $21.9 billion, representing growth of close to 12.8% compared to the prior twelve-month period. Net income for this time frame was approximately $4.8 billion, reflecting the tech giant's ability to maintain high net margins while expanding its user base. This steady financial expansion is largely driven by consistent, recurring demand for its digital accounting and tax preparation platforms among mid-market businesses.

As of its July 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is nearly 0.4x, a metric that compares total debt against the value of shareholder equity to assess financial leverage. The current ratio is approximately 1.5x, reflecting a healthy ability to pay short-term obligations using its most liquid assets. In the fiscal year ended July 31, 2026, free cash flow reached nearly $8.7 billion, though note that stock-based compensation represented roughly 23.3% of operating cash flow, which inflates reported cash generation since SBC is a non-cash expense added back in the cash flow statement.

Oracle offers enterprise software, cloud applications, and infrastructure services through a global delivery model that serves corporations, government agencies, and educational institutions. The company distributes its complex database systems and cloud applications in more than 145 countries and operates through a network of resellers and system integrators. It recently focused on expanding its presence in tech stocks by building out massive data center capacity specifically designed for high-performance artificial intelligence workloads.

It has been revealed that in the 12 months ended Aug. 31, 2026, revenue reached close to $71.8 billion, a significant growth rate of approximately 21.6%. Net income for this period was nearly $18.9 billion, showcasing the scale of its operations as it transitions legacy customers to its newer cloud models. This robust increase in sales is largely attributed to the rapid adoption of its cloud infrastructure services and the ongoing modernization of enterprise data systems.

As of its May 2026 balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is approximately 3.7x, indicating that the tech giant uses a substantial amount of debt relative to its equity to fund its capital-intensive operations. The current ratio is roughly 1.1x, suggesting that its ability to meet immediate financial obligations is right now lean compared to more liquid software rivals. In the fiscal year ended May 31, 2026, free cash flow was a negative $23.7 billion because its massive investments in data center equipment and physical assets exceeded the cash generated from day-to-day operations.

Sources indicate that intuit faces intense competition from established rivals and start-ups in the financial software space, particularly those offering lower-cost tax preparation alternatives. The business is heavily dependent on the highly seasonal U.S. tax filing period, which creates significant fluctuations in its annual results and can amplify the impact of any operational delays. It also faces ongoing risks from cybersecurity threats and complex regulatory oversight regarding data privacy and the implementation of artificial intelligence within its consumer products.

Industry observers note that oracle contends with fierce competition in the cloud and software industry from giants like Microsoft (MSFT -2.77%) and Amazon. The company deals with a complex supply chain and potential volatility from international operations, including geopolitical instability and trade restrictions that could affect global deployments. Its large-scale transition to a cloud-first strategy requires consistent execution and significant capital spending to avoid losing market share to more specialized infrastructure providers.

With a slightly lower Forward P/E relative to future earnings estimates and a more attractive P/S ratio, Intuit currently trades at a valuation discount relative to Oracle.

Valuation metrics include sourcing from Financial Modeling Prep (FMP) and may differ from other data providers.

I'd go with Intuit. Small businesses build their books, payroll, and payments around its software, which makes it hard to leave and gives Intuit a dependable base to grow from. And it is now beginning to win larger companies. QuickBooks, TurboTax, and Credit Karma are brands many people already know, and its software for mid-sized businesses is growing about 40% year over year.

According to recent reports, what I like is that Intuit keeps returning cash to shareholders, with a dividend that rose 15% over the past year, and it is building AI into its products, so customers can now use QuickBooks inside AI tools like ChatGPT and Claude. Its latest quarter also came in above the organization's own targets, and it expects solid growth this year.

In a recent development, to give Oracle its due, it rents out the computing power AI companies need, and its cloud infrastructure sales have more than doubled year over year. But it is borrowing heavily to build data centers and burning cash while it does, and a significant share of its contracts comes from one customer, OpenAI.

Recent findings suggest that intuit's growth, by contrast, comes with healthy profits and cash returned to shareholders, not a heavy debt load. For long-term investors, a profitable software organization that is expanding into bigger businesses makes Intuit the better buy for 2027.

Recent findings suggest that XBOX controllers have changed considerably during the company’s 25-year history. In fact, XBOX recently released a 25th anniversary console and controller as a throwback to the translucent green days. From the iconic XBOX 360 controller to the sleek peripheral we see today with the XBOX Series X|S versions, each generation has still managed to maintain a familiar feel, despite its evolution.

Industry observers note that however, the most impactful XBOX controller change could be on the horizon, if XBOX goes ahead with a patent filed by Microsoft. It appears that Microsoft has an interest in swappable thumbstick modules, allowing players to replace damaged thumbsticks rather than purchasing an entirely new controller to resolve a thumbstick problem. (Related: Industry News: Hyperparameter Search with Transformers ...)

As spotted by Insider Gaming, the patent was first filed in May 2025 and was published in November 2026. Thanks to the filing being published publicly, we can get a peek into what XBOX could be planning for the future of its hardware.

The Microsoft patent application relates to a game controller thumbstick that pops in and out through the use of a single screw that is secure enough so that it never falls out of the module.

Information provided by Patentlyze indicates that the replaceable input module has three parts. A seating protrusion (a small lip), which hooks into an opening in the controller's side wall, a locating pin on the opposite side neatly slides into a matching hole to line everything up, and fastener that acts as a hole for the screw that pulls the module down into place.

Recent findings suggest that tightening the screw is done to press the flat bottom of the module against a flat surface inside the controller. According to the patent, this mechanism also pushes angled surfaces on the lip against matching angled surfaces on the controller, to keep the module in place.

According to recent reports, if the modules were to be made available to the public, they would have to be easy for everybody to install. The drawings filed show that the process of removing the thumbcap and switching the swappable unit is fairly straightforward. There are no details on whether the replaceable thumbstick will come in different colors for further customization options, or if Microsoft will stick with the default black design. The benefit of this is innovation lies in your thumbstick being damaged, worn out, or you’re experiencing stick drift, and you don’t have to purchase an entirely newly introduced controller or send your controller in to be repaired. Instead, you can simply attach a new thumbstick and continue playing. Depending on its price point, swappable thumbsticks would likely be a welcome option, since most gamers know how expensive it can be to buy a new gamepad.

The patent mainly uses an XBOX game controller as an example, but it mentions keyboards, laptops, and even drone controllers, too.

It’s worth noting that the application was rejected in the examiner's first review, but the reasons are unknown. Usually, this allows the manufacturer to respond or make amendments where needed, so the original filing could be far from the end product.

Industry observers note that it’s unknown whether Microsoft will make use of this patent for its hardware until an official announcement has been made. With Project Helix in the works, who knows, perhaps the next generation of XBOX controllers could feature the ability to swap out controller thumbsticks.

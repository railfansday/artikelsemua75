“We question neither the sincerity nor the reasonableness of the commonwealth’s objections to masked and otherwise-unidentified law enforcement activities, and we emphasize that states may limit or forbid such conduct by their own state and local officers,” remarked U.S. Circuit Judge Toby Heytens, a Joe Biden appointee who authored the opinion. “But just as Congress may not ‘problem orders directly to the states,’ states may not issue orders directly to the federal government.”. U.S. District Judge Robert Payne, a George H.W. Bush appointee, had blocked the laws over the summer after the Democrat-led Virginia General Assembly passed the bills in spring.

Sources indicate that heytens said Payne correctly concluded that the law prohibiting ICE officials from wearing masks violates the intergovernmental immunity doctrine. But Heytens disagreed on Payne’s conclusion that a law restricting 287(g) cooperation agreements violates the contracts clause and the intergovernmental immunity doctrine.

The conditions the law imposes on localities to continue working with ICE include ICE refraining from enforcement actions at schools, churches, and polling places and refraining from using surveillance technology. The law also bars the localities from assisting in immigration operations unless presented with a valid judicial warrant, subpoena or detainer.

“The intergovernmental immunity doctrine does not apply because the 287(g) law neither directly regulates the federal government nor prevents private parties from contracting with it,” Heytens said. “Its arguments get basic federalism principles backward.”.

Heytens pointed to the Supreme Court’s 1992 ruling in *Fresh York v. United States,*where the majority ruled that the federal government could not commandeer the state government to participate in a federal regulatory program. He also cited other Supreme Court cases that prohibit the federal government from forcing state or local officials to enforce federal law.

According to recent reports, “The federal government could not order the commonwealth’s own agents â be they state or local officials â to enforce federal law, much less do so at their own expense,” Heytens wrote. “Just as the commonwealth was allowed to make one choice in the past, it is allowed to make a different choice in the future. Indeed, that is the essence of democratic self-government.”.

According to recent reports, heytens said that, unlike in the cases the federal government cites where a state prevents private parties from contracting with the federal government, the Virginia law concerns local governments. Heytens also said the law doesn’t violate the contract clause because it doesn’t affect existing contracts; instead, it restricts future ones.

“The federal government cannot conscript our local officers into its mass deportation machine, and the General Assembly has every right to set the terms on which our own agencies cooperate,” State Senator Saddam Azlan Salim, a Democrat, remarked in a statement. “Our communities are safer for it, because public safety depends on trust, and trust disappears when a call for help can turn into a deportation.”.

The masking law required ICE officials to wear identifiable items on their clothing and prohibits facial coverings. Heytens agreed with Payne that the state lacks the authority to tell immigration officials how to do their jobs.

Antonio Conte’s recent interview has fuelled speculation of a return to Juventus, but the Bianconeri might have other ideas if Luciano Spalletti leaves.

Conte is undoubtedly part of Juventus’ history, both as a player and as a coach. A serial Serie A champion and a 1996 Champions League winner with the Old Lady, Conte left his mark in Turin also as a coach, winning the first three Scudetti in the post-Calciopoli era. He returned in 2011 as a coach and left in the summer of 2014 after winning three consecutive Serie A titles.

Sources indicate that his exit still hurts some Juventus supporters, as it materialised one day into the 2014-15 pre-season. Juventus and Conte no longer shared the same view, especially on transfer activity, prompting the Italian tactician to step back and paving the way for Massimiliano Allegri’s appointment.

The way it ended, however, upset many Bianconeri supporters, some of whom even started a petition to remove a “star” named after Conte outside the Allianz Stadium after he accepted an offer to become Inter’s coach in 2021.

According to recent reports, over the last 15 years, Conte won Serie A titles with the Nerazzurri and Napoli as well as an FA Cup and a Premier League title with Chelsea. He also coached Tottenham and the Italy national team, remarkably reaching the quarter-finals at the 2016 Euros.

Now, Conte feels the time has come to return to Juventus.

It has been revealed that in his latest interview with Cronache di Spogliatoio, he noted: “I think that sooner or later the circle will close. I know I left something behind, and I know that eventually the circle will close with Juventus.

“There have been moments in the past where we could have reunited, but it’s been difficult because two things have to align. I have to be available, and Juventus have to be looking for a coach who isn’t presently employed.”.

However, as reported by Tuttosport, it remains to be seen whether the Lecce-born coach will fulfil his dream.

Juventus CEO Giovanni Carnevali is not considering sacking Spalletti, who is under contract until 2028, and if Spalletti were to leave at the end of the season, Carnevali would prefer Simone Inzaghi, another ex-Inter coach, over Conte’s return.

Carnevali has often shown his support to Spalletti, including in an interview released in September, after Juventus’ first defeat in 2026-27 against Sassuolo: “Spalletti is an amazing coach, one of the best ever. Changing coaches is not in my nature; we must try to program and build; we must give time and be patient, knowing that we will have to swallow some bitter pills,” remarked the Bianconeri director.

It’s been about a year and a half since rapper and streamer PlaqueboyMax first spoke to Rolling Stone. In that time, the music industry has caught up with the popularity of streamers, as a growing set of major names — from Drake to Mariah Carey — have dabbled in the format. For Max, who has spent years making music in front of viewers, that means being something of an established figure in an evolving rap landscape.

Recent findings suggest that “It’s been crazy,” he says. “I’m really passionate about music, and I’m just happy that I’m able to merge my worlds and my passions together to make it one major world.”.

Max’s recent “Rising Star” competition, something like Star Search or American Idol for the underground rap generation, brought together six rappers with growing fanbases on one livestream where they’d create music and eventually perform for a real audience. The contest has its origins in two of Max’s streaming formats. “In the Booth,” which he started in 2024, lets viewers watch music being made. He was also doing “Song Wars” around then, putting songs up for an audience’s judgment. As he kept doing those streams, he wanted to spend more time with the artists. Sources indicate that “These are concepts where people are making songs and new art is being created live,” he states. “It’s very fascinating. And ‘Rising Star’ is basically a byproduct of these things, but taken even further.”.

According to recent reports, the weekend format lets the audience spend hours with the contestants, who have personalities and frustrations to contend with as they’re making music. Max would like some of that audience to stick with them afterward.

He recruited Lil Yachty, with his years of experience making records and maintaining a career, alongside creators Cuffem and Funny Marco. Music advice from the rapper, entertainment from the creators: Max wanted both on the panel, with enough to keep his viewers watching.

“So I would say picking judges came down [to] just me picking people I appreciate, who I mess with, and just bringing them into my world and making it all one amazing product,” he says.

Yachty had plenty to say. Max remembers him taking five minutes to get into an individual artist’s work, which could make for an uncomfortable critique. (Related: Umamusume horse girls are taking over a Denver Broncos ...)

It has been revealed that “Some of them were getting grilled, but it’s just like he’s giving them real feedback that if they listen, it will help them as an artist, and I love that,” Max notes. “That’s what the purpose was.”.

In a recent development, getting grilled in public can’t be much fun. I ask Max if anybody went away upset about it. Some artists were frustrated with the criticism, he states, or with their placement in the fan vote, but he stops short of calling them offended.

“They were frustrated either with their placements or I’d say sometimes the feedback they were getting, but I wouldn’t say that any artist that was competing took it the wrong way,” he notes. “They were definitely just working hard and just passionate and hungry.”.

Industry observers note that but the thing Max remembers most is the show at the Belasco that closed the weekend. He’d spent all that time watching the artists work, and then got to watch them perform. Sources indicate that “It was just so amazing to see everyone work their ass off all weekend and then come together at the show and just put it all out there on stage,” he says. “I love that.”.

It would be the buzzing underground rapper Matt Proxy who’d ultimately take the prize. “He really went on that stage and gave it his all, and I respect that so much,” Max tells me. Proxy is known for a dynamic style infused with an experimental-leaning sonic maximalism — 808s rip through speakers like sawblades, punctuated by melodic and emotive vocals, with a lyricism rooted in Proxy’s own life experiences. “He has a star on him for sure,” Max claims.Proxy’s winnings amounted to $25,000 and access to Red Bull’s music studio in Los Angeles, after a weekend spent making music and performing with five other contestants. Max was already a fan. He liked the different influences he could hear in Proxy’s music without being able to pin a genre on it. The set at the Belasco gave him more to go on.

“Matt doesn’t belong in any box, he’s just himself, and I really like that,” Max notes. “That’s why I chose him for this because I value creativity greatly.”.

Proxy had come to his attention through a video earlier in the year. By the time Max started researching contestants, he knew the music. This is something he already does with his chat: asks them to send him up-and-coming artists he hasn’t heard. People put him on to what they like, and he listens. “He kind of does things his own way and combines different influences he has to make his own product, and that really fascinates me,” Max says of Proxy.

Industry observers note that with this year’s contest finished, Max is pivoting his attention back to his own music. He’s working on his debut album, Elliot, which he indicates fans can expect soon. As streaming and content creation become a bigger part of the music world, Max says he’s worked all this time to find a balance, splitting his attention between the two. “I love what I do,” he says. “And so it’s just about allocating time to each and staying diligent on that so that I stay up to date and stay moving forward and don’t let anything slip through the cracks.”.

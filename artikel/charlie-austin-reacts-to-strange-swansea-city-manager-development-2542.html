Ex-Premier League and EFL striker Charlie Austin has branded the decision for Vitor Matos to leave Swansea City for Strasbourg as "strange," but does admit that the French club can be a "stepping stone" to bigger things.

The Portuguese boss's departure hasn't been formally unveiled by Swansea just yet, but Strasbourg themselves revealed that he'd joined their side to replace the outgoing Hugo Oliveira.

In the space of a little under a year, Matos took the South Wales side from 20th in the Championship to a top-half finish last season. Then, he took them to the league's summit during the opening stages of this campaign, before a run of four defeats in five saw them slip down to sixth.

Nevertheless, he had Swansea playing an incredibly attractive, high-intensity brand of football, while also being controlled in possession, too, and it was universally agreed that the Swans had their best chances of promotion in around six years under him.

In a recent development, strasbourg currently sit 13th in Ligue 1 with three wins through their opening 10 games. They aren't in Europe, and some critics would say that the challenge of getting a team like Swansea back into the Premier League would be more satisfying than taking over a struggling team in France.

However, Strasbourg are under the BlueCo banner, the multi-club ownership group which mainly oversees Chelsea, and the French top-flight side is often seen as the little sister, and sometimes even a feeder club, to its Premier League siblings.

Speaking on his "Chaz Kicks Off" YouTube podcast show, former Burnley, QPR and Southampton striker Charlie Austin spoke about how now being under that umbrella could open doors for Matos that Swansea simply couldn't.

"The Swansea manager has departed and gone to Strasbourg, which is strange because they're in a good position," he said.

"But he's obviously been promised the world [by Strasbourg]; it's the stepping stone, isn't it? And we've all seen where it could lead. We saw it with that PE teacher who went to Chelsea, et cetera, so it opens up fresh things.".

While a slightly disrespectful way to refer to him, the "PE teacher" that Austin referenced was Liam Rosenior, who was given the Chelsea job back in January following the departure of Enzo Maresca.

The 42-year-old had spent the previous season and a half at Strasbourg, leading them to a seventh-placed finish in his only full year at the club, and left them in seventh midway through the 2025/26 campaign.

In a recent development, at Chelsea, despite a promising start, seeing only Arsenal beat his side for the first two months of his time at the club, things swiftly unravelled, and he was sacked in late April. He's now back in France, and has Paris FC sitting third in Ligue 1, three points off their neighbours PSG atop the table.

Like Matos, Rosenior was in a Championship job before moving to Strasbourg, and was shortlisted for Manager of the Season in his year at Hull City. The two are following a similar path, and the Portuguese boss may have taken the job with that potentially in mind.

Industry observers note that in an ideal world for Chelsea, Xabi Alonso will be a massive success at Stamford Bridge, but if things start to unravel in a year or so, and Matos performs well at Strasbourg, he could be earmarked for that high-profile move. For Swansea, they now have to make sure that their position in the early-season promotion hunt wasn't down to Matos, and look to maintain the form that got them into the top six in the first place.

The Swans have a few big tests coming up to begin life post-Matos, with West Brom travelling to South Wales on Saturday, followed by a midweek trip to Middlesbrough and then the South Wales derby against Cardiff City a week on Sunday.

This decision from Vitor Matos to leave couldn't have come at a worse time, with not long left of the international break, and Swansea fans will be hoping that the disappointment surrounding it can help bring the squad closer together to pull off some big results.

Hopefully then, by the end of the month, Matos' successor will be in place, and they can help finish the good job that the Portuguese boss has started in the early stages of the campaign.

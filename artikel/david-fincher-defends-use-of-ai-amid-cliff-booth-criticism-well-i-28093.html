Although The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth is steeped with nostalgia for Hollywood’s past, David Fincher claims to represent the future.

While many have criticized the Quentin Tarantino-penned sequel to Once Upon a Time in Hollywood (2019) for the apparent onscreen use of Gen AI, the 3x Oscar-nominated director recently shared his thoughts on the rapidly growing tech.

“I’m not a Luddite, so I don’t fear the unknown,” Fincher told the Los Angeles Times.

It has been revealed that “Is everyone satisfied that we have figured this art form out enough that what we really need to do is go back to celluloid?” he continued. “How long have people painted? 40,000 years? We’re talking about a thing that’s a hundred years old and everyone’s going, ‘Stop. Pencils down. No more iterating. Sources indicate that fincher added, “Well, it exists. Should we not use it?”.

The comments come after several fans have taken to social media to criticize the apparent use of AI in the film as “astounding to an absurd degree,” with one viewer saying they “walked out of Cliff Booth completely gutted.”. (Related: The Hidden Social Cost Of Smoking Few People Talk About...)

Set in 1977, eight years after Once Upon a Time…, The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth follows Brad Pitt’s eponymous stuntman, now a projectionist who moonlights as a Hollywood fixer, getting caught up in a conspiracy that involves a Filipino gang and a stolen Oscar.

Premiering Thanksgiving on Imax, The Further Mis-Adventures of Cliff Booth premieres Dec. 23 on Netflix.

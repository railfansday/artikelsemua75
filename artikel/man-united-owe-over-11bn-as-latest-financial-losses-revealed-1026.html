Manchester United owe more than £1.1bn after their long-term debt went up to £577m, according to their latest financial results.

United also owe £207m, including interest, as part of their revolving credit facility and a further £332m in trade liabilities – largely the sums due to other clubs in outstanding amounts for transfer fees.

In a recent development, their net finance costs for a three-month period to the end of September were £14.5m, meaning they will spend almost £60m a year in servicing the debt the club has, as a consequence of the leveraged buyout of the club by the Glazer family in 2005. The club had been debt free before then.

Sources indicate that united expect to their turnover for the current financial year will be between £740m and £760m. For the three-month period that ended on 30 September, their wage bill rose by 8.6 percent to £79.9m – equating to an annual wage bill of almost £320m – which they attributed to Champions League qualification.

For the first quarter of the financial year, they made an operating loss of £3.0m and had total revenues of £146.1m.

The figures do not include the cost of paying off manager Michael Carrick after his sacking, or the compensation required to hire Mauricio Pochettino and his backroom staff, thought to be at least £7.5m.

Recent findings suggest that united spent an initial £148m in the summer on midfielders Carlos Baleba, Youri Tielemans and Andrey Santos, after paying around £220m the previous summer for Matheus Cunha, Bryan Mbeumo, Benjamin Sesko and Senne Lammens. They are also thought to owe money for previous signings and, in 2024, bought Manuel Ugarte, Joshua Zirkzee, Leny Yoro, Matthijs de Ligt and Noussair Mazraoui. (Related: Summer at Hugging Face...)

Chief executive Omar Berrada remarked: “Our revenue remains strong and on track, while we continue to grow sustainably. We are pleased to welcome Mauricio Pochettino and his staff to the club and our priority is to support them as we look continuously to improve our performances on the pitch.

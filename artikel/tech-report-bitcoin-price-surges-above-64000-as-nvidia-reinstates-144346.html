📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The bitcoin price surged to above $64,000 on Monday morning, according to Look Into Bitcoin data, as Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as chief executive and sent stocks sharply higher. Bitcoin is up about $8,500 from its weekend low of roughly $55,100, which came at about 1:17 a.m. ET on Saturday.

It has been revealed that nvidia’s board voted unanimously to bring Huang back as president, CEO and director, effective immediately, the organization noted Monday morning. The board said it spoke with Huang and received input from “senior levels of the United States government,” concluding that his return “poses no national security risk.”.

Huang resigned on Nov. 11 after reports of an affair with a woman U.S. intelligence officials believe is a Chinese spy. CFO Colette Kress took over as interim CEO. The board has stated an investigation into the reported foreign intelligence effort will continue, and Huang said he supports it.

Recent findings suggest that nvidia chair Harvey Jones mentioned the board believes Huang “is the right person to lead” the organization. Huang called it an honor to return and said he “can’t wait to get back to work.” The company also formed a national security and export control committee and is searching for a chief compliance officer.

The stock had lost nearly half its value during a seven-week losing streak, even after Nvidia reported better-than-expected quarterly results on Nov. 17. Investors worried about life without Huang, and bitcoin traded in step with the rout in technology shares.

Nvidia (NVDA) shares were up roughly 31% to near $165 in premarket trading. Shares had tumbled 14% on Friday, closing out that seventh straight weekly decline, after a report that the SEC is examining the company’s accounting. Nasdaq-100 futures were up about 6.6% and S&P 500 futures about 4.2% at 9:21 a.m. ET.

Bitcoin-linked equities rallied with the chipmaker. Coinbase (COIN) was up about 9% to near $111 in premarket trading, and Strategy (MSTR) gained about 16% to near $90.50. Miners with AI data center businesses led the move, with IREN up about 27%, MARA up about 16%, Riot Platforms (RIOT) up about 14% and Core Scientific (CORZ) up about 17%.

Strategy also disclosed in an 8-K that it bought 457 bitcoin last week at an average of about $64,898, lifting its total to 851,305 BTC. The organization’s average cost is about $75,446 per coin, so its stack is still under water at current prices.

Recent findings suggest that the rally follows a brutal stretch. The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.28% on Friday, and the S&P 500 lost 8.61% for the week while the Nasdaq dropped 10.02%. Bitcoin slid with stocks and finished last week down about 6%, its seventh straight red weekly candle.

It has been revealed that spot bitcoin ETFs shed about $1.79 billion last week, their fourth straight week of net outflows, including roughly $669 million on Friday. Lyn Alden captured the gloom on Saturday, posting that “Bitcoin is dead right now. It fell 56%,” before telling followers to check back in two to three years.

Recent findings suggest that treasury Secretary Scott Bessent also warned the Fed on Sunday that it may be making a “policy mistake” if it overtightens. The central bank’s next decision is Dec. 9, and a quarter-point hike is still the base case.

The relief has limits. Nvidia still faces the internal investigation and the SEC inquiry, and the Fed meeting is still ahead. A reversal in chip stocks could rapidly pull bitcoin lower again. (Related: The Reformer - Pushing the limits of language modeling...)

Bitcoin remains nearly 50% below its October 2025 peak above $126,000, and traders will watch whether the Nvidia relief rally holds once regular trading opens.

At the time of writing, the bitcoin price is near $63,700.

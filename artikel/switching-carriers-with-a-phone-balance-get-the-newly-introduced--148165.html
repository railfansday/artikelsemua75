📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Owing on your phone doesn’t tie you to your carrier. Leaving makes the remaining balance due and stops any monthly credits, so pick a new carrier whose switching offer reimburses that balance. Check how long you must stay to keep the money, and if you’re keeping the phone, get it unlocked before you move your number.

Industry observers note that say you owe $480 on your phone, and a $30 monthly credit covers each $30 payment. Staying lets the credits pay it off, while leaving puts all $480 on your old carrier’s final bill. (Related: Industry News: Block Sparse Matrices for Smaller and Fa...)

That $480 is what a switching offer covers. T-Mobile’s Keep and Switch pays back up to $800 a line, and AT&T’s switcher reward card up to $800, once you move your number to a new line. T-Mobile pays only for a phone you bring with three payments made on it, while AT&T wants four payments and also pays if you buy a new phone there.

Both pay on a card after you switch, about 15 days after you send T-Mobile your old bill and 8 to 10 weeks after you upload it to AT&T. AT&T can also take back the full card if you cancel the fresh line within 12 months, half of it through month 24, and a quarter through month 36.

Industry observers note that if a lower monthly bill matters more than the card, compare these offers with a prepaid plan on your current network.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Avelo Airlines' network for the winter 2026/27 season looks rather different to how it did in 2025/26. Most notably, 31 of the routes the carrier served this time last year are not being operated this time around, with 25 domestic and six international. Of the domestic cuts, a third are concentrated at one airport, highlighting the brutally competitive nature of the airline industry in the States.

On the international side, scheduling data from Cirium, an aviation analytics firm, indicates that all of the cuts impact short-haul services to key leisure markets in Mexico and the Caribbean. While the last year has seen Avelo make various route cuts, the airline is in flux, and it has a lot to look forward to fleet-wise with the Embraer E2 set to modernize its ops. Wilmington International Airport (ILM) in North Carolina is a common factor when examining Avelo's winter network versus 12 months ago. Overall, the facility has lost winter service to nine destinations, four of which are in Florida: Fort Lauderdale-Hollywood International Airport (FLL), Southwest Florida International Airport (RSW), Orlando International Airport (MCO), and West Palm Beach. Avelo Airlines' Current Operating Bases.

The remaining five route cuts concerned services from Wilmington to destinations in the northeast of the US. These included major hubs such as Detroit Metropolitan Wayne County Airport (DTW) and Washington Dulles International Airport (IAD), as well as smaller destinations such as Islip, Manchester, and Rochester.

Avelo commented on its presence in Wilmington in a statement obtained earlier this year by Wilmington Biz.

"Wilmington is also not a protected airport. Most of the affected routes cannot handle more than one airline serving them due to demand.".

While Wilmington has been the hardest-hit airport by Avelo's winter cuts, it is far from the only US facility to have lost routes. Elsewhere in North Carolina, the carrier is no longer serving five destinations from Raleigh-Durham International (RDU) that it did this time last year: these are Albany, Fort Myers, Grand Rapids, Manchester, and Rochester. Of course, the cuts extend beyond North Carolina.

For instance, Tweed New Haven Airport (HVN) has lost three winter Avelo Airlines routes: William P. Hobby Airport (HOU), Louis Armstrong International Airport (MSY), and Washington Dulles International Airport (IAD). Wilmington in Delaware (ILG) saw three cuts, namely to Jacksonville (JAX), Nashville (BNA), and Raleigh-Durham, while Nashville itself lost Rochester.

Returning to the state of North Carolina, Concord-Padgett Regional Airport (USA) no longer handles winter Avelo Airlines flights to West Palm Beach. Elsewhere, the American ultra-low-cost carrier also no longer flies from Islip to Lakeland Linder International Airport (LAL) in Florida in the winter. Still, it remains relatively busy, with 1,616 flights scheduled to operate this November. These will offer 300,784 seats.

On the international side, Wilmington International Airport has also lost winter Avelo Airlines flights to Punta Cana International Airport (PUJ) in the Dominican Republic. The carrier has also ceased its winter services to this popular Caribbean leisure destination from Bradley International Airport (BDL) in Windsor Locks, Connecticut, and Raleigh-Durham International Airport (RDU).

Sources indicate that windsor Locks has also lost winter Avelo Airlines flights to Cancún International Airport (CUN) in Mexico, and the carrier has ceased winter service from both Windsor Locks and Raleigh-Durham to Sangster International Airport (MBJ) in Montego Bay, Jamaica. The cessation of these routes marked the end of Avelo Airlines' international flights, which had only commenced operations back in 2024.

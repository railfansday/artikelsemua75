Patrick Stewart‘s final film is set to be Avengers: Doomsday, where he reprises his role as Professor Charles Xavier one last time.

To honor the actor, Marvel’s Kevin Feige revealed they are planning to add a special tribute to the film, set for release on December 18.

“We’re long done filming that movie, and he did an amazing job for us,” the studio head told Entertainment Weekly. “We’re looking now at how we can put something together that honors and respects our friend.”.

Stewart died on October 28 from Alzheimer’s disease and Lewy body dementia and was originally offered the chance to appear in both Avengers: Doomsday and Avengers: Secret Wars, which will be released at the end of 2027. However, the actor opted to do only the first film, EW noted. Feige recalled that on his last day on the set, Stewart “stood up and he gave a speech to the crew.”.

Director Anthony Russo noted, “It was clear that he was struggling with his health, and he worked so hard to deliver a performance and a presence — for us as directors, for his other actors, for the crew.”.

Sources indicate that “You could see it wasn’t just the effort of a regular actor showing up and delivering that kind of presence and performance and experience for others, collaboration,” Anthony Russo continued. “It was someone who was really being challenged in a very deep way on a health level, showing up and doing that. For him to have muscled through and delivered the performance that he did in that movie, and the experience that he delivered for all of us as collaborators, it made it intensely emotional.”.

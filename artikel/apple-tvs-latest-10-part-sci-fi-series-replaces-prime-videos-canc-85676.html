It has been revealed that william Gibson is one of the most influential authors in the sci-fi genre. Often credited for pioneering the cyberpunk subgenre, his most seminal novel, Neuromancer, remains a sci-fi masterpiece in 2026. Despite the timelessness and popularity of his literary work, however, his cyberpunk stories and books have hardly been adapted for the screen.

Recent findings suggest that while many video games, movies, and TV reveals have loosely drawn from his work, there are only a handful of direct mainstream adaptations of his stories. Two of these, Johnny Mnemonic and Latest Rose Hotel, premiered in the '90s and struggled to leave their mark. It has been revealed that most cyberpunk TV shows and movies focus a little too much on the visual aspect, featuring neon-soaked cityscapes and edgy characters. Prime Video's The Peripheral, on the other hand, truly understood the essence of William Gibson's near-future cyberpunk. In its 2032 timeline, the show features a very familiar, economically broken rural America.

Technology is deeply grafted onto the blue-collar lives of regular people. There are 3-D printed pharmaceuticals, while even veterans with disabilities use military implants called "haptics." The series also reveals its central VR technology as a form of escapism for the main characters who place it in a run-down trailer.

Then comes the show's 2099 timeline where it contrasts 2032's "low lives" with London's high-tech and elite. This is when The Peripheral also introduces "The Jackpot," to reveal the high stakes of its apocalyptic conflict and how humanity's unchecked ambition in the future has pushed civilization to the brink of collapse.

In its 8-episode runtime, Prime Video's The Peripheral did enough to earn more follow-ups and expand its sci-fi vision in season 2. It was a rare adaptation that embraced the hard-edged sci-fi and weirdness of its source material. Unfortunately, despite being promising, its renewal was reversed by Prime Video after SAG-AFTRA and Writers Guild of America's strikes in 2023.

Recent findings suggest that the works of many sci-fi authors, like Philip K. Dick, are not easy to adapt. Yet, Philip K. Dick's stories have already received multiple worthy on-screen adaptations. William Gibson's adaptations, in contrast, have almost been cursed. The ones like Johnny Mnemonic became cult classics, but never achieved mainstream acclaim or success.

Industry observers note that meanwhile, The Peripheral had immense potential, but failed to last beyond one season. After being considered "unfilmable" for quite some time, William Gibson's Neuromancer is finally being adapted by Apple TV. And so far, it looks like the show will finally spell redemption for William Gibson's adaptations.

It has been revealed that apple TV's history of bringing challenging adaptations like Isaac Asimov's Foundation to the screen alone is a proof of concept for its ability to successfully translate "unfilmable" sci-fi literature. William Gibson, too, has been directly involved with the show's production, which also heavily favors the adaptation.

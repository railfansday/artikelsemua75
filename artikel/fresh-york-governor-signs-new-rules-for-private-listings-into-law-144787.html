Fresh York Governor signs new rules for private listings into law, Compass praises it

Latest York’s governor has joined the growing lists of state lawmakers enacting laws to govern the use of private listing networks.

On Friday, Governor Kathy Hochul signed Senate Bill S10274, also known as the “Fair and Transparent Real Estate Listings Act,” into law, just seven days after it was delivered to her desk.

The legislation was first introduced in mid-March before going on to pass the Assembly in late May and the state Senate on June 1, 2026. (Related: Latest Update: How to train a newly introduced language...)

The law requires residential listings to be publicly advertised or marketed on platforms that are accessible to the general public. According to the law, this means that the publication, platform or website the listing is advertised on does not require payment or for the consumer to work with a certain brokerage or agent in order to view the property information. Additionally, the law specifies that any licensed real estate broker or agent working with a buyer without limitation to a particular brokerage, franchise, or select group of licensees should also be able to view information about the listing.

Here’s how it will impact real estate professionals and brokerages:.

Despite this emphasis on ensuring listing information is easily accessible, the law does note that sellers who sign a state-provided standardized disclosure form explaining the risks and tradeoffs associated with non-public marketing, may withhold their listing from public marketing. In the disclosure included in the text of the law, sellers must acknowledge that their property “may not be marketed or distributed outside the real estate brokerage that the listing agent is associated with through mass-marketing channels,” and that due to this, the seller understands that the reduced exposure of the property may lead to a reduction in the number of offers they receive, potentially negatively impacting their ability to sell the house.

Recent findings suggest that those who break this law can be fined up to $5,000. In addition, under the law, the Department of State may suspend or revoke a real estate professional’s license. The law is set to go into effect in late May 2027.

Bess Freedman, the CEO of New York City-based Brown Harris Stevens, a RealTrends Verified brokerage,  is celebrating the passage of the law.

“Buying a home shouldn’t depend on who you know or which brokerage you work with. I’m proud that New York is taking on private listing networks and making public marketing the standard,” Freedman wrote in an emailed statement. “Buyers deserve to see what’s available and choose who represents them. Sellers deserve the chance to reach the widest possible audience, and those who want privacy — a choice that has always existed — can still choose it with a clear understanding of what they may give up.”.

In addition to Freedman, Caroline Burton, the general manager of StreetEasy, which is owned by Zillow, is also applauding the news and thanking the state legislature for “putting consumers first.”.

“When homes are kept out of public view, Fresh Yorkers lose out, especially in a market where homes for sale are already scarce,” Burton wrote in an emailed statement. “Finding a home in the most challenging market shouldn’t depend on privilege, who you know or who you work with. In one of the biggest decisions in a person’s life, New Yorkers deserve an open and transparent marketplace so they can make an informed choice. This is a win for all buyers, sellers, and the agents who serve them.”.

Beyond these two firms, which had been vocal proponents of listing transparency, Compass International Holdings, which has been promoting its three-phased marketing proposal, which includes a private listing phase, is also praising the enacting of the law.

In a recent development, A Compass spokesperson told HousingWire that the fresh law “preserves homeowners’ right to choose how they market their homes,” which includes marketing the property through Compass’s See It First initiative. Compass rolled out See It First in September. The manufacturer said it is intended to expand inventory visibility for buyers, allow sellers to “test and learn” without public days on market and provide agents with an additional marketing lever.

“See It First early access homes comply with the new law and continue to give sellers flexibility in how and when they bring their homes to market,” a Compass spokesperson told HousingWire via email.

In enacting this law, Newly introduced York is joining Connecticut, Wisconsin and Washington in creating a law governing the public marketing of residential properties. New York’s law is similar to the ones passed in Connecticut and Wisconsin, both of which provide sellers the ability to opt-out of public marketing with a signed disclosure. The Washington law, however, does not include a pathway for sellers to opt-out of public marketing besides a health or safety concern. These laws are right now in effect in Connecticut and Wisconsin, with Washington’s law slated to go into effect on Jan. 1, 2027.

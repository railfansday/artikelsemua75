Industry observers note that the key isn't just packing more gear, but packing high-quality items that are durable, versatile, and work for your style of camping. That last bit is the most vital. Focus on the things that actually make your experience better. For me, that means an ultra-comfortable sleep setup (with a luxurious portable mattress and a legit camp pillow) that's almost as good as my bed at home, and a solid camp kitchen (with a portable fridge and premium cookware set) because I love to cook. But that changes from the frontcountry to the backcountry.

Depending on how remote you’re going, you should figure out your own preferences for your style of camping. Weight savings is always more crucial when you step up from car camping and into more involved situations in the backcountry.

Weather and climate are always a factor when camping. Not just for what to pack and bring, but for the type of gear you invest in. Are you just a summer camper? Then you can probably get away with three-season camping gear. If you do want to do winter camping, then having two sets of gear (one for warmer and one for colder) might make sense or opting for four-season gear might be the way to go.

Recent findings suggest that there are solo campers all the way to massive group camping excursions, and everything in between. Making sure you have the right size tent for the people you tend to camp with is a first start. Maybe you like solo camping often, but you do a group trip every summer as well. You’ll probably need two tents then, or at least making sure that someone in the group has more of a three-person and up tent. The same thinking should go into your camp kitchen setup.

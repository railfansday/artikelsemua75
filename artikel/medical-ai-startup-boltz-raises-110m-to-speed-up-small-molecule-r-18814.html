Medical AI startup Boltz raises $110M to speed up small molecule research

UPDATED 16:17 EST / NOVEMBER 19 2026.

Boltz PBC, a startup that develops artificial intelligence models for pharmaceutical researchers, has raised $110 million in funding.

Bessemer led the Series A investment. Boltz stated on Wednesday that the fund was joined by a large group of new and returning backers. The consortium included Greycroft, Andreessen Horowitz, Amplify Partners, F-Prime Capital, Hummingbird and others. (Related: Industry News: Anthropic gave $40M to an AI safety grou...)

Small molecules, the building blocks of many drugs, can only neutralize a pathogen after binding to it. As a result, researchers start drug discovery projects by looking for molecules that have a high binding affinity with pathogens. They often review hundreds of thousands of candidate molecules per project. The process can take months and often costs millions of dollars. (Related: Industry News: Judge blocks construction of Trump’s arc...)

It has been revealed that boltz offers an AI model called BoltzMol-1 that speeds up the workflow. It generates binding affinity data by reviewing only a few dozen, particularly promising molecules instead of the hundreds of thousands usually needed. The fewer molecules need to be studied, the less time the analysis takes.

In a recent development, binding affinity evaluations don’t yield drug discoveries right away. Instead, they produce data points called small molecule hits. Those are essentially faint clues that help researchers pinpoint the area where they should focus their efforts.

Boltz staffers asked BoltzMol-1 to look for small molecule hits across 10 “challenging and therapeutically-relevant targets.” Targets are biological building blocks that play a role in disease processes. According to the organization, the model found small molecule hits for six of the targets.

In a recent development, scientists can adapt BoltzMol-1 to project-specific constraints. If a research team has access to a relatively limited number of compounds, it could configure the model to only consider small molecules that can be made from those compounds. When material procurement is not an obstacle, BoltzMol-1 evaluates a much larger number of candidates.

The tech giant also offers a second model called BoltzProt-1. It can generate tens of thousands of protein designs based on data gleaned from small molecule hits. Additionally, BoltzProt-1 automatically reviews each protein to determine if it could be used to develop a upcoming drug.

According to recent reports, usually, AI models evaluate protein designs based on rubrics that provide only an indirect view of their effectiveness. BoltzProt-1 uses more direct metrics. The manufacturer claims that BoltzProt-1’s protein evaluation mechanism demonstrated “higher success” than several competing models during internal tests.

According to recent reports, boltz sells its algorithms via an application programming interface that is billed based on token usage. According to the manufacturer, customers’ token consumption has grown by a factor of 50 since BoltzMol-1 and BoltzProt-1 rolled out in June. Boltz counts six of the world’s 20 largest pharmaceutical companies as clients.

According to recent reports, the tech giant will use its latest funding to grow its headcount and procure more computing infrastructure. The extra hardware should make it easier for Boltz to keep up with leading AI labs such as newly public Anthropic PBC and OpenAI Group. Over the past year, both companies have introduced models that are highly adapted to biology research tasks.

In a recent development, boltz has already started training a latest generation of molecule design algorithms. The firm says that they are “showing substantial improvements” over its current models.

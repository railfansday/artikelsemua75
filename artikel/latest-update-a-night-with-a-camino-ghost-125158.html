In a recent development, we slept surprisingly well last night, even though our room sat directly above a lively bar. People partied and ate dinner late into the night. That is completely normal here, as most businesses close for siesta and do not reopen until after 8:00 PM.

Sources indicate that with the window shutters closed, we blocked out all the street noise. Unfortunately, closing them also trapped the heat inside, making the room quite warm.

This morning, I walked out to the kitchen and opened the tall doors leading to the courtyard to let the cool air in. I made a cup of coffee and ate fresh blueberries with yogurt.

In a recent development, because it was Sunday morning, the street noise was minimal. Listening to the seagulls and mourning doves while sipping coffee was a peaceful way to start the day.

We finally set off at 9:00 AM. Pontevedra is a good-sized city with an intact medieval center, so it took us a little while to walk out of town. Before long, however, we found ourselves back on a quiet country road.

The Camino wound alongside a river before merging onto a paved road. We took the road route, which was poorly marked, but we soon reconnected with the main trail. Along the way, we passed hundreds of pilgrims heading in the opposite direction toward Santiago.

From there, the trail entered the woods and began to climb. It got quite steep for a short stretch before leveling out into a pleasant forest path. We hiked downhill over rocky terrain, passing cool, shaded creeks along the way.

Eventually, we emerged into Pontesampaio, a well-kept medieval village. We wound our way through its narrow alleyways down to a wide river. To cross, we had to wait our turn on a narrow, 300-foot arched stone bridge, letting oncoming cars pass before we walked across.

Once across, we stopped at a local restaurant serving ultra-fresh food. I ordered grilled squid that had just come off the docks that morning, and the restaurant even harvests its own chickens. Best of all, the meal was a fraction of what it would cost back in the States.

After a relaxing siesta lunch, we still had about 7.5 kilometers (4.6 miles) left to hike.

As we walked through several connected medieval neighborhoods, the Camino suddenly split into two routes:.

At first, the split confused me, and seeing the mountain crest ahead was intimidating because we were already hot and tired. Fortunately, we chose the shorter Coastal route, and the trail skirted the side of the mountain rather than going straight over the top.

Industry observers note that however, the descent brought us onto the shoulder of a busy highway. We endured two miserable miles of roaring traffic and exhaust fumes before finally reaching Redondela. (Related: Industry News: Hyperparameter Search with Transformers ...)

Redondela felt a bit rundown in spots, though it features a charming medieval quarter beneath a massive, 100-foot-tall iron train trestle. (Related: How to train a new language model from scratch using Tr...)

We arrived at our apartment at 4:00 PM, completely exhausted. Our host was wonderful and had prepared a special herbal tea for us to soak our feet. The recipe belonged to his grandmother, who was actually the very first pharmacist in Redondela. (Unlike in the States, pharmacists in Spain can prescribe and dispense many medications without a doctor’s visit.).

It has been revealed that while soaking our feet, we drank some mediocre Spanish wine. Because it was Sunday afternoon, almost everything was closed except for a small mini-market with limited options.

Sources indicate that later, we visited a local bakery our host recommended. Hikers always love talking about food—when you are burning thousands of calories a day, you rapidly get tired of trail snacks and start dreaming about town food. We loaded up on a few local treats:.

After the bakery, we wandered through the narrow alleyways of the old city, admiring the elevated rail bridge towering overhead. By 8:00 PM, the town came alive as bars and restaurants reopened after siesta.

Sources indicate that this time, the waitress brought out two small bowls of white noodles soaked in olive oil with bread on the side. The noodles had dark streaks of squid ink running down them, adding a mild, salty seafood flavor.

It has been revealed that exhausted from the long walk, we headed back to the apartment. Our host had preserved the space just as his grandmother left it—it truly felt like she still lived there. We threw the windows wide open to let the cool night air roll in and fell fast asleep, though it felt like Grandma’s presence lingered in the room all night long.

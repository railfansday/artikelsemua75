RB Leipzig do not have the necessary financial power to tempt Everton into selling striker Thierno Barry any time soon.

That is according to former Toffees chief executive Keith Wyness, speaking exclusively to Football Insider, who believes that his former club have nothing to worry about when it comes to the Bundesliga interest.

Leipzig previously made contact over a move for Barry in the summer, and are showing a renewed interest after he has scored six goals in his opening 10 Premier League games this season. (Related: Industry News: Summer at Hugging Face...)

However, Wyness does not believe that the Red Bull-backed side are capable of "threatening" Everton with a lucrative offer.

Barry has become a hugely notable part of David Moyes' project this term, and he remains the only senior striker on Everton's books right now.

Wyness believes that the 24-year-old still has plenty to prove in English football, and does not expect him to depart Merseyside any time soon. (Related: Breaking: Fine-Tune Wav2Vec2 for English ASR in Hugging...)

Everton’s former chief Wyness – who served as CEO at Goodison Park between 2004 and 2009 and now runs a football consultancy advising elite clubs – believes that Leipzig is the only potential move that Barry's agent may "promote" in the coming months.

However, he does not expect a switch to Germany to be something that the Frenchman gives any consideration to at all.

Speaking on the latest edition of Football Insider's Inside Track podcast, Wyness explained Everton's stance on Barry's future.

According to recent reports, "The club that I've heard have been looking, or at least his agent would be trying to promote, is Leipzig. I don't think they're going to threaten Everton in terms of the sort of money they're going to be able to offer," he remarked.

"Look, to me he's still not proven. Yes, he's got some goals. They haven't been classic striker's goals, as many know. A few have been coming in off different parts of the body.

"But listen, they're goals, and we all appreciate it, and those goals have been a big part of Everton's good start to the season.

"But yet to me he's not proven himself as a prolific striker, and it's going to take another at least until Easter, and if he's consistent and he keeps on going until Easter then we're talking about a better valuation for a player.".

Former Manchester United chief scout Mick Brown told Football Insider recently that Everton could yet accept a "huge" offer for Harrison Armstrong in January.

The Toffees blocked a £40million move to Nottingham Forest in the summer, but may yet take the chance to cash in and reinvest the profit into their squad.

Brown believes that Armstrong is one of few sellable assets in Moyes' squad, and also feels that they have ample cover for him in the midfield if he were to depart.

However, the homegrown ace is hugely popular among the fanbase, and it would take an eye-watering offer to tempt Everton to sell.

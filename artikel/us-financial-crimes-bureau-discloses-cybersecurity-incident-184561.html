The Financial Crimes Enforcement Network (FinCEN) disclosed on Tuesday that it’s investigating a “cybersecurity incident.” The bureau, which is part of the Treasury Department, said it’s looking into an “incident that resulted in unauthorized access to a limited subset of its systems.” It hasn’t offered any information about the full scope or source of the breach, but FinCEN has taken some systems offline as a precaution.

The news comes hours after The New York Times reported that the Central Intelligence Agency and Office of the Director of National Security discovered a breach of an open source intelligence system, exposing unclassified information. It’s unclear if the FinCEN breach is related. Americans have been on high alert about security incidents after a series of seemingly unrelated outages earlier this month. FinCEN’s role is to protect the financial system from illegal activity, including by combatting money laundering and terrorism financing. The bureau maintains financial transaction data and distributes it for law enforcement purposes. FinCEN also houses reports of suspicious activity that financial institutions file as part of the Bank Secrecy Act.

It has been revealed that finCEN says in its alert that banks can still file reports about suspicious activity online, but some services may not be immediately available. Filers that need to expedite their reports in the meantime can still call the Financial Institutions Hotline, which it says was not impacted by the disruption.

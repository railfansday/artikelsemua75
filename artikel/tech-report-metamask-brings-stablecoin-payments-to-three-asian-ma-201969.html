MetaMask expands its payment card to Japan, Thailand, and Vietnam for Mastercard transactions.

Cryptocurrency wallet provider MetaMask rolled out its payment card in Japan, Thailand and Vietnam on November 25, allowing customers to spend its dollar-pegged mUSD stablecoin at merchants that accept Mastercard.

Industry observers note that the move gives consumers in the three markets another way to use stablecoins for everyday purchases. Rather than transferring funds to a centralized cryptocurrency exchange before spending them, users can make payments directly through a card linked to their MetaMask wallet. MetaMask Card uses mUSD, a US dollar-pegged stablecoin, to make payments at merchants that accept Mastercard. Consumers can pay online with their virtual card or in stores through Apple Pay and Google Pay. At checkout, the required amount of mUSD is converted into the local currency, such as Japanese yen. (Related: Welcome Gemma - Google’s new open LLM...)

According to recent reports, in Japan, the card is available only as a virtual version and carries no annual fee. ATM cash withdrawals are not supported. Consumers can spend up to $10,000 per transaction and $15,000 per day.

Payments made with an mUSD balance at domestic merchants carry no transaction fee, while purchases at merchants outside the user’s country of residence incur a 1% fee.

To apply, customers must access the Money tab in the MetaMask mobile app, register an email address and phone number, complete identity verification (KYC), and provide proof of residence.

According to recent reports, however, the card does not support direct payments with every cryptocurrency held in MetaMask. People must first convert assets such as USDC into mUSD before adding funds to their MetaMask Money account.

In a recent development, the Japanese cards are issued by New Zealand-based financial services provider Immersve Limited. MetaMask claims its Money account retains a self-custodial structure, allowing users to maintain control of their private keys.

The expansion comes as stablecoin activity continues to grow across Southeast Asia, where digital assets are increasingly used for cross-border payments and transfers.

Chainalysis’s 2026 regional research shows that Thailand and Vietnam, alongside the Philippines, accounted for 14.4% of global small-value peer-to-peer transfers. The report also found that cross-border stablecoin activity exceeded domestic volumes in both countries, reaching $10.4 billion in Thailand and $6.9 billion in Vietnam.

Japan offers a different opportunity. Its developing regulatory framework and growing interest in stablecoins provide another market for MetaMask to test whether crypto wallets can become practical payment tools.

In a recent development, metaMask first tested its payment card in the UK and Europe in August 2024 before launching it to US customers in February 2026. The service has since expanded to the European Economic Area, Canada, and parts of Central and South America.

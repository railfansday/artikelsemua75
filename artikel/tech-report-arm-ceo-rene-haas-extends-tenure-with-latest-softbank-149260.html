Recent findings suggest that arm announced on November 23, 2026 that CEO Rene Haas has a upcoming agreement extending his leadership through 2033 and will become vice chairman of SoftBank Group on December 1, 2026. He will remain Arm CEO as the manufacturer expands from licensing processor technology into supplying its own AI data-center silicon.

SoftBank’s separate personnel notice lists Haas as Board Director, Vice Chairman and confirms that he will continue to lead Arm. The appointment gives him a formal leadership role at Arm’s controlling shareholder while preserving executive continuity at the chip company during its AI infrastructure expansion.

Haas connected the commitment to Arm’s existing direction. “I believe deeply in our people, our strategy and the opportunity ahead,” he mentioned, adding that he was committed to realizing the organization’s full potential for years to come. Haas’s group responsibilities were already growing. SoftBank appointed him CEO of SoftBank Group International in April 2026, while he remained Arm CEO and a SoftBank board member. SBGI oversees certain international subsidiaries, and the role supports coordination across portfolio companies focused on semiconductors and AI, with the stated aim of improving operational efficiency.

Arm’s SEC filing on that appointment described the SBGI position as limited and part-time and stated it was not intended to detract from his Arm responsibilities. The vice-chairmanship adds a senior governance role to that coordination work, while the new agreement establishes long-term continuity in his Arm operating role.

In a recent development, haas became CEO and joined Arm’s board on February 8, 2022, succeeding Simon Segars, the tech giant noted in its appointment announcement. He joined Arm in 2013 and had led its IP Products Group since 2017. Before that, he spent seven years at Nvidia as vice president and general manager of its computing products business.

Arm’s latest shareholder letter reported revenue of $1.443 billion for the quarter ended September 30, 2026, up 27% year over year. License and other revenue rose 50% to $773 million, while data-center royalties doubled. Those figures show both the scale of the licensing operation and the growing importance of AI infrastructure to the business Haas has committed to lead. The letter divides Arm’s business into two areas: the established intellectual-property operation, where partners license Arm technology for their own chips, and the newer silicon business, where Arm designs products for direct delivery to customers. Both use the Arm compute platform and software ecosystem. That extends the significance of the leadership term beyond licensing and into execution of a production-silicon business with customer orders, supply requirements and delivery schedules.

Industry observers note that arm mentioned purchase orders for its Arm AGI CPU from multiple customers extend through calendar 2027, first commercial deliveries are on track for its fiscal fourth quarter of 2027, and its confidence has increased in achieving up to $2 billion of Arm AGI CPU revenue across fiscal 2027 and 2028. The revenue figure is a tech giant target rather than sales already recorded.

Haas is therefore committed to Arm through the first commercial delivery ramp and the period covered by that target. His renewed mandate ties leadership continuity to a demanding operational transition: turning customer commitments into Arm-designed silicon shipments while coordinating more closely with SoftBank’s wider AI and semiconductor portfolio.

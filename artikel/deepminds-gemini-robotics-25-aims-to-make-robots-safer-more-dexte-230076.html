InfoQ Homepage News DeepMind's Gemini Robotics 2.5 Aims to Make Robots Safer, More Dexterous, and Faster Learners.

Nov 25, 2026 2 min read.

Google DeepMind introduced Gemini Robotics 2.5, a vision-language-action (VLA) model designed to improve robots' dexterity, reliability, and learning speed, while enabling safer operation in unstructured real-world environments. (Related: Director of Machine Learning Insights...)

According to DeepMind's vice president and head of robotics Carolina Parada, the model "marks a leap forward in three key areas": dexterity, reliability, and safety. She says its performance has been evaluated across more than 200 tasks, with reported improvements of 30% to 100% in success rates and 20% to 30% in task completion compared with prior versions. Demonstrated capabilities include serving food with tongs, tying a bow on a gift box, and pipetting liquids.

These tasks represent a step toward human-level dexterity by combining a number of skills, including advanced tool use, complex manipulation of deformables, and handling delicate objects with finesse.

The model is designed to work natively with different types of robots, including humanoid platforms such as Apptronik's Apollo 2 and tabletop pick & place systems such as Franka Duo platform.

Industry observers note that parada highlights the industrial trials conducted with Mercedes Benz, in which Apollo 2 humanoid robots were deployed on an existing industrial line, achieving a 96% success rate in tasks involving automotive kitting and sorting mixed metal brackets. In the trial, the Gemini Robotics ER embodied reasoning model enabled the robot to keep track of the workflow over multiple steps and adapt to changeing conditions, while Gemini Robotics 2.5 controlled the robot's hands and arms. Another key aspect highlighted by Parada is Gemini Robotics 2.5's safety features, which include using reasoning guidance to identify and manage physical hazards:.

It has been revealed that much like safety classifiers add a protective layer around Gemini’s responses, this guidance allows the robot to navigate complex environments securely. In our evaluations, this capability boosted safe task completion around obstacles by 33% and around nearby people by 43%.

DeepMind also open-sourced the fresh safety benchmark ASIMOV-jailbreak, containing hundreds of unsafe prompts across dozens of scenes.

Industry observers note that commenting on the announcement on LinkedIn, GEICO director of software Sean P. observed that "the combination of simulation, learning from mistakes, and real-world testing is what will make robots more capable and reliable in everyday tasks".

LightSpeed founder and CEO Biju Kulathakal emphasized that 96% accuracy may not be enough in real contexts involving humans:.

In a recent development, pausing when a person gets close is what gets robots out from behind the fence. And on a real line, customers live in the 4%. Learning from its own mistakes is how that closes.

Releasing an open benchmark for jailbreaks on a VLA model is the part I find most interesting, because a prompt injection that ends in a physical action is a very different failure from a bad chat answer.

He also raised the question of how to handle situations "when the robot refuses the unsafe step but leaves the task half done in a risky state".

As a final note, Gemini Robotics 2.5's development was accelerated using Gemini 4 Argon as a simulation engine, leveraging its robotics research capabilities to generate simulated environments.

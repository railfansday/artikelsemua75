According to recent reports, nvidia (NVDA -3.91%) and Broadcom (AVGO -0.07%) have both taken a beating, with their shares now trading roughly 36% and 39% below their 52-week highs, respectively. But history suggests that corrections don't necessarily mark the end of a bull cycle.

With both companies still benefiting from demand for artificial intelligence (AI) infrastructure, could this sell-off present an opportunity for long-term investors?

In a recent development, nvidia remains at the center of the AI boom, supplying the graphics processing units (GPUs), networking equipment, and software that power the world's largest data centers. That said, the recent weakness in its share price is notable, given that the tech giant continues to deliver exceptional performance.

Industry observers note that in its third-quarter fiscal 2027 results, Nvidia reported revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% year over year. Data center revenue alone reached $107.6 billion, up 110% from the same period a year earlier. (Related: Industry News: Train a Sentence Embedding Model with 1B...)

These results suggest that demand for AI compute infrastructure remains remarkably strong, even as investors become more cautious about how much tech companies are spending.

And while Nvidia stock trades around 36% below its 52-week high, it has eventually recovered to its previous highs each time. In fact, similar drops in 2020 and 2025 were erased within 56 and 82 days, respectively, of their lows.

Industry observers note that specifically, across eight recoveries from declines of at least 20%, Nvidia took a median of around 75 days from its trough to regain its previous high. But some recoveries took considerably longer, particularly following the late 2018 sell-off.

Sources indicate that of course, owning Nvidia stock comes with risks. For one, the company's recent leadership issues have introduced uncertainty, while U.S. export restrictions and competition from custom AI chips could limit some of its future growth. (Related: Course Launch Community Event...)

Despite that, at a forward price-to-earnings (P/E) ratio of just 10 times, Nvidia stock looks modestly priced for a firm still growing revenue at triple-digit rates.

Meanwhile, the next company is focused on custom chips to drive long-term growth.

Sources indicate that broadcom is benefiting from a different part of the AI infrastructure opportunity: custom-designed chips and high-speed networking. This is where the company has established a strong competitive position.

In practice, Broadcom works with hyperscalers to design custom AI chips and supplies networking hardware to connect complex infrastructure for running AI workloads. Here's how that has shown up in the numbers.

Industry observers note that in its third-quarter fiscal 2026 results, Broadcom reported revenue of $29.6 billion, up 86% year over year. More importantly, AI semiconductor revenue reached $16.7 billion, representing 221% growth. Management also projected AI semiconductor revenue of $21.7 billion for the following quarter, an increase of 236% from the previous year.

Right now, Broadcom's shares trade around 39% below their 52-week high. And like Nvidia, they've declined about as much in the past.

Industry observers note that for example, in the 2022 bear market, Broadcom's shares declined by around 36%; by 48% during the COVID-19 crash; and by 40% during the 2025 tech sell-off. Yet the stock eventually recovered from those downturns. In fact, following its 2025 correction, Broadcom stock finished the year with a gain of about 49%.

In a recent development, there are still risks to owning the stock. For one, Broadcom faces competition from other custom chip designers, while a slowdown in hyperscaler spending could affect demand for its AI products. These are reflected in the stock's forward P/E of 17 times, which also looks modest given its strong semiconductor growth and solid free cash flow (FCF).

Nvidia and Broadcom are among the biggest companies in the AI infrastructure boom, but their recent pullbacks raise an notable question: Is today's volatility a good buying opportunity?

In a recent development, both companies have experienced painful corrections, but their underlying businesses continue to benefit from the ongoing AI build-out. Indeed, history indicates that major drawdowns don't necessarily signal the end of a manufacturer's bull case. Ultimately, if earnings continue expanding and AI spending remains strong, today's lower share prices could look like attractive entry points.

As for what comes next, Nvidia could suit value-minded investors, given its lower forward P/E. Broadcom could appeal to those seeking faster AI semiconductor growth and greater upside to analyst targets.

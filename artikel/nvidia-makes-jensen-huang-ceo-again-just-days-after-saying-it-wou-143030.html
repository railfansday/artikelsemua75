To recap, Huang admitted to an affair with a Chinese national which U.S. intelligence reportedly believed could have been a spy. She seemed involved in a scheme to get Nvidia’s chips into China and to bypass current laws restrict that. But Huang has always been in favor of selling chips to China, and many in the AI-adjacent tech world think it’s good policy. It’s certainly good for the bottom lines of companies like Nvidia.

But then reports started coming out about Huang’s additional affairs, specifically with two alleged Nvidia employees, something that seems likely to be in violation of tech giant ethics policies. There is also the matter of what Huang did or didn’t inform Nvidia’s Board of Directors about related to all of this. They’re supposed to represent shareholders, and lying to them is a major no-no. Plus, with Democrats now in Congress, Huang is likely to face all sorts of hearings on Capitol Hill about the whole mess.

Less than a week ago, Huang started lobbying to come back to Nvidia. The Board of Directors at the time noted that possibility was not even under consideration, a stance that was reiterated during another blowout earnings report the same week. Something certainly changed between then and now. Was it just President Trump doubling down on pressuring the Board to bring Huang back? Whether this is actually good news for Nvidia as a company remains to be seen. At least Tim Sweeney will be happy.

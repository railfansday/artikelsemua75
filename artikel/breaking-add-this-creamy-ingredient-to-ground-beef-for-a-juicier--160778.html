Breaking: Add This Creamy Ingredient To Ground Beef For A Juicier Burger With Extra Protein

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Sources indicate that incorporating secret ingredients into your burger patties elevates the dish in unexpected ways. Some components flavor the meat while others enhance the texture. For an add-in that does both — with a delicious underlying creaminess — look no further than yogurt. The dairy works as a ground beef tenderizer, making a burger that's softer and full of moisture. Not to mention, yogurt is an effective enhancement in chicken or turkey patties, too.

It has been revealed that yogurt's contained lactic acid functions as a mild, but nevertheless efficient, tenderizer, lightly marinating the meat. The inclusion of a liquid naturally adds lots of moisture to a burger — you certainly won't have to worry about a disappointingly dry result. Plus, the ingredient lends the burger a lightly creamy mouthfeel.

And if you use Greek yogurt for the task, you'll get a nice dose of added protein, too. Mix in a quarter cup of whole milk Greek yogurt — a quantity suitable for a pound of meat — and you'll up the dish by 5.5 grams of protein. This yogurt's extra fat content will further prevent dryness, all while forming a patty that's not too loose, making a burger rendition that stands out.

Recent findings suggest that working yogurt into your beef patties doesn't require any special technique. You can simply stir the dairy with the beef in a bowl, seasoning with salt and pepper in parallel. Follow by forming the patties as usual, then lightly brush the exterior with oil to prevent unwanted adhesion. Prepare the yogurt burgers using your preferred method, whether you're fond of using a skillet or grilling burgers like a pro. However, if you notice your mixture appears too wet, consider adding a binder — chia seeds are a fantastic option to absorb moisture but keep things tender and juicy; panko breadcrumbs will also absorb excess liquid while keeping the bite of each burger soft. Sources indicate that such a burger tastes delicious with classic seasonings, but don't rule out creative modifications, too. The pairing of yogurt and beef recalls Greek and Middle Eastern cuisine, so consider integrating Eastern Mediterranean seasonings into the build. A Greek burger spin could fold in oregano, lemon, and feta — core flavors of a Greek salad. A kofta-style patty might up the spice, working in earthy cumin, a dash of spicy harissa, and grated garlic into the mix. Alternatively, season the meat in a sweet direction by using simply turmeric and onions; the ingredients will set you up for a wide array of flavorful condiments. Build out the meal with an aromatic herbal yogurt sauce, a bold dose of roasted peppers, or a marinated arugula salad, and you'll make a burger rendition that'll become a frequent part of the rotation.

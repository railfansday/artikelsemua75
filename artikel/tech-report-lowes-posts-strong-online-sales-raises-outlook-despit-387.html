Lowe's on Wednesday posted strong quarterly results and raised its full-year outlook as the organization said it saw strength among professional shoppers.

The organization stated comparable sales grew 1.8% for the fiscal third quarter, driven by a 17.4% increase in online sales and "solid" performance in pro and home services sales. Net sales rose to $22.76 billion.

Because of the strength, Lowe's now remarked it expects full-year sales of roughly $92.5 billion, up from a previous projection of $92 billion. It also expects comparable sales to be up 0.5%, compared with previous guidance of flat.

According to recent reports, CEO Marvin Ellison told CNBC that the company is focused on growing despite economic headwinds and a sluggish housing market. He stated Lowe's posted its sixth straight quarterly comparable sales increase and saw particular strength in appliances, which saw its largest revenue growth in five years.

"We believe irrespective of the macro environment we're in, we're going to take share because there's a lot of share out there that we can go and get, and we're going to be aggressive to go and get that share," Ellison told CNBC.

Lowe's also reported a pretax benefit of approximately $250 million from tariff refunds during the quarter, which contributed a 34-cent boost to earnings per share. Ellison said the tech giant intends to use those funds to enhance the customer experience and bring value to shoppers.

"We are giving those pros what they need," he said. "We're able to improve our fulfillment capabilities and our pro loyalty program.".

According to recent reports, for the quarter ended Oct. 30, Lowe's reported net income of $1.67 billion, or $2.98 per share, compared with $1.62 billion, or $2.88 per share, the year prior.

It has been revealed that the home improvement retailer has previously said it's seeing "pressure" on spending on projects, especially as consumers are stretched thin by inflation and macroeconomic uncertainty. The manufacturer is also battling a slower housing market and higher mortgage rates.

Ellison previously told CNBC he expects consumers to remain cautious through the end of the year.

In a recent development, lowe's executives noted on a call with analysts Wednesday that the tech giant is continuing to battle high fuel costs, which they said accelerated through the third quarter. CFO Brandon Sink said diesel prices are currently around $6 per gallon, up about 15% overall compared to what the company saw in the second quarter.

"So we're trying to continue to manage that, balance that, and we're reflecting that into our Q4 guide," Sink said.

Ellison told CNBC the manufacturer remains optimistic about consumer spending this holiday season and that November sales are in line with expectations so far.

Lowe's competitor Home Depot mentioned earlier this week that it's seeing resilience among professional shoppers and reported comparable sales growth.

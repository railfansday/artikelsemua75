Rival fans and frustrated supporters feel Kobbie Mainoo has forgotten just how bad it's been for Manchester United after his reaction to a 4-0 loss away to Liverpool on Sunday.

Mauricio Pochettino took charge of his first game since replacing Michael Carrick and there had been some optimism of getting a result at Anfield. Such hopes rapidly evaporated after eight minutes when Leny Yoro received a red card for bringing Alexander Isak down, although whether his sending off was justified is up for debate.

What isn't up for debate is the capitulation from Pochettino's men as Dominik Szoboszlai scored from the free-kick Isak won. Three minutes later, Florian Wirtz made it 2-0 before goals from Rio Ngumoha and Victor Munoz in the second half handed the Red Devils their fifth league defeat of the season.

United's defeat is the worst a latest manager has suffered in 100 years, another nightmare record in a season of history being made by the club for all the wrong reasons. Sitting 19th just over a week before December means the possibility of Pochettino's side spending the campaign fighting relegation is becoming increasingly likely.

Mainoo has been one of the very few positives at Old Trafford this season and the young English midfielder firmly believes Yoro's sending off is responsible for the loss on Merseyside. He spoke to Sky Sports after the game and made his stance clear over the red card:. (Related: Breaking: Fine-Tune Wav2Vec2 for English ASR in Hugging...)

In a recent development, "This doesn't define us, you know? I feel like the game has been taken out of our hands early by a red card that I don't think can be a red card.".

It has been revealed that he added: "But we have to come together because the games are coming thick and fast. We can't dwell on it too long, but we have to take the pain and use it to bring us together.".

Industry observers note that upcoming United boss Pochettino hit out at Sam Barrott's decision to send Yoro off and compared the challenge to Jeremy Jacquet's on Bryan Mbeumo just minutes prior. And Mainoo agreed with his coach's verdict:. (Related: Industry News: Hyperparameter Search with Transformers ...)

It has been revealed that "No chance. The ball's already with the keeper, they're both running full speed, what can he do? But it is what it is. It's 10 vs 11. When you come to a place like Anfield it's already a tough game. You lose a man and it makes it hard. But I can't fault the boys for their fight in the game right until the end. We just have to take the pain and move on with it.".

United's problems extend further than a defeat to rivals Liverpool and Yoro's sending off means he'll miss Brentford's visit to Old Trafford on Saturday. After shipping four at Anfield, they have now conceded more goals (34) than any club across Europe's top five leagues and have just eight points from 11 league games.

Some rival fans have interpreted Mainoo to be considering the defeat to Liverpool an anomaly and were quick to point out United's results so far this season. With just one win in 11 top-flight games, many disagree that this game doesn't define Pochettino's strugglers.

In a recent development, A Liverpool fan responded: "When City went down to 10 men early against Utd away, they conceded just 0.8 xG for the rest of the game. United conceded 4.6 xG today.".

Another member of the Kop fanbase added: "Brother you've only won a single game since the league started in August we're almost hitting December.".

One supporter agreed with this take: "Course it doesn't define ya, being 19th after 11 games does lil Bro.".

Even an Aston Villa fan weighed in: "Talking like it’s a one off, what about the other 15 games you’ve played this season.".

Recent findings suggest that A United supporter also took concern with Mainoo's comments: "Bro do your talking on the pitch. Too many of these players have fat lot to say outside of the pitch but nothing to show on it.".

That last response sums up how United fans have felt throughout the disastrous start to the season. Many of the players who are letting their team down have chosen to either air their grievances publicly or send out repetitive apologetic social media posts.

It has been revealed that it's hard not to sympathise with Mainoo, who is playing for his boyhood club and only wants to see them battle their way out of such a precarious situation. He unfortunately finds himself in a squad that CEO Omar Berrada feels has improved since the summer, but sitting 19th in the league with Christmas on the horizon surely suggests otherwise.

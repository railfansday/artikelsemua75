Microsoft refreshes Copilot app search for owners without an AI subscription

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Meanwhile, Google's homepage demo leaves a familiar button out of the picture.

It has been revealed that microsoft is updating search in its Microsoft 365 Copilot app for users without a Copilot license, with a toggle to restore the classic interface. Google, meanwhile, is giving AI searches more prominence on its homepage, where the familiar Search button is absent from a newly demonstrated layout. Sources indicate that microsoft's change is rolling out now and will hit Deferred Release by mid-January 2027. It will impact users with a Microsoft 365 license who do not have a Copilot license and "provides modern search controls to help users find, refine, and navigate search results more easily.". (Related: Tech Report: Deploy Hugging Face models easily with Ama...)

Recent findings suggest that it's enabled by default, which is a shame since administrators would likely prefer an opt-in rather than opt-out approach, and users can switch back to the "classic search experience" via a toggle on the search results page. Microsoft has not specified how long this toggle will remain available. Its separate Copilot Search experience had a fallback toggle scheduled for removal 60 days after general availability.

"With the updated search experience, owners can continue to find files using keywords, use modern search controls to refine and navigate results, and search supported connected third-party content when their organization has configured connected sources," Microsoft remarked.

The results will still only display content to which people have permission. Microsoft advises administrators to brief users and help desks on the changed interface and the option to switch back.

Microsoft's search tweaks arrive as the future of the Google Search button on Google's desktop homepage looks uncertain.

In a post, Google VP of Product Robby Stein described a collapsible sidebar that lists consumers' recent AI conversations, pushing AI deeper into the search experience. Stein called it "a much simpler way to pick up where you left off," and remarked it would help customers "easily find & jump back into your recent AI searches.".

However, something else was obvious in Stein's post: the Google Search button was not present.

Stein explained in August that Google had performed a "small test on desktop to help people find new things they can do with Search." Part of that test was the removal of the Google Search button.

Recent findings suggest that users can still submit queries by pressing Enter. Stein noted the test would have "no impact to how the core search works.".

The sidebar rollout doesn't itself confirm the Search button's permanent retirement. For now, its absence from Stein's demonstration is another sign of which features Google wants people to notice. ®.

Many people are able to pay off their mortgages ahead of retirement. But don't assume that your housing costs will be negligible just because you're mortgage-free. Your home will continue to require maintenance. And as it ages, you might need to sink more money into repairs that can be surprisingly expensive.

There's also the possibility that you may eventually need to modify your home to make it safer and more accessible as you age. Installing grab bars, adding better lighting, or creating wider doorways to accommodate a walker could all require additional spending.

In a recent development, to avoid a shock, maintain a cash emergency fund outside of your IRA or 401(k), which may be more heavily invested in stocks and other assets whose value can fluctuate. Home repairs in particular are often things that can't wait, so having cash on hand to cover them is key. You may also want to maintain a separate account for eventual renovations if your goal is to age in place.

Once you retire, you may have more time to spend money on hobbies, travel, dining out, and social activities. Some of these expenses, like newly introduced subscriptions, might seem relatively small individually, but they can add up over time. And if you want to travel a lot, that's something your budget will need to accommodate.

It's important to create a realistic entertainment budget that reflects the retirement lifestyle you actually want. If you're looking to go overseas three times a year, work that into your financial initiative instead of winging it on the spot.

You may also want to take some strategic steps to make your desired lifestyle possible. Working an extra year or two to boost savings could help, as could delaying your Social Security benefits for larger monthly checks.

According to recent reports, you might assume that Medicare will cover most of your healthcare needs as you get older. But Medicare generally doesn't cover ongoing custodial care, such as assistance with bathing, dressing, eating, and other everyday activities. That means you could be responsible for a substantial portion of the cost, if not all of it, if you eventually need in-home assistance, assisted living, or nursing home care.

Research long-term-care insurance, hybrid life insurance policies with long-term-care benefits, and other potential funding options. Also think about what type of care you'd prefer as you get older and whether your retirement savings can support those expenses.

In a recent development, ultimately, retirement planning isn't just about having enough money to stop working. It's about having enough money to handle the unexpected while still enjoying the lifestyle you've worked so hard to achieve. Preparing for these three expenses in advance could help prevent unwanted financial stress later.

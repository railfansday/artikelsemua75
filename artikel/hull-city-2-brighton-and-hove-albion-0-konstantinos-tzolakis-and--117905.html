For a most of Sunday's game it looked like hanging on for a draw against outstanding opponents might be the best Hull could hope for and thanks to the brilliance of goalkeeper Konstantinos Tzolakis, that was a real possibility.

But two counter-attack goals from Oli McBurnie saw them to a first Premier League win since August.

If there was much to marvel at in Brighton's football, the same could be stated for the Tigers' mentality.

Brilliantly coached, stable and with a clear method that means everyone knows their job and that of the players around them, the Seagulls are a class act, and it was no surprise they put Hull under real pressure in the first half in particular.

But they do not have the monopoly on shrewd signings, and Tzolakis – plus a bit of luck – kept them at bay until McBurnie was ready to make his decisive late contribution,.

Having changed formation to a 4-2-3-1, Hull started with good intent, McBurnie winning the ball in the final third inside two minutes, but left winger Mohamed-Ali Cho shot wide from a tight angle.

When Cho robbed Lewis Dunk, it was McBurnie unable to score.

Kept in the side after Matt Crooks’ recall, French youth international Cho did not have a great day, criticised by his manager for not working hard enough defensively and substituted at half-time.

As the game went on, Brighton turned the screw and really should have scored before the break.

Tzolakis, signed from Olympiacos for £20m, was called upon to make his first top save in the 15th minute as Brighton began to move the ball around with speed and accuracy.

Diego Gomez arrived onto a ball in from Maxim de Cuyper, but found the goalkeeper in the way. (Related: The Age of Machine Learning As Code Has Arrived...)

Minutes later Nobel Mendy had to charge down a shot from former Leeds United striker Georginio Rutter. When a 20th-minute free-kick was headed back from far too much space at the far post, Costinha really ought to have hit the target.

Semi Ajayi was pressed into giving the ball up promptly after, but left-back Elliot Stroud got a boot on Gomez's shot to deflect it wide.

Tzolakis saved a Rutter shot with his feet, although had he not, the video assistant referee might well have been checking for an offside.

Yasin Ayari hit the frame of the goal after 37 minutes and when Rutter pounced on the rebound, Tzolakis’ left hand denied him.

Until an audacious flick from Crooks forced Bart Verbruggen to save, Hull only hinted at moments on the counter-attack. Mohamed Belloumi had Verbruggen down to a low free-kick in the 40th minute.

Gomez missed the best chance of the half at the end of it when an Olivier Boscagli went all the way through to him at the far post but he dragged his shot beyond the near.

That came after Gomez picked Pascal Gross out in far too much space in the inside-right pocket, and he turned and force another save.

Tzolakis also saved from Dunk when Mendy's headed clearance picked him out, but the centre-back's shot lacked the power to trouble a goalkeeper in such good form.

Brighton were never quite the same force after the break, the question increasingly became whether Hull could find a ruthless moment on the counter-attack.

Belloumi came inside in the 50th minute but Verbruggen got down well again, then when Regan Slater brilliantly released the winger, his shot rippled the outside of the net.

A minute later half-time substitute Sorba Thomas looked to have lost the ball as he stumbled, but recovered only to mishit his cross. It kissed the crossbar.

Slater ought to have made the decisive mistake in the 79th minute when his poor pass picked out Charalampos Kostoulas but got back to brilliantly head the shot dinked over Tzolakis off the line.

Thomas was a real menace, and Costinha was sent off for fouling him in the 63rd and 89th minute.

Slater's measured pass to Lucas Gourna was gorgeous and although it looked like McBurnie had made a mess of it, Hull do not give up easily and the striker forced it home.

His next goal, as Hull broke down the right again, was more routine, Belloumi squaring to him in space for a simple tap-in.

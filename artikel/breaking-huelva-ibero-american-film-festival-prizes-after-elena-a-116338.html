Chief International Correspondent.

Spain’s Huelva Ibero-American Film Festival, one of Europe’s foremost Latin American cinema platforms, prized two of the region’s cinema greats, awarding top prizes to Chilean actor Alfredo Castro and Argentine writer-director Lucía Puenzo on Saturday night.

Recent findings suggest that “La Bola Negra” also reaped yet another plaudit, its scribe-helmer-producer duo Javier Calvo and Javier Ambrossi – Los Javis – scoring Best Spanish Direction. Huelva’s prizes highlight the festival’s focus under director Manuel H. Martín on crossover films, often mixing genre-bending and social challenges to surprising effect. Top winner “After Elena.” from Chile’s Shawn Garry, turns on mourning but is a comedy; Lucía Puenzo’s “Pepita, the Gunslinger” is a gangster movie but the gangster is a woman who battles corruption. “Funk,” which won Duda Santos best actress, is a musical but “revolutionary,” claims director Aly Muritiba, in its centering “Black joy.”. The 2026 awards also may be the gayest in Huelva’s history, with nearly half going to LGBT movies in a year where Variety has asked whether the 2026-27 Oscars season is their gayest ever.

The double nods for the Shawn Garry directed “After Elena”(“Después de Elena”) also underscore what Huelva does best: Highlighting titles that deserve further recognition which in the case of Latin America includes most of its movies, given they are under-covered by the international media.

A TIFF world premiere sold by Meikincine and lead produced by Gabriela Sandoval out of her Cine Matriz label, “After Elena” is not just a study of grief but also a family dynamics comedy with the children of Roberto, 70, moving into his home to supposedly to look after him but also to get their hands on his wife and their mother Elena’s inheritance.

Sluiced by a playful irony, “After Elena” took home Huelva’s Golden Columbus and Best Actor for Castro, once described by Variety as “reliably superb” though here acting against type, abandoning the “heightened performances I’m used to” for “strict, grounded realism,” he told Variety.

In a recent development, lucía Puenzo, the celebrated Argentine auteur (“XXY”) and pioneer of Latin American premium TV (“La Jauría,” “Señorita 89”) scored Best Director for “Pepita, the Gunslinger,” which reflects one of the things she does best: Wrapping gender-agenda issues in engrossing thriller narratives. Here Luisana Lopilato (“In Love and in Hate”) plays the real-life Margarita Di Tullio who rebelled against the deaths of fellow sex workers in 1980s Mar del Plata by founding her own brothel and nightlife business empire.

It has been revealed that “La Bola Negra’s” Best Spanish Direction Award in Huelva’s Acento Cine Español section marks the latest recognition from Los Javis’ native Spain, which scored the San Sebastián Festival Audience Award and has grossed a massive €40.4 million ($44.4 million) through Nov. 22, passing “Spider-Man: Brand Fresh Day” to become the highest-grossing movie of any nationality in Spain his year, according to Rentrak Spain.

Allan Deberton’s “Gugu’s World,” Brazil’s crowdpleasing International Feature Oscar entry nabbed Huelva’s Audience Award, adding to other kudos such as a Berlin Generation Plus Crystal Bear and Guadalajara Maguey Best Film Award. Spain’s Rubén Ochandiano (“Broken Embraces”) scooped Best Supporting Actor for his performance as a musician who galvanizes the protagonist’s life in Mexican Lorena Villarreal’s “Life Is” (“La Vida Es”).

Elsewhere, Huelva’s awards spotlighted disparate talents to track. Sharon Kleinberg’s “I Am Mario” walked off with Huelva’s Special Jury Prize. It turns on Mario, a forty-year-old trans taxi driver who faces an unexpected pregnancy which fulfils his dream to become a father but shatters some of his beliefs about masculinity. “I Am Mario” also won at Guadalajara Film Fest’s Maguey section , scooping its Jury Award in April.

Best Actress was won by Brazil’s Duda Santos the skyrocketing star of telenovelas “Garota do Momento” and “A Nobreza do Amor” who plays in Aly Muritiba’s “Funk” a sassy, sexually frank funk singer MC Sabrina whose on-stage persona hides a fragility exposed during her pursuit of fame.

Recent findings suggest that santos already shared with MC Nem a Tribeca 2026 Special Jury Mention for Best Performance in an International Feature. Huelva can now claim it was one of the first festivals in the world to recognise and prize Santos.

Huelva Ibero-American Film Festival 2026 Awards.

Golden Colombus, Best Feature Film: “After Elena,” directed by Shawn Garr.

Special Jury Prize: “I am Mario,” directed by Sharon Kleiberg.

Best Director: Lucía Puenzo for “Pepita, the Gunslinger” (“Pepita, la pistolera”).

Best Actor: Alfredo Castro, for “After Elena”.

Best Actress: Duda Santos, for “Funk”.

Best Supporting Actor: Rubén Ochandiano, for “Life Is”.

Best Spanish Direction: Javier Calvo, Javier Ambrossi for “La Bola Negra”.

Silver Columbus Audience Award: “Gugu’s World,” directed by Allan Deberton.

City of Huelva Honorary Award: Paulina García.

Silver Columbus, Best Andalusian Direction: Elías Pérez for “Plus Ultra”.

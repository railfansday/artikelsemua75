Recent findings suggest that wes Gordon, global creative director of Carolina Herrera, and Daniel Kaner, a cofounder of the hair care brand Oribe, stopped by the PMC Studio in Midtown Manhattan to chat with WWD editor in chief Jenny B. Fine about how they effortlessly integrate art into their personal and professional lives. The conversation took place during the inaugural Art Week NYC, a citywide festival of arts programming hosted by ARTnews, Art in America, and Artforum.

Despite being from different generations, Kaner and Gordon have a lot in common. They are both from Minnesota, both now live near each other in Connecticut, and both are deeply enmeshed in the art world. Gordon spoke about his experience with art almost reverentially, referring to art as his “safe space” as a child and the happiest 45 minutes of his school day. He went on to study art at Central St. Martins in London and eventually debuted his own line in Upcoming York. Lena Dunham wore one of his designs to her first Met Gala in 2012, and it was through Dunham that Gordon met her mother, photographer Laurie Simmons. He and Simmons traded clothing for art, which is how Gordon ended up with the first real piece of art in his collection: a photo from Simmons’s “Love Doll” series, a piece he states he will have forever.

At Carolina Herrera, Gordon has continued to champion women artists and feminine creative energy; the brand’s fall 2026 ready to wear collection featured many artists as models, among them Amy Sherald, Ming Smith, Eliza Douglas, and Anh Duong, and the 2027 resort collection was an homage to Georgia O’Keeffe’s color palette and personal style. Kaner is so committed to creating a “beautiful, interesting world” that the sign on his office door says “art project,” not “Oribe.” To hear him talk about art, it’s clear he thinks of himself as an artist as much as, if not more than, a businessperson.

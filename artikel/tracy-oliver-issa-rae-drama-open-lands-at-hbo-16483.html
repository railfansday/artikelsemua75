EXCLUSIVE: In a competitive situation, HBO has landed Tracy Oliver and Issa Rae‘s provocative drama Open for development.

The deal, which is stated to include penalty, caps a lengthy sales process that started in August when the open marriage drama was taken out by Paramount Television Studios, quickly garnering seven offers.

The field was eventually narrowed down to three suitors, HBO, where Rae has a long history, PTVS sibling Paramount+, and Peacock, where Oliver has a Bel-Air spinoff in the works. (Related: See where votes are being counted in Senate and House m...)

Interestingly, HBO and Paramount+ both competed for Open up until the Oct. 6 completion of the Paramount-Warner Bros. Discovery merger, which saw them become corporate siblings at the combined Skydance company, with HBO/HBO Max Content chief Casey Bloys and his team taking charge of both. HBO stayed in the race alongside Peacock, eventually winning the project. Open is co-written by Rae and Oliver a decade and a half after they worked together on Rae’s Web series Awkward Black Girl, which inspired Rae’s acclaimed HBO comedy Insecure. In Open, when a married couple who did everything “right” opens their marriage to save it, the decision sends shockwaves through their Houston friend group, igniting affairs, exposing secrets and forcing everyone to confront the lies holding their relationships together.

Industry observers note that oliver will serve as showrunner on the potential series, which she executive produces alongside Rae and Jax Clark via Rae’s Hoorae banner, E. Brian Dobbins for Artists First and David Becky and Jonathan Berry for 3 Arts Entertainment.

Sources indicate that open stems from the first-look deal Rae has with PTVS, which will serve as co-studio alongside HBO. In early 2027, PTVS is slated to merge with the larger Warner Bros. Television Group into Warner Bros. Television Studios.

In a recent development, the deal extends Rae’s streak of every series she has created/executive produced to date being on HBO or HBO Max, this being her first drama. She is writer and executive producer on HBO’s untitled Male Friendship comedy pilot, starring Shane Paul McGhie and Dasan Frazier.

Rae co-created, headlined and showran HBO’s Insecure, which earned 14 Emmys and a Peabody over its five-season run. She went on to executive produce HBO’s A Black Lady Sketch Show, on which she also performed, and HBO Max’s Rap Sh!t. Rae is repped by CAA, 3 Arts Entertainment, and Hansen, Jacobson, Teller.

It has been revealed that oliver is writing and executive producing Hilary, a Bel-Air spinoff headlined by Coco Jones as Hilary Banks, which is in development at Peacock. She also is co-writing with Scott King a romantic comedy movie for Universal, with Keke Palmer eyed to star.

With the 2017 Girls Trip, Oliver became the first Black woman to write a film that grossed more than $100 million. In TV, she created and exec produced the series First Wives Club and Harlem. Oliver also has in the works The Wedding Date, a movie for Netflix based on Jasmine Guillory’s book, Girls Trip 2, The Blackening 2, the holiday film Deck the Y’alls starring Octavia Spencer and Jessica Chastain as well as thriller drama series Paper Gods at Netflix and Not a Happy Family at Starz. She is repped by CAA, Artists First and Myman Greenspan Fox.

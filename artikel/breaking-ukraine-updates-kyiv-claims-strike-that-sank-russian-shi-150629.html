German Chancellor Friedrich Merz stated not allowing Ukraine intoNATO in 2008 was a mistake.

At the2008 summit in Bucharest, Romania, German Chancellor Angela Merkel and French President Nicolas Sarkozy argued that they weren't ready to extend NATO membership to Ukraine.

In a recent development, US President George W. Bush mentioned he was in favor of having Ukraine enter into NATO.

But both Germany and France mentioned that that would hurt relations with Russian President Vladimir Putin.

The NATO summit then decided that Ukraine would eventually join the alliance, but offered no timetable.

At a press conference on Monday, Merz was asked about whether he thinks the time was right to talk about Ukraine's membership in NATO.

To which Merz said, "If it [Ukraine] had become a member of NATO, this war could probably have been prevented.".

Merz meant Russia's war against Ukraine when he stated "this war" at the press conference after a meeting with NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte.

Angela Merkel defended her decision at the Bucharest summit as of 2024 as well, saying a large proportion of the Ukrainian population had opposed joining NATO at the time.

German Chancellor Friedrich Merz called on Russia to end its "dangerous provocations" after two Russian missiles and a jet-powered drone crashed in Poland on Friday.

Merz delivered those remarks after having met with NATO Secretary General Mark Rutte in Berlin on Monday.

"Poland can count on our full solidarity. And if necessary, NATO will of course defend every inch of allied territory," Merz mentioned.

Merz mentioned Europe and NATO must strengthen their defenses.

Drones have repeatedly strayed into NATO airspace in the year since Russia introduced its full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022.

The incident also came at a time as Poland was hosting a two-week military exercise by Ukraine's allies.

The European Commission has proposed a new package of sanctions against Russia, laid out by Commission President Ursula von der Leyen.

The package, the 22nd of its kind, targets financial institutions and crypto platforms, as well as electricity companies.

It would also ban EU citizens from working for companies in Russia as well as travel bans on ex-combatants.

At the same time, the Commission is proposing a ban on Russian athletes and sports teams from competing within the bloc. Such bans already exist in many major sports competitions, having been implemented by the event organisers rather than the EU.

"Sports events cannot be a forum for propaganda activities. Today, for any sports events in the EU, we are introducing bans on Russian teams and athletes competing under the Russian flag," Von der Leyen mentioned.

The Russian Foreign Ministry accused Estonia of letting Ukraine use its airspace "on the quiet" to carry out attacks on Russia.

Moscow summoned the Estonian charge d'affaires, issuing a formal protest in what it called a provocative move and remarked the Baltic country was risking open war.

Estonia has denied the accusation.

A Russian cargo ship was hit in a drone attack in the Baltic Sea on Saturday, with Finland saying that Ukraine was likely behind the attack.

Speaking at the NATO parliamentary session in Berlin, NATO chief Mark Rutte said there was still no consensus on Ukraine being admitted to the alliance, something that Kyiv has long called for to protect itself from future Russian aggression.

Rutte noted there was "no unanimity" despite Ukraine being granted the "perspective" of potential membership.

He did go on to say that membership could be a chip used in negotiations with Russia. "But the problem speculating about those talks is: To speculate about talks, we need talks," he added.

Some 353,000 homes have been left without power after Russia attacked four energy facilities repeatedly in Ukraine's Dnipropetrovsk region, the private energy company DTEK reported in a post on Telegram.

The company remarked repairs were ongoing, but with priority being given to critical infrastructure facilities.

Russia has repeatedly targeted Ukrainian energy facilities, especially with the cold of winter approaching.

The alliance chief, Mark Rutte, remarked they were still assessing whether this was an intentional act by Moscow or not.

Sources indicate that "It is ​clear ‌that this is a dangerous incident. It ‌comes from Russia," Rutte mentioned.

Following a drone attack on a Russian cargo ship over the weekend, Moscow has warned against a an "escalation fo tension" in the region.

In a recent development, "It is indeed a very dangerous trend, the expansion of terrorist activity in the Baltic region... This is very dangerous. It is, of course, leading to an escalation of tensions in the region," Kremlin spokesman Dmitry Peskov mentioned.

Finland stated the cargo ship strike had been carried out by Ukraine.

Peskov declined to comment on the drone strike that sunk a Russian ship off the coast of Libya which Ukraine claimed responsibility for on Monday.

The ‌Novoshakhtinsk in the Russian state of Rostov has suspended its operations after a barrage of attacks overnight, the state governort Yuri Slyusar remarked on Telegram.

Slyusar stated the attack, which involved both drones and missiles, lasted over six hours.

He added that emergency services were working to contain the aftermath.

Ukraine has repeatedly targeted Russian refineries as a means to hamper the Russian economy.

The Novoshakhtinsk refinery lies close to Ukraine's eastern border with Russia. It mainly refines oil for export and has a capacity of 100,000 barrels per day.

The UN Support Mission in Libya (UNSMIL) has expressed concern over the Ukrainian attack that sunk a Russian ship off the coast of Libya, saying in a post on X: "Libya’s sovereignty, territorial integrity and the security of its citizens and assets must be fully respected.".

"UNSMIL calls for an immediate, independent and transparent investigation to establish the facts and hold those responsible to account," it added.

Libya has been split between two competing powers claiming to be the sovereign government of the country for several years.

"UNSMIL calls on all actors to act responsibly to preserve the country’s stability and build on the hard-won gains in the political process towards a durable settlement," the statement said.

Germany is preparing for a war it hopes will never happen. The Bundeswehr has begun testing single-use — so-called 'kamikaze drones' — and AI-assisted targeting technology during its largest military exercise yet in Lithuania, close to Russia's ally Belarus.

The drills come as Germany builds up a permanent combat brigade of around 5,000 troops on NATO's eastern flank. But after years of debate over armed drones, is the German military adapting fast enough to the lessons of Russia's war in Ukraine?

Defense journalist Thomas Wiegold, who observed the exercises in Lithuania, tells DW that Western militaries have been slow to integrate drones. He explains how AI and software are changing warfare, and why Germany's deterrence depends on the readiness of its troops.

The governor of Russia's Perm region, Dmitry Makhonin, said one person was killed in a Ukrainian drone attack that hit and damaged several industrial sites.

Two others were injured by shrapnel, Makhonin remarked in a post on Telegram.

The governor said 56 drones had been shot down. It wasn't clear how many drones were involved in the attack.

Perm lies some 1,700 km (1,050 miles) from Ukrainian-held territory and is home to an oil refinery that has repeatedly come under attack.

Ukraine on Monday claimed attacks on Russian vessels off the coast of Libya, with President Volodymyr Zelenskyy lauding the nation's defense forces for the "exemplary Ukrainian operation.".

In a recent development, "Our warriors deployed seven Ukrainian-made naval drones. Despite all the enemy's efforts to counter the attack, the Russian corvette Merkury was sunk. It's one of their newest ships, commissioned in 2023, during the full-scale war," Zelenskyy remarked in a post on X.

Recent findings suggest that "A Ka-27 combat helicopter was also destroyed along with the corvette. Our second target was the Russian dry cargo vessel MV ANGARA, which the enemy used to transport weapons. Result: target hit," he said.

This comes a day after a Russian vessel, docked at the Libyan port of Benghazi, was attacked by a suspected drone.

Zelenskyy further said Ukraine was continuing "operations in designated Waters," adding that it was in response to Russia's aggressions.

Oracle Stock Just Closed at Its Lowest Price Since April 2024. Is It a Buy Now?

Oracle (ORCL -2.92%) stock finished Wednesday, Nov. 18, at $113.89, its lowest close since April 30, 2024. Shares are now around 65% below their record close of $328.33 in September 2025, and they're off about 42% so far in 2026.

According to recent reports, that price is even under what Oracle's latest shareholders paid. On Nov. 2, the cloud giant priced 86.2 million new shares at $116 each. After underwriting fees, Oracle kept around $114.59 a share. (Related: My Journey to a serverless transformers pipeline on Goo...)

It has been revealed that still, the business keeps speeding up. Oracle expects revenue to grow 30% to 34% year over year in its fiscal second quarter, which ends later this month.

But I think the profit side of that forecast matters more for the stock, and it doesn't look as strong.

In a recent development, showing how fast its cloud infrastructure business is ramping, Oracle's revenue climbed 30% year over year to $19.3 billion in the fiscal first quarter of 2027 (the three months ended Aug. 31, 2026). That was an acceleration from the 21% and 22% growth in the two quarters before it -- and from just 14% in the quarter before that. Cloud infrastructure revenue alone surged 121% to $7.4 billion.

It has been revealed that also, Oracle booked over $30 billion in new artificial intelligence (AI) cloud contracts during fiscal Q1. That raised its remaining performance obligations (contracted revenue it hasn't recognized yet) to $664 billion.

The fiscal Q2 guidance calls for that rate to hold or even pick up.

Earnings, however, aren't expected to keep up.

According to recent reports, over the past three reported quarters, Oracle's non-GAAP (adjusted) earnings per share climbed about as fast as revenue did. It rose 21% in fiscal Q3, 20% in fiscal Q4 (excluding one-time investment gains), and 30% in fiscal Q1.

The fiscal Q2 forecast breaks that pattern. Management sees earnings per share between $1.85 and $1.93 on an adjusted basis. When a one-time gain from selling Oracle's stake in chipmaker Ampere is taken out of the year-ago quarter, that's growth of 21% to 25%, or around 9 percentage points behind the revenue guide.

It has been revealed that the gap probably comes from costs that show up before the revenue does. Oracle's depreciation more than doubled to $3.2 billion in fiscal Q1 as new data centers came online. Interest expense rose 55% as its borrowing grew. And the $20 billion of stock Oracle sold under an at-the-market program that quarter spreads its earnings across more shares.

Sure, Oracle's long-term plan already allows for some of this. At its Oct. 28 investor day, management targeted a non-GAAP operating margin of 40% or higher in fiscal 2030, a little under the 42% it hit in fiscal Q1. But it still calls for at least $21 a share in adjusted earnings that year. Getting there from the $8.10 Oracle sees this fiscal year would take earnings growth of around 37% a year.

In a recent development, demand has to hold up, too. Bloomberg reported on Nov. 6 that Microsoft is phasing out a 2023 deal to rent AI computing capacity from Oracle, reportedly because the servers had older graphics chips. Oracle said demand for its AI infrastructure is still strong, older chips included.

Recent findings suggest that at roughly $114, Oracle shares trade at about 14 times this fiscal year's expected earnings. That uses the $8.10 in adjusted earnings per share management is guiding for in the year ending May 31, 2027. For comparison, the S&P 500 is priced at about 18 times the earnings its companies are expected to make in the next 12 months. That's a discount to the average substantial organization, though Oracle's $8.10 target is about 19% above fiscal 2026's adjusted earnings per share, excluding one-time gains.

Sources indicate that when I covered Oracle's latest funding cap in late October, the stock was around $145, or about 18 times the same expected earnings. I called that a fair price. Oracle reaffirmed its $8.10 target at its investor day, and the shares have since dropped about 21%.

It has been revealed that does that make Oracle stock worth buying now? I think it's worth a look, even with the heaviest spending still ahead. Management expects capital expenditures to peak this fiscal year and next, and it doesn't see positive free cash flow until the second half of fiscal 2028.

Sources indicate that fiscal Q2 still needs to show earnings growth in the 21%-to-25% range, and a miss might drive shares down. But at this price, I think the stock already allows profits to trail sales for a while.

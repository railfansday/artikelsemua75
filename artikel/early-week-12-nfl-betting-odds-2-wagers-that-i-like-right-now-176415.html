The Week 12 NFL slate has plenty of marquee matchups due to the Thanksgiving holiday. This year we have a Wednesday night "Thanksgiving Eve" game, the three Thanksgiving Day games and the third year of the Black Friday game as well. With games on three different weekdays, we are seeing some wrinkles in the calendar that we've never seen before.

Sources indicate that the Packers are at the Rams on Wednesday night, and both teams are on nine days rest after a bye week in Week 11 into an early week game. The Thanksgiving teams all have three off days between games; on Friday, the Denver Broncos and Pittsburgh Steelers are teams with four rest days. There are no bye weeks this week, as all 32 teams are active. In the 16 scheduled games, only two of them feature teams that played last Sunday and will play this Sunday. This is the week of rest advantages and disadvantages, and hopefully there can be some angles to exploit where the market is not appropriately pricing these manual adjustments.

Here are two early-week NFL Week 12 wagers I like right now.

Using market ratings — a reverse engineering of the closing lines of previous games — to forecast upcoming lines, I would assume this line was Broncos -3.1. Normally, a 0.6-point gap is not that significant, but relative to the NFL key number of -3 — the most important number in sports betting — it certainly is. The manual adjustment worth making to this matchup given the schedule spot also has me downgrading the Steelers. (Related: What Is Considered Upper Middle Class? Income and Net W...)

Aaron Rodgers is the oldest active player in the NFL at 42 years old, and he plays the most notable position as a quarterback. But the age dilemma extends to other key players on the Steelers. Cameron Hayward, a star defensive lineman, is 37 and in his 16th season. Defensive back Jalen Ramsey is 32 and in his 11th season. Defensive end TJ Watt is also 32 and playing in his 10th season. The rest and recovery for these Steelers focal points presents a key disadvantage not present for the Broncos.

While a veteran coaching staff is on both sides of the ball, this is only the second short week game for the Steelers under Mike McCarthy. The first was a Thursday Night Football loss to the Cleveland Browns. One-game sample sizes really don't matter much to me, but the approach and institutional knowledge of how to handle and what to expect on short weeks does. The game prep time is different, the practice schedule is different, the workout schedule changes, meetings for positional groups change, etc. There is a lot that goes into weekly prepping for an NFL game and the short week throws a wrench into formulaic aims.

Denver is in its fourth season with Sean Payton as head coach and likely has a lot more foundational knowledge on how to handle these situations.

I think the Broncos' game preparation will be sharper, and their bodies may be fresher. We also have seen Denver steadily improving while Pittsburgh has been regressing from its hot start. This week the first bet in for me was the Broncos moneyline.

Industry observers note that the Colts will turn to newly-minted starting QB Anthony Richardson in this game after releasing Daniel Jones. The change sparked a lot of negative PR for the Colts organization, as they could have traded Jones at the trade deadline just a week before cutting him. Given the reports of the offers that came in, nothing was particularly moving and it's probably a negligible difference.

Recent findings suggest that the one thing I still believe in for the Colts franchise is having a strong offensive mind leading the team in coach Shane Steichen. While he may not be with the franchise next year, he is very likely to get quickly rehired elsewhere because he is a great play-caller.

With nine days off between games, the Colts made the QB change at the right time. This allows ample opportunity to prepare and install a friendly game proposal around Richardson's strengths. He is obviously very mobile, but what is most underestimated about his game is his arm strength.

According to recent reports, steichen has already shown the ability to build a great offense around a similar archetype quarterback dating back to his days with the Eagles and a young Jalen Hurts. I think Richardson is going to be slinging this downfield far more often than Jones, and the chance for explosive plays by the Colts goes way up. This likely means faster drives than Jones, who would methodically try and plod the Colts offense down field before too frequently settling for a field goal. Explosive plays can generate instant touchdowns or scoring opportunities in a flash.

On the Fresh York side, the run game has come full circle. The Giants struggled to identify their running back, benched Cam Skattebo for a game after a crucial fumble, tried to trade for reinforcements at the deadline and ultimately have gone back to Skattebo. Allowing backup QB Jameis Winston to run the team's tush-push QB sneaks has also proven incredibly effective. Malik Nabers has played himself into full health, and Isaiah Likely has fully blossomed into a huge receiving threat. The Giants offense is buzzing as well right now.

I like the over in this game, as I believe the run game is peaking for the Giants, and the explosive play rate is going to jump for the Colts.

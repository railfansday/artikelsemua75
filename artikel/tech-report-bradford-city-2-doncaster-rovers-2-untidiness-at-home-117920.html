In the club's last two league games at Valley Parade, derbies against Huddersfield Town and McCann's Doncaster Rovers, there has been some disorder at home again.

After letting in three goals in uncharacteristic fashion against Town, City's famed defence was opened up twice in Saturday's final quarter.

The first, like Town's opener, was a majestic overhead kick which you have to doff your cap to - Jordan Thomas, take a bow. The second, in the fifth minute of stoppage time, was a different matter as Rovers helped themselves to a second equaliser in a finale which was as lively as the opening hour was eminently forgettable.

McCann’s side needed a spark. The game definitely got one when George Broadbent clashed with Bantams rival Kieran Morgan in the second half, the prelude to a brief melee among players and kerfuffle between both benches. The crowd got involved and it became a proper derby.

Duff, cautioned alongside McCann, remarked: "I've been doing that with Grant for 30 years. We used to have arguments on PlayStation games, when we were 20 years old and who's going to walk the dog? And we'd kick lumps out of each other in training.

"So that's just two blokes who want to win. I think that's why we're still in a job. We're both nearly 50 years old. You look back, it's pathetic, really.".

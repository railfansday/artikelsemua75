For those of us in Britain who care about democracy, November 20 marked a disturbing political development that Americans should take notice of. Prime Minister Andy Burnham traveled to Brussels for the U.K.-EU Summit — a renegotiation of Britain’s trading terms with the EU. Standing alongside the European Union’s chief bureaucrats, Burnham confirmed a series of deals — concentrating on food standards, youth mobility between Britain and the EU, and carbon and energy trading — that will erect fresh trade barriers between the U.K. and the United States.

In June 2016, over 17 million Britons voted to leave the European Union and take back control of our lawmaking abilities from Brussels. The principle of parliamentary sovereignty — the foundation of the British constitution, which holds that Parliament is the supreme lawmaking authority in the land — had been eroded during our membership in the EU as we increasingly outsourced decision-making to unelected foreign bureaucrats.

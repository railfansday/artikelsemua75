With that in mind, the example of starting somewhat small with reliable dividend growth stocks, including tobacco behemoth Altria (MO +0.25%), is worth examining.

According to recent reports, to be fair, $5,000 isn't a small amount of money, but it's not enough to retire on, either. However, it is a solid starting point for investors seeking a fair amount of income from Altria.

Here are the particulars on $5,000 worth of Altria shares. This Dividend King, or one of the companies with a dividend increase of at least 50 consecutive years, yields 5.8%, as of Thursday, Nov. 19. That's nearly 80 basis points above the yield on 10-year Treasuries. Based on the tobacco stock's closing price of $74.68 on Nov. 19, $5,000 buys 67 shares (I slightly rounded up there). Factor in the $4.44 per share annual dividend, and a $5,000 stake in Altria today generates nearly $297.50 in annual income.

According to recent reports, that's not too shabby. Call it $300 and Altria's dividend, assuming it's not being reinvested, could cover a couple of months of phone bills or a nice dinner out. On the other hand, $300 isn't an earth-shattering amount of money, suggesting that investors should maintain perspective on Altria's income-generating capabilities.

NYSE · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

According to recent reports, that $300 in annual Altria payouts from a $5,000 stake is, well, today's figure. It doesn't account for dividend growth, something this consumer staples stock is a master of, as highlighted by its 57-year run of rising payouts. Put another way, investors buying Altria today get a $4.44-per-share annual dividend, but there's a solid chance that figure will be higher in 2027, 2028, and beyond.

In a recent development, altria offers cash-strapped investors other reasons to get off the sidelines. Not only does the stock yield more than 10-year Treasuries, but its dividend yield is more than 5x that of the S&P 500.

In a recent development, next, there's the matter of inflation. Take a leap of faith and assume/hope that the Federal Reserve's interest rate hikes are enough to drive inflation down to the central bank's stated target of 2%. Or assume the worst and say that the Personal Consumer Expenditures (PCE) Price Index remains stuck at 3.6% as it read in September.

In either case, Altria's five- and 10-year dividend growth rates of 4.3% and 6.4%, respectively, vanquish inflation, and that's a good thing.

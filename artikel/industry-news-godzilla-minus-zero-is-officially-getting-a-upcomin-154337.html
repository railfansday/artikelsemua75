Sources indicate that seventy-two years after Godzilla first emerged from the waters off the coast of Odo Island, Takashi Yamazaki has given the King of the Monsters two of his most celebrated movies. The Oscar-winning filmmaker has a tremendous appreciation for Ishirō Honda’s 1954 original, something that became especially apparent when he revisited Godzilla Minus One for its black-and-white theatrical release in 2024. Seeing Godzilla emerge from Tokyo Bay in monochrome was about as close as modern audiences could get to experiencing the monster the way Japanese moviegoers did seven decades earlier. Yamazaki’s sequel has already enjoyed a historic theatrical run of its own. Godzilla Minus Zero opened to $30.6 million domestically, setting a record for a live-action foreign-language movie in North America. With its worldwide box office now estimated at over $110 million, the sequel is getting another opportunity to bring Godzilla fans back to theaters before the Christmas blockbuster season gets underway. Recent findings suggest that according to a Regal listing, Godzilla Minus Zero/Minus Color is scheduled for a theatrical release on Friday, December 11. The black-and-white edition is listed with the same two-hour-and-15-minute runtime as the original, with Japanese audio and subtitles. Toho and GKIDS have yet to formally announce the release or confirm how long the alternate version will be playing in theaters.

According to recent reports, when Yamazaki worked on Godzilla Minus One/Minus Color, he and his colorist individually adjusted the contrast of different elements within each shot. Turning the movie monochrome required making new creative decisions about everything from the visibility of Godzilla’s scales to the lighting on the actors’ faces. Yamazaki previously described the resulting version as “way scarier” than its color counterpart. It'll be interesting to see how that same approach affects Minus Zero, especially given the considerably larger scale of its kaiju encounters. Yamazaki’s sequel already draws heavily from the franchise’s Showa-era history, and seeing his latest monsters rendered in black and white should be an absolute treat for longtime Godzilla fans.

Sources indicate that the December 11 release also gives the sequel a week's head start before Avengers: Doomsday and Dune: Part Three open on December 18. For anyone who missed the original monochrome theatrical run of Minus One, which lasted just one week in January 2024, this is another opportunity to experience Yamazaki’s Godzilla on the big screen in a format that pays homage to the franchise's beginnings.

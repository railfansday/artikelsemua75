CNBC reported on November 23, 2026, that Democratic and Republican lawmakers criticized Nvidia’s decision to reinstate Jensen Huang as president and CEO, renewing demands for oversight of AI chip exports and the Trump administration’s role. The backlash followed Huang’s return 11 days after his resignation.

Nvidia confirmed that Huang’s reinstatement was effective immediately after a unanimous board vote, and that he had also returned as a director. Harvey Jones remains board chair, and Colette Kress continues as chief financial officer after serving as interim CEO. (Related: Director of Machine Learning Insights...)

Recent findings suggest that the board remarked a series of discussions and meetings left it satisfied with Huang’s candor and ability to lead. It also received input from senior levels of the U.S. government and, based on that input, is confident that his reinstatement poses no national-security risk.

According to recent reports, representative Don Bacon, the Nebraska Republican who chairs the House Subcommittee on Cyber, Information Technologies, and Innovation, told CNBC he wants thorough oversight in the next Congress and an examination of whether Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick bears responsibility. Bacon called the decision inexplicable and a “failure of character.” The Commerce Department is responsible for setting U.S. export controls on AI chips. Senator Ron Wyden of Oregon, the Senate Finance Committee’s top Democrat, said in an emailed statement to CNBC that Huang’s return revealed national security was for sale under Trump, Vance and Rubio, and that Democrats would pursue accountability for administration officials who put corrupt deals ahead of the country’s security.

Republican Senator Rick Scott of Florida requested a congressional briefing as soon as possible. He told CNBC that Nvidia’s board was free to vote as it chose, but the federal government “owes the American people answers about what actually happened,” including the full impact of Huang’s actions on national security and the economy.

Industry observers note that representative Gabe Amo, a Rhode Island Democrat on the House Foreign Affairs Committee, told CNBC the reinstatement could broaden the inquiry to Lutnick and Cantor Fitzgerald, the financial firm run by Lutnick’s sons. Cantor Fitzgerald declined to comment directly on Amo’s remarks. The firm remarked it held no material proprietary position in Nvidia and that its Nvidia positions arose primarily from equity swaps executed for clients.

David Rudenstine, a constitutional law professor and national security scholar at Cardozo School of Law, told CNBC that primary responsibility for national security rests with the administration rather than the CEO of a private tech giant.

Industry observers note that the board’s vote did not end Nvidia’s internal review. Nvidia said the independent-director committee’s investigation will proceed to conclusion, with independent counsel assisting. The committee is examining reporting that Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort; Huang supports the investigation and is committed to cooperating with it.

Recent findings suggest that the board also formed a National Security and Export Control Committee composed solely of independent directors to oversee trade compliance and national-security risks. Nvidia is commencing a search for a chief compliance officer to oversee compliance across the manufacturer and report to the Nominating and Corporate Governance Committee. Nvidia said it would continue cooperating with ongoing government investigations and inquiries.

Senator Elizabeth Warren of Massachusetts, the top Democrat on the Senate Banking Committee, noted the decision did not remove the obligation of Huang, Nvidia executives and the board to answer Congress’s questions about the firm’s compliance with U.S. export controls.

Republican Senator John Cornyn of Texas separately questioned the basis for saying Huang posed no national-security risk. Democratic Representative Raja Krishnamoorthi of Illinois mentioned neither the government nor Nvidia’s board had completed its investigation, and that the information Chinese intelligence may have obtained remained unknown.

According to recent reports, the Senate Banking Committee’s hearing notice lists Huang as the witness for Protecting America’s AI Advantage: Oversight of U.S. Export Controls, scheduled for December 3, 2026, at 10:00 a.m. The full committee session will be open and hybrid.

The hearing sets up a public examination of the central gap between the board’s position and Congress’s demands: Nvidia cites senior government input for its conclusion that Huang’s return poses no national-security risk, while lawmakers are still seeking the basis for that assurance and answers on chip-export compliance.

Even six decades into his Hollywood career, Steven Spielberg is still synonymous with a brand of movie magic and spectacle that very few can rival. And when he decides to direct a new popcorn blockbuster like “Disclosure Day,” you know you’re in for a few dazzling sequences.

One of the most exciting moments in the film comes when a train collides into a car with stars Josh O’Connor and Emily Blunt close at hand. The sequence tested every department, from camera and production design to visual effects. And during IndieWire’s recent Craft Roundtables, VFX Supervisor Matthew Butler explained the unique challenge of incorporating subtle VFX work into a scene where so much happens in camera. Recent findings suggest that but while Spielberg’s preferences were clear, Butler clarified that blockbusters on this scale rarely come down to a binary choice between practical effects and VFX. The biggest scenes often contain elements of both, as the pairing between in-camera human acting and cutting edge post-production is what leads to movie magic.

Industry observers note that “When you combine some of the live action blue screen work, clearly that’s your lead actor and actress there, and your stunt work where it’s like ‘Oh my God, that looks really real’ and the stuff that you just can’t do that’s just too dangerous … that’s where visual effects came in,” he said. “But I wouldn’t say it was a visual effects sequence. It was a sequence that contained all of those, and it came together in the right way.”.

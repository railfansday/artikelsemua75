President Donald Trump suggested Saturday that there could be a bright spot in the latest incursion into the FBI computer database.

The president confirmed in an interview with Fox News that a system has been breached that houses information used by law enforcement at all levels to share criminal justice information. (Related: University launches $5m smart system that checks who is...)

Sources indicate that the hackers accessed the National Criminal Information Center (NCIC), a database that includes FBI fingerprint records, wanted persons, violent felons, missing persons and related information, two people familiar with the matter told MS NOW. The database is restricted to law enforcement use but not classified and it is unclear what data was extracted.

CNN had first reported on the suspected hack, citing sources familiar with the glitch.

In a wide-ranging interview on Fox, Trump mentioned there could be upside from the news.

In a recent development, "Maybe we'll learn about the voter fraud, all the voter fraud that took place," he noted. "I always like to put things on a positive spin.". (Related: Breaking: AI for Game Development: Creating a Farming G...)

In a recent development, this would be the second recent intrusion into the FBI system; the previous one came in September, when a group known as "ShinyHunters" broke into an FBI jobs website. The result compromised data on current and former FBI employees and shut down part of the agency's operations until the glitch was resolved.

Trump noted it's possible there could be information gleaned from the latest hack.

It has been revealed that "I wonder who's doing it, because wouldn't it be great to learn about Russia, Russia, Russia?" he said. "People should hear about it, because it actually put our country in danger, and so maybe we'll learn things that we wouldn't have learned otherwise.".

The FBI did not immediately respond to a CNBC request for comment.

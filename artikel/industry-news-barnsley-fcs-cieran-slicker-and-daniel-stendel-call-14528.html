Fans group Tykes Union are encouraging supporters to gather outside the ground before kick-off to protest against the running of the club, with a chant planned four minutes into the game to mark the number of years the current regime has been in charge.

But with 5,700 away fans expected to make up a third of the crowd and much more concentrated in the away end, Slicker notes their Barnsley counterparts have an key part to play, just as they did when the young team battled to win a point against Oxford United despite a ninth-minute red card for Jack Shepherd.

"Whether it's the home crowd, the away crowd, I think there's a buzz from both. There might be a few beer bottles up at my head or whatever but I thrive off that (atmosphere) and I'm excited for being at both ends of the pitch. (Related: See where votes are being counted in Senate and House m...)

"The crowd is there, you feel it but in reality, it doesn't change my focus.

"You could have no people watching, you could be training, but still as focused as what I will be whenever really.

"You might hear more and even the communication might be different but I think it's still focused, even more focused maybe.

It has been revealed that "Communication as a goalkeeper is one of the most notable aspects. I'm going to try and communicate whether the boys hear it or not.

"They might be quite glad that they're not hearing my voice.".

Stendel is without the suspended Cameron McGeehan and Luca Connell is a doubt with illness, so he hopes the fans can compensate.

Sources indicate that "What I would like is that we have a significant support in the game," he says. "Not just because it's a derby but also these young guys, without the captain (McGeehan), need the push from the stands.

Sources indicate that "It was really positive in the last big game against Oxford here. With ten men after nine minutes, they pushed the team so much.".

This is Slicker's first South Yorkshire derby, and he is fully aware of its importance.

"When I first joined, looking at the stadium, I walked around sort of imagining it full,” he recalls.

"There's a lot of (Yorkshire) derbies in League One but Sheffield Wednesday will be the major one – one of, if not the biggest, game of my career.

Industry observers note that "I played for Scotland internationally. This will probably be a similar level to it.".

If a win would do wonders for Barnsley's belief after nine games without a league victory, wins at Oakwell are special for away teams who bring a substantial following.

It has been revealed that "In terms of an away stand, for a big club, it's a brilliant one," comments Sheffield Wednesday manager Dave Challinor. "That end will be full, it won't be half in one bit, half in the other.

"It's a huge stand. We've got to make the biggest advantage of that we can.".

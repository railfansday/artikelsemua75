Recent findings suggest that early Unix was available through inexpensive source-code licenses because a 1956 antitrust settlement kept AT&T out of the commercial computer business [6]. Some early licenses cost just $99 [1].

CSRG decided to rewrite AT&T's tools and C library. With help from outside contributors, most of the code was rewritten in eighteen months.

With most of AT&T's Unix code removed, a completely free BSD Unix was finally released in June 1991. The source was still available on tape for the $1,000 copying fee, or as a free download by FTP.

Industry observers note that CSRG founded a organization called BSDi to sell BSD Unix commercially. The University of California also began selling BSD Unix in the early 1990s. An advertisement claimed it was 95% cheaper than AT&T Unix. That did not go down well with AT&T.

In April 1992, AT&T subsidiary USL sued BSDi. UC Berkeley was added to the lawsuit later.

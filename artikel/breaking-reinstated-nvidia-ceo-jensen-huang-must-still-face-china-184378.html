In a recent development, even though Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as its CEO earlier this week under pressure from President Donald Trump, members of the U.S. House of Representatives and the U.S. Senate say that he must still appear before them and testify as they dive into their respective investigations. The Select Committee on China posted on X that the Nvidia CEO and the organization’s general counsel, Tim Teter, are set to face the committee on December 1 and 2, respectively, while Bloomberg reports that Jensen will publicly testify before the powerful U.S. Senate Committee on Banking, Housing, and Urban Affairs on December 3.

In a recent development, the chairpersons of the House Committees on China, Foreign Affairs, and Intelligence first called upon the Nvidia CEO on November 7 for a closed-door meeting to discuss the allegations that he had an affair with a suspected Chinese spy, which lawmakers say “raise significant national security concerns.” Just a day later, Jensen admitted to having an affair, although he did not mention the name of the third party. On the same day that the Nvidia board reinstated Mr. Huang, Senate Banking Committee Chairperson Tim Scott remarked that Jensen will appear before the committee and publicly testify during a hearing about U.S. export controls on advanced AI hardware.

One of the biggest complications that Jensen Huang is facing is the numerous reports of chip diversion involving Nvidia’s products. The U.S. first put export controls on Nvidia H100 chips in 2022 as it feared that China was using it for military purposes. However, there have been rumors that you could easily get these chips in China as long as you know who to ask as early as 2023, even though the Nvidia CEO answered, “There’s no evidence of any chip diversion,” when asked about it one time. Furthermore, Alice Huang, one of the people that he had an affair with, was allegedly instrumental in securing over $7.5 billion worth of illicit Nvidia GPU imports to China. Jensen Huang himself is known to be against export controls and has called them a failure.

In a recent development, the Select Committee on China’s meeting with the Nvidia CEO will be conducted privately. Although there is going to be a transcription of the interview, it’s unclear if and when the data will be released publicly. On the other hand, the Senate Banking Committee hearing is open to the public and will be broadcast live and online, which will also be the first time that Jensen Huang will face a formal congressional hearing.

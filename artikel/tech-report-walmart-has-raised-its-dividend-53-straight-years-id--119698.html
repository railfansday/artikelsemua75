In a recent development, walmart (WMT +1.51%) had a good morning on Thursday, Nov. 19. Sales and profit topped Wall Street's estimates, and the tech giant raised its full-year forecast. Then the stock fell about 2% in early trading anyway.

I've learned to love earnings days like this. A dip on good news is often the cheapest way into a company that has raised its dividend for 53 straight years. This move of 50 consecutive dividend raises places Walmart in the coveted Dividend King category. These are companies that have raised their payouts for 50 years or more.

NASDAQ · As of 4:00 PM ET 11/20/26.

CEO John Furner summed up the quarter in one line: "The strength of our omnichannel model gives us room to do even more.". (Related: How to train a new language model from scratch using Tr...)

What he means is that Walmart's stores are no longer just places you walk into to make a purchase. They have become launchpads for online orders, and more than a third of the orders filled from stores reached shoppers in under three hours. That speed is winning new kinds of customers, too, as Walmart stated its share gains were led by upper-income households. (Related: Industry News: How we sped up transformer inference 100...)

CFO John David Rainey said the quarter also brought Walmart's biggest market share gains in food in three years. "We continue to gain share while continuing to improve the profitability of our digital businesses," he told CNBC.

It has been revealed that the part I find most exciting doesn't involve a shopping cart at all. Walmart's U.S. advertising business grew 37%, its membership fee revenue rose 17% worldwide, and sales on its online marketplace, where outside sellers pay to reach Walmart's shoppers, jumped 49%.

Industry observers note that brands pay to show up where millions of people shop, and members pay a yearly fee whether they buy a TV or a gallon of milk. That's how Walmart can keep cutting prices and still grow profit, and it's what makes the dividend feel so durable to me.

Then there's Sparky, Walmart's AI shopping assistant. Its U.S. usage has more than doubled from a year ago, and Furner noted shoppers who use it build baskets about 40% bigger on average. Shopping may increasingly start with a question typed into an app instead of a walk down an aisle, and Walmart wants to be the one answering it.

Recent findings suggest that the newest piece is a $120-a-year bundle of Walmart+ and Sam's Club Plus. Furner told CNBC it has brought about 2 million new people into Sam's Club since it released in late October, and Rainey said bundle members are "spending appreciably more." There's a catch, though: Walmart expects the bundle to weigh on Sam's Club profit growth until late 2027.

My reason is a practical one. Walmart's next dividend goes to shareholders of record on Dec. 11, so you need to own shares by the ex-dividend date. Walmart has also repurchased $7.8 billion of its own stock so far this fiscal year, so the dividend isn't the only way it returns cash. The holiday season is arriving, too, and Rainey mentioned he's "optimistic" about holiday spending, adding that the fourth quarter is "off to a pretty good start.".

Here's what gives me pause. Diesel prices are climbing. "The reality is that increases in diesel fuel and transportation, they do work their way into the cost of goods," Furner told CNBC, adding that "we'll keep prices as low as we can.".

U.S. same-store sales growth also slowed to 2.9%, from 4.5% a year ago. Rainey stated Walmart is keeping "flexibility" in its forecast, and the stock already trades at about 46 times earnings with a dividend yield under 1%. That's a rich P/E ratio for a store, and the yield won't make anyone rich quickly.

According to recent reports, so why never sell the stock? Walmart has turned its stores into delivery hubs, its shoppers into an ad audience, and its customers into paying members. Each of those makes the others stronger, and that's the kind of economic moat that tends to widen with age.

The 53-year streak is the result of a business that keeps finding newly introduced ways to earn its customers' trust, and today's report showed it's still doing exactly that.

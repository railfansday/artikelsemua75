Tech Report: This dietary supplement may have an unexpected benefit for the brain

Industry observers note that A dietary supplement known as urolithin A may help the brain meet its increased demand for oxygen and nutrients during mentally challenging activities. In a clinical trial led by researchers at the University of Oklahoma, middle-aged adults with obesity who took the supplement experienced increased blood flow to the brain while performing a memory task. The findings, published in JAMA Network Open, could shed new light on how urolithin A influences blood vessel function and brain health.

According to recent reports, obesity can affect how blood vessels function, potentially disrupting circulation throughout the body and brain. To investigate whether urolithin A could help counter these effects, the research team recruited 50 adults with obesity between the ages of 40 and 64 for a randomized clinical trial. For four weeks, participants took either 1,000 milligrams of urolithin A each day or a placebo.

Urolithin A Increases Blood Flow to the Brain.

During a demanding memory exercise, participants who received urolithin A showed a greater increase in brain blood flow than those in the placebo group. The difference was especially noticeable in the left dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, an area that plays an key role in working memory, the ability to temporarily hold and process information. Industry observers note that however, the researchers observed no significant improvement in blood vessel function in the arm or in participants' performance on memory tests. According to the research team, this is the first study in humans to document an effect of urolithin A on the function of blood vessels in the brain. Industry observers note that "The brain needs an adequate supply of blood to meet the demands of mental activity," stated the study's first author, Ana Clara da Costa Pinaffi Langley, Ph.D., an assistant professor in the Department of Nutritional Sciences at the OU College of Allied Health on the OU Harold Hamm Health Campus. "We found that urolithin A increased blood flow to the brain during a memory task, suggesting the supplement may help the brain meet its increased need for oxygen and nutrients when it is working harder. We plan to conduct more research to understand what this finding means for brain health.".

A Natural Compound Produced by Gut Bacteria.

The human body can produce urolithin A with the help of certain gut bacteria. These microorganisms generate the substance by breaking down naturally occurring compounds in foods such as pomegranates, strawberries, and walnuts.

According to recent reports, not everyone produces the same amount of urolithin A. Previous research suggests that the ability to generate the compound naturally tends to diminish as people grow older and their body mass index increases, Langley explained. Urolithin A can also be obtained directly through dietary supplements.

It has been revealed that participants tolerated the supplement well throughout the trial. However, the researchers cautioned that the study involved a relatively small number of people at a single research site. Larger, longer clinical trials will be necessary to better understand the supplement's effects.

Could Urolithin A Support Healthier Brain Aging?

Although the increase in brain blood flow is encouraging, it remains unclear whether this change could translate into lasting improvements in cognitive health.

"These findings offer an interesting starting point for understanding how urolithin A affects the brain," said study co-author Andriy Yabluchanskiy, M.D., Ph.D., an associate professor in the Department of Neurosurgery at the OU College of Medicine. "Although we saw increased blood flow to the brain during mental activity, participants did not demonstrate measurable improvements in memory or attention. Future studies will need to determine whether taking the supplement for a longer period leads to meaningful improvements in cognitive function or other health benefits.".

For Langley, the broader aim is to extend healthspan, the portion of a person's life spent in good health.

It has been revealed that "The idea is to mitigate cognitive decline," she said. "We want to expand the time that people are living healthier lives.".

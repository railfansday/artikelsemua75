It has been revealed that holiday romances are a phenomenon unto themselves. They take a familiar romance trope and set it during Christmas, the most magical time of the year. Romance is heightened at Christmas because the holiday is all about giving and sharing love. Thoughtful gifts under the tree show people they care, mistletoe encourages couples to kiss, and families gathered around the fire are the perfect setting for a proposal.

Industry observers note that christmas levels up any romance, making it just that much sweeter, more spectacular, more special somehow. Watching Christmas romances is a great way for audiences to feel the warmth and sentiment of the season. Netflix has a wide catalog of holiday romances, some better than others. One of Netflix's first forays into Christmas romance, Christmas Inheritance is about as classic and cheesy as a holiday romantic comedy can get, but this inherent cheesiness adds to the film's charm. The film follows a spoiled Fresh York City socialite (Eliza Taylor) who must visit her father's small hometown in order to inherit his manufacturer. However, in the process, and after forging an unlikely connection with a local inn manager (Jake Lacy), she ends up learning a valuable lesson on the importance of hard work and supporting one another.

Sources indicate that the film may be doing the furthest thing from reinventing the wheel as far as Christmas rom-coms are concerned, but for what was one of Netflix's first outings with the genre, the film helped pave the way for many more Christmas movies to come. Taylor and Lacy have some genuine on-screen chemistry, the plot has a basic but effective message of being kind around the holidays, and Andie MacDowell does a great job as the standout supporting character.

In a recent development, while the wide array of English-language films that Netflix releases often comes to mind when considering their Christmas romance films, the platform has amassed such overwhelming global capital that it has released prominent original Christmas films from filmmakers and regions all around the world. One such standout that frankly deserved to get more recognition in North America is Christmas As Usual, a Norwegian production tackling the clashing cultures of India and Norway amidst a chaotic Christmas engagement party.

The must-see Scandinavian film plays into the classic conventions of other Christmas in-laws films, with the distinct flavor and passion for both of its respective cultures and the beauty that can come from the blending of cultures. Ida Ursin-Holm and Karan Gill make for a charming central couple, with their love and connection being clear from the very beginning of the film, largely having to deal with hurdles that attempt to get in the way of their love.

Industry observers note that dressing like an elf to photograph kids on Santa’s lap isn’t exactly Abby’s (Kat Graham) dream job, but it pays the bills. In The Holiday Calendar, Abby’s best friend Josh (Quincy Brown) is back in town, which helps ease the pain of the recent loss of her grandmother, who left her an antique advent calendar as her Christmas inheritance. The calendar seems to predict the future with small gifts that point Abby toward a new love interest, Ty (Ethan Peck).

The Holiday Calendar is a low-angst love story. Graham is absurdly talented, and her chemistry with Brown feels natural and authentic. Together, they elevate the material. The story features a warm and supportive social circle, including a wonderful turn by Ron Cephas Jones as Gramps. Watching with mulled wine and faux fur-lined slippers is optional but recommended.

A Christmas Romance entirely centered around the joy and whimsy that music can provide during the holiday season, Christmas With You tackles the difficulties of impostor syndrome and fading star power and how the holiday season and genuine love can help put things into perspective. The film sees a pop star (Aimee Garcia) granting a young fan's wish by visiting her Quinceañera, meeting her in person, in the process forming an unexpected bond with her musician father (Freddie Prinze Jr.).

Both Garcia and Prinze Jr. are fan-favorite stars of the past, so having them return and have joyous fun in this film was certainly a treat for fans of these iconic stars. The film itself makes great use of its central music and themes to be all sorts of fun in its execution, although it shares many similarities with another prominent streaming rom-com, Marry Me, only with more approachable Christmas energy over the whole thing.
